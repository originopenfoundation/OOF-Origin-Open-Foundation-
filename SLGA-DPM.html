Canonical Definition

DPM — Deployment Planning Module is the Core Module within SDPS that
governs how an identifiable software release is prepared for
deployment into a defined operational environment through the
establishment of a deployment purpose, target environment, deployment
scope, execution strategy, operational prerequisites, resource
requirements, verification conditions, intervention provisions, and
rollback or recovery planning references.

DPM establishes deployment planning as an identifiable, evidence-supported,

and reassessable governance mechanism rather than an informal
technical preparation activity.

It requires the intended deployment to remain distinguishable from its

authorization, execution, resulting production state, and operational
acceptance.

The module applies to manually planned deployments, automated delivery

pipelines, autonomous AI software agents, cloud-native infrastructure,
distributed systems, embedded devices, and robotic platforms.

Its principal governance output is the Software Deployment Plan
Profile.

Operational Role

DPM establishes the planning conditions required for a software release to

be considered for deployment into a particular operational
environment.

It governs:

  • Deployment purpose and intended outcome
  • Release-to-deployment association
  • Target environment identification
  • Deployment scope and boundaries
  • Deployment strategy selection
  • Deployment sequence and dependencies
  • Operational prerequisites
  • Resource and capability requirements
  • Execution actor and tool requirements
  • Deployment timing and coordination
  • Verification and acceptance conditions
  • Intervention and escalation provisions
  • Rollback and recovery planning
  • Planning exceptions and uncertainties
  • Deployment plan changes
  • Planning evidence and traceability
  • Plan readiness classification
  • Handoff to deployment authorization

DPM does not authorize deployment, execute deployment actions, verify
the resulting production state, or independently establish recovery
success.

Operational Question

How can an identifiable software release be prepared for deployment
into a defined operational environment through a sufficiently
specified, feasible, evidence-supported, and reassessable plan that
preserves deployment purpose, scope, prerequisites, execution
boundaries, verification conditions, and recovery provisions without
confusing planning readiness with deployment authorization or
operational success?

Its planning references also support subsequent deployment execution,

  • Governed Space

Software Deployment Planning Governance

This space governs the formation, evaluation, maintenance, and reassessment

of plans for introducing identifiable software releases into defined
operational environments.

It begins when a release is proposed for deployment into a particular
environment.

It ends its initial planning cycle when the plan receives a supported
classification and is submitted for authorization, deferred, rejected,
or withdrawn from consideration.

Planning governance may reopen when relevant release, environment,
authority, execution, operational, or recovery conditions change.

Foundational Principle

A deployment plan must establish what is intended, where it is
intended, under which conditions it may be attempted, how its outcome
will be verified, and how unacceptable outcomes will be addressed.

The existence of a technically executable deployment procedure does
not establish that the deployment is sufficiently planned.

Deployment Script Available ≠ Deployment Plan Sufficient.

Deployment Plan Complete ≠ Deployment Authorized.

Deployment Technically Feasible ≠ Deployment Operationally Acceptable.

Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum DPM implementation must establish eight governance layers.
Layer 1 — Deployment Identity

Identify the deployment plan, software release, deployment purpose, and

target environment. Layer 2 — Scope and State

Define the deployment boundary, existing operational state, and intended

operational state. Layer 3 — Strategy and Execution

Establish the deployment strategy, execution sequence, relevant actors,

tools, and dependencies. Layer 4 — Prerequisites and Feasibility

Identify mandatory prerequisites, operational constraints, resource

requirements, and material feasibility conditions. Layer 5 —
Verification

Define execution completion criteria, operational acceptance conditions,

monitoring requirements, and evidence sources. Layer 6 — Intervention
and Recovery

Identify failure scenarios, stop conditions, rollback feasibility, recovery

provisions, and irreversible actions. Layer 7 — Evidence and
Classification

Preserve planning evidence, uncertainties, exceptions, consistency

findings, and planning readiness classification. Layer 8 — Continuity
and Handoff

Preserve plan versions, material revisions, reassessment conditions, and

the traceable handoff to DAM.

The implementation may be manual, software-supported, automated, or
agent-assisted.

The governance requirements remain applicable regardless of implementation

technology.

Cross-Module Boundary

DPM → DAM

DPM establishes the proposed deployment and its planning basis.

DAM evaluates whether the deployment is legitimately authorized.

DPM → PVCM

DPM identifies the intended production version and target operational
state.

PVCM governs the identity and consistency of software versions actually

present in production.

DPM → DSMM

DPM defines verification conditions, monitoring requirements, and intended

operational outcomes.

DSMM evaluates the actual deployment state and available operational

evidence.

DPM → RRVM

DPM identifies intervention, rollback, and recovery provisions.

RRVM governs the applicable rollback or recovery decisions, execution

references, and restoration verification.

Planning Ready ≠ Deployment Authorized.

Intended Production State ≠ Observed Production State.

Recovery Planned ≠ Recovery Successfully Executed.

Use Case 1 — An AI Agent Produces a Technically Valid but Operationally

Incomplete Deployment Plan

An autonomous software agent prepares a deployment plan for a production

application.

The plan includes executable commands and valid infrastructure
credentials.

However, it does not identify the current production version,
operational verification criteria, or rollback conditions.

The deployment is technically executable.

DPM evaluates:

Release → Target Environment → Existing State → Execution Plan →

Verification Conditions → Recovery Provisions → Planning Evidence

The assessment identifies:

  • Execution commands are available
  • The target environment is identifiable
  • The existing operational state is insufficiently established
  • Operational verification criteria are incomplete
  • Recovery feasibility is unknown

The appropriate classification is:

Information Incomplete — Plan Not Ready for Authorization Assessment.

Technical Executability ≠ Planning Sufficiency.

A company prepares a software update requiring a database schema

DPM evaluates:

The assessment identifies:

  • The data transformation is materially irreversible

  • The stated rollback procedure is insufficient
  • Alternative recovery provisions are required

The appropriate classification is:

Recovery Feasibility Not Established.

Previous Version Available ≠ Operational Rollback Feasible.

Use Case 3 — A Global Deployment Plan Conceals Regional Operational

Differences

A software provider plans a simultaneous global rollout.

The plan identifies one release and one deployment procedure.

However, regional environments have different dependency versions,

infrastructure capacity, and maintenance restrictions.

The plan assumes uniform conditions.

DPM evaluates:

Release → Regional Environment Identity → Environment Conditions →

Strategy Suitability → Prerequisites → Execution Boundaries

The assessment identifies:

  • The software release is identifiable
  • The global deployment scope is defined
  • Regional operating conditions materially differ
  • The deployment strategy is not equally suitable across all
  • Environment-specific planning conditions are required

environments.

The appropriate classification is:

Plan Reassessment Required — Environment-Specific Conditions.

One Release ≠ One Universally Suitable Deployment Plan.

Canonical Boundary

DPM governs the formation, evaluation, maintenance, and reassessment of

software deployment plans for identifiable releases entering defined
operational environments.

It establishes deployment purpose, target environment, scope, strategy,

execution sequence, prerequisites, feasibility, actor and tool
requirements, verification conditions, intervention provisions,
rollback and recovery planning, evidence, and planning readiness
classification.

DPM does not independently authorize deployment, perform deployment
execution, establish actual production version identity, verify
operational deployment state, or execute rollback and recovery.

Its principal governance output is the:

Software Deployment Plan Profile

This output provides the planning basis for DAM and supporting references

for the remaining SDPS Core Modules.

Canonical Closing Statement

A software deployment plan is not sufficient merely because its
commands can execute.

A technically valid deployment procedure may target the wrong
environment.

A complete execution sequence may depend on resources that are
unavailable.

An automated pipeline may possess the necessary tools without
possessing legitimate deployment authority.

A deployment may reach its intended software version while violating
operational constraints.

And a rollback procedure may restore an earlier artifact without
restoring a compatible operational state.

DPM establishes the governance structure required to identify these

conditions before deployment authorization and execution.

It requires the intended release, target environment, operational state

transition, deployment strategy, prerequisites, verification
conditions, and recovery provisions to remain explicit and
evidence-supported.

It preserves planning uncertainty instead of converting assumptions into

verified facts.

It requires material changes to trigger reassessment rather than silently

modifying the basis of a deployment decision.

And it ensures that human operators, automated pipelines, and
autonomous agents remain subject to the same underlying planning
requirements.

A deployment plan must demonstrate more than how software can be
installed. It must establish what is intended to change, why the
transition is appropriate, under which conditions it may proceed, how
its outcome will be verified, and what must happen when the intended
operational state cannot be achieved.

DPM transforms deployment planning from a sequence of technical

instructions into a traceable, evidence-supported, and reassessable
governance basis for legitimate software deployment.
