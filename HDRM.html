HDRM — Harm Determination Record Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HDRM — Harm Determination Record Module governs the formation,
structure, identity, traceability, integrity, qualification, versioning,
correction, supersession, linkage, preservation, accessibility, and
lifecycle continuity of the authoritative Governed Harm Record produced
through formal Harm Determination.

HDRM converts the completed HDS Determination structure into a durable
methodological record that preserves what Harm was determined, its
governed boundary and Determination State, the basis and methodology
through which the determination was reached, the material limitations and
uncertainty that remain, and the history of subsequent changes.

HDRM establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Determination State + Determination Basis + Governed Harm
Boundary → Record Formation → Record Qualification → Record Integrity →
Governed Harm Record

Its central question is:

How must a formally determined Harm be represented and preserved so that
its identity, scope, Determination basis, limitations, history, and future
governance remain traceable?

  • Operational Role


HDS has four preceding methodological stages:

HDIM governs the qualified inputs.

HDCM-D governs the Determination Criteria.

HDEM governs the evaluation.

HDSM governs the formal Determination State.

HDRM converts those governed results into the durable Harm object that can
continue through the HAGA™ lifecycle.

Its operational role is:

to ensure that a Harm Determination does not disappear into a report,
database entry, decision, claim, case file, or temporary system state, but
becomes a persistent, identifiable, traceable, and governable Harm Record.

  • Position Within HDS


The HDS internal flow is:

Qualified Inputs → Determination Criteria → Evaluation → Determination
State → Governed Harm Record

The Core Module sequence is:

HDIM → HDCM-D → HDEM → HDSM → HDRM

HDRM receives:

Governed Harm Determination State

together with the relevant governed HDS basis.

It produces:

Governed Harm Record

for the next HAGA™ Parent Standard:

Harm State Standard (HSS)

  • Governed Object


The primary governed object is:

It is not merely:

  • Foundational Record Principle


The foundational principle is:

A Harm Determination must remain governable after the moment of
Determination has passed.

Therefore:

Determination Conclusion ≠ Complete Governed Harm Record

The record must preserve sufficient structure for future governance to
understand what was actually determined.

Record Creation Time ≠ Harm Emergence Time

It does not create it.

  • Minimum Implementation Framework


A minimum HDRM implementation requires eight capabilities.

  • Harm Record Formation


Create a distinguishable Governed Harm Record from the Governed Harm
Determination State and relevant HDS basis.

  • Boundary & State Preservation


Preserve the exact Harm Boundary, Determination State, Established and
unresolved scope, uncertainty, confidence, and material limitations.

  • Determination Lineage


Maintain traceability from the record to the Determination State,
evaluation, criteria, and qualified input basis without requiring
unnecessary duplication of underlying evidence.

  • Identity & Relationship Governance


Maintain persistent Harm identity and support governed relationships,
merge, separation, composite, distributed, and collective Harm structures.

  • Versioning & Historical Continuity


Support versioning, correction, supersession, reassessment, reopening, and
preservation of previous record states.

  • Semantic Integrity & Interoperability


Preserve HDS meanings across human-readable, machine-readable, and
interoperable representations without collapsing distinct Determination
States.

  • Integrity, Accessibility & Persistence


Protect the record against unauthorized alteration or loss while
maintaining legitimate governance accessibility and continuity across
technical or organizational change.

  • Governed Handoff


Produce the authoritative Governed Harm Record required for Harm State
governance under HSS.

A system that stores only a conclusion such as “Harm: Yes/No” without
Boundary, Determination State, lineage, limitations, uncertainty, and
history does not fully implement HDRM.

  • Governance Requirements


HDRM requires that:

  • every formally governed Harm remain distinguishable as a Harm
    object
  • record identity remain separate from Harm origin
  • the exact Governed Harm Boundary be preserved
  • Determination States retain their canonical meaning
  • Partial Establishment preserve established and unresolved scope
  • Determination lineage remain traceable
  • the Harm Record remain separate from incident, evidence, claim,


accountability, and liability records; - Harm identity remain stable
through legitimate lifecycle-state change; - record versioning not create
new Harm identity by default; - corrections and supersession preserve
history; - reassessment and reversal not erase earlier governance states;
- temporal distinctions between emergence, Recognition, Determination, and
record creation remain preserved; - uncertainty and limitations not
disappear through documentation; - Unknown not silently become No or Zero;
- relationships between Harm objects remain separate from identity; -
merge and separation preserve provenance; - composite, distributed, and
collective Harm remain representable; - machine readability preserve
methodological semantics; - interoperability preserve Determination State
and Boundary meaning; - unauthorized alteration or history loss be
prevented; - the record remain accessible to legitimate governance
processes; - technical, organizational, or mechanism change not
automatically terminate record continuity; - the Governed Harm Record
provide the authoritative handoff to HSS.

  • Governance Logic


HDRM applies the following logic:

Has HDSM produced a Governed Harm Determination State?

If no:

→ no final Governed Harm Record is formed through HDRM.

If yes:

→ identify the governed Harm object.

What exact Harm Boundary was determined?

→ preserve it.

What Determination State applies?

→ preserve it without semantic simplification.

Is the Harm Partially Established?

→ record Established Scope and Unresolved Scope separately.

What Determination lineage must remain traceable?

→ link the State to HDEM, HDCM-D, HDIM, and relevant governed basis.

What uncertainty, confidence, or limitations remain?

→ preserve them.

Does an existing Harm Record already represent the same Harm?

If yes:

→ update or version that record rather than create a duplicate Harm
identity.

Has the Harm materially changed identity or separated into another Harm
object?

→ apply governed relationship, merge, or separation logic.

Has new information changed the Determination?

→ preserve the prior state and create the appropriate reassessed version.

Has an error been corrected?

→ preserve both correction and history.

Can another system receive the record without losing its methodological
meaning?

If no:

→ do not treat data transfer as complete governance interoperability.

Then produce:

Governed Harm Record

for HSS.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HDRM — Harm Determination Record Module governs the formation,
identity, structure, qualification, lineage, versioning, correction,
supersession, reopening, relationship, merge, separation, integrity,
semantic preservation, interoperability, accessibility, persistence, and
historical continuity of the Governed Harm Record produced through HDS. It
preserves the formal Harm Determination and provides the durable governed
Harm object required for subsequent HAGA™ governance, but does not qualify
Determination inputs, establish Determination Criteria, perform
Determination Evaluation, independently assign the Determination State,
govern the evolving Harm lifecycle State, determine propagation,
containment, mitigation, recovery, Residual Harm, Resolution or Finality,
establish general causation, assign accountability or responsibility,
determine liability, govern claims or compensation, or replace evidence,
validation, integrity, operational reality, identity, AI behavior,
autonomous execution, simulation, prediction, Value, tax-event, or
jurisdiction-specific governance.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


A Harm Determination that cannot survive the system, report, organization,
or process in which it was created cannot provide reliable lifecycle
governance.

HDRM gives determined Harm a persistent methodological identity —
preserving what was established, what remained unresolved, how the
determination was reached, and how that understanding changed over time.

The record must follow the Harm, not the administrative systems that
happen to store it.

Once Harm becomes a governed object, its governance history must remain
part of that object.
