Canonical Definition

Authority-Aligned Decision Integrity Module (AADIM) defines the structural conditions
under which runtime operational decisions, delegated decision authority, distributed
decision coordination, and consequence-bearing decision states remain materially aligned
with governance-valid authority conditions across operational environments.

A system satisfies AADIM only if:
  • runtime operational decisions remain materially authority-aligned
  • delegated decision authority preserves governance validity
  • distributed decision coordination remains operationally coherent
  • consequence-bearing operational decisions remain authority-traceable
  • authority-invalid decision conditions do not destabilize operational
  • legitimacy


A system that preserves runtime execution while operational decisions materially diverge
from governance-valid authority

conditions does not satisfy AADIM.

Module Operational Role

AADIM defines the authority-aligned decision governance layer of ODIS by preserving
governance-valid decision authority continuity across runtime environments.

Module Operational Space

AADIM governs the authority-aligned decision space of ODIS by preserving delegated
authority continuity, runtime decision legitimacy, distributed authority coordination,
consequence-bearing decision accountability, and governance-valid operational alignment
across runtime systems.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:
  • authority-aligned runtime decisions
  • delegated decision legitimacy
  • distributed authority coordination
  • consequence-bearing decision accountability
  • governance-valid operational alignment
  • runtime authority traceability


Its function is to ensure that operational decisions remain materially aligned with
governance-valid authority conditions strongly enough to preserve operational legitimacy
across execution environments.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Authority-Aligned Decision Object

The organization must define which operational decision structures require
authority-alignment governance.

This may include:
  • delegated runtime decisions
  • orchestration-level authority routing
  • autonomous operational actions
  • escalation-sensitive authority conditions
  • distributed decision hierarchies
  • consequence-bearing runtime choices
  • operational-critical decision pathways


2. Define Authority-Aligned Decision Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which operational decisions remain materially
aligned with governance-valid authority conditions.

This includes:
  • delegated authority continuity
  • runtime authority traceability
  • distributed authority coherence
  • operational legitimacy alignment
  • governance-valid decision continuity


3. Define Authority-Invalid Decision Detection Logic

The system must define how materially unstable authority alignment or authority-invalid
operational decisions are identified.

This may include:
  • delegated authority corruption
  • runtime authority divergence
  • distributed authority fragmentation
  • escalation-sensitive authority instability
  • consequence-bearing authority misalignment


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for materially unstable authority-aligned decision
conditions.

Governance response may include:
  • authority synchronization
  • delegated authority restriction
  • escalation review activation
  • operational legitimacy reconstruction
  • distributed coordination stabilization
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of authority-aligned decision
continuity and authority-invalid decision states. A system must not remain decision-valid if
runtime operational decisions materially diverge from governance-valid authority conditions
while systems continue assuming operational legitimacy remains preserved.

Application
AADIM preserves governance-valid decision legitimacy through distributed authority

Result

Canonical Closing Statement

If runtime operational decisions cannot remain materially aligned with governance-valid
authority conditions, systems may preserve operational execution while decision
legitimacy progressively separates from operational accountability.
