HCRM — Harm Context Relationship Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HCRM — Harm Context Relationship Module governs how materially
relevant relationships among Harm Context elements are identified,
distinguished, qualified, structured, updated, and preserved so that
subjects, objects, systems, domains, temporal states, environmental
conditions, operational mechanisms, and other Harm-relevant elements can
be understood in relation to one another without converting contextual
association into causation, accountability, responsibility, or liability.

HCRM establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Context + Harm Subject Map + Harm Domain Context + Harm
Temporal Context → Relationship Identification → Relationship
Qualification → Relationship Structure → Relationship Sufficiency →
Governed Harm Context Relationship Map

Its central question is:

Which relationships among Harm-relevant elements must be governed in order
to understand the Harm Context without overstating what those
relationships prove?

  • Operational Role


Harm Context does not become meaningful merely because its elements have
been identified.

A person may be present.

A machine may be operating.

An AI system may have produced an output.

A harmful consequence may appear later.

An organization may control the relevant process.

An environmental condition may persist.

These facts become methodologically useful only when their relevant
relationships are understood.

HCRM provides that relational structure.

Its responsibility is:

to govern how Harm Context elements relate without allowing relationship
to become an unsupported conclusion about cause, responsibility, or
liability.

  • Position Within Harm Context Standard (HCS)

HCRM completes the Harm Context Standard by linking the elements already

Its output provides the relational foundation required for subsequent Harm

These may include relationships between:

  • subject and object

  • system and condition

  • condition and time period

  • upstream and downstream elements


  • Relationship Governance Trigger


HCRM activates where understanding the Harm Context requires more than
knowing that individual elements exist.

The trigger arises when the relation between two or more elements could
materially affect downstream Harm governance.

Co-Presence ≠ Material Relationship

A relationship should be governed because it matters, not merely because
elements occur within the same broader context.

Materially changed relationship maps should remain reconstructable.

Conceptually:

  • Relationship Sufficiency


The relationship structure is sufficiently governed when downstream Harm
governance can proceed without relying on material unstated, conflated, or
unsupported relationships.

The governing test is:

Would the next Harm governance step materially change if one of the
currently assumed relationships were wrong, missing, or misunderstood?

If yes:

→ further relationship governance is required.

  • Governance Logic


HCRM applies the following logic:

Are multiple Harm Context elements present?

If no:

→ preserve the absence of material contextual relationships.

If yes:

→ identify candidate relationships.

Is each candidate relationship materially relevant to Harm governance?

If no:

→ exclude it from the governed relationship map.

If yes:

→ determine its relationship type.

Is the relationship direct, indirect, sequential, concurrent, dependent,
conditional, or multi-step?

→ preserve the appropriate structure.

Is direction or asymmetry supported?

If yes:

→ represent it.

If no:

→ avoid unsupported directional interpretation.

Is the relationship established, uncertain, disputed, or unknown?

→ qualify it explicitly.

Would the relationship be interpreted as causation, responsibility,
accountability, or liability if left unqualified?

If yes:

→ apply the appropriate governance boundary.

Has new information materially changed the relationship structure?

If yes:

→ reassess while preserving historical versions.

Is the relationship map sufficient for the next legitimate Harm governance
step?

If no:

→ preserve a qualified or incomplete relationship state.

If yes:

→ produce the Governed Harm Context Relationship Map.

At every stage:

Association ≠ Causation

Control ≠ Liability

Participation ≠ Responsibility

Relationship ≠ Harm

  • Canonical Boundary


The HCRM — Harm Context Relationship Module governs the identification,
typing, qualification, direction, structure, uncertainty, evolution,
reassessment, and historical preservation of materially relevant
relationships among Harm Context elements. It supports direct, indirect,
multi-step, physical, digital, informational, organizational, operational,
environmental, temporal, dependency, control, authority, delegation,
ownership, permission, exposure-relevant, sequential, concurrent,
correlated, and associated relationships while preventing those
relationships from being converted automatically into Harm, causation,
responsibility, accountability, or liability. It does not independently
determine causation, establish Harm Exposure, determine Harm Emergence,
reconstruct Operational Reality in its entirety, govern authority or
accountability itself, establish evidence integrity, determine legal
responsibility, assign liability, or identify a guilty party.

HCM establishes the contextual elements.

HSM establishes who or what may be affected.

HDCM establishes the relevant Harm domains.

HTCM establishes the temporal structure.

HCRM establishes how those elements materially relate to one another
without allowing relationship to become conclusion.

Its principal output completes the contextual foundation required for the
next HAGA™ lifecycle stage:

Governed Harm Context → Harm Exposure Governance

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm Context is not merely a collection of facts. It is a governed
structure of relationships whose meaning must remain separate from the
conclusions those relationships may later support.

The HCRM — Harm Context Relationship Module prevents proximity from
becoming causation, control from becoming liability, participation from
becoming responsibility, and association from becoming proof.

HAGA™ can govern Harm legitimately only when it knows not just what exists
within the context, but how those elements relate — and exactly what those
relationships do not yet prove.
