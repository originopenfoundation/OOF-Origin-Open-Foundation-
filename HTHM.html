Canonical Definition

The HTHM — Harm Transition Module governs the final transition by which a
mapped, developing, condition-qualified, and materially apparent Harm
formation process crosses from pre-emergence governance into Existing Harm
.

HTHM determines whether the governed combination of formation pathway,
development state, emergence conditions, manifestation, timing,
uncertainty, and supporting reality is sufficient to establish that Harm
has begun to exist.

It establishes the transition:

Emergence Formation Map → Governed Harm Development State → Governed Harm
Emergence Condition Set → Governed Harm Appearance State → Harm Transition
Decision → Existing Harm State

Its central question is:

Has the governed reality legitimately crossed from the possibility and
formation of Harm into Harm that now exists?

  • Operational Role


HEMM determines:

Through what pathway may Harm be forming?

HDHM determines:

How is that pathway developing?

HCHM determines:

Under what conditions can that development become sufficient for Harm
Emergence?

HAHM determines:

How has the Harm-relevant consequence become apparent?

HTHM performs the final emergence-layer decision:

Has Harm actually begun to exist?

Its operational role is:

to establish the governed boundary between pre-emergence Harm formation
and Existing Harm without confusing Exposure, risk, adverse conditions,
appearance, reporting, recognition, evidence, causation, accountability,
or liability with Harm itself.

Existing Harm State

Harm Recognition Standard (HRS)

transition through which a mapped, developing, condition-qualified, and
materially supported Harm formation process crosses from pre-emergence

determination.

HTHM asks:

Has governed reality now crossed into Existing Harm?

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm does not begin when a report is filed, when an organization opens an
incident, when evidence becomes convenient, when accountability is
assigned, or when liability is finally decided.

Harm begins when governed reality crosses from the possibility and
formation of Harm into a materially harmful state.

HTHM closes the Harm Emergence layer by governing that boundary
explicitly, preserving the exact distinction on which the rest of HAGA™
depends: the difference between Harm that might occur and Harm that has
begun to exist.
