Canonical Definition

The Quality Conformity & Acceptance Module (QCAM) governs the assessment,

determination, authorization, recognition, limitation, documentation,
and lifecycle continuity of software quality conformity and
acceptance.

QCAM establishes the methodological conditions under which software quality

can be recognized as conforming to applicable quality criteria,
sufficiently validated for a defined intended use, and accepted by
competent authority within explicitly established software,
configuration, operational, evidentiary, and temporal boundaries.

It governs the relationships among software quality requirements,

assessable quality criteria, testing and coverage evidence, defect
governance states, intended-use conditions, quality conformity
determinations, residual quality limitations, acceptance authority,
acceptance decisions, acceptance conditions, and subsequent
reassessment.

QCAM distinguishes quality conformity from intended-use fitness, technical

assessment from acceptance authority, acceptance from release
authorization, conditional acceptance from unconditional acceptance,
and accepted software quality from proof of defect-free software.

It applies to software assessed or accepted by humans, organizations,

automated quality systems, AI coding assistants, autonomous
development agents, and software governance infrastructure.

QCAM is technology-independent and does not prescribe a particular
quality management framework, certification scheme, testing platform,
programming language, or software development methodology.

Its principal output is:

Governed Software Quality Conformity & Acceptance State

Its foundational distinction is:

Quality Acceptance ≠ Proof of Defect-Free Software.

Operational Role

The operational role of QCAM is:

To ensure that software quality conformity and acceptance decisions
are based on identifiable quality requirements, applicable criteria,
competent testing and defect evidence, demonstrated correspondence
with intended use, explicit residual limitations, and legitimate
acceptance authority, while preserving the boundaries and continuing
validity of each determination.

QCAM establishes the conformity and acceptance governance mechanism of
SQS.

It determines:

  • What software quality conditions are being assessed
  • Which criteria apply
  • What evidence supports conformity
  • Whether the evidence is sufficient
  • Which intended-use conditions have been validated
  • Which defects and limitations remain
  • Who has legitimate authority to accept the quality state
  • What the acceptance decision actually covers
  • Which conditions limit or invalidate that decision

QCAM does not independently establish software requirements, execute
tests, resolve defects, authorize software release, approve
deployment, or determine legal compliance.

Operational Question

Under which defined quality criteria, demonstrated software behavior,

intended-use conditions, evidence requirements, residual limitations,
and legitimate authority can a specific software artifact and
configuration be recognized as quality-conforming and accepted without
treating that acceptance as proof of universal correctness,
unrestricted fitness, or authorization for release and deployment?

Quality Conformity Governance Object

Every material quality conformity assessment shall establish a Quality

Conformity Governance Object.

The object represents an identifiable assessment of whether a defined

software artifact and configuration satisfy applicable quality
criteria within an established scope.

It should identify:

Assessment Identity: The identifiable conformity assessment.

Software Object: The software being evaluated.

Artifact Identity: The relevant software artifact.

Configuration Reference: The configuration under assessment.

Quality Requirements: The applicable governed requirements.

Quality Criteria: The criteria used for conformity assessment.

Intended Use: The operational purpose relevant to the assessment.

Testing Evidence: The supporting testing and coverage state.

Defect Evidence: The relevant defect governance state.

Residual Limitations: Known unresolved quality conditions.

Conformity Determination: The supported assessment outcome.

Evidence Basis: The competent evidence supporting that outcome.

Governance State: The recognized conformity condition.

The conformity governance object shall remain distinguishable from a

quality acceptance decision.

Quality Conformity Determination ≠ Quality Acceptance Decision.

Quality Acceptance Governance Object

Every material quality acceptance decision shall establish a Quality

Acceptance Governance Object.

The object represents the identifiable decision to accept, conditionally

accept, reject, defer, or otherwise determine the acceptance
eligibility of a defined software quality state.

It should identify:

Acceptance Identity: The identifiable decision.

Acceptance Subject: The software artifact and configuration.

Conformity Reference: The relevant quality conformity determination.

Intended-Use Scope: The operational conditions covered.

Acceptance Authority: The competent decision authority.

Decision Basis: The supporting evidence and criteria.

Residual Limitations: Known unresolved quality conditions.

Acceptance Conditions: Any restrictions or prerequisites.

Decision Outcome: The recognized acceptance determination.

Decision Validity: The conditions under which the decision remains

applicable.

Continuity Reference: The relationship to later changes and
reassessments.

An acceptance record shall not independently establish that its
underlying conformity evidence is competent.

Documented Acceptance ≠ Evidence-Supported Acceptance.

Quality Assessment Purpose

QCAM shall identify the purpose of each material conformity or acceptance

assessment.

Purposes may include:

  • Verification of quality requirement satisfaction
  • Assessment of software readiness for intended use
  • Evaluation of residual quality limitations
  • Determination of acceptance eligibility
  • Reassessment following software changes
  • Review of previously accepted software quality

The assessment purpose shall remain connected to the applicable software

object and quality criteria.

Assessment Purpose ≠ Assessment Outcome.

Quality Assessment Scope

QCAM shall establish the boundaries of each material quality
assessment.

Scope may concern:

  • Software functions
  • Components
  • Applications
  • Integrated systems
  • Artifact versions
  • Configurations
  • Operating environments
  • Intended-use scenarios
  • User populations
  • Relevant quality characteristics

A conformity determination shall not be generalized beyond its established

scope without competent justification.

Quality Conformity Within Scope ≠ Universal Software Conformity.

Software Object Correspondence

QCAM shall identify the software object to which conformity and acceptance

determinations apply.

The object may be:

  • A software component
  • An application
  • A service
  • An embedded software system
  • An integrated software product
  • A defined software configuration

Acceptance of one software component shall not automatically establish

acceptance of the entire integrated system.

Component Quality Acceptance ≠ System Quality Acceptance.

Artifact Identity Governance

QCAM shall preserve the identity of the software artifact actually
assessed.

Relevant information may include:

  • Artifact identifier
  • Version
  • Build reference
  • Content integrity reference
  • Relevant provenance
  • Configuration reference

A declared version label shall not independently establish the identity of

the artifact assessed.

Declared Accepted Version ≠ Verified Accepted Artifact.

Artifact Version Correspondence

QCAM shall preserve the relationship between quality evidence and the

artifact version for which acceptance is sought.

Evidence from one version shall not automatically establish conformity of

another.

Material version differences shall remain explicit.

Quality Acceptance of Version A ≠ Quality Acceptance of Version B.

Configuration Correspondence

QCAM shall identify the software configuration to which conformity and

acceptance apply.

Relevant configuration conditions may include:

  • Runtime settings
  • Dependency versions
  • Feature flags
  • Build parameters
  • Integration configuration
  • Relevant external services

Acceptance of one configuration shall not automatically extend to

materially different configurations.

Accepted Configuration ≠ Every Supported Configuration.

Quality Requirement Correspondence

QCAM shall preserve the relationship between conformity determinations and

governed quality requirements.

Requirements should remain identifiable as:

  • Satisfied by competent evidence
  • Partially satisfied
  • Not satisfied
  • Not assessed
  • Not applicable
  • Unresolved

A declared requirement status shall not independently establish
conformity.

Requirement Marked Satisfied ≠ Demonstrated Requirement Satisfaction.

Quality Criteria Applicability

QCAM shall determine which governed quality criteria apply to the assessed

software and intended use.

Applicability may depend on:

  • Software purpose
  • Quality characteristics
  • Operational environment
  • User requirements
  • Integration conditions
  • Relevant limitations

QCAM shall not silently modify criteria to produce a favorable conformity

result.

Quality Criterion Applicability ≠ Quality Criterion Satisfaction.

Quality Criteria Validity

QCAM shall assess whether the criteria used for conformity determination

remain applicable and sufficiently established.

Relevant considerations may include:

  • Criterion identity
  • Approved interpretation
  • Applicable threshold
  • Assessment method
  • Evidence requirements
  • Revision status
  • Material limitations

Criteria definition remains governed by QCDM.

QCAM evaluates whether the established criteria are suitable for the
conformity determination being made.

Established Criterion ≠ Currently Applicable Criterion.

Intended-Use Definition

QCAM shall identify the intended use against which software quality fitness

is evaluated.

Intended use may concern:

  • Operational purpose
  • Relevant users
  • Required functionality
  • Expected workload
  • Operating environment
  • Critical operating conditions
  • Relevant dependencies

A general statement that software is ready shall not substitute for
identifiable intended-use conditions.

Declared Intended Use ≠ Demonstrated Intended-Use Fitness.

Intended-Use Boundaries

QCAM shall establish the boundaries of the intended use covered by a

quality assessment.

Boundaries may include:

  • Supported functions
  • User groups
  • Workload ranges
  • Operating conditions
  • Environmental restrictions
  • Data characteristics
  • Relevant exclusions

Acceptance shall remain bounded by these conditions.

Accepted Intended Use ≠ Unrestricted Software Use.

Operational Context Governance

QCAM shall preserve the operational context relevant to quality conformity

and acceptance.

Context may include:

  • Expected operating conditions
  • User interactions
  • Integration dependencies
  • Data volume
  • Resource availability
  • Failure conditions
  • Recovery expectations

A technically successful assessment in a limited environment shall not

automatically establish operational fitness in materially different
conditions.

Technical Test Success ≠ Demonstrated Operational Fitness.

Quality Evidence Sufficiency

QCAM shall determine whether available evidence is sufficient to
support the intended conformity claim.

Evidence sufficiency may depend on:

  • Applicable criteria
  • Testing scope
  • Test validity
  • Coverage limitations
  • Defect states
  • Artifact correspondence
  • Configuration correspondence
  • Intended-use conditions

Evidence may be authentic and technically valid while insufficient for
the claimed acceptance scope.

Valid Quality Evidence ≠ Sufficient Quality Evidence.

Testing Evidence Correspondence

QCAM shall preserve correspondence between conformity determinations and

the testing evidence governed by TCMM.

Relevant information may include:

  • Tested artifact
  • Tested configuration
  • Applicable criteria
  • Execution evidence
  • Verified results
  • Coverage scope
  • Testing exclusions
  • Unresolved testing conditions

A passing test report shall not independently establish quality
conformity.

Passing Test Report ≠ Quality Conformity Determination.

Coverage Adequacy Assessment

QCAM shall evaluate whether the testing coverage available is adequate
for the quality determination sought.

Adequacy shall be assessed relative to:

  • Applicable requirements
  • Quality criteria
  • Intended use
  • Operational consequences
  • Relevant failure conditions
  • Known coverage gaps

High numerical coverage shall not automatically establish adequacy.

High Test Coverage ≠ Sufficient Quality Evidence.

Defect State Correspondence

QCAM shall preserve correspondence with defect governance states

established by DFM.

Relevant conditions may include:

  • Confirmed defects
  • Unconfirmed defects
  • Corrected defects
  • Verified resolutions
  • Residual defects
  • Deferred defects
  • Critical defect gaps
  • Unresolved verification evidence

Administrative defect closure shall not independently establish defect

resolution.

Closed Defect Record ≠ Verified Quality Conformity.

Known Defect Assessment

QCAM shall assess the significance of known defects for the relevant

conformity and acceptance determination.

Assessment may consider:

  • Defect severity
  • Operational impact
  • Exposure
  • Affected functionality
  • Available workarounds
  • Residual limitations
  • Relevant intended-use conditions

Known defects shall not automatically invalidate every quality acceptance

determination.

Their significance must be assessed against the applicable criteria
and acceptance scope.

Known Defect Existence ≠ Automatic Quality Rejection.

Residual Quality Limitations

QCAM shall identify material quality limitations remaining after
assessment.

Limitations may include:

  • Known defects
  • Incomplete coverage
  • Unverified operating conditions
  • Unsupported configurations
  • Performance restrictions
  • Reliability uncertainty
  • Incomplete intended-use validation

Residual limitations shall remain explicit in the acceptance decision.

Residual Limitation Disclosure ≠ Residual Limitation Acceptance.

Quality Conformity Determination

QCAM shall establish a supported determination of whether applicable

quality criteria have been satisfied.

Possible determinations include:

Conforming: Competent evidence supports satisfaction of the applicable

criteria within the assessed scope.

Partially Conforming: Evidence supports satisfaction of some but not all

applicable criteria.

Nonconforming: Competent evidence demonstrates failure to satisfy one or

more applicable criteria.

Inconclusive: Available evidence does not support a sufficient
determination.

Not Assessed: The relevant criteria have not been evaluated.

Evidence-Limited: Material evidence deficiencies prevent competent

interpretation.

Conformity determination shall remain distinct from acceptance
authority.

Demonstrated Quality Conformity ≠ Authorized Quality Acceptance.

Partial Quality Conformity

QCAM shall recognize conditions where software satisfies only part of
the applicable quality criteria.

Partial conformity may arise from:

  • Incomplete testing
  • Unresolved quality requirements
  • Known defects
  • Limited environment coverage
  • Conditional performance
  • Unverified functionality

Partial conformity shall not be represented as complete conformity.

Partial Quality Conformity ≠ Full Quality Conformity.

Quality Nonconformity

QCAM shall recognize demonstrated failure to satisfy applicable quality

criteria.

Nonconformity shall preserve:

  • Criterion identity
  • Affected software object
  • Supporting evidence
  • Relevant defect references
  • Operational significance
  • Applicable scope

A nonconformity determination shall remain distinguishable from an

administrative decision to reject software acceptance.

Quality Nonconformity ≠ Automatically Rejected Acceptance Decision.

Conformity Uncertainty

QCAM shall preserve uncertainty affecting quality conformity
determinations.

Uncertainty may arise where:

  • Evidence is incomplete
  • Test results conflict
  • Coverage is insufficient
  • Artifact correspondence is unresolved
  • Defect verification remains pending
  • Intended-use conditions are insufficiently represented

Uncertainty shall not be silently converted into conformity.

Unresolved Quality Evidence ≠ Demonstrated Conformity.

Intended-Use Validation

QCAM shall assess whether the software quality evidence supports fitness

for the defined intended use.

Validation shall consider:

  • Operational purpose
  • Applicable quality requirements
  • Relevant quality criteria
  • Tested scenarios
  • Observed behavior
  • Defect conditions
  • Environmental correspondence
  • Residual limitations

Conformity with individual technical criteria shall not automatically

establish fitness for the complete intended use.

Technical Quality Conformity ≠ Intended-Use Validation.

Operational Fitness Assessment

QCAM shall evaluate whether the assessed software quality is
sufficiently aligned with the relevant operational conditions.

Operational fitness may depend on:

  • Expected workload
  • User interaction
  • Reliability expectations
  • Performance constraints
  • Integration behavior
  • Recovery requirements
  • Environmental conditions

Operational fitness shall remain bounded by available evidence.

Fitness for One Operational Context ≠ Fitness for Every Operational

Context.

Quality Acceptance Eligibility

QCAM shall determine whether a software quality state is eligible for
acceptance consideration.

Eligibility may require:

  • Identifiable software artifact
  • Applicable quality criteria
  • Competent conformity assessment
  • Relevant intended-use validation
  • Known defect assessment
  • Residual limitation disclosure
  • Sufficient evidence
  • Available competent acceptance authority

Eligibility shall remain distinct from an actual acceptance decision.

Acceptance Eligibility ≠ Acceptance Authorization.

Acceptance Authority Governance

QCAM shall preserve the legitimate authority under which quality acceptance

decisions are made.

Authority may derive from:

  • Organizational governance
  • Assigned quality responsibility
  • Contractual arrangements
  • Delegated decision authority
  • Applicable institutional procedures

Authority shall remain identifiable and appropriately scoped.

Technical expertise alone shall not establish legitimate acceptance

authority.

Quality Assessment Competence ≠ Quality Acceptance Authority.

Acceptance Authority Scope

QCAM shall establish the boundaries of acceptance authority.

Authority scope may concern:

  • Software product
  • Component
  • Artifact version
  • Quality characteristics
  • Intended use
  • Operational context
  • Permitted residual limitations
  • Decision duration

Authority to accept one quality condition shall not automatically extend to

every software quality condition.

Authority to Accept One Scope ≠ Authority to Accept Every Scope.

Human Acceptance Authority

QCAM shall preserve the role of competent human or organizational authority

where required.

Human acceptance may be necessary where:

  • Material operational consequences exist
  • Residual limitations require judgment
  • Institutional accountability applies
  • Delegated authority requires human confirmation
  • Automated conclusions remain insufficient

Human involvement shall not automatically establish the competence of the

decision.

Human Approval ≠ Competent Quality Acceptance.

Automated Acceptance Authority

QCAM shall recognize acceptance activities supported or performed by

automated systems.

Automated systems may:

  • Aggregate evidence
  • Evaluate predefined criteria
  • Identify conformity gaps
  • Generate acceptance recommendations
  • Execute bounded acceptance decisions where legitimately
    authorized

Automated execution shall remain within explicit authority boundaries.

An AI system's ability to produce an acceptance recommendation shall not

establish authority to approve software quality.

Automated Acceptance Recommendation ≠ Authorized Acceptance Decision.

Acceptance Decision Identity

QCAM shall establish identifiable references for material acceptance

decisions.

A decision should preserve:

  • Decision identity
  • Decision subject
  • Acceptance authority
  • Applicable criteria
  • Evidence basis
  • Intended-use scope
  • Decision outcome
  • Relevant conditions
  • Decision time
  • Validity boundaries

Repeated decisions shall remain distinguishable.

Same Software Artifact ≠ Same Acceptance Decision.

Acceptance Decision Basis

QCAM shall preserve the evidentiary and methodological basis supporting

each acceptance decision.

The basis may include:

  • Quality requirements
  • Applicable criteria
  • Conformity determination
  • Intended-use validation
  • Testing evidence
  • Defect states
  • Residual limitations
  • Competent authority

A decision without sufficient supporting evidence shall remain

distinguishable from evidence-supported acceptance.

Recorded Acceptance Decision ≠ Justified Acceptance Decision.

Acceptance Decision Outcomes

QCAM shall support identifiable acceptance outcomes.

Possible outcomes include:

Accepted: The software quality state is accepted within defined
conditions and scope.

Conditionally Accepted: Acceptance is granted subject to explicit
limitations or prerequisites.

Rejected: The quality state is not accepted for the relevant purpose.

Deferred: The acceptance decision is postponed pending additional
evidence or action.

Suspended: A previously recognized acceptance is temporarily not
relied upon.

Withdrawn: A previously recognized acceptance has been revoked by

competent authority.

Superseded: A later decision has replaced the earlier acceptance

reference.

Unresolved: Available information does not support a sufficiently
determined outcome.

Decision outcomes shall remain distinguishable from technical conformity

states.

Accepted Quality State ≠ Universally Conforming Software.

Acceptance Conditions

QCAM shall preserve explicit conditions attached to quality
acceptance.

Conditions may concern:

  • Supported software version
  • Required configuration
  • Intended-use restrictions
  • Workload limitations
  • Known defects
  • Required monitoring
  • Required corrective action
  • Reassessment triggers
  • Time-limited validity

Acceptance conditions shall remain identifiable and enforceable
through competent downstream governance.

Acceptance with Conditions ≠ Unrestricted Acceptance.

Conditional Quality Acceptance

QCAM shall support quality acceptance under explicitly bounded conditions

where competent authority permits it.

Conditional acceptance may be appropriate where:

  • A known defect has limited impact
  • A noncritical quality criterion remains partially satisfied
  • A specific configuration is verified
  • An intended-use restriction is established
  • Additional evidence is required before broader acceptance

Conditional acceptance shall not conceal unresolved critical quality

conditions.

Conditional Quality Acceptance ≠ Complete Quality Conformity.

Acceptance Exclusions

QCAM shall identify software functions, configurations, environments, or

use cases excluded from acceptance.

Exclusions may include:

  • Unsupported functionality
  • Untested integrations
  • Unverified platforms
  • Specific workload conditions
  • Known problematic configurations
  • Experimental features

Exclusions shall remain distinguishable from accepted scope.

Acceptance Exclusion ≠ Accepted Quality Condition.

Acceptance Restrictions

QCAM shall preserve restrictions governing reliance on an accepted quality

state.

Restrictions may concern:

  • Permitted use
  • Operational scale
  • Supported users
  • Workload
  • Environment
  • Duration
  • Required safeguards

A restriction shall not be interpreted as evidence that the underlying
software defect or limitation has been eliminated.

Operational Restriction ≠ Technical Defect Resolution.

Acceptance Validity

QCAM shall establish the conditions under which a quality acceptance

decision remains valid.

Validity may depend on:

  • Artifact identity
  • Configuration
  • Intended use
  • Applicable criteria
  • Supporting evidence
  • Defect conditions
  • Operational assumptions
  • Decision authority

Acceptance shall not be treated as permanently valid independent of

material changes.

Previously Accepted Quality ≠ Permanently Accepted Quality.

Temporal Acceptance Boundaries

QCAM shall preserve time-related limitations affecting quality
acceptance.

These may include:

  • Acceptance date
  • Relevant evidence date
  • Time-limited conditions
  • Review requirements
  • Reassessment deadlines
  • Supersession events

The passage of time alone shall not necessarily invalidate acceptance.

However, material changes or expired acceptance conditions may require
reassessment.

Historical Acceptance ≠ Current Acceptance Validity.

Acceptance Evidence Governance

QCAM shall preserve competent evidence supporting acceptance
decisions.

Evidence may include:

  • Conformity assessment
  • Quality criteria references
  • Testing and coverage records
  • Defect governance states
  • Intended-use validation
  • Residual limitation records
  • Authority references
  • Decision records

Evidence shall remain connected to the actual acceptance subject.

Acceptance Documentation ≠ Acceptance Evidence Sufficiency.

Acceptance Evidence Competence

QCAM shall assess whether the evidence supporting acceptance is
suitable for the decision being made.

Competence may depend on:

  • Relevant software artifact
  • Applicable criteria
  • Suitable testing evidence
  • Defect verification
  • Intended-use correspondence
  • Known limitations
  • Valid authority

Evidence sufficient for a limited component acceptance may be
insufficient for an integrated system acceptance.

Competent Component Evidence ≠ Competent System Acceptance Evidence.

Acceptance Evidence Integrity

QCAM shall preserve the integrity of material acceptance evidence.

Relevant considerations may include:

  • Evidence source
  • Artifact correspondence
  • Record integrity
  • Decision identity
  • Temporal relevance
  • Material contradictions
  • Preservation of historical records

An intact acceptance record shall not independently establish that the
decision was competent.

Intact Acceptance Record ≠ Valid Quality Acceptance.

Acceptance Conflict

An Acceptance Conflict exists where material acceptance evidence,
authority references, conditions, or decisions are incompatible.

Examples may include:

  • Conflicting conformity assessments
  • Contradictory defect states
  • Incompatible acceptance decisions
  • Conflicting authority claims
  • Contradictory artifact references
  • Inconsistent intended-use conditions

QCAM shall preserve the conflict and its supporting evidence.

Conflicting Acceptance Evidence ≠ Permission to Select the Preferred

Decision.

Acceptance Evidence Gap

An Acceptance Evidence Gap exists where material information required
for competent acceptance determination remains unavailable or
insufficient.

Examples may include:

  • Missing testing evidence
  • Unverified artifact identity
  • Incomplete coverage assessment
  • Unresolved defect verification
  • Missing intended-use validation
  • Unclear acceptance authority
  • Missing residual limitation assessment

An evidence gap shall not automatically establish nonconformity.

Acceptance Evidence Gap ≠ Proven Software Nonconformity.

Critical Acceptance Gap

A Critical Acceptance Gap exists where missing or unresolved evidence
prevents competent acceptance determination for a materially
consequential quality condition.

Examples may include:

- A critical financial calculation without sufficient quality

verification. - A safety-relevant function without competent
intended-use validation. - An unresolved high-impact defect whose
correction has not been verified. - An acceptance decision lacking
identifiable authority. - A release candidate whose tested artifact
identity is unresolved.

QCAM shall preserve the critical gap for competent governance.

A critical acceptance gap prevents an unsupported positive acceptance

determination for the affected scope; it does not independently
establish that the software has failed or that release must be
rejected under another standard.

Critical Acceptance Gap ≠ Automatic Software Failure Determination.

Acceptance Continuity

QCAM shall preserve acceptance relationships across software lifecycle

changes.

Continuity may involve:

  • Historical acceptance decisions
  • Artifact revisions
  • Configuration changes
  • Updated quality criteria
  • New defect evidence
  • Intended-use changes
  • Authority changes
  • Reassessment history

Historical acceptance records shall remain identifiable.

Acceptance Record Continuity ≠ Automatic Acceptance Continuation.

Acceptance Reassessment

QCAM shall support reassessment when material conditions affecting
acceptance change.

Triggers may include:

  • New software versions
  • Configuration changes
  • Dependency changes
  • Revised quality requirements
  • Updated quality criteria
  • Newly discovered defects
  • Failed defect verification
  • New intended-use conditions
  • Contradictory evidence
  • Changes in acceptance authority

Reassessment shall preserve relevant historical determinations.

Acceptance Reassessment ≠ Historical Decision Erasure.

Acceptance Suspension

QCAM shall recognize temporary suspension of reliance on an acceptance

decision.

Suspension may arise where:

  • Material evidence becomes unreliable
  • Critical quality conditions become unresolved
  • Acceptance conditions are violated
  • Relevant software correspondence is lost
  • Competent authority requires reassessment

Suspension shall remain distinguishable from withdrawal.

Acceptance Suspension ≠ Acceptance Withdrawal.

Acceptance Withdrawal

QCAM shall recognize withdrawal of previously granted quality acceptance by

competent authority.

Withdrawal may arise from:

  • Material nonconformity
  • Invalid supporting evidence
  • Significant defect findings
  • Invalid acceptance authority
  • Loss of essential acceptance conditions

Withdrawal shall preserve the original decision and its historical
basis.

Acceptance Withdrawal ≠ Erasure of Historical Acceptance.

Acceptance Supersession

QCAM shall preserve relationships between earlier and later acceptance

decisions.

A later acceptance may supersede an earlier decision where:

  • A new artifact is assessed
  • Criteria change
  • Intended use changes
  • Additional evidence becomes available
  • Acceptance conditions are revised

Conformity and Acceptance Governance States

QCAM shall support identifiable governance states.

Possible conformity states include:

Assessment-Pending: Conformity assessment has not been completed.

Assessment-Active: Conformity assessment is underway.

Conforming: Competent evidence supports applicable quality criteria.

Partially-Conforming: Only part of the applicable criteria is
sufficiently satisfied.

Nonconforming: Competent evidence demonstrates non-satisfaction of

applicable criteria.

Inconclusive: Available evidence does not support a sufficient
determination.

Evidence-Limited: Material evidence deficiencies remain.

Conflict-Detected: Material conformity evidence is incompatible.

Critical-Gap: A materially consequential quality condition lacks

sufficient evidence.

Possible acceptance states include:

Acceptance-Pending: Acceptance has not yet been determined.

Acceptance-Eligible: Conditions for competent acceptance consideration

are established.

Accepted: Competent authority has accepted the quality state within
defined boundaries.

Conditionally-Accepted: Acceptance is subject to explicit conditions.

Rejected: Competent authority has not accepted the quality state.

Deferred: Acceptance consideration has been postponed.

Suspended: Reliance on an earlier acceptance is temporarily suspended.

Withdrawn: An earlier acceptance has been revoked.

Superseded: A later acceptance decision has replaced the earlier

reference.

Reassessment-Required: Material changes require renewed evaluation.

Unresolved: Available information does not support a sufficiently
determined state.

Conformity states and acceptance states shall remain distinguishable.

Software may be technically conforming without an authorized
acceptance decision.

A software quality state may also be conditionally accepted while
known limitations remain explicitly governed.

Quality Conformity State ≠ Quality Acceptance State.

Partially-Conforming

for the assessed quality scope.

Evidence-Limited

Canonical Closing Statement

Software quality is not accepted merely because testing is complete,
defects are closed, quality metrics appear favorable, or an automated
system declares that the software is ready.

A software artifact may satisfy defined technical criteria while
remaining insufficiently validated for its intended use.

A quality assessment may be technically competent while the actor
issuing acceptance lacks legitimate authority.

A software system may be accepted for one configuration while
remaining unverified for another.

A known defect may be compatible with explicitly bounded acceptance
while an undisclosed residual limitation may invalidate the basis of
that decision.

And a previously accepted software quality state may require
reassessment when the artifact, configuration, intended use, or
supporting evidence materially changes.

Quality Conformity Determination ≠ Quality Acceptance Decision.

Technical Quality Conformity ≠ Intended-Use Validation.

Acceptance Eligibility ≠ Acceptance Authorization.

Conditional Quality Acceptance ≠ Complete Quality Conformity.

Quality-Accepted ≠ Release-Authorized.

Quality Acceptance ≠ Proof of Defect-Free Software.

These distinctions become increasingly important as software creation,

testing, assessment, and decision support become more automated.

An autonomous system may generate convincing quality evidence.

It may identify apparent conformity.

It may recommend acceptance.

But the technical ability to produce an assessment does not
independently establish legitimate authority to accept software
quality or determine the conditions under which others may rely on
that acceptance.

QCAM therefore governs the relationship between demonstrated quality

conditions, intended-use fitness, competent evidence, residual
limitations, legitimate acceptance authority, and the continuing
validity of acceptance decisions.

It does not require software to be free of every possible defect
before acceptance can be considered.

It requires known limitations to remain explicit and acceptance decisions

to be bounded by competent evidence and legitimate authority.

It does not equate acceptance with release or deployment.

It establishes the quality-specific state upon which those separate

governance decisions may subsequently rely.

QCAM transforms software quality acceptance from a declaration, checklist

completion, management approval, or automated readiness status into a
governed determination — ensuring that software organizations can
establish which quality conditions have actually been demonstrated,
which intended uses are sufficiently supported, what limitations
remain, who is authorized to accept them, and under which conditions
that acceptance remains valid.

Software quality is not governed because it has been declared
acceptable. It is governed when conformity, intended-use validation,
evidence sufficiency, residual limitations, acceptance authority,
decision boundaries, and lifecycle continuity remain sufficiently
established to support competent and accountable quality acceptance.
