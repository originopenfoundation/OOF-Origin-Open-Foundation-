Governed Space

Integrity Conditions

Core Question

Which conditions must remain true for integrity to exist?

Canonical Definition

Integrity Condition Standard (ICS) defines the mandatory governance conditions
that must remain continuously satisfied for a governed entity to preserve its
integrity throughout its complete lifecycle. It establishes the governing
rules, constraints, dependencies, and operational conditions under which
integrity remains valid, measurable, and governable.

Governance Flow Position

Definition → Structure → Conditions → Measurement → State → Deviation → Breach
→ Response → Restoration → Assurance

Standard Abstract

A defined integrity model and a structurally sound architecture alone do not
guarantee that integrity will be preserved. Integrity exists only while
specific governance conditions continue to be satisfied.

Building upon the structural foundation established by STIS, Integrity
Condition Standard defines the mandatory conditions required for integrity to
remain valid throughout the governed lifecycle.

ICS establishes these mandatory governance conditions and provides the
operational foundation upon which integrity can later be measured, monitored,
evaluated, and protected.

When one or more mandatory conditions cease to exist, integrity enters a
governable state of degradation that can subsequently be detected, classified,
and managed by later standards within INTEGROS®.

Operational Role

Integrity Condition Standard governs the mandatory conditions required to
preserve integrity. It establishes the governance rules that determine whether
integrity remains continuously valid before integrity is measured, classified,
or evaluated.

Operational Space

ICS governs:

  • integrity conditions
  • governance constraints
  • integrity rules
  • dependency conditions
  • boundary conditions
  • operational validity
  • integrity prerequisites.


This governed space ensures that integrity is maintained through continuously
satisfied governance conditions rather than assumptions.

Why This Standard Exists

Integrity is not maintained simply because it has been defined or supported by
an appropriate structure.

It remains valid only while its governing conditions continue to exist.

ICS exists because Integrity Conditions represent the third governed space of
INTEGROS® — Integrity Governance Architecture, establishing the mandatory
governance requirements that preserve integrity before measurement and state
evaluation begin.

Foundational Principle

Integrity exists only while its governing conditions remain true. When
mandatory integrity conditions are no longer satisfied, integrity begins to
degrade regardless of the underlying structure.

Canonical Closing Statement

Integrity Condition Standard establishes the mandatory governance conditions
required for integrity to remain continuously valid. By governing the rules,
constraints, dependencies, and operational requirements that preserve
integrity, ICS provides the essential bridge between structural integrity and
measurable integrity throughout the complete lifecycle of every governed
entity.
