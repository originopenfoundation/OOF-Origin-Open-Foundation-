Canonical Definition

The HEBM — Harm Effect Boundary Module governs how the effects belonging
to a recognized Harm object are identified, differentiated, associated,
included, excluded, grouped, separated, expanded, contracted, disputed,
reassessed, and preserved across subjects, domains, systems, locations,
and time.

HEBM establishes the transition:

Governed Boundary Identification State + Governed Harm Subject Boundary +
Governed Harm Temporal Boundary → Effect Candidates → Effect Relationship
Assessment → Effect Inclusion / Exclusion → Governed Harm Effect Boundary

Its central question is:

Which effects legitimately belong to the governed Harm and which effects
remain outside it?

  • Operational Role


A recognized Harm may produce more than one observable consequence.

It may involve:

direct effects

indirect effects

delayed effects

persistent effects

functional effects

cross-domain effects

collective effects

distributed effects

and effects that only become visible after the originating mechanism has
stopped.

HEBM determines which of those effects belong to the Harm being bounded.

Its operational role is:

to establish the legitimate effect extent of Harm without reducing Harm to
its first visible manifestation or expanding it to every remotely
associated consequence.

  • Foundational Effect Principle


The foundational principle is:

The first visible effect is not necessarily the complete Harm.

Therefore:

Observed Effect ≠ Complete Harm Effect Boundary

  • Effect vs Manifestation


A Harm Manifestation is how Harm becomes apparent or observable.

A Harm Effect is a materially affected condition belonging to the Harm.

The two may overlap but are not identical.

Therefore:

Manifestation ≠ Complete Harm

and:

Manifestation ≠ Effect by Default

One underlying Harm effect may generate several manifestations.

Several effects may also become visible through one manifestation.

  • Effect Inclusion


An effect may be included where a materially supportable relationship
exists between the effect and the governed Harm.

The relationship may be:

direct

indirect

persistent

delayed

functional

cross-domain

collective

distributed

or:

composite

Inclusion establishes that the effect belongs within the governed Harm.

It does not establish general causation, accountability, liability, or
compensation.

  • Effect Exclusion


An effect may be excluded where it is:

unrelated

insufficiently associated

duplicative

better represented as another Harm object

or:

outside the supported effect scope

Exclusion must remain reasoned and traceable.

Canonical distinction:

Excluded Effect ≠ No Harm Effect Elsewhere

  • Direct Effects


A Direct Effect has a materially immediate relationship to the governed
Harm.

Examples may include:

physical impairment

system degradation

data corruption

functional loss

environmental damage

Directness may strengthen inclusion.

It does not mean the direct effect represents the complete Harm.

Therefore:

Direct Effect ≠ Complete Harm

  • Indirect Effects


An effect may arise through one or more intermediate relationships.

Conceptually:

Harm → Effect A → Effect B

Effect B may still belong to the Harm where sufficient continuity exists.

Therefore:

Indirect Effect ≠ Irrelevant Effect

Indirectness requires assessment, not automatic exclusion.

  • Governance Logic


HEBM applies the following logic:

Have Effect Candidates been identified?

If no:

→ maintain the Effect Boundary as incomplete.

If yes:

→ classify candidate effects.

Is the effect materially associated with the recognized Harm?

If sufficiently supported:

→ include it.

If plausible but unresolved:

→ preserve Provisional or Uncertain state.

If unsupported:

→ exclude it.

Is the effect indirect, delayed, hidden, or cross-domain?

→ do not exclude it solely for that reason.

Does the effect cross technical or organizational boundaries?

→ follow the effect rather than the system boundary.

Do several individually insufficient effects form one material composite
effect?

If yes:

→ assess the composite structure.

Is the effect merely another representation of an existing effect?

→ prevent duplication.

Does an independent condition separate the effect from the original Harm?

If yes:

→ identify potential Boundary Break or Separate Harm Candidate.

Is the effect being included only because it is temporally close,
financially relevant, claimed, or highly visible?

→ reassess the material relationship.

Has new information changed the effect structure?

→ expand, contract, reclassify, separate, merge, or reopen while
preserving history.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HEBM — Harm Effect Boundary Module governs which direct, indirect,
immediate, delayed, persistent, functional, hidden, collective,
distributed, composite, and cross-domain effects legitimately belong
within the effect boundary of a recognized Harm object. It governs effect
classification, inclusion, exclusion, relationships, manifestations,
persistence, composite formation, cross-domain continuity, uncertainty,
dispute, duplication, expansion, contraction, reclassification,
separation, reopening, and historical Effect Boundary continuity. It does
not establish the complete Downstream Boundary, formally determine Harm,
establish general causation, assign accountability or responsibility,
determine liability or compensation, govern Harm State, propagation,
containment, mitigation, recovery, or replace operational reality,
evidence, validation, integrity, identity, AI behavior, autonomous
execution, simulation, Value, or tax-event governance.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm is rarely exhausted by the first effect through which it becomes
visible.

Its effects may be direct or indirect, immediate or delayed, visible or
hidden, individual or collective, local or distributed, and may cross
domains and systems without ceasing to belong to the same Harm.

HEBM ensures that those effects are neither excluded because they are
inconveniently distant from the first manifestation nor included merely
because some association can be found.

The Effect Boundary follows the materially supportable consequences of
Harm — not the limits of visibility, reporting, technology, or
administrative convenience.
