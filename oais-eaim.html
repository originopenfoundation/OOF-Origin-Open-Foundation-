Canonical Definition

Escalation Authority Integrity Module (EAIM) defines the structural conditions under
which escalation-sensitive operational authority, runtime escalation legitimacy,
distributed escalation coordination, and consequence-bearing escalation authority states
remain materially stable, traceable, and operationally governable across runtime
operational environments.

A system satisfies EAIM only if:
  • escalation-sensitive operational authority remains materially legitimate
  • runtime escalation continuity preserves governance-valid alignment
  • distributed escalation coordination remains coherent
  • consequence-bearing escalation authority remains traceable
  • escalation-authority corruption does not destabilize operational
  • legitimacy


A system that preserves runtime execution while escalation authority materially diverges
from governance-valid conditions

does not satisfy EAIM.

Module Operational Role

EAIM defines the escalation authority integrity layer of OAIS by preserving
governance-valid escalation continuity across runtime operational environments.

Module Operational Space

EAIM governs the escalation authority space of OAIS by preserving runtime escalation
legitimacy, distributed escalation coherence, authority traceability,
consequence-bearing escalation continuity, and governance-valid operational alignment
across runtime systems.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:
  • escalation authority legitimacy
  • runtime escalation continuity
  • distributed escalation coordination
  • consequence-bearing escalation authority
  • governance-valid operational alignment
  • escalation traceability


Its function is to ensure that escalation-sensitive operational authority remains materially
legitimate strongly enough to preserve governance-valid runtime coordination across
operational environments.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Escalation Authority Object

The organization must define which escalation-sensitive operational authority structures
require governance preservation.

This may include:
  • escalation delegation pathways
  • runtime override authority
  • operational escalation routing
  • distributed escalation hierarchies
  • adaptive escalation coordination
  • consequence-bearing escalation actions
  • operational-critical escalation chains


2. Define Escalation Authority Integrity Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which escalation authority remains materially
stable and operationally aligned.

This includes:
  • runtime escalation legitimacy
  • distributed escalation coherence
  • escalation traceability continuity
  • operational alignment stability
  • governance-valid escalation continuity


3. Define Escalation Authority Corruption Detection Logic

The system must define how materially unstable escalation authority or escalation-authority
corruption is identified.

This may include:
  • unauthorized escalation
  • escalation override instability
  • distributed escalation fragmentation
  • authority-traceability degradation
  • consequence-bearing escalation divergence


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for materially unstable escalation-authority
conditions.

Governance response may include:
  • escalation restriction
  • authority synchronization
  • escalation review activation
  • distributed coordination correction
  • escalation reconstruction
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of escalation-authority continuity and
escalation-authority corruption states. A system must not remain authority-valid if
escalation-sensitive operational authority materially diverges from governance- valid
legitimacy while systems continue assuming runtime escalation continuity remains preserved.

Canonical Closing Statement

If escalation-sensitive operational authority cannot remain materially legitimate across
runtime environments, systems may preserve operational execution while escalation
legitimacy progressively separates from governance-valid operational accountability.
