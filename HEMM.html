Canonical Definition

The HEMM — Harm Emergence Mapping Module governs how the formation pathway
through which Harm may begin to exist is identified, structured, mapped,
differentiated, and preserved from an Emergence-Ready Exposure State
without treating Exposure, an adverse occurrence, a detected effect, or an
emergence signal as automatic proof that Harm has emerged.

HEMM establishes the transition:

Emergence-Ready Exposure State → Emergence-Relevant Developments →
Emergence Pathway Identification → Emergence Formation Map

Its central question is:

Through what materially relevant pathway may governed Exposure be forming
or have formed Harm?

  • Operational Role


Harm does not always emerge through one visible event.

It may form through:

  • a single occurrence
  • continuous interaction
  • repeated Exposure
  • accumulation
  • several interacting conditions
  • distributed operations
  • delayed development
  • progressive deterioration
  • multiple systems or agents
  • effects appearing after Exposure ends


HEMM therefore creates the structural map of Harm formation before
downstream HEMS modules evaluate the specific emergence mode, threshold,
transition, and resulting existing Harm state.

Its role is:

to make the pathway from Exposure toward possible Harm formation
governable without prematurely declaring that Harm exists.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HEMM — Harm Emergence Mapping Module governs the identification,
structuring, mapping, qualification, and preservation of the materially
relevant formation pathways through which governed Harm Exposure may
develop toward Harm. It governs direct, indirect, continuous, repeated,
accumulated, composite, distributed, delayed, progressive, parallel,
convergent, divergent, multi-subject, collective, and cross-domain
formation structures, including uncertainty, alternative pathways,
temporal structure, reassessment, and historical continuity. It does not
determine that Harm has emerged, establish the final Harm Emergence
threshold, govern Harm Recognition or formal Harm Determination, establish
general causation, accountability, responsibility, liability, operational
execution, evidence governance, validation, integrity, simulation,
identity, Value lifecycle, or tax-event determination.

The Harm Exposure Standard establishes:

What Exposure exists?

HEMM asks:

Through what materially relevant pathway may that Exposure be forming
Harm?

The remaining HEMS modules determine:

How does that formation develop, under what conditions does it become
sufficient, how does Harm appear, and when does the lifecycle legitimately
transition into existing Harm?

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm formation must not disappear merely because reality does not provide
one event, one actor, one system, one session, one pathway, or one
immediately visible consequence.

HEMM makes the pathway toward Harm governable before Harm is declared,
preserving complexity without converting possibility into conclusion.

If Harm is formed by the whole, governance must be able to see the whole.
