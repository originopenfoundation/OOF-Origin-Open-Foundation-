OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HRCM — Harm Recognition Confirmation Module governs when and on what
basis a Harm Recognition process becomes sufficiently supported to
establish a governed Recognition state, including confirmation criteria,
threshold sufficiency, evidence fitness, signal convergence, contradiction
handling, provisional Recognition, confidence, uncertainty, confirmation
strength, reassessment, withdrawal, reopening, and transition toward Harm
Determination readiness.

HRCM establishes the transition:

Governed Recognition Identification State → Governed Recognition Signal
State → Governed Recognition Delay State → Governed Harm Recognition
Latency State → Recognition Confirmation Assessment → Governed Harm
Recognition State

Its central question is:

Has the available governed basis become sufficient to confirm that Harm
should now be recognized as an explicit governance object?

  • Operational Role


The earlier HRS modules establish:

what information is Recognition-relevant

how that information exists as governed Recognition Signals

why Recognition may have been delayed

how much temporal separation exists between Harm and Recognition

HRCM governs the point at which those inputs become sufficient to
establish:

Recognized Harm

Its operational role is:

to convert a sufficiently supported Recognition Basis into a governed Harm
Recognition state without converting Recognition into formal Harm
Determination, causation, accountability, or liability.

  • Governance Logic


HRCM applies the following logic:

Is there a governed Recognition Basis?

If no:

→ maintain Not Ready for Confirmation.

If yes:

→ assess readiness.

Are the applicable Recognition criteria explicit?

If no:

→ Confirmation should not proceed as though a valid threshold exists.

If yes:

→ evaluate the Recognition Basis.

Does one direct signal establish sufficient Recognition support?

If yes:

→ proceed to Confirmation assessment.

If no:

→ assess collective, composite, distributed, historical, and cross-domain
support.

Are signals duplicated rather than independent?

If yes:

→ correct artificial amplification.

Are relevant signals conflicting?

If yes:

→ preserve conflict and determine whether Provisional, Disputed, or
Pending Recognition is appropriate.

Is material uncertainty present?

If yes:

→ determine whether provisional Recognition is justified.

Is the Recognition Threshold satisfied?

If no:

→ maintain Pending or Not Ready state.

If yes:

→ establish an appropriate Recognition state.

Does the supported scope remain limited?

If yes:

→ confirm only that supported scope.

Does causation remain unresolved?

→ Recognition may still proceed where the Recognition Basis is sufficient.

Is accountability unresolved?

→ Recognition may still proceed.

Is liability unresolved?

→ Recognition may still proceed.

Did new information materially weaken the prior Recognition Basis?

If yes:

→ reassess, dispute, limit, or withdraw Recognition as justified.

Is the Recognized Harm object sufficiently structured for HBS?

If yes:

→ produce the Governed Harm Recognition State.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HRCM — Harm Recognition Confirmation Module governs when and on what
basis the complete governed Recognition Basis becomes sufficient to
establish a Harm Recognition state. It governs Confirmation readiness,
criteria, thresholds, direct and indirect support, provisional
Recognition, Recognition confidence, signal convergence, independent
corroboration, composite and distributed confirmation, subject-specific
and collective Recognition, domain-specific and cross-domain Recognition,
uncertainty, contradiction, dispute, limitations, over-confirmation,
under-confirmation, suppression, withdrawal, reopening, reassessment,
historical continuity, and Determination Readiness. It does not determine
when Harm originally emerged, establish the complete Harm Boundary,
perform formal Harm Determination, establish general causation, assign
accountability or responsibility, determine liability, govern operational
execution, evidence formation, validation, integrity, identity,
simulation, Value lifecycle, or tax-event determination.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm Recognition should not require impossible certainty, but it must
require a governed basis.

Recognition that is too weak can manufacture Harm that has not been
sufficiently supported. Recognition that is withheld until every
downstream question is solved can leave real Harm outside governance for
far too long.

HRCM governs the boundary between those failures: the point at which the
available reality becomes sufficient to say that Harm must now be
recognized and governed, while leaving formal Determination,
accountability, causation, and liability where they belong.

Recognition becomes legitimate when uncertainty is governed — not when
uncertainty disappears.
