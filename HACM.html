HACM — Harm Accumulation Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HACM — Harm Accumulation Module governs how an already governed Harm
increases through repeated, continuing, additive, or temporally
distributed Harm contributions that collectively produce a materially
greater Harm condition than any individual contribution represents alone.

HACM establishes whether multiple Harm-relevant contributions belong to a
coherent accumulation process, how they relate to the existing governed
Harm, how accumulation develops across time, subjects, objects, systems,
or domains, and when accumulated Harm becomes materially significant for
State, Propagation, Containment, or other downstream HAGA™ governance.

It establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Change Identification + Harm Contributions → Contribution
Association → Accumulation Pattern → Accumulation Development →
Accumulated Harm Condition → Governed Harm Accumulation State

Its central question is:

Is existing Harm materially increasing through repeated, continuing, or
additive contributions, and what accumulated Harm condition has resulted?

  • Operational Role


HCIM establishes:

that the Harm materially changed.

HACM determines whether that change represents:

Harm Accumulation.

Its operational role is:

to govern the progressive build-up of Harm where the complete harmful
condition cannot be adequately represented by examining individual
contributions in isolation.

The foundational logic is:

Contribution₁ + Contribution₂ + Contribution₃ + … → Accumulated Harm

  • Position Within HCPS


The HCPS governance flow is:

Change Identification → Accumulation → Compounding → Propagation →
Cascading

HACM receives:

Governed Harm Change Identification

together with materially relevant Harm contributions.

It produces:

Governed Harm Accumulation State

for subsequent HCPS and HAGA™ governance.

  • Governed Object


The primary governed object is:

Accumulation of Existing Harm

HACM governs the relationship between multiple contributions and the
resulting accumulated Harm condition.

A contribution may be individually limited while remaining materially
relevant to the accumulation.

Therefore:

Individually Limited Contribution ≠ Irrelevant Contribution

  • Foundational Accumulation Principle


The foundational rule is:

Harm may become material through accumulation even when no single
contribution independently represents the complete Harm-producing
condition.

Therefore:

No Single Dominant Harm-Producing Event ≠ No Accumulated Harm

This allows HAGA™ to govern Harm that develops through:

repetition

continuity

frequency

duration

or:

distributed contributions

rather than requiring one discrete incident.

  • Harm Contribution


A Harm Contribution is a materially relevant addition to an existing Harm
accumulation process.

A contribution may arise through:

repeated exposure

continuing exposure

repeated action

repeated output

repeated physical interaction

continuing system condition

repeated financial effect

repeated environmental effect

or:

other Harm-relevant occurrence

A contribution is not automatically a separate Harm object.

  • Contribution Association


Before aggregation, HACM must establish that contributions are
sufficiently related to the governed Harm accumulation.

Relevant relationships may include:

same governed Harm

same affected subject or object

same material Harm dimension

same continuing condition

same accumulation pathway

temporal continuity

or:

other governed relationship

Canonical principle:

Temporal Proximity Alone ≠ Common Harm Accumulation

  • Contribution Identity


HACM must preserve contribution-level traceability where materially
necessary.

This allows governance to distinguish:

one contribution repeated many times

from:

many materially distinct contributions

without automatically converting every contribution into a new Harm
object.

Therefore:

Contribution Identity ≠ Harm Identity

  • Discrete Accumulation


Harm may accumulate through separate events.

Conceptually:

Event₁ → Contribution₁

Event₂ → Contribution₂

Event₃ → Contribution₃

leading to:

Accumulated Harm₁

No single event needs to establish the complete accumulated Harm.

  • Continuous Accumulation


Harm may also accumulate through a continuing condition without clearly
separable events.

Conceptually:

Continuous Harm-Producing Condition → Continuing Contribution → Increasing
Accumulated Harm

This is particularly important where forcing reality into artificial event
units would distort the Harm lifecycle.

Canonical principle:

Continuous Harm Does Not Require Artificial Event Fragmentation.

  • Temporal Accumulation


Accumulation may occur across:

seconds

days

months

years

or another materially relevant period.

HACM must preserve the accumulation interval.

The relevant question is not merely:

How many contributions occurred?

but:

What Harm accumulated across the governed period?

Possible patterns include:

Slow

Accelerating

Uncertain

Accumulation rate may influence Monitoring and Containment requirements.

But:

Accumulation Rate ≠ Harm Severity

  • Accumulation Magnitude


HACM may govern the material amount or extent of accumulated Harm where
supportable.

This may be represented through:

quantitative measures

qualitative measures

thresholds

or:

combined methodological assessment

The representation must follow the Harm domain and available basis.

  • Accumulation Threshold


Some Harm becomes materially governable through accumulation only after
repeated contributions collectively cross a meaningful threshold.

Conceptually:

Contribution₁ — individually limited

Contribution₂ — individually limited

Contribution₃ — individually limited

Combined Condition → Material Accumulated Harm

Canonical principle:

Individual Insufficiency Does Not Establish Composite Insufficiency.

  • Threshold ≠ Origin of Harm


Where an accumulation threshold is used, crossing it does not necessarily
mean Harm began only at that moment.

The threshold may represent:

governance materiality

detectability

classification significance

or:

intervention relevance

Therefore:

Threshold Crossing ≠ Harm Emergence Time by Default

  • Accumulation Without Threshold


Not every Harm accumulation requires a fixed numerical threshold.

Where the Harm domain does not support one, HACM may use governed
qualitative or hybrid criteria.

Canonical principle:

Absence of a Numeric Threshold ≠ Absence of Governable Accumulation.

  • Accumulation and Persistence


Accumulation may continue while the Harm remains in the same canonical
Harm State.

For example:

Active → additional accumulated Harm → Active

until sufficient material change supports a State transition.

Therefore:

Accumulation ≠ Automatic State Transition

HACM governs the accumulation dynamic.

HSS governs the State.

  • Accumulation and Escalation


Accumulation may eventually contribute to an:

Active → Escalating

State transition.

But:

Accumulating Harm ≠ Escalating State by Default

The relationship must be governed rather than assumed.

  • Accumulation vs Compounding


Accumulation and Compounding are fundamentally different.

Accumulation concerns Harm increasing through repeated, continuing, or
additive contributions.

Compounding concerns materially amplifying interactions among Harm
conditions or effects.

Conceptually:

Accumulation: A + A + A → Greater A

Compounding: A + B → materially amplified consequence through interaction

Therefore:

Multiple Contributions ≠ Compounding by Default

  • Accumulation vs Propagation


Accumulation may occur without Harm spreading into additional scope.

For example:

same subject + same domain + repeated contributions → greater Harm

without propagation.

Therefore:

Increasing Harm ≠ Propagating Harm

Propagation requires additional scope or materially relevant spread.

  • Accumulation vs Cascading


Repeated contributions to the same Harm are not necessarily cascading.

Cascading involves sequential downstream Harm relationships.

Therefore:

Repeated Contribution ≠ Harm Cascade

  • Multi-Source Accumulation


Accumulated Harm may receive contributions from multiple operational
sources.

For example:

System A → Contribution

System B → Contribution

Human Process C → Contribution

may collectively contribute to the same governed Harm.

HACM may govern the accumulation without determining legal causation or
responsibility for each contribution.

Canonical principle:

Multiple Sources ≠ Multiple Harms by Default

  • Distributed Accumulation


Contributions may be distributed across:

agents

systems

sessions

organizations

locations

or:

execution environments

No individual operational environment may contain the complete
accumulation history.

HACM must therefore preserve accumulation continuity across those
boundaries.

Canonical principle:

Operational Fragmentation Must Not Fragment Accumulated Harm.

  • Composite Operations


A composite operation may create accumulated Harm through individually
insufficient actions.

Conceptually:

Action₁ + Action₂ + Action₃ → Material Accumulated Harm

even where:

Action₁ ≠ Complete Harm

Action₂ ≠ Complete Harm

Action₃ ≠ Complete Harm

Therefore:

Absence of a Single Harm-Producing Action ≠ Absence of Accumulated Harm

  • Subject-Level Accumulation


Different subjects may accumulate Harm at different rates.

HACM should preserve:

Subject A — High Accumulation

Subject B — Limited Accumulation

Subject C — No Established Accumulation

where materially relevant.

Therefore:

One Subject's Accumulation ≠ Collective Accumulation State by Default

  • Collective Accumulation


Harm may accumulate at a collective level even where no single subject
represents the complete Harm.

The collective Harm object may involve:

distributed small effects

repeated population-level effects

systemic degradation

or:

aggregate consequences

Canonical principle:

The absence of a single dominant harmed subject does not preclude

  • Multi-Dimensional Accumulation


Accumulation may differ across Harm dimensions.

For example:

Physical — Stable

Financial — Accumulating

Organizational — Accumulating Slowly

Digital — No Further Accumulation

HACM must preserve these distinctions rather than create an unsupported

  • Accumulation After Mechanism Termination


The original mechanism may stop while already initiated processes continue
adding to Harm.

Therefore:

Mechanism Termination ≠ Accumulation Termination

HACM follows the actual Harm contribution pathway.

  • Accumulation During Intervention


Containment or Mitigation may begin while Harm continues accumulating.

Therefore:

Intervention Started ≠ Accumulation Stopped

The effectiveness of intervention must be reflected in actual Harm
dynamics.

  • Accumulation Visibility


Improved observation may reveal previously accumulated Harm.

HACM must distinguish:

Accumulation Occurring Now

from:

Previously Accumulated Harm Becoming Visible Now

Therefore:

Newly Observed Accumulation ≠ Newly Occurring Accumulation by Default

Accumulation ends when new material contributions cease.

But accumulated Harm may remain.

Therefore:

Accumulation Stopped ≠ Harm Ended

  • Governance Logic


HACM applies the following logic:

Has HCIM established material Harm Change or a materially relevant
accumulation signal?

If no:

→ formal Harm Accumulation governance is not established.

If yes:

→ identify potentially relevant contributions.

Are multiple or continuing contributions present?

If no:

→ the Harm Change may require another HCPS dynamic.

If yes:

→ determine whether the contributions belong to the same governed
accumulation process.

Are the contributions merely temporally close but otherwise unrelated?

If yes:

→ do not aggregate by default.

Are the contributions repeated, continuing, additive, or temporally
distributed in a materially coherent way?

If yes:

→ establish Harm Accumulation.

Then determine, where supportable:

accumulation pattern

duration

rate

magnitude

threshold condition

affected scope

and:

uncertainty

Next:

Is interaction among conditions amplifying Harm beyond additive
accumulation?

If yes:

→ continue into Compounding governance.

Is Harm extending into additional materially affected scope?

If yes:

→ continue into Propagation governance.

Is a sequential downstream Harm chain forming?

If yes:

→ continue into Cascading governance.

Has accumulation stopped?

If yes:

→ stop accumulation status while preserving the accumulated Harm that
remains.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HACM — Harm Accumulation Module governs how repeated, continuing,
additive, temporally distributed, multi-source, composite, collective, or
distributed contributions build into an existing governed Harm over time.
It governs contribution identification and association, accumulation
pattern, duration, rate, magnitude, thresholds, uncertainty, cessation,
and historical continuity. It does not determine whether Harm originally
exists, perform Recognition or Determination, redefine the Governed Harm
Boundary, classify or transition the Harm State, govern materially
amplifying interactions as Compounding, govern spread as Propagation,
govern downstream chains as Cascading, perform Containment or Mitigation,
govern Recovery, Residual Harm, Resolution or Finality, determine general
causation, assign accountability or liability, or replace operational
reality, evidence, validation, integrity, prediction, simulation, AI
behavior, autonomous execution, identity, Value, tax-event, or
jurisdiction-specific governance.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm does not need a single decisive event to become materially
significant.

It may be built contribution by contribution, interaction by interaction,
exposure by exposure, or across distributed systems that never
individually contain the complete Harm picture.

HACM preserves that cumulative reality without turning every contribution
into a separate Harm or confusing accumulation with compounding and
propagation.

What is individually limited may become materially significant through
accumulation.
