Canonical Definition

Decision Scope Integrity Module (DSIM) defines the structural
conditions under which decision boundaries, decision authority
limits, decision objectives, decision obligations, decision
constraints, decision expectations, decision impacts, and decision
outcomes remain identifiable, traceable, governable,
reconstructable, reviewable, and operationally valid throughout
the decision lifecycle.

DSIM governs decision scope.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine exactly what was decided, what was not decided, which
authority boundaries applied, what impacts were expected, and where
the decision scope begins and ends.

Module Operational Role

DSIM defines the decision-scope governance layer of DAS.

Its role is to preserve visibility into decision boundaries
and decision coverage.

Module Operational Space

DSIM governs:

  • decision scope
  • decision boundaries
  • decision authority limits
  • decision objectives
  • decision constraints
  • decision expectations
  • decision impacts
  • decision coverage


The module applies wherever governance requires clarification
of decision boundaries.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • clearly defined decisions
  • visible decision boundaries
  • reconstructable decision domains
  • accountable decision structures
  • governance-valid decision limits
  • operational decision clarity


Its function is to ensure that governance can determine exactly what
was decided and what remained outside the decision.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Decision Scope Object

The organization must define which decision environments
require scope governance.

This may include:

  • organizational structures
  • management systems
  • boards
  • governance frameworks
  • contractor environments
  • AI governance systems
  • Human-AI decision environments
  • operational decision systems


2. Define Decision Scope Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which decision
scope remains valid.

This includes:

  • decision-boundary requirements
  • authority-limit requirements
  • objective requirements
  • constraint requirements
  • governance requirements
  • decision-scope conditions


3. Define Scope Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how decision-scope failures are identified.

This may include:

  • unclear decisions
  • decision ambiguity
  • overlapping decision authority
  • scope expansion without approval
  • governance-blind decision activity
  • undefined decision boundaries


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for decision-scope failures.

Governance response may include:

  • decision review
  • scope verification
  • governance intervention
  • decision clarification
  • corrective actions
  • decision reassessment
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • decision boundaries
  • decision objectives
  • governance reviews
  • validation activities
  • intervention procedures
  • resulting decision states


A decision-accountability environment must not remain scope-valid if
materially significant decision boundaries cannot be reconstructed,
reviewed, validated, clarified, or governed.

Use Case 1 — Executive Decision Environment

Scenario

A management board approves a strategic initiative that later
creates operational consequences.

Application

DSIM determines exactly what was approved, what remained outside the
approval scope, and where decision authority boundaries existed.

Result

Governance gains visibility into decision coverage and
decision limitations.

Use Case 2 — Human-AI Decision Environment

Scenario

An AI-assisted decision platform provides recommendations that are
reviewed and approved by human supervisors.

Application

DSIM determines exactly which parts of the recommendation became
part of the final decision and which did not.

Result

Governance gains visibility into decision boundaries across
intelligent operational ecosystems.

Canonical Closing Statement

Decision Scope Integrity Module (DSIM) defines the structural
conditions under which decision boundaries, decision authority
limits, decision objectives, decision obligations, decision
constraints, decision expectations, decision impacts, and decision
outcomes remain identifiable, traceable, governable,
reconstructable, reviewable, and operationally valid throughout
the decision lifecycle.

Decision accountability cannot be preserved if decision boundaries
are unclear. Decision Scope Integrity therefore becomes a
foundational condition of trustworthy decision
accountability governance.
