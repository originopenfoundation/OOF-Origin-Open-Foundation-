HROM — Harm Reopening Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HROM — Harm Reopening Module governs when and how a previously
resolved or closed Harm lifecycle must return to active governance because
materially relevant Harm reality, the Resolution basis, the Closure basis,
or conditions supporting either have changed.

HROM establishes the methodological structure for identifying potential
Reopening conditions, determining whether a material trigger has occurred,
distinguishing continuation or recurrence of an existing Harm from
genuinely new Harm, identifying which parts of the HAGA™ lifecycle must
reactivate, preserving historical Resolution and Closure states, and
forming a new active governance state without rewriting the earlier Harm
lifecycle.

It governs the transition:

Governed Harm Closure State → Reopening Signal → Reopening Trigger
Evaluation → Harm Relationship Determination → Governance Reactivation →
Reopening State → Governed Harm Reopening State

Its central question is:

When must a previously resolved or closed Harm lifecycle return to active
governance, and where in that lifecycle must governance resume?

  • Operational Role


HCLM establishes:

when active Harm lifecycle governance can legitimately close.

HROM establishes:

when that Closure can no longer legitimately remain the current governance
state.

Its operational role is:

to restore active Harm governance when post-Closure reality materially
invalidates, weakens, exceeds, or changes the basis on which Resolution or
Closure was established.

Its foundational principle is:

Closure must never make changed Harm reality ungovernable.

  • Position Within HRFS


The HRFS governance flow is:

Resolution State → Resolution Criteria → Closure → Reopening → Finality

HROM governs the fourth stage:

Reopening

It receives principally:

Governed Harm Closure State

together with materially relevant post-Closure signals.

It produces:

Governed Harm Reopening State

and identifies the appropriate HAGA™ lifecycle re-entry point.

Where Reopening is not required and the Closure basis remains sufficiently
durable, the lifecycle may continue toward:

HFFM — Harm Finality Formation Module

  • Governed Object


The primary governed object is:

Reactivation of a Previously Resolved or Closed Harm Lifecycle

HROM does not treat every post-Closure event as Reopening.

It determines whether a materially relevant relationship exists between
the new condition and the governed Harm lifecycle.

Therefore:

New Post-Closure Event ≠ Harm Reopening by Default

  • Foundational Reopening Principle


A Harm lifecycle must remain capable of returning to active governance
where the reality supporting Resolution or Closure materially changes.

Therefore:

Closure ≠ Irreversibility

Before legitimate Finality, Closure remains subject to governed Reopening.

A Reopening Trigger is a sufficiently established condition requiring
reconsideration of the current Resolution or Closure state.

A trigger must be materially relevant to the governed Harm.

Canonical distinction:

New Information ≠ Reopening Trigger by Default

The information must materially affect the Harm lifecycle or its
governance basis.

  • Materiality


HROM must distinguish materially relevant change from immaterial
variation.

Reopening should not occur merely because:

administrative records change

or:

a hypothetical possibility is raised without sufficient Harm relevance

Canonical principle:

Reopening follows material Harm relevance, not informational noise.

→ initiate the appropriate new HAGA™ lifecycle.

  • Harm Identity Continuity

Therefore:

Reopened Harm ≠ New Harm Identity by Default

The lifecycle may contain multiple active periods without becoming
multiple unrelated Harm objects.

  • Historical Resolution Preservation


Reopening does not erase the fact that Resolution may previously have been

Therefore:

Current Reopening ≠ Historical Resolution Never Existed

The lifecycle may legitimately contain:

Resolution₁ → Closure₁ → Reopening → Resolution₂

  • Historical Closure Preservation

Reopening ≠ Historical Closure Invalid by Default

and governed at that time.

HROM preserves historical state while updating current state.

  • Invalid Historical Basis


Where materially new information establishes that the original Resolution
or Closure basis was already invalid when formed, HROM must preserve that
distinction.

This is different from reality changing later.

Therefore:

Changed Reality After Closure ≠ Invalid Closure at Time of Formation

The governance record should distinguish:

historically legitimate Closure later reopened

from:

Closure formed on a materially invalid basis

  • Minimum Implementation Framework


A minimum HROM implementation requires exactly eight capabilities.

  • Reopening Signal Detection


Identify materially relevant post-Resolution or post-Closure signals that
may require renewed Harm governance.

  • Reopening Trigger Evaluation


Determine whether a signal is sufficiently material to establish a
legitimate Reopening Trigger.

  • Harm Relationship & Identity Determination


Distinguish continuing, recurrent, latent, expanded, derived, related,
new, and uncertain Harm relationships while preserving Harm identity
continuity where appropriate.

  • Reopening Scope & Re-Entry Determination


Determine what portion of the Harm lifecycle must reopen and identify the
minimum appropriate HAGA™ governance re-entry point.

  • Composite, Collective, Distributed & Cross-Domain Reopening
    Governance


Preserve materially different Reopening conditions across components,
subjects, groups, locations, systems, and domains.

  • Resolution, Closure & Historical Continuity Governance


Update current Resolution and Closure states without erasing legitimate
historical Resolution, Closure, or prior Reopening periods.

  • Uncertainty, Urgency & Reopening Persistence Governance


Preserve uncertain Harm relationships, allow necessary Harm control under
uncertainty, and maintain active governance until reopened conditions are
sufficiently addressed.

  • Governed Output & Finality Handoff


Produce a traceable Governed Harm Reopening State and, where no material
Reopening condition remains, establish readiness for Finality formation.
63. Use Case 1 — Latent Harm Appears After Closure

A governed Harm reaches Resolution and Closure after available Recovery
and Residual Harm conditions are sufficiently understood.

Months later, a materially significant Harm effect becomes observable that
was not detectable at Closure.

HROM evaluates whether the condition represents:

new Harm

or:

latent manifestation of the original Harm

Same Harm — Latent Manifestation

The original Harm identity is preserved.

HROM determines that the appropriate re-entry point is:

Harm State

Harm Mitigation and Recovery governance

The earlier Resolution and Closure remain part of the historical
lifecycle.

The governing principle is:

Late manifestation may reopen the same Harm without rewriting the
legitimacy of its earlier history.

  • Use Case 2 — One Distributed Component Reopens


A distributed infrastructure Harm reaches Resolution and Closure across

HROM determines:

same Harm relationship

local Reopening scope

and:

The architecture does not automatically reopen every location.

The state becomes:

Distributed Harm — Partial Reopening

The governing principle is:

Reopening follows the Harm that returned; it does not automatically reset

  • Canonical Boundary


The HROM — Harm Reopening Module governs whether and how a previously
resolved or closed Harm lifecycle must return to active governance when
materially relevant Harm reality or the basis supporting Resolution or
Closure changes. It governs Reopening Signals, Reopening Triggers,
materiality, Harm relationship and identity continuity, recurrence, latent
manifestation, Reopening scope, lifecycle re-entry, governance
reactivation, historical Resolution and Closure preservation, uncertainty,
repeated Reopening, and Finality readiness handoff. HROM does not
independently govern the substantive operation of the HAGA™ lifecycle
stages to which Harm is returned, create new Harm merely because a later
event occurs, determine general causation, accountability, responsibility,
liability, claims, compensation or value consequences, or treat
administrative, legal, insurance, audit, validation, or operational
changes as Harm Reopening without a materially established Harm
relationship.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Closure must never become a mechanism for forcing changed Harm reality
into an obsolete governance state.

When Harm returns, changes, expands, becomes newly visible, or invalidates
the conditions that supported Closure, governance must be capable of
returning with it—without erasing what was legitimately resolved before.

Reopening therefore protects both sides of the Harm lifecycle: the
integrity of historical Resolution and the governability of present
reality.

A closed Harm can remain closed only while the reality that justified its
Closure remains sufficiently true.
