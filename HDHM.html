HDHM — Harm Development Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HDHM — Harm Development Module governs how an identified Harm
Emergence formation pathway develops over time toward possible Harm by
establishing the direction, progression, accumulation, persistence,
acceleration, deceleration, stabilization, interruption, reversal,
convergence, divergence, and uncertainty of emergence-relevant development
without treating development itself as proof that Harm has emerged.

HDHM establishes the transition:

Emergence Formation Map → Development Identification → Development Pattern
→ Development State → Development Trajectory → Governed Harm Development
State

Its central question is:

How is the mapped Harm formation pathway developing, and is that
development materially moving toward, away from, or remaining relevant to
possible Harm Emergence?

Does a sufficiently governed Emergence Formation Map exist?

If no:

→ do not manufacture Harm Development.

If yes:

→ identify materially relevant development.

Is the formation pathway materially changing?

If no:

→ determine whether it is inactive, stable, persistent, or insufficiently
known.

If yes:

→ determine development direction and pattern.

Is development progressing toward possible Harm?

→ classify progression and relevant trajectory.

Is development accumulating or compounding?

→ preserve the appropriate structure.

Is the rate changing?

→ distinguish acceleration, deceleration, or variable development.

Has development stopped temporarily?

→ classify interruption where appropriate.

Has it resumed?

→ determine continuity.

Is development moving away from Harm?

→ classify regression or reversal without calling it Harm Recovery.

→ establish terminated development and preserve its history.

→ preserve their independent and combined trajectories.

Is the development distributed across systems or agents?

→ govern the composite consequence where justified.

Is the state uncertain or disputed?

→ preserve uncertainty.

Is the development state sufficiently structured for Emergence Conditions
governance?

If yes:

→ produce the Governed Harm Development State.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HDHM — Harm Development Module governs how an identified Harm
formation pathway develops toward, away from, or remains materially
relevant to possible Harm Emergence, including progression, persistence,
accumulation, compounding, acceleration, deceleration, stabilization,
oscillation, interruption, resumption, reversal, termination, latency,
composite development, distributed development, multi-subject development,
cross-domain development, uncertainty, reassessment, and historical
continuity. It does not determine that Harm has emerged, establish the
final Harm Emergence threshold, govern existing Harm State or Harm
propagation, determine Harm Recognition, causation, accountability,
responsibility, liability, operational execution, evidence, validation,
integrity, identity, simulation, Value lifecycle, or tax-event
determination.

HEMM asks:

Through what materially relevant pathway may Exposure be forming Harm?

HDHM asks:

How is that formation pathway developing?

HCHM next asks:

Under what governed conditions can that development become sufficient for
Harm Emergence?

  • Canonical Closing Statement


A pathway toward Harm is not static simply because Harm has not yet been
established.

It may progress, accumulate, compound, stabilize, reverse, disappear, or
continue beyond the Exposure that initiated it. HDHM governs that
development without converting movement toward Harm into premature proof
that Harm already exists.

Harm governance must understand not only the pathway through which Harm
may form, but how that pathway is changing before the emergence boundary
is crossed.
