HRIM — Harm Recognition Identification Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HRIM — Harm Recognition Identification Module governs how signals,
observations, reports, detections, experiences, anomalies, patterns,
manifestations, and other Harm-relevant information are identified as
potentially belonging to an existing or emerging Harm object and admitted
into Harm Recognition governance without treating the presence of a signal
as automatic proof that Harm has been recognized, determined, caused, or
attributed.

HRIM establishes the transition:

Harm-Relevant Information → Recognition Signal Identification → Relevance
Qualification → Harm Association → Governed Recognition Identification
State

Its central question is:

Which signals, observations, reports, detections, experiences, or patterns
are materially relevant to possible existing Harm and should enter Harm
Recognition governance?

  • Operational Role


Harm Recognition cannot begin reliably unless governance first determines
what information is materially relevant to Recognition.

Operational environments may generate enormous volumes of:

alerts

reports

observations

complaints

measurements

system events

sensor outputs

anomalies

behavioral signals

operational failures

subject experiences

historical information

Not every signal represents Harm.

But potentially important signals must not disappear merely because their
Harm meaning is not yet fully understood.

HRIM therefore performs the first filtering and admission function of HRS:

identify what deserves Harm Recognition governance without prematurely
declaring what the Harm ultimately is.

  • Position Within HRS


The internal HRS flow is:

Recognition Signal → Recognition Identification → Recognition Latency /
Delay → Recognition Confirmation → Determination Readiness

Corresponding modules:

HRIM → HRSM → HRDM → HLRM → HRCM

HRIM is the first Core Module.

It receives:

Existing Harm State

where already available,

together with:

Harm-relevant signals, observations, reports, detections, experiences,
manifestations, patterns, and operational information.

It produces:

Governed Recognition Identification State

for downstream Recognition Signal governance.

  • Governed Object


The primary governed object is:

Recognition Identification State

This represents whether incoming information has a sufficiently material
relationship to possible existing or emerging Harm to enter HRS
governance.

Possible states include:

Not Assessed

Potentially Relevant

Materially Relevant

Provisionally Relevant

Related but Insufficiently Qualified

Conflicting Relevance

Not Harm-Relevant

The state must not itself be treated as Harm Recognition.

  • Foundational Identification Principle


The foundational distinction is:

Harm-Relevant Information ≠ Recognized Harm

Identification answers:

Should this information enter Harm Recognition governance?

It does not answer:

Has Harm been confirmed?

  • Governance Logic


HRIM applies the following logic:

Has Harm-relevant information entered the governance environment?

If no:

→ maintain no Recognition Identification state.

If yes:

→ identify its source and information type.

Could the information materially relate to existing or emerging Harm?

If no:

→ classify Not Harm-Relevant with rationale.

If yes or uncertain:

→ preserve it for relevance qualification.

Which subject, domain, context, time, system, or manifestation may it
concern?

→ establish preliminary associations.

Is the signal individually sufficient to establish material relevance?

If yes:

→ classify accordingly.

If no:

→ determine whether composite, collective, distributed, or historical
correlation changes its relevance.

Is the signal duplicated?

→ prevent artificial weighting.

Is the signal weak but potentially material when combined?

→ preserve it.

Are sources conflicting?

→ preserve conflict.

Is information missing?

→ record unknown rather than assuming absence.

Has technical distribution fragmented the signal?

→ evaluate at the appropriate composite level.

Does the information establish causation, accountability, liability, or
formal Harm Determination?

→ HRIM does not decide those questions.

Is the information materially relevant to Harm Recognition governance?

If yes:

→ produce Governed Recognition Identification State.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HRIM — Harm Recognition Identification Module governs how signals,
observations, reports, detections, experiences, measurements, anomalies,
manifestations, patterns, historical information, and other Harm-relevant
information are identified, qualified, correlated, and admitted into Harm
Recognition governance. It governs direct, indirect, contextual, temporal,
subject-specific, multi-domain, collective, composite, distributed,
conflicting, weak, hidden, suppressed, and historically reconstructed
Recognition relevance, including uncertainty, duplication, signal
fragmentation, blind spots, reassessment, and historical continuity. It
does not confirm Harm Recognition, determine when Harm emerged, establish
the complete Harm Boundary, formally determine Harm, establish general
causation, assign accountability or responsibility, determine liability,
govern operational execution, evidence governance, validation, integrity,
identity, simulation, Value lifecycle, or tax-event determination.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm Recognition cannot depend only on the signal that is easiest to see,
the report that arrives through the correct channel, or the system that
happens to hold the largest fragment of information.

Harm-relevant information may be weak individually, distributed across
systems, delayed in time, hidden from institutional view, or meaningful
only when combined with other signals.

HRIM ensures that potentially meaningful Harm information can enter
governance without being prematurely converted into a conclusion.

What is fragmented in information must not become invisible in Harm
governance.
