Canonical Definition

Institutional Cognition Integrity Module (ICIM) defines the structural conditions
under which cognition emerging from institutions, governance bodies, regulatory systems,
public authorities, professional organizations, and long-lived decision structures
remains traceable, coherent, accountable, reality-connected, governance-valid,
and operationally reliable across institutional cognition environments.

ICIM governs institutional cognition.

The module ensures that institutions remain capable of maintaining valid cognition over time
rather than becoming trapped by institutional inertia, self-reinforcing assumptions,
outdated reality models, procedural distortions, or governance-invalid cognition states.

A system satisfies ICIM only if:

  • institutional cognition remains traceable
  • institutional assumptions remain reviewable
  • reality alignment remains assessable
  • cognition accountability remains preservable
  • institutional reasoning remains governable
  • cognition outputs remain operationally valid

An institution that cannot explain how its conclusions emerged does not satisfy ICIM.

Module Operational Role

ICIM defines the institutional-cognition governance layer of CCIS.

Its role is to preserve cognition integrity within institutional structures.

Module Operational Space

ICIM governs:

  • institutional cognition
  • governance cognition
  • regulatory cognition
  • policy cognition
  • institutional reasoning
  • institutional reality models
  • institutional interpretation
  • cognition accountability

The module applies wherever cognition emerges through institutional structures
rather than individual participants.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • accountable institutional reasoning
  • governance-valid cognition
  • reality-connected policy formation
  • traceable institutional conclusions
  • coherent institutional understanding
  • long-term cognition integrity

Its function is to ensure that institutions remain cognitively reliable as they evolve over time.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Institutional Cognition Object
The institution must define which cognition processes require governance.

This may include:

  • policy formation
  • regulatory interpretation
  • governance decisions
  • strategic planning
  • institutional forecasting
  • risk evaluation
  • compliance reasoning
  • public-interest assessments

2. Define Institutional Cognition Conditions
The system must define the conditions under which institutional cognition remains valid.

This includes:

  • traceability requirements
  • accountability requirements
  • reviewability requirements
  • reality-alignment requirements
  • governance requirements
  • cognition-validity requirements

3. Define Institutional Cognition Degradation Detection Logic
The system must define how institutional cognition degradation is identified.

This may include:

  • institutional blind spots
  • outdated assumptions
  • policy-reality divergence
  • reasoning stagnation
  • governance distortions
  • accountability failures

4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic
The system must define governance logic for institutional-cognition failures.

Governance response may include:

  • cognition review
  • policy reassessment
  • institutional audits
  • governance intervention
  • escalation
  • structural reform
  • operational invalidation where required

5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions
The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • institutional assumptions
  • reasoning processes
  • policy interpretations
  • governance decisions
  • intervention actions
  • resulting institutional conclusions

An institution must not remain cognition-valid if materially significant cognition processes
cannot be reconstructed, reviewed, or governed.
