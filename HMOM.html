HMOM — Harm Mitigation Outcome Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HMOM — Harm Mitigation Outcome Module governs how one or more observed
Harm Mitigation Effects are interpreted together to establish the
resulting governed Mitigation condition of an existing Harm.

HMOM determines what the complete Mitigation result means after
considering beneficial, partial, neutral, adverse, delayed, temporary,
persistent, distributed, collective, cross-domain, displaced, substituted,
and intervention-induced effects.

It establishes whether the Mitigation Objective has been achieved fully,
partially, insufficiently, adversely, or remains uncertain, while
preserving materially relevant Harm that continues to exist.

HMOM establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Mitigation Effect → Effect Integration → Objective
Comparison → Remaining Harm Assessment → Outcome Determination → Governed
Harm Mitigation Outcome

Its central question is:

Given the material effects that occurred, what is the resulting governed
Mitigation condition of the Harm?

  • Operational Role


HMNM establishes:

what Harm should improve.

HMAM establishes:

what action attempts to produce that improvement.

HMEM establishes:

what materially changed.

HMOM establishes:

what those changes mean for the overall Mitigation condition.

Its operational role is:

to convert individual Mitigation Effects into a coherent Harm-level
Mitigation Outcome without hiding remaining, redistributed, substituted,
or newly created Harm.

  • Position Within HMS


The HMS governance flow is:

Mitigation Need → Mitigation Action → Mitigation Effect → Mitigation
Outcome → Mitigation Verification

HMOM governs the fourth stage:

Mitigation Outcome

It receives:

Governed Harm Mitigation Effect

together with relevant:

Mitigation Need

Mitigation Target

Mitigation Baseline

Mitigation Objective

current Harm State

and:

materially relevant continuing Harm information

It produces:

Governed Harm Mitigation Outcome

for final Mitigation Verification.

  • Governed Object


The primary governed object is:

Resulting Mitigation Condition of the Governed Harm

An Outcome is not simply a collection of observed effects.

It represents what those effects mean when interpreted against:

the original Mitigation Objective

the complete governed Harm

the affected scope

and:

the Harm that remains

Therefore:

Effect ≠ Outcome

  • Foundational Outcome Principle


The foundational rule is:

Mitigation Outcome must represent the complete materially relevant Harm
condition produced by Mitigation, not merely the strongest beneficial
effect observed.

Therefore:

Beneficial Effect ≠ Successful Overall Mitigation by Default

and:

Improvement Somewhere ≠ Improvement Everywhere

  • Effect Integration


One Mitigation process may produce several effects.

Conceptually:

Effect₁ + Effect₂ + Effect₃ + … → Mitigation Outcome

These effects may be:

beneficial

neutral

adverse

mixed

temporary

persistent

distributed

or:

uncertain

HMOM integrates them without erasing materially important differences.

  • Objective Comparison


The Mitigation Outcome must remain linked to the original Mitigation
Objective.

HMOM asks:

What reduction was sought?

What reduction actually occurred?

What materially relevant gap remains?

Conceptually:

Mitigation Objective ↔ Governed Harm Mitigation Effect → Outcome

A beneficial effect that does not materially satisfy the objective may
still represent only Partial or Insufficient Mitigation.

  • Objective Achievement


Objective achievement may be represented as:

Achieved

Substantially Achieved

Partially Achieved

Not Achieved

Adversely Affected

or:

Uncertain

Objective achievement does not itself establish final Verification.

It forms part of the Outcome requiring verification.

  • Remaining Harm


HMOM must explicitly identify materially relevant Harm remaining after
Mitigation.

This may include:

unreduced Harm

partially reduced Harm

persistent consequences

unaffected Harm components

continuing functional impairment

remaining collective Harm

cross-domain Harm

or:

newly produced Harm

Canonical principle:

Mitigation Outcome must preserve what remains, not only what improved.

  • Partial Mitigation Outcome


Partial Mitigation occurs where meaningful Harm reduction has occurred but
the complete Mitigation Objective has not been achieved.

Examples include:

some Harm components reduced

some subjects improved

one domain improved

some consequences remain

or:

reduction is materially below the intended objective

Partial Mitigation is a legitimate governed result.

Therefore:

Partial Mitigation ≠ Mitigation Failure

  • Insufficient Mitigation


An intervention may produce a real beneficial effect that remains
insufficient relative to the Mitigation Need.

Therefore:

Beneficial Effect ≠ Sufficient Mitigation

HMOM should support:

Mitigation Insufficient

where improvement exists but does not materially satisfy the required Harm
reduction.

  • Complete Mitigation


A Mitigation Outcome may be considered complete relative to its governed
Mitigation Objective where the targeted existing Harm or consequence has
been reduced to the required governed condition.

This does not mean:

the entire Harm lifecycle is complete

or:

all effects of the Harm have disappeared

Therefore:

Complete Mitigation ≠ Harm Finality

  • Composite Harm Outcome


Where Harm contains multiple components, HMOM evaluates the complete
Mitigation condition.

For example:

Component A → substantial improvement

Component B → unchanged

Component C → deterioration

The overall Outcome must reflect all materially relevant components.

Therefore:

Component Success ≠ Composite Mitigation Success

  • Collective Mitigation Outcome


Where Harm affects a collective, HMOM evaluates the relevant collective
Harm condition.

A subset of individuals improving cannot automatically establish
successful collective Mitigation.

Therefore:

Selected Individual Improvement ≠ Collective Mitigation Outcome

Materially different distributions must remain visible.

  • Distributed Mitigation Outcome


Mitigation may operate across multiple:

systems

organizations

locations

providers

agents

or:

infrastructure components

Local outcomes must not automatically represent the complete distributed
Harm condition.

Canonical principle:

Local Mitigation Success ≠ Distributed Mitigation Success by Default

  • Cross-Domain Outcome


A Harm may span several domains.

For example:

Physical Harm ↓

Organizational Harm ↓

Financial Harm unchanged

Cognitive Harm ↑

HMOM must preserve this structure.

Therefore:

Domain-Specific Success ≠ Complete Mitigation Outcome

  • Unequal Outcome Distribution


Mitigation may produce different outcomes across affected subjects.

HMOM should preserve materially relevant:

benefit concentration

unequal improvement

unchanged subgroups

adverse subgroup effects

and:

distribution uncertainty

An average result must not erase significant adverse outcomes.

Canonical distinction:

Average Outcome ≠ Outcome for Every Affected Subject

  • Temporary Outcome


A Mitigation Outcome may initially appear successful but depend on
temporary effects.

HMOM must distinguish:

Temporary Mitigation Outcome

from:

Persistent Mitigation Outcome

A temporary outcome may still be useful, but it must not be represented as
durable where persistence has not been established.

  • Outcome Degradation


A previously beneficial Outcome may degrade as:

effects decay

Harm returns

new consequences emerge

Mitigation conditions change

or:

the affected scope expands

HMOM must permit Outcome reassessment.

Canonical principle:

A Mitigation Outcome is not permanently fixed by its first determination.

  • Outcome Reversal


Where previously reduced Harm materially returns, the Outcome may reverse.

Conceptually:

Mitigated → Degrading → Mitigation Lost

Historical Mitigation remains part of the lifecycle record.

However:

Historical Mitigation Success ≠ Current Mitigation Success

  • Harm Displacement in Outcome


HMEM may identify that Harm decreased in one location while increasing
elsewhere.

HMOM determines what this means for the complete Mitigation Outcome.

If Harm is materially transferred rather than reduced:

Local Improvement ≠ Overall Harm Reduction

The Outcome must preserve the displaced Harm.

  • Harm Substitution in Outcome


Where:

Harm₁ decreases

but:

Harm₂ emerges or increases

HMOM must not automatically declare successful overall Mitigation.

The relationship must be evaluated within the complete Harm context.

Canonical principle:

Replacing one Harm with another does not automatically produce a
legitimate Mitigation Outcome.

  • Intervention-Induced Harm


New Harm produced by Mitigation remains independently governable.

HMOM must preserve it when determining the Outcome.

A substantial reduction of Harm₁ may coexist with material Harm₂ caused by
the intervention.

Therefore:

Mitigation Benefit ≠ Permission to Ignore Mitigation-Induced Harm

  • Net Mitigation Outcome


A net representation may sometimes support understanding of the overall
direction of Harm.

However:

Net Benefit ≠ Complete Harm Representation

Materially distinct Harm must not disappear into aggregation.

For example:

large benefit for one population + severe Harm to a smaller population

must not automatically be reduced to a single positive number.

HMOM therefore preserves both:

aggregate direction

and:

materially distinct Harm conditions

where required.

  • Continuing Harm Production


A Mitigation Outcome may be beneficial while additional Harm continues to
be produced.

Conceptually:

Existing Harm Reduction > Continuing Harm Production

may produce net improvement.

But:

Net Improvement ≠ Containment

HMOM must preserve the active Harm production signal for HCOS and broader
HAGA™ governance.

  • Outcome and Harm State


Mitigation Outcome may contribute to a Harm State transition.

For example:

Active → Reduced

or:

Stabilized → Reduced

However, HMOM does not independently own Harm State classification.

Therefore:

Mitigation Outcome ≠ Harm State

HSS remains responsible for formal Harm State governance.

  • Outcome and Mitigation Need


An Outcome may:

satisfy

reduce

change

divide

create

or:

leave active

a Mitigation Need.

Therefore:

Mitigation Outcome Established ≠ Mitigation Need Closed

Further Mitigation may remain necessary.

  • Outcome and Recovery


A successful Mitigation Outcome does not establish Recovery.

A subject, system, organization, or environment may remain substantially
impaired after Harm has been reduced.

Therefore:

Mitigated ≠ Recovered

The resulting Mitigation condition becomes relevant input to HRRHS.

  • Outcome and Residual Harm


Remaining Harm after Mitigation may later become:

Residual Harm

but HMOM does not make the formal Residual Harm determination.

It preserves the Harm that remains for subsequent governance.

Canonical distinction:

Remaining Harm After Mitigation ≠ Formally Determined Residual Harm by
Default

HRRHS owns that determination.

  • Outcome and Resolution


Mitigation Outcome does not establish Resolution.

Even successful Mitigation may leave:

Recovery requirements

Residual Harm

continuing monitoring

unresolved Harm conditions

or:

future lifecycle requirements

Therefore:

Mitigation Success ≠ Harm Resolution

  • Outcome and Finality


No Mitigation Outcome, including complete achievement of the Mitigation
Objective, automatically establishes Finality.

Therefore:

Mitigation Objective Achieved ≠ Harm Lifecycle Final

Finality remains governed by HRFS.

  • Outcome Uncertainty


Outcome uncertainty may result from:

uncertain effects

incomplete affected-scope information

unknown persistence

conflicting component effects

distributed Harm

delayed consequences

or:

unresolved intervention-induced Harm

HMOM must preserve this uncertainty.

Canonical principle:

Incomplete Outcome Basis ≠ Successful Mitigation Outcome

  • Outcome-to-Verification Handoff


HMOM produces:

  • Minimum Implementation Framework


A minimum HMOM implementation requires eight capabilities.

  • Effect Integration


Integrate materially relevant beneficial, neutral, adverse, mixed,
temporary, persistent, distributed, and uncertain Mitigation Effects.

  • Objective Achievement Assessment


Compare observed effects against the Governed Mitigation Objective and
determine the materially relevant achievement gap.

  • Remaining Harm Preservation


Identify and preserve unreduced, partially reduced, persistent,
unaffected, redistributed, and newly produced Harm.

  • Composite, Collective & Distributed Outcome Governance


Determine Mitigation Outcome across multiple Harm components, subjects,
groups, systems, organizations, locations, and domains without treating
local success as complete success.

  • Adverse, Displaced & Substituted Harm Integration


Incorporate Harm displacement, substitution, intervention-induced Harm,
and materially relevant adverse effects into the complete Outcome.

  • Persistence, Degradation & Reassessment Governance


Distinguish temporary from persistent outcomes and support degradation,
reversal, and reassessment when Harm reality changes.

  • Outcome State & Lifecycle Handoff


Establish the appropriate Mitigation Outcome State while preserving
boundaries with Harm State, Recovery, Residual Harm, Resolution, and
Finality.

  • Governed Output


Produce a traceable Governed Harm Mitigation Outcome suitable for final
Mitigation Verification. 37. Use Case 1 — Most Harm Improves but One
Critical Component Remains

A Mitigation process produces substantial reduction across several
components of a governed Harm.

However, one materially significant Harm component remains unchanged.

HMOM does not convert:

Several Successful Effects

into:

Complete Mitigation

It establishes the appropriate Partial or Insufficient Mitigation Outcome
and preserves the remaining Harm for further governance.

The governing principle is:

A Mitigation Outcome must represent the Harm that remains as clearly as
the Harm that improved.

  • Use Case 2 — Overall Improvement With New Harm Elsewhere


A Mitigation intervention substantially improves the original Harm
condition for most affected subjects.

The same intervention produces a materially significant new Harm affecting
another group.

HMOM preserves both:

Original Harm Reduction

and:

New Harm Condition

when determining the overall Mitigation Outcome.

It does not hide the new Harm inside an average positive result.

The governing principle is:

A positive overall direction does not make materially distinct adverse
Harm disappear.

  • Governance Requirements


HMOM requires that:

  • Outcome remain distinct from individual Mitigation Effects
  • all materially relevant effects be integrated
  • Outcome remain traceable to the Mitigation Objective
  • objective achievement be evaluated against actual Harm change
  • remaining Harm remain explicit
  • Partial Mitigation remain a legitimate Outcome
  • beneficial effect not automatically establish sufficient
    Mitigation
  • component success not automatically establish composite success
  • individual improvement not automatically establish collective


Mitigation; - local success not automatically establish distributed
Mitigation; - domain-specific success not automatically establish complete
Mitigation; - unequal effect distribution remain visible; - temporary
Outcome remain distinguishable from persistent Outcome; - degradation and
reversal remain governable; - displaced and substituted Harm remain
visible; - intervention-induced Harm remain part of the complete Outcome
context; - net representations not erase materially distinct Harm; -
continuing Harm production remain visible; - Mitigation Outcome remain
distinct from Harm State; - Mitigation Outcome not automatically close
Mitigation Need; - Mitigation remain distinct from Recovery; - remaining
Harm not automatically become formal Residual Harm; - Mitigation Success
remain distinct from Resolution; - Mitigation Objective achievement remain
distinct from Finality; - uncertainty remain explicit; - material changes
trigger Outcome reassessment; - Outcome remain subject to final Mitigation
Verification.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HMOM — Harm Mitigation Outcome Module governs how materially relevant
Mitigation Effects are integrated and interpreted to establish the
resulting Mitigation condition of an existing governed Harm. It governs
objective achievement, remaining Harm, Partial and Insufficient
Mitigation, composite, collective, distributed and cross-domain outcomes,
unequal distribution, persistence, degradation, reversal, Harm
displacement, Harm substitution, intervention-induced Harm, net Mitigation
direction, uncertainty, further Mitigation requirements, and Outcome
reassessment. HMOM does not establish the original Mitigation Need, select
or execute Mitigation Action, independently determine Mitigation Effect,
perform final Mitigation Verification, independently classify Harm State,
perform Harm Containment, formally determine Recovery or Residual Harm,
establish Resolution or Finality, determine general causation, or assign
accountability, responsibility, or liability.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


A Mitigation Outcome is not the best improvement produced by an
intervention. It is the complete governed meaning of what changed and what
still remains.

Some Harm may decrease while other Harm persists, one group may improve
while another worsens, and a strong local result may coexist with an
incomplete overall outcome.

HMOM preserves that complete picture before Mitigation is allowed to
become a verified governance conclusion.

Mitigation Outcome is defined not only by the Harm that improved, but also
by the Harm that remains.
