Canonical Definition

DSMM — Deployment State Monitoring Module is the Core Module within
SDPS that governs how the actual state of a software deployment is
observed, evaluated, classified, and reassessed against its authorized
scope, intended operational conditions, production version identity,
verification requirements, and applicable intervention thresholds.

DSMM establishes deployment-state monitoring as an evidence-supported

governance mechanism rather than a collection of technical metrics,
pipeline completion signals, or automated health reports.

It governs the relationship between deployment execution, production

version state, monitoring coverage, operational observations, expected
conditions, material deviations, and the decisions required when the
resulting operational reality cannot be sufficiently established or
accepted.

The module distinguishes technical execution completion from verified

deployment establishment, monitoring activity from sufficient
observability, software availability from operational acceptability,
and observed anomalies from confirmed causes.

DSMM applies to manually operated deployments, continuous delivery systems,

distributed applications, cloud infrastructure, autonomous AI agents,
embedded software, industrial systems, and robotic platforms.

Its principal governance output is the Software Deployment State
Profile.

Operational Role

DSMM establishes and maintains an evidence-supported representation of the

actual deployment state.

It governs:

  • Deployment-state identity
  • Monitoring purpose and scope
  • Intended operational state references
  • Deployment execution observations
  • Production version references
  • Monitoring coverage
  • Observation source identification
  • Evidence reliability and freshness
  • Deployment progression monitoring
  • Operational condition verification
  • Deployment-state consistency
  • Monitoring gaps
  • Deployment anomalies
  • State uncertainty and conflicts
  • Intervention thresholds
  • Monitoring-driven escalation
  • Deployment-state classification
  • Material-change reassessment
  • Historical state continuity
  • Handoff to rollback and recovery governance

DSMM does not independently authorize deployments, redefine production
artifact identity, execute rollback actions, or determine recovery
success without the applicable evidence and governance references.

Operational Question

How can the actual state of a software deployment be observed,
evaluated, and reassessed when execution outcomes, production
versions, operational conditions, monitoring coverage, and material
changes may differ from the intended deployment state, while ensuring
that insufficient evidence, conflicting observations, unacceptable
conditions, and intervention requirements remain identifiable and
governable?

DSMM is the fourth Core Module within SDPS.

It receives the intended deployment conditions from DPM, authorization

conditions from DAM, and production version evidence from PVCM.

It evaluates whether the observed deployment state supports continuation,

operational acceptance, restriction, intervention, rollback
consideration, or recovery escalation.

Its outputs provide the operational evidence basis for RRVM and other

applicable software lifecycle governance mechanisms.

  • Governed Space

Software Deployment State Monitoring Governance

This governed space concerns the observation, assessment, classification,

and reassessment of software deployment states within defined
operational environments.

It begins when deployment execution or an applicable deployment-state
observation becomes relevant.

It continues through deployment progression, activation, operational
verification, exception handling, stabilization, and material
deployment-related changes.

Monitoring may continue after technical execution is complete where
the deployment's operational conditions, version consistency, or
verification obligations remain unresolved.

The scope of DSMM is deployment-state governance, not unrestricted
monitoring of every aspect of ongoing software operations.

Foundational Principle

A deployment state must be established from evidence of the
operational reality that exists, not merely from reports that
deployment actions were completed or that monitoring systems are
active.

Deployment Executed ≠ Deployment Successfully Established.

Monitoring Active ≠ Deployment State Fully Observable.

Service Available ≠ Deployment Operationally Acceptable.

No Alert ≠ No Material Problem.

Observed Condition ≠ Verified Cause.

Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum DSMM implementation must establish eight governance layers.
Layer 1 — Deployment State Identity

Identify the deployment, environment, production version references, and

monitored operational scope. Layer 2 — Intended and Authorized
Conditions

Preserve the intended deployment state, operational acceptance criteria,

and applicable authorization restrictions. Layer 3 — Observation

Collect relevant execution, version, configuration, dependency, and

operational condition evidence. Layer 4 — Monitoring Coverage

Identify which required conditions and operational segments are
observable and where material gaps remain. Layer 5 — Verification

Compare observed conditions against intended and authorized states using

evidence of sufficient relevance and reliability. Layer 6 — Anomaly
and Intervention

Identify material deviations, monitoring failures, intervention thresholds,

and escalation requirements. Layer 7 — Classification and Continuity

Establish the deployment-state classification, preserve historical

observations, and reassess material changes. Layer 8 — Governed Output
and Handoff

Establish the Software Deployment State Profile and provide relevant

intervention, rollback, or recovery references to RRVM.

The implementation may use manual observation, monitoring platforms,
automated analysis, autonomous agents, or other suitable mechanisms.

The methodological requirements remain independent of the chosen monitoring

technology.

Cross-Module Boundary

DPM → DSMM

DPM establishes intended deployment conditions and verification

requirements.

DSMM evaluates whether the observed deployment state satisfies those

conditions.

DAM → DSMM

DAM establishes authorization conditions and restrictions.

DSMM identifies observed deviations from those conditions and supports

authorization reassessment where required.

PVCM → DSMM

PVCM establishes production version identity and consistency.

DSMM evaluates the operational consequences of the observed production

version state.

DSMM → RRVM

DSMM identifies unacceptable conditions, intervention triggers, and

evidence supporting rollback or recovery assessment.

RRVM governs corrective action selection, authority, execution, and

recovery verification.

Use Case 1 — Deployment Pipeline Reports Success While Production

Service Is Degraded

A software provider deploys an authorized release into production.

The deployment pipeline reports successful completion.

The expected artifact is present and activated.

However, application response times increase substantially, and a critical

service dependency begins failing.

DSMM evaluates:

Execution Completion → Production Version → Operational Metrics → Required

Conditions → Monitoring Coverage → Operational Acceptance

The assessment identifies:

  • Deployment execution completed
  • The intended software version is present
  • Critical operational conditions are not satisfied
  • The deployment cannot be classified as operationally established
  • Intervention assessment is required

The appropriate classification is:

Operationally Unacceptable — Intervention Required.

Deployment Executed ≠ Deployment Successfully Established.

Use Case 2 — A Robotic Fleet Reports Healthy Devices but Monitoring

Coverage Is Incomplete

A robotics operator deploys a software update across autonomous mobile

robots.

The management system reports healthy operation for connected devices.

Several robots are temporarily disconnected and provide no current
telemetry.

The platform displays an overall successful deployment state.

DSMM evaluates:

Deployment Scope → Device Coverage → Observed Conditions → Missing

Telemetry → Evidence Sufficiency → Deployment State Classification

The assessment identifies:

  • Connected devices provide acceptable operational observations
  • Disconnected devices remain unobserved
  • The monitoring scope does not cover the complete fleet
  • The available evidence cannot support a fleet-wide operational

acceptance claim.

The appropriate classification is:

Monitoring Insufficient — Partial Operational Verification.

Healthy Observed Devices ≠ Entire Fleet Operationally Verified.

Canonical Boundary

DSMM governs the observation, evidence assessment, verification,

classification, and reassessment of actual software deployment states
within defined operational environments.

It establishes monitoring scope, observation requirements, evidence

coverage, operational condition verification, intended–observed state
alignment, anomaly identification, intervention trigger references,
and historical deployment-state continuity.

DSMM does not authorize deployments, independently establish
production artifact identity, execute corrective actions, or determine
recovery success outside applicable governance mechanisms.

Its principal governance output is the:

Software Deployment State Profile

This output provides the evidence-supported deployment-state basis for

operational intervention, rollback or recovery assessment, and
relevant software lifecycle governance.

Canonical Closing Statement

A deployment is not successfully established merely because its
execution has finished or its monitoring dashboard appears healthy.

A pipeline may report success while the deployed service is degraded.

A software version may be correct while its configuration is
incompatible with the operational environment.

A monitoring system may report normal conditions while critical
devices remain unobserved.

An autonomous agent may detect a deployment failure without possessing
authority to execute corrective actions.

And a previously verified deployment state may become unacceptable
when operational conditions materially change.

DSMM establishes the governance structure required to distinguish these

realities.

It requires deployment-state conclusions to be supported by observations

that are relevant, sufficiently complete, reliable, and current.

It preserves monitoring blind spots, conflicting evidence, and unresolved

conditions instead of allowing them to disappear behind simplified
success indicators.

It connects deployment execution, production version identity, operational

verification, and intervention thresholds without collapsing their
separate governance responsibilities.

And it ensures that operational acceptance remains a verifiable
conclusion rather than an assumption derived from technical execution.

The purpose of deployment monitoring is not merely to observe whether
software is running. It is to establish whether the actual deployment
state remains consistent with its intended, authorized, and
operationally acceptable conditions—and to identify when that
conclusion can no longer be supported.

DSMM transforms deployment monitoring from passive technical observation

into an evidence-supported governance mechanism for operational
reality, intervention readiness, and software lifecycle continuity.
