Canonical Definition

Augmented Cognition Module (ACM) defines the structural conditions under which
AI-assisted enhancement of human cognition remains traceable, accountable,
reality-connected, governance-valid, and operationally reliable without creating
uncontrolled dependency, cognition displacement, authority confusion,
or degradation of human cognitive autonomy.

ACM governs augmented cognition.

The module ensures that AI enhances human cognition rather than replacing,
obscuring, distorting, or silently controlling it.

A system satisfies ACM only if:

  • augmentation remains visible
  • augmentation influence remains assessable
  • human autonomy remains preservable
  • augmentation effects remain traceable
  • cognitive dependency remains governable
  • augmentation outcomes remain operationally valid

A Human-AI cognition environment that cannot distinguish augmentation from replacement
does not satisfy ACM.

Module Operational Role

ACM defines the cognitive-augmentation governance layer of HAICS.

Its role is to preserve valid enhancement of human cognition.

Module Operational Space

ACM governs:

  • cognitive augmentation
  • AI-assisted cognition
  • augmentation influence
  • human cognitive autonomy
  • augmentation traceability
  • cognitive dependency
  • augmentation accountability
  • Human-AI cognitive enhancement

The module applies wherever AI systems enhance human cognitive capabilities.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • accountable augmentation
  • human cognitive sovereignty
  • visible AI influence
  • governance-valid enhancement
  • reality-connected cognition
  • operationally reliable Human-AI collaboration

Its function is to ensure that cognitive enhancement remains beneficial
without undermining human cognitive integrity.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Augmented Cognition Object
The organization must define which augmentation activities require governance.

This may include:

  • AI-assisted analysis
  • AI-assisted decision support
  • AI-assisted planning
  • AI-assisted research
  • AI-assisted interpretation
  • AI-assisted learning
  • AI-assisted forecasting
  • AI-assisted operational cognition

2. Define Augmented Cognition Conditions
The system must define the conditions under which augmentation remains valid.

This includes:

  • transparency requirements
  • autonomy requirements
  • influence-visibility requirements
  • accountability requirements
  • dependency-control requirements
  • governance-valid augmentation conditions

3. Define Augmentation Degradation Detection Logic
The system must define how augmentation degradation is identified.

This may include:

  • cognitive dependency growth
  • augmentation opacity
  • authority confusion
  • human disengagement
  • cognition displacement
  • hidden influence conditions

4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic
The system must define governance logic for augmentation-integrity failures.

Governance response may include:

  • augmentation review
  • autonomy reassessment
  • influence analysis
  • governance intervention
  • escalation
  • augmentation restriction
  • operational invalidation where required

5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions
The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • augmentation activities
  • AI contributions
  • human decisions
  • governance reviews
  • intervention actions
  • resulting cognition outcomes

A Human-AI cognition environment must not remain augmentation-valid if materially significant
augmentation effects cannot be identified, reviewed, or governed.

Use Case 1 — AI-Augmented Financial Analyst

Scenario
A financial analyst continuously uses AI systems to evaluate markets, identify risks,
generate forecasts, and assess investment opportunities.

Application
ACM governs augmentation visibility, influence traceability,
and preservation of human decision authority.

Result
The organization gains stronger analytical capability while preserving accountability
and human cognitive autonomy.

Use Case 2 — AI-Augmented Medical Specialist

Scenario
A medical professional uses AI systems to support diagnosis, treatment planning,
and clinical interpretation.

Application
ACM governs cognitive augmentation, influence transparency, and autonomy preservation
throughout clinical reasoning processes.

Result
The environment gains stronger decision quality, improved patient safety,
and reduced exposure to uncontrolled AI dependency.
