Methodology

The platform strictly separates Source Fact, Evidence, Automated OOF® Analysis, and OOF® Approved Assessment. Automated analysis is generated intelligence and never becomes OOF® Approved without an explicit authorized review.

Unlike a general incident archive, this platform uses source-reported cases to raise preliminary questions about governance boundaries, possible controls and ways to verify them. Reported events, candidate explanations and unknown causes remain distinct. A recommendation is not evidence that a control was implemented or tested.

Source Collection and Verification

Each factual field retains source provenance, publication time, retrieval time, and event time where available. Independent sources are distinguished from copied or syndicated reporting. Copyrighted articles are not reproduced.

Public evidence status uses five explicit states: Source-Reported, Independently Corroborated, Officially Documented, Evidence Insufficient, and Contradicted / Disputed. The label describes the available evidence, not whether an allegation is true.

Incident Monitoring and Assessment

The normal lifecycle is Discovery, Acquisition, Incident Resolution, Deduplication, Evidence Extraction, Corroboration, Fact Reconstruction, Governance Mechanism Detection, Architecture Analysis, Severity Analysis, Governance Findings, Automated Publication, and Continuous Reassessment. Human review is optional and is not required for normal automated operation.

The Human Review Priority Queue is a selected subset awaiting authorized human review. It is not a count of every record marked Not performed. Queue selection is triggered by High or Critical automated severity, weak or unresolved methodological assignment, or causal wording supported by a single source.

Discovery is scheduled daily. New candidates and source corrections use separate processing limits; the default limit for each is 10, and the new-incident daily budget persists across retries. Limits can be configured within operational bounds, but a higher setting does not guarantee quality or throughput.

Architecture Mapping

The engine retrieves canonical architectures, Parent Standards, and Core Modules dynamically from the existing OOF® Knowledge Layer. Assignments include matched governed terms, supporting source evidence, confidence, uncertainty, and relevant standards or modules. No fixed architecture list is embedded in the public analysis.

Coverage States

Public monitoring distinguishes Automated Analysis from Insufficient Evidence. Coverage remains Under Review unless supported evidence justifies a stronger state. A missing match or missing evidence is never converted into a governance failure.

Continuous Reassessment

New records are analyzed as they enter the system. A change to source evidence can trigger reassessment; a methodology or engine version change alone does not routinely reopen historical cases. Previous versions remain auditable.

External Frameworks and Engine Status

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0, EU AI Act, MITRE ATLAS and OWASP GenAI guidance are external references. Their presence here is not a verified mapping, an equivalence claim or a compliance determination. Specific mappings require an exact version, scope, rationale and supporting evidence.

The local analysis pipeline operates independently. A production incident-assessment connection to the Governance Intelligence Engine™ has not been verified; no GIE-generated results are claimed here.

Prevention Guidance

Each full incident report separates the governance gap to examine, recommended preventive control, verification method, relevant OOF® methodology, and evidence limitation. Recommendations remain preliminary until the underlying evidence and control are reviewed in the incident context.

Dataset Scope

Counts and rates describe only the approved public dataset and must not be treated as absolute proof of architecture quality, company fault, legal liability, regulatory non-compliance, or global incident prevalence.

V3 Trust and Assessment Quality Layer

Every public result is labelled Automated Preliminary Governance Assessment. The interface separates known record facts, reported claims, material unknowns, evidence gaps, architecture relevance, preliminary findings, and governance questions. Assessment confidence is categorical: High, Moderate, Limited, or Evidence Insufficient.

V3 Data Model and Boundaries

Source Fact remains immutable in the incident store. Evidence Snapshot records provenance and corroboration. Automated Preliminary Governance Assessment contains versioned architecture analysis, standards/modules, findings, evidence gaps, categorical confidence, governance questions, and reassessment history. It is not an official investigation, legal determination, regulatory finding, certification, or OOF® Approved Assessment.

Migration and Test Strategy

All existing incidents receive a first automated assessment without modifying their original source record. Tests verify source immutability, stable identity, dynamic knowledge retrieval, explainable assignments, evidence links, continuous version numbers, and the rule that automated output cannot become OOF® Approved. Future acquisition adapters remain independent of governance reasoning.
