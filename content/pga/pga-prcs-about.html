About the Prediction Record & Continuity Standard

What Is the Prediction Record & Continuity Standard?

The Prediction Record & Continuity Standard (PRCS) is the final Parent Standard within PGA™ — Prediction Governance Architecture .

It governs how a prediction and its complete lifecycle history remain identifiable, versioned, temporally ordered, reconstructable, and historically continuous as the prediction changes over time.

A prediction may be formed, communicated, relied upon, revised, superseded, withdrawn, associated with an outcome, evaluated for performance, archived, or later brought back into use.

PRCS ensures that these changes do not destroy the historical record connecting one prediction state to another.

Its foundational principle is:

The current prediction state must not erase the historical prediction record from which it evolved.

Why Does PRCS Exist?

Predictions rarely remain static.

A prediction may begin at:

35% probability

later change to:

55%

then:

78%

and finally:

92%

before the outcome becomes known.

If a system stores only the final 92% prediction, the historical record becomes misleading.

The system can no longer reliably answer:

What was originally predicted? What probability existed six months before the outcome? When did the prediction materially change? What information was available at each stage? Which version was communicated? Which version was relied upon? Which version existed when a decision was made? Was the prediction later superseded or withdrawn? Which historical state was used for performance assessment?

PRCS exists to preserve those answers.

Prediction Change Must Preserve Prediction History.

What Does PRCS Govern?

PRCS governs the historical continuity of a Governed Prediction across its complete lifecycle.

This includes governance of:

Prediction Identity; historical Prediction States; version history; temporal ordering; state transitions; Prediction Lineage; methodology-version references; Prediction Basis history; assumption history; probability and uncertainty history; Prediction Horizon changes; Predictive Boundary changes; communication history; reliance history; revision history; supersession history; withdrawal history; outcome-association history; Predictive Performance history; archival continuity; migration continuity; lifecycle re-entry; Historical Gaps; reconstruction; Record Integrity.

The objective is not merely data retention.

The objective is:

historical reconstructability.

Current State vs Historical Record

A system may legitimately need to know the current prediction.

But the current prediction represents only:

what the prediction says now.

PRCS preserves:

what the prediction has said throughout its lifecycle.

These are fundamentally different governance objects.

Current Prediction State ≠ Complete Prediction History

Prediction Versioning

Material changes should remain historically distinguishable.

A prediction may evolve:

P1 → P2 → P3 → P4

PRCS preserves that lineage rather than allowing P4 to overwrite P1, P2, and P3.

Each material state can therefore remain associated with the conditions under which it existed.

This is essential for later reconstruction and performance assessment.

Revision, Supersession, and Withdrawal

PRCS distinguishes lifecycle change from historical destruction.

Revision ≠ Historical Replacement

A revised prediction does not erase the prediction that preceded it.

Supersession ≠ Deletion

A newer prediction becoming operationally current does not make the earlier prediction historically nonexistent.

Withdrawal ≠ Historical Erasure

A prediction leaving active use does not remove it from the historical Prediction Record.

Historical Reconstruction

PRCS enables a governed system to reconstruct questions such as:

What exactly was predicted at time T?

What probability applied at that time?

Which assumptions applied?

Which Prediction Basis was available?

Which methodology version produced the prediction?

Had the prediction already been revised?

Was it communicated or relied upon?

Which version existed before the outcome became known?

Which version was later evaluated for performance?

This creates a temporal governance layer around the prediction.

Decision-Time Prediction

The prediction available when a decision was made may differ significantly from the final prediction.

PRCS therefore preserves the relevant historical state.

Decision-Time Prediction ≠ Final Prediction

A system must not evaluate a historical decision using a prediction version that did not yet exist when the decision occurred.

Probability & Uncertainty History

Historical probability must remain version-specific.

For example:

P1 — 28%

P2 — 46%

P3 — 71%

P4 — 91%

The final 91% probability must not replace the earlier probability states.

The same principle applies to uncertainty, confidence, assumptions, horizons, boundaries, and conditions.

Outcome knowledge must not retrospectively make an earlier prediction appear more certain than it actually was.

Prediction Lineage

Prediction history may be more complex than a simple sequence.

A prediction may:

branch into multiple scenario predictions; be derived from another prediction; merge with another predictive path; move between systems; become archived; later re-enter active governance.

PRCS preserves the relationships among these historical states.

Prediction Lineage ≠ Prediction Identity

Related predictions may share lineage without being the same Prediction Object.

Outcome & Performance Continuity

Once an outcome becomes sufficiently governed, POFPS may associate the historical prediction with that outcome and determine Predictive Performance.

PRCS then preserves those relationships within the continuing Prediction Record.

This prevents performance analysis from becoming detached from the prediction state that actually existed before the outcome was known.

If performance is later reassessed, both assessments can remain historically reconstructable.

Performance Reassessment ≠ Historical Performance Erasure

Historical Corrections

Historical records may sometimes require correction.

PRCS does not prohibit legitimate correction.

It prevents correction from becoming silent rewriting.

Where appropriate:

Original Historical State → Correction → Corrected State

should remain reconstructable.

Correction ≠ Historical Replacement

Historical Gaps

Prediction history may be incomplete.

For example:

the original prediction may be missing; a revision may not have been recorded; a timestamp may be unavailable; the applicable methodology version may be unknown; probability history may be incomplete.

PRCS makes these gaps governable.

A Historical Gap does not automatically invalidate everything that remains.

Historical Gap ≠ Automatic Invalidity

Instead, the degree of reconstructability should be explicitly qualified.

Record Integrity

PRCS protects Prediction Records against historical distortions such as:

silent deletion; retrospective rewriting; version substitution; backdating; broken lineage; selective preservation; deletion of failed predictions; deletion of withdrawn predictions; preservation only of final predictions; detachment of outcome or performance records.

A Prediction Record should preserve what actually existed historically, not merely the version most convenient to show later.

Cross-System Continuity

Prediction Records may move between:

platforms; databases; organizations; AI systems; agents; registries; archival systems.

Migration should preserve historical identity and lineage.

Migration ≠ New Origin

Likewise:

Format Transformation ≠ Prediction Transformation

Changing where or how a Prediction Record is stored should not silently change what that record historically means.

Machine-Readable Historical Governance

PRCS supports machine-readable Prediction Records.

A governed system should be capable of answering questions such as:

Which prediction version was active on a specific date?

Which probability was communicated?

Which version was relied upon?

What changed between P2 and P3?

Which version was superseded?

Was the prediction later withdrawn?

Which outcome became associated with it?

Which performance assessment applies to which historical prediction state?

This enables Prediction Record governance to become machine-interpretable and, where appropriate, machine- enforceable.

What PRCS Does Not Govern

PRCS does not:

create predictions; determine Prediction Methodology; select Prediction Basis; establish assumptions; form probabilities; determine Prediction Feasibility; compose Collective Predictions;

determine Communication or Reliance legitimacy; decide whether a prediction should be revised or withdrawn; establish Operational Reality; determine Predictive Performance; establish legal ownership; determine liability; replace general evidence governance; replace general memory governance.

PRCS governs:

the historical continuity of the prediction across those lifecycle events.

Relationship to the PGA™ Governance Flow

PRCS occupies the final governed space within PGA™:

Prediction Object & Purpose → Prediction Methodology → Prediction Basis → Prediction Assumptions → Prediction Formation → Probability & Uncertainty → Prediction Feasibility → Collective Prediction where applicable → Communication & Reliance → Monitoring & Revision → Outcome & Performance → Prediction Record & Continuity

PRCS closes the lifecycle by ensuring that the historical outputs generated across the architecture remain connected and reconstructable.

It transforms:

Prediction Lifecycle History

into:

Governed Historical Prediction Continuity

Why PRCS Matters

Without historical continuity, prediction governance can become vulnerable to hindsight.

A system can appear more accurate simply because:

earlier probabilities disappeared; failed predictions were removed; final versions replaced original versions; withdrawn predictions vanished; methodologies were retrospectively relabeled; decision-time predictions were replaced by later forecasts; outcome knowledge changed the interpretation of earlier records.

PRCS prevents the historical record from being reconstructed around the outcome after the outcome is already known.

This matters for:

AI forecasting; enterprise forecasting; investment analysis; prediction markets; risk forecasting; industrial systems; autonomous agents; public forecasting; model evaluation; predictive audits; methodology assessment; long-term performance comparison.

Use Case 1 — The Prediction That Became “Correct” After Being Revised Four Times

An AI forecasting system predicts that a major industrial project will become operational:

P1 — March 2028

Later:

P2 — June 2028

Later:

P3 — September 2028

Finally:

P4 — 18 September 2028

Actual operational date:

20 September 2028

The platform displays only the latest forecast and reports:

“Prediction error: 2 days.”

The critical governance question is:

Does the final prediction legitimately represent the complete historical predictive record?

PRCS answers:

No.

The complete lineage remains:

P1 → P2 → P3 → P4 → Outcome

P4 may legitimately have a two-day error.

But P4 must not erase the earlier predictive states.

PRCS preserves:

original prediction; every material revision; revision timestamps; applicable methodology state; applicable Prediction Basis; Decision-Time Prediction where relevant; final prediction; Outcome Association; subsequent performance assessment.

This allows POFPS to distinguish:

Original Prediction Performance

from:

Final Prediction Performance

and:

Complete Lifecycle Performance

The historical record cannot be rewritten around the final successful prediction.

Final Prediction State ≠ Complete Prediction Lifecycle

Revision ≠ Historical Replacement

Use Case 2 — A Prediction Platform Deletes Failed and Withdrawn Predictions

A public forecasting platform has operated for five years.

Users see that a prominent predictor has:

89% historical accuracy.

During an independent governance review, historical system records show that:

several incorrect predictions were deleted; withdrawn predictions disappeared from public performance history; superseded predictions were replaced by their final versions; earlier probability states were no longer accessible; only successfully resolved predictions remained fully visible.

The displayed 89% accuracy may therefore describe:

the surviving prediction record

rather than:

the complete prediction history.

The critical governance question becomes:

Can predictive performance be considered historically trustworthy when the population from which performance is calculated cannot itself be reconstructed?

PRCS requires preservation of:

Original Prediction → Revisions → Supersession / Withdrawal → Outcome → Performance

where those states materially existed.

A withdrawn prediction may cease to be active.

It does not cease to have existed.

An incorrect prediction may reduce performance.

It does not become eligible for historical deletion merely because it was incorrect.

PRCS therefore enables the platform's actual historical prediction population to be reconstructed before POFPS evaluates performance.

Withdrawal ≠ Historical Erasure

Failure ≠ Permission to Delete

Visible Prediction History ≠ Complete Prediction History by Default

Canonical Boundary

PRCS governs the identity, versioning, temporal ordering, lineage, historical reconstruction, lifecycle persistence, record sufficiency, integrity, migration continuity, archival continuity, outcome and performance

linkage, and legitimate lifecycle re-entry of Governed Predictions. It ensures that material historical prediction states remain reconstructable without allowing revision, supersession, withdrawal, outcome knowledge, methodology change, model upgrade, migration, or performance reassessment to silently rewrite the Prediction Record. It does not create or revise predictions, establish Operational Reality, determine Predictive Performance, determine legal ownership or liability, or replace broader evidence, identity, provenance, or memory governance.

Canonical Closing Statement

A prediction cannot be governed across time if every new version is allowed to redefine what existed before it.

PRCS preserves the historical chain from the first governed prediction state to its revisions, use, withdrawal or supersession, outcome, performance, archival state, and possible future re-entry.

The current prediction tells us what the system believes now. The Prediction Record tells us what the system actually predicted then.

The future may change the status of a prediction. It must not change its past.
