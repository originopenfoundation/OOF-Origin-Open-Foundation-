Prediction Object Identification Module

Module: POIM — Prediction Object Identification Module

Parent Standard: POPS — Prediction Object & Purpose Standard

Architecture: PGA™ — Prediction Governance Architecture

Category: Governance & Enforcement

Subcategory: Prediction Governance

Governed Space: Prediction Object Identification

Primary Governed Object: Prediction Object

Module Type: Core Module

Canonical Language: UCL — Universal Canonical Language

Protection: MIP® — Methodological Intellectual Property

1. Canonical Definition

The Prediction Object Identification Module (POIM) governs the identification, differentiation, and establishment of the specific future or unresolved outcome, state, condition, event, value, behavior, development, or other predictive subject that is to become the governed Prediction Object.

POIM transforms an intended subject of prediction into an identifiable predictive object capable of remaining distinguishable throughout subsequent PGA™ governance.

A topic, domain, question, expectation, scenario, or area of interest does not become a governed Prediction Object merely because predictive activity concerns it.

2. Operational Role

POIM is the first operational module of the PGA™ Prediction Lifecycle.

Its role is to establish what is entering prediction governance before purpose, target, horizon, boundary, methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, or predictive output are established.

POIM prevents predictive processes from beginning with an ambiguous or shifting object.

Its principal transition is:

Intended Predictive Subject → Identified Prediction Object

3. Internal Module Flow

→ Prediction Object Identification → Predictive Purpose Definition → Prediction Target Specification → Prediction Horizon Definition → Predictive Boundary Establishment

Current Position: Prediction Object Identification

POIM produces the identified Prediction Object required by PPDM — Predictive Purpose Definition Module and the remaining POPS governance sequence.

4. Operational Space

POIM operates within the Prediction Object Identification space.

It governs the point at which a subject requiring predictive treatment becomes identifiable as a distinct Prediction Object.

The operational space includes:

predictive subject recognition; object identification; object differentiation; object identity establishment; object ambiguity detection; object overlap recognition; object granularity assessment; object continuity establishment; identification-state determination.

POIM does not determine the predicted result.

5. Operational Scope

POIM applies where a human, organization, model, AI system, autonomous system, forecasting mechanism, prediction market, analytical process, or hybrid system intends to generate or govern a prediction concerning a future or unresolved condition.

It applies regardless of whether the Prediction Object concerns:

physical events; environmental conditions; economic developments; financial or digital assets; organizational outcomes; human behavior; technological developments; political or institutional outcomes; operational conditions; machine behavior; market outcomes; measurable values; unresolved events; other legitimately identifiable predictive subjects.

The nature of the underlying domain does not transfer governance ownership of that domain to POIM.

6. Predictive Subject Recognition

POIM must establish that an identifiable subject exists to which predictive treatment is intended to apply.

The subject may concern something that has not yet occurred or an unresolved state whose outcome is not yet sufficiently established.

Recognition alone does not establish a governed Prediction Object.

Predictive Subject Recognition ≠ Prediction Object Identification

Recognition establishes that something is intended to be predicted.

Identification establishes what that something is.

7. Prediction Object Identification

The Prediction Object must be identifiable independently of the prediction subsequently made about it.

POIM must establish sufficient object identity to answer:

What is the distinct subject to which this predictive lifecycle will belong?

The Prediction Object may concern an event, state, condition, value, behavior, development, relationship, transition, or another sufficiently distinguishable predictive subject.

Identification must not depend solely upon the eventual predicted value or outcome.

Prediction Object ≠ Predicted Outcome

8. Object Differentiation

POIM must distinguish the intended Prediction Object from materially different adjacent objects.

Related predictive subjects may share:

domain; actors; location; timeframe; data; methodology;

underlying systems; events; terminology.

Such overlap does not make them the same Prediction Object.

Where two predictive propositions concern materially different subjects, they must remain distinguishable.

Related Predictive Objects ≠ Same Prediction Object

9. Object Granularity

The Prediction Object must be established at a level of granularity sufficient for subsequent governance.

An object may be too broad to support meaningful methodology, basis, assumptions, target specification, probability representation, feasibility assessment, outcome association, or performance evaluation.

An object may also be defined with artificial specificity unsupported by the predictive purpose or available predictive structure.

POIM therefore requires sufficient rather than maximal granularity.

Object Specificity ≠ Artificial Precision

10. Object Ambiguity

POIM must identify material ambiguity capable of causing different actors or systems to interpret the Prediction Object as different predictive subjects.

Material ambiguity may arise from:

undefined terminology; multiple possible objects; unclear object composition; unresolved object scope; changing object identity; conflation of object and outcome; conflation of object and domain.

A materially ambiguous object must not be treated as fully identified solely because a prediction can technically be produced for it.

Predictability ≠ Sufficient Object Identification

11. Composite Prediction Objects

A predictive subject may contain multiple components.

POIM must determine whether those components constitute:

one legitimate composite Prediction Object

or

multiple distinct Prediction Objects requiring separate predictive governance.

A composite object is legitimate only where its components form a sufficiently coherent predictive subject.

Aggregation of unrelated predictive subjects does not automatically establish a single Prediction Object.

Multiple Components ≠ Single Prediction Object

12. Prediction Object Identity

Once established, the Prediction Object must possess sufficient identity to remain referentially stable throughout its governed lifecycle.

Subsequent methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, predictive outputs, revisions, outcome references, performance results, and historical records must be capable of referring back to the relevant Prediction Object.

Object identity does not require that every property of the underlying subject remain unchanged.

It requires that the predictive subject remain sufficiently identifiable.

13. Prediction Object Continuity

POIM establishes the initial basis for Prediction Object continuity.

Where the underlying subject changes, later PGA™ governance must be capable of determining whether the original Prediction Object continues or whether a materially different Prediction Object has emerged.

POIM does not govern later lifecycle revision or record continuity.

It establishes the object identity against which such determinations can subsequently occur.

Object Evolution ≠ Automatic Object Replacement

Materially Different Object ≠ Continuation by Default

14. Object–Domain Separation

The domain in which a prediction occurs must remain distinguishable from the Prediction Object itself.

Examples of domains may include weather, finance, energy, healthcare, transportation, politics, manufacturing, digital assets, or autonomous systems.

A domain establishes contextual territory.

A Prediction Object identifies the specific subject entering predictive governance.

Predictive Domain ≠ Prediction Object

This separation allows PGA™ to remain domain-neutral.

15. Object–Target Separation

POIM governs identification of the Prediction Object.

It does not specify the precise Prediction Target.

For example, a governed object may concern a defined asset, system, event, population, or operational condition, while the Prediction Target subsequently specifies the particular future value, state, outcome, behavior, or development being predicted concerning that object.

Target specification belongs to PTSM — Prediction Target Specification Module .

Prediction Object ≠ Prediction Target

16. Object–Purpose Separation

Identification of what is being predicted does not determine why the prediction is being produced.

A single Prediction Object may support multiple legitimate predictive purposes.

Predictive purpose belongs to PPDM — Predictive Purpose Definition Module .

Prediction Object Identity ≠ Predictive Purpose

17. Object–Horizon Separation

POIM does not determine the temporal horizon over which the Prediction Object is evaluated.

Prediction Horizon belongs to PHDM — Prediction Horizon Definition Module .

The same Prediction Object may support predictions across different horizons while remaining the same underlying predictive object.

Prediction Object ≠ Prediction Horizon

18. Object–Boundary Separation

POIM identifies the predictive object.

It does not establish the complete inclusion and exclusion boundary governing the predictive proposition.

That function belongs to PBEM — Predictive Boundary Establishment Module .

Object identification must nevertheless be sufficiently precise for a predictive boundary to be subsequently established.

Object Identification ≠ Predictive Boundary Establishment

19. Identification Sufficiency

Prediction Object identification is sufficient when the object can be:

distinctly referenced; differentiated from materially different objects; carried into subsequent POPS governance; associated with later predictive states; preserved as a coherent reference throughout the Prediction Lifecycle; connected to an eventual governed outcome reference without redefining the original predictive subject.

Identification sufficiency does not establish methodological validity, predictive feasibility, probability quality, or prediction correctness.

20. Identification Failure Conditions

A Prediction Object must not be treated as sufficiently identified where material uncertainty remains concerning what predictive subject is actually being governed.

Potential failure conditions include:

no distinct predictive subject; unresolved object ambiguity; incompatible object interpretations; conflation of domain and object; conflation of object and predicted outcome; materially incoherent composite objects; insufficient differentiation from adjacent Prediction Objects;

object definition incapable of supporting subsequent governance.

Failure to establish the Prediction Object prevents legitimate progression through the POPS Internal Module Flow.

21. Governance Requirements

POIM requires that:

1. the intended predictive subject be recognized; 2. the Prediction Object be explicitly identifiable; 3. the object be distinguishable from its broader domain; 4. the object be distinguishable from the Prediction Target; 5. materially different adjacent objects remain distinguishable; 6. object granularity be sufficient for subsequent predictive governance; 7. material ambiguity be identified and resolved before identification is treated as complete; 8. composite objects possess sufficient predictive coherence; 9. Prediction Object identity be capable of supporting lifecycle continuity; 10. object identification remain within PGA™ governance boundaries.

22. Governance Logic

POIM applies the following operational logic:

Predictive Subject Presented

↓

Subject Recognition

↓

Candidate Prediction Object Identified

↓

Object Differentiation

↓

Granularity Assessment

↓

Ambiguity Assessment

↓

Composite Object Assessment where applicable

↓

Object Identity Establishment

↓

Identification Sufficiency Determination

↓

Identified Prediction Object

↓

PPDM — Predictive Purpose Definition Module

Where identification sufficiency is not established:

Identification Gap → Clarification / Differentiation → Reassessment

The object must not silently proceed as sufficiently identified.

23. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum implementation of POIM must establish:

A. Predictive Subject

A recognizable subject requiring predictive treatment.

B. Candidate Prediction Object

A specific object proposed for governance.

C. Object Description

A sufficiently precise description distinguishing the object from the broader predictive domain.

D. Object Differentiation

Identification of materially adjacent or potentially confusable Prediction Objects where relevant.

E. Granularity Determination

Confirmation that the object is neither materially underdefined nor artificially specified beyond what can legitimately be governed.

F. Ambiguity Status

Identification of unresolved ambiguity capable of changing the meaning of the Prediction Object.

G. Composite Status

Where applicable, determination of whether multiple components legitimately form one Prediction Object.

H. Object Identity Reference

A stable reference allowing subsequent PGA™ states to remain associated with the same Prediction Object.

I. Identification State

At minimum:

Unidentified → Candidate → Clarification Required → Identified

Only an Identified Prediction Object may proceed as sufficiently established into subsequent POPS governance.

24. Governance Outputs

POIM may produce:

Identified Prediction Object; Prediction Object identity reference;

object description; differentiation context; granularity determination; ambiguity status; composite-object status where applicable; identification state; unresolved identification requirements.

The primary governed output is:

Identified Prediction Object

This becomes the principal input into:

PPDM — Predictive Purpose Definition Module

26. Enterprise Application

POIM provides a common object-identification layer for predictive environments in which multiple people, models, systems, departments, agents, or external providers may generate predictions concerning related subjects.

A stable Prediction Object allows methodologies, Prediction Basis, assumptions, predictive outputs, revisions, outcome references, and performance records to remain associated with the correct predictive subject across systems and over time.

This supports interoperability between human forecasting, AI prediction, model-based forecasting, prediction markets, enterprise planning, risk systems, autonomous systems, and future machine-readable prediction governance.

27. Use Case 1 — Energy Demand Prediction

An energy operator intends to forecast electricity demand.

“Electricity demand” identifies a predictive domain but remains insufficient as a governed Prediction Object.

POIM establishes the specific predictive subject, such as electricity demand for a defined operational grid or system, and distinguishes it from related predictive objects such as generation availability, electricity price, reserve requirement, or

renewable output.

Once the Prediction Object is identified, subsequent POPS modules can establish its purpose, target, horizon, and predictive boundary.

28. Use Case 2 — Prediction Market Event

A prediction market offers contracts concerning a future institutional decision.

The market question, the institution, the decision process, the contract settlement condition, and the actual Prediction Object may not automatically be methodologically identical.

POIM identifies the specific predictive subject that the governed prediction concerns and separates it from adjacent objects or broader interpretations.

This allows subsequent PGA™ governance to determine what the prediction targets, what its market-derived probability represents, what information supports it, and how the prediction may legitimately be interpreted without allowing the Prediction Object itself to shift retrospectively.

29. Canonical Boundary

POIM governs the identification, differentiation, granularity, ambiguity, coherence, and initial continuity of the Prediction Object. It does not govern predictive purpose, target specification, prediction horizon, predictive boundary establishment, methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, probability, uncertainty, feasibility, collective prediction, communication, reliance, revision, outcome determination, performance evaluation, or Prediction Record continuity.

POIM identifies what enters prediction governance .

The subsequent POPS modules determine the remaining elements of the governed predictive frame.

Canonical Closing Statement

Prediction governance cannot begin with an undefined subject.

POIM establishes the identifiable Prediction Object to which the remainder of the governed Prediction Lifecycle can remain anchored.
