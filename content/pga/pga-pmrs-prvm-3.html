Governed Space

PRVM governs the controlled revision of an existing Governed Prediction when formal review establishes that its current predictive state can no longer remain unchanged without materially misrepresenting the prediction's present support, conditions, probability, uncertainty, feasibility, scope, horizon, or qualification.

It governs the transition:

Governed Prediction Review Determination + Existing Prediction State + Revision Basis → Governed Revised Prediction State

PRVM determines what may legitimately change, what must remain preserved, how the revised state relates to the prior state, and when proposed change exceeds ordinary revision and requires re-entry into earlier PGA™ governance.

PRVM does not erase, overwrite, or retrospectively replace the prediction that existed before revision.

Canonical Definition

The Prediction Revision Module (PRVM) defines the operational governance requirements by which an existing Governed Prediction may be substantively modified after formal review establishes a legitimate Revision Basis.

PRVM governs revision scope, revision proportionality, revision basis, affected predictive dimensions, preserved dimensions, probability and uncertainty changes, assumption changes, Prediction Basis changes, feasibility changes, horizon changes, boundary changes, qualification changes, revision timing, version creation, revision rationale, historical continuity, and downstream revision signaling.

Its foundational rule is:

A revised prediction is a new governed prediction state — not a rewritten historical prediction.

The legitimate structure is:

P1 → Revision Basis → Governed Revision → P2

not:

P1 silently becomes P2.

Operational Role

PRVM is the third operational module within PMRS.

Its focused operational question is:

When formal review establishes that the current prediction can no longer legitimately remain unchanged, what may be revised, how far may the revision extend, and how must the new predictive state remain connected to the prediction that preceded it?

PRVM receives a governed review determination arising from PMRS Review Trigger governance.

It may produce:

preserved prediction; qualified prediction; partially revised prediction; materially revised prediction; reformation requirement; new governed prediction version.

PRVM does not determine final supersession or withdrawal.

Those lifecycle transitions belong respectively to PSSM and PWM.

Internal Module Flow

Prediction Change Monitoring → Review Trigger → Prediction Revision → Prediction Supersession → Prediction Withdrawal

Current Position:

Prediction Revision

1. Revision Basis

No substantive prediction revision should occur without an identifiable Revision Basis.

A Revision Basis may include:

material new information; Prediction Basis deterioration; assumption change; assumption failure; probability-relevant change; uncertainty change; feasibility change; structural environmental change; collective prediction change; operational-condition change; corrected evidence; methodological correction; formal review finding.

Desire to Change Prediction ≠ Revision Basis

2. Revision Eligibility

A prediction becomes Revision-Eligible where formal review establishes that maintaining its current state would no longer adequately represent its governed predictive condition.

Revision eligibility does not require that every part of the prediction change.

Revision Eligibility ≠ Full Prediction Reconstruction

3. Prediction Preservation Outcome

Formal review may establish that no substantive revision is required.

The legitimate result is then:

Prediction Preserved

or:

Prediction Preserved with Qualification

PRVM must support non-revision as a legitimate outcome.

Review ≠ Mandatory Revision

4. Revision Necessity

Revision is necessary where material predictive meaning can no longer be preserved through qualification alone.

The question is not:

Can the prediction be changed?

The question is:

Must the predictive state change to remain methodologically legitimate?

5. Revision Proportionality

Revision should be proportionate to the material change that justified it.

A local change should not automatically rewrite the entire prediction.

Limited Material Change → Limited Revision where sufficient

Foundational Material Change → Broader Governance Reassessment

6. Revision Scope

Revision Scope identifies which predictive dimensions require change.

Possible scopes include:

Qualification-Only Revision

Component Revision

Multi-Component Revision

Material Prediction Revision

Prediction Reformation Required

7. Qualification-Only Revision

Some changes may be addressed by adding or modifying a qualification without changing the core prediction.

Example:

70% probability

remains 70%, but:

subject to materially increased uncertainty

must now accompany the prediction.

This remains a governed predictive change and should be versioned where material.

8. Component Revision

A Component Revision changes one primary dimension while preserving the remainder.

Examples:

probability; uncertainty; range; feasibility qualification; assumption state; Prediction Horizon; Predictive Boundary.

9. Multi-Component Revision

Multiple predictive dimensions may change together.

Example:

Probability + uncertainty + assumption qualification

may require simultaneous revision.

The relationship among those changes should remain traceable.

10. Material Prediction Revision

A Material Prediction Revision changes substantive predictive meaning.

Examples:

75% → 42%

Feasible → Infeasible

12 months → 24 months

Region A → Region A only under Condition X

Such revisions require explicit historical preservation.

11. Prediction Reformation

A proposed change may exceed legitimate revision.

Prediction Reformation may be required where there is fundamental change to:

Prediction Object; Predictive Purpose; methodology; fundamental Prediction Basis; core assumption structure; Predictive Boundary; outcome definition.

Revision Must Not Be Used to Hide a New Prediction

12. Same Prediction Test

PRVM should determine whether the revised object remains sufficiently continuous with the prior prediction to remain within the same prediction lineage.

Relevant questions include:

Is the Prediction Object still materially the same? Is the predicted outcome still materially the same? Is the Predictive Purpose still compatible? Is the methodology sufficiently continuous? Is the Prediction Horizon still meaningfully comparable? Is the Predictive Boundary still recognizably continuous?

If not:

New Prediction Formation May Be Required

13. Prediction Object Change

A material change in what is being predicted may exceed revision.

Example:

Will Company X receive regulatory approval?

cannot silently become:

Will Company X successfully commercialize Product Y?

These are different Prediction Objects.

Related Prediction Object ≠ Same Prediction Object

14. Predictive Purpose Change

A prediction originally created for one purpose may later be proposed for a fundamentally different purpose.

Purpose expansion belongs partly to PCRS/PUBM.

Where the predictive purpose itself is materially reconstructed, earlier PGA governance may need to be revisited.

15. Methodology Change

A material methodology change may create a new predictive state whose comparability with the prior prediction is limited.

PRVM should distinguish:

Revision Under Same Methodology

from:

Revision Under Modified Methodology

and:

Prediction Reformation Under New Methodology

PMES retains ownership of Prediction Methodology.

16. Prediction Basis Revision

New information may require the Prediction Basis supporting the revised prediction to change.

PBAS owns Prediction Basis governance.

PRVM records that the revised prediction depends upon a changed basis and preserves the relationship between:

Prior Prediction Basis → Revision Basis → Updated Prediction Basis

17. Prediction Basis Addition

New information may be added to the basis without invalidating earlier information.

The revision should identify:

added information; relevance; timing; resulting predictive effect.

18. Prediction Basis Removal

A source or information element may need to be removed because it becomes:

invalid; irrelevant; duplicated; corrupted; superseded.

Removal should remain historically visible.

Removed Basis Element ≠ Basis Element That Never Existed

19. Prediction Basis Correction

Where a material basis element was incorrect, correction should preserve:

original basis state; identified error; correction time; corrected state; resulting prediction effect.

This prevents retrospective reconstruction.

20. Assumption Revision

A material Predictive Assumption may be revised where current conditions no longer support the earlier assumption.

PAS governs the assumption.

PRVM governs the resulting prediction-state transition.

21. Assumption Removal

A prior assumption may become unnecessary.

Its removal should remain historically visible where it materially affected earlier predictive meaning.

Removed Assumption ≠ Assumption That Never Existed

22. New Assumption

Revision may introduce a new material assumption.

The revised prediction should not conceal that its predictive support now depends upon a condition absent from the prior version.

23. Assumption Failure and Revision

Assumption Failure may require:

probability revision; feasibility revision; qualification; scope reduction; withdrawal.

It does not automatically determine which response is appropriate.

24. Probability Revision

A probability may be revised where PPUS-governed reconsideration supports a new representation.

Example:

P1: 68%

→ material new information →

P2: 44%

PRVM governs the transition and historical relationship.

It does not independently calculate the 44%.

25. Probability Increase

An increased probability must remain attributable to a legitimate Revision Basis.

Higher Probability ≠ Improved Prediction by Default

The prediction has changed.

Its eventual quality remains unknown.

26. Probability Decrease

A lower probability is not necessarily evidence that the earlier prediction was defective.

New conditions may legitimately justify the decrease.

Probability Revision ≠ Admission of Earlier Prediction Error

27. Probability Stability with Other Revision

A prediction may retain the same probability while materially changing elsewhere.

Example:

70% → 70%

while uncertainty widens or assumptions change.

Therefore:

Same Probability ≠ No Prediction Revision

28. Uncertainty Revision

A revised prediction should preserve material changes in uncertainty.

Uncertainty may:

increase; decrease; change structure; become asymmetric; become less determinable.

Uncertainty revision must not be hidden behind a stable point estimate.

29. Confidence Revision

Confidence-related change should remain distinct from probability change.

Confidence Revision ≠ Probability Revision

Both may occur independently.

30. Range Revision

Where prediction ranges change, PRVM should preserve:

prior range; revised range; rationale; time; conditions.

Wider Range ≠ Weaker Prediction by Definition

Interpretation depends upon context.

31. Conditionality Revision

A revised prediction may introduce, remove, or alter conditions.

Example:

70% probability

becomes:

70% probability provided regulatory approval occurs before Date X.

That is a substantive predictive revision even if the numerical probability remains unchanged.

32. Feasibility Revision

A change in PFES-governed feasibility may require revision of the prediction's qualifications or scope.

Possible transitions include:

Feasible → Conditionally Feasible

Conditionally Feasible → Infeasible

Infeasible → Feasible

PRVM preserves the resulting prediction-state change.

33. Prediction Horizon Revision

A Prediction Horizon may require revision where new conditions materially alter expected timing.

Example:

Expected within 12 months → Expected within 24 months

This is substantive.

The original horizon must remain preserved.

Horizon Revision ≠ Original Horizon Never Existed

34. Predictive Boundary Revision

The Predictive Boundary may narrow or expand.

Examples:

Europe → Germany

Product Family → Product X

All Customers → Enterprise Customers

Boundary change must remain explicit.

35. Boundary Narrowing

Boundary narrowing may preserve legitimacy where the original scope becomes too broad.

It should not be represented as though the narrower boundary had always applied.

36. Boundary Expansion

Boundary expansion may exceed ordinary revision because it introduces predictive claims not previously governed.

Boundary Expansion ≠ Automatic Revision Eligibility

Earlier PGA governance may need to be repeated.

37. Collective Prediction Revision

A collective prediction may require revision because:

participants change; independence changes; aggregation changes; concentration changes; source diversity changes.

CPGS governs the collective predictive structure.

PRVM governs the resulting lifecycle revision state.

38. Collective Probability Revision

A market or collective probability may change naturally over time.

Not every market movement constitutes formal prediction revision.

PRVM applies where the governed Collective Prediction itself is being revised as a lifecycle object.

Market Movement ≠ Governed Prediction Revision by Default

39. Predictor–Reality Proximity Revision Context

A change in Predictor–Reality Proximity may affect the qualifications surrounding a revised prediction.

It does not itself establish the revised predictive result.

Changed Information Position ≠ Automatic Predictive Revision

40. Operational Reality Change

A change in Operational Reality may justify revision.

ORA™ may establish the relevant operational state.

PRVM consumes that governed condition.

It does not own Operational Reality.

41. Intervening Event Revision

An Intervening Event may justify revision where formal review establishes material predictive effect.

The revision should preserve:

event; timing; predictive dimensions affected; resulting changes.

42. Reflexive Revision

Where prediction use materially changes Operational Reality, the prediction may require revision.

Example:

Failure Prediction → Preventive Intervention → Changed Failure Conditions

The revision must not rewrite the fact that the earlier prediction preceded the intervention.

43. Revision Timing

Revision timing must remain explicit.

Relevant times may include:

original prediction formation; Change Signal occurrence; Change Signal detection; Review Trigger; review completion; revision decision; revised prediction publication.

These times must not be collapsed.

44. Effective Revision Time

The Effective Revision Time identifies when the revised prediction becomes the current governed predictive state, subject to supersession governance.

It may differ from the time the change originally occurred.

Change Time ≠ Revision Effective Time

45. Revision Latency

Revision Latency is the interval between material change recognition and effective revision.

Excessive latency may allow stale predictions to remain active.

Very low latency may also create premature revision.

Fast Revision ≠ Good Revision by Default

46. Revision Rationale

Every material revision should have an explicit rationale.

The rationale should answer:

what changed; why it matters; what was reviewed; what is being revised; what remains unchanged; why the revised state is legitimate.

47. Revision Evidence Reference

Where evidence supports revision, relevant evidence should remain referenceable.

ADIT® owns evidence governance.

PRVM preserves the relationship:

Governed Evidence Reference → Revision Basis

Evidence ≠ Revision Decision

48. Revision Validation

Where relevant, revision may require validation.

VALIDOS™ may govern applicable validation processes.

Validated Input ≠ Automatically Valid Revision

Revision legitimacy depends on the complete governed transition.

49. Revision Authority

The actor or system initiating, approving, or executing material revision should remain identifiable where relevant.

Possible actors include:

human analyst; forecasting team; institutional authority; AI system; autonomous agent.

PRVM does not create organizational authority.

50. Revision Initiator

The Revision Initiator identifies who or what initiated the revision process.

Initiation is distinct from approval or execution.

51. Revision Approval

Where an implementation requires approval, the approval state should remain explicit.

Revision Initiation ≠ Revision Approval

52. Human Revision

Human revision remains subject to:

Revision Basis; proportionality; traceability; historical preservation; version continuity.

Human Judgment ≠ Permission to Rewrite History

53. AI-Assisted Revision

AI may:

identify revision candidates; summarize new information; compare prediction versions; propose probability changes; identify assumption deterioration.

AI assistance does not remove governance requirements.

AI-Generated Revision Proposal ≠ Governed Revised Prediction

54. Autonomous Revision

An autonomous system may revise predictions where properly authorized.

It must still preserve:

trigger; Revision Basis; prior state; revised state; rationale; timestamp; version relationship.

Autonomous Revision ≠ Autonomous Historical Editing

55. Revision State

PRVM may classify revision as:

No Revision Required

Qualification Revision Required

Component Revision Required

Multi-Component Revision Required

Material Revision Required

Prediction Reformation Required

Revision Unable to Determine

These are revision governance states.

56. Revised Prediction State

The revised prediction should identify:

revised predictive claim; revised probability where applicable; revised uncertainty; revised assumptions; revised basis references; revised feasibility; revised horizon; revised boundary; qualifications; revision relationship.

Only dimensions that actually changed should be represented as changed.

57. Preserved Dimensions

PRVM should explicitly identify material dimensions that remain unchanged.

This prevents ambiguity about whether revision affected the entire prediction.

Example:

Changed: Probability, uncertainty.

Preserved: Prediction Object, methodology, horizon, boundary.

58. Revision Delta

The Revision Delta represents the difference between prior and revised predictive states.

Canonical relationship:

Prediction State P1 + Governed Revision Delta → Prediction State P2

The Revision Delta should remain reconstructable.

59. Revision Version

A material revision creates a distinct prediction version.

Example:

PGA-PRED-00421-v1

→

PGA-PRED-00421-v2

The exact implementation may differ.

The methodological requirement is stable version distinction.

60. Version Lineage

Each revised state should remain linked to its predecessor.

Example:

P1 → P2 → P3

The lineage should not become:

P3 only

61. Historical Prediction Preservation

The prior prediction must remain available as historical state.

It should preserve what was legitimately known and predicted at that time.

Current Prediction State ≠ Complete Prediction History

62. Immutable Historical Fields

Implementations may define fields that become historically immutable after a Lock Point.

Examples may include:

original probability; original horizon; original prediction text; original timestamp; original Prediction Object.

Later corrections should be added as new governed records.

63. Prediction Lock Point

A Lock Point may occur at:

publication; communication; first reliance; first operational use; predefined lifecycle milestone.

After the Lock Point:

Revision Creates New State

rather than:

Revision Overwrites Old State

64. Outcome Knowledge Boundary

Once outcome information becomes available, historical predictive claims require special protection.

The prediction must not be retrospectively changed to match known reality.

Outcome Knowledge ≠ Revision Basis for Historical Prediction

65. Post-Outcome Correction

A genuine technical error discovered after outcome may be corrected through annotation or correction record.

It must not be represented as though the corrected state was the prediction originally made.

Correction After Outcome ≠ Historical Prediction Revision

66. Revision Gaming

PRVM must prevent revision processes from being used to artificially improve predictive appearance.

Potential Revision Gaming includes:

late probability adjustment; horizon manipulation; outcome-definition change; boundary narrowing after adverse information; deleting failed assumptions; silent version replacement.

67. Hindsight Revision

Hindsight Revision occurs where later knowledge is incorporated into the historical prediction as if it had existed earlier.

Later Knowledge ≠ Earlier Predictive Knowledge

Hindsight Revision is incompatible with governed prediction history.

68. Revision Frequency

Predictions may be revised:

rarely; periodically; frequently; dynamically.

Frequency should reflect legitimate predictive need.

More Revision ≠ Better Prediction Governance

69. Over-Revision

Over-Revision may occur where predictions are repeatedly changed in response to low-materiality noise.

It may:

destroy stability; obscure predictive skill; reduce comparability; encourage hindsight adaptation.

70. Under-Revision

Under-Revision occurs where materially changed conditions do not produce necessary predictive revision.

It may create stale prediction persistence.

Prediction Stability ≠ Continuing Prediction Legitimacy

71. Revision Stability

A revised prediction should remain stable until new material conditions justify another review.

Revision is not an invitation to continuous uncontrolled adjustment.

72. Revision Propagation

Once a revised state becomes current, downstream systems may need to receive the revision.

Potential destinations include:

dashboards; APIs; AI agents; planning systems; operational systems; human decision-makers.

PCRS governs downstream representation, reliance, and use.

PRVM provides the governed revision state.

73. Stale Version Detection

Where downstream systems continue using an earlier prediction after revision, the stale version should remain detectable.

Available Historical Version ≠ Current Prediction Version

74. Revision Notification

A material revision may generate a notification or machine-readable state change.

Notification should identify:

prediction; prior version; new version; revision time; material changes; applicable qualification.

Notification itself does not govern downstream reliance.

75. Revision and Reliance Reassessment

Material revision may require RLCM reassessment.

Prediction Revision ≠ Automatic Reliance Continuation

A new probability, uncertainty state, horizon, or feasibility condition may change legitimate reliance.

76. Revision and Use Reassessment

PUBM may need to reconsider permitted use.

A prediction previously permitted for autonomous use may become restricted after material revision.

Likewise, a restriction may later become unnecessary.

77. Revision and Performance Assessment

POFPS should later assess predictions using the prediction states that actually existed at relevant times.

A later revised version must not replace an earlier version in historical performance assessment.

78. Revision and Decision History

Historical decisions should remain associated with the prediction version actually available at Decision Time.

Later Revised Prediction ≠ Earlier Decision Input

79. Revision Traceability

A material revision should permit reconstruction of:

Prior Prediction → Review Determination → Revision Basis → Revision Scope → Revision Delta → Revised Prediction → Version Relationship

This is the minimum conceptual chain required for governed revision.

80. Revision History

Revision History should preserve:

all material versions; revision timestamps; Revision Bases; Revision Deltas; preserved dimensions; revised dimensions; revision initiators; applicable approval; supersession relationship.

82. Governance Logic

PRVM applies the following operational logic:

Has formal review established that the current prediction may remain unchanged?

If yes:

→ No Revision Required / Prediction Preserved

If the prediction remains legitimate but requires additional qualification:

→ Qualification Revision Required

If substantive change is necessary:

→ identify Revision Basis.

Which predictive dimensions are materially affected?

→ establish Revision Scope.

Can the required change remain within the same Prediction Object, Predictive Purpose, methodology lineage, horizon structure, and Predictive Boundary?

If yes:

→ proceed with governed revision.

If no:

→ classify Prediction Reformation Required and return to the appropriate earlier PGA™ governance stage.

Is one predictive dimension affected?

→ Component Revision.

Are multiple dimensions affected?

→ Multi-Component Revision.

Does the change materially alter predictive meaning?

→ Material Prediction Revision.

For every substantive revision:

→ preserve prior state;

→ identify Revision Delta;

→ create new prediction version;

→ establish Effective Revision Time;

→ preserve Version Lineage;

→ produce Revised Prediction State.

At no point may the revised state overwrite the historical prediction.

P1 → Revision → P2

never:

P1 = retrospectively replaced by P2

87. Use Case 1 — Regulatory Approval Prediction Falls from 74% to 38%

A Governed Prediction states:

P1 — 74% probability that a new industrial technology will receive regulatory approval within twelve months.

The prediction is based partly upon:

successful preliminary review; expected completion of safety testing; stable regulatory requirements.

Four months later:

a new safety requirement is introduced; additional testing becomes mandatory; approval timing becomes materially less certain.

PCHM detects the changes.

RTM establishes a formal Review Trigger.

Review determines that the existing 74% probability and twelve-month timing can no longer remain unchanged.

PPUS-governed reconsideration establishes:

38% probability within the original twelve-month horizon

with materially increased uncertainty.

PRVM creates:

P1 — 74%

→ Regulatory Change

→ Formal Review

→ P2 — 38%

The original prediction remains preserved.

PRVM records:

Revised Dimensions: Probability · Uncertainty · Regulatory Assumption

Preserved Dimensions: Prediction Object · Predictive Purpose · Core Methodology · Original Horizon

The critical governance distinction is:

P2 does not replace the historical existence of P1.

Both remain part of the Prediction Version Chain.

88. Use Case 2 — A “Revision” Attempts to Change What Was Actually Predicted

A technology forecasting organization publishes:

P1 — 65% probability that fully autonomous delivery vehicles will achieve commercial deployment in Country A by 2028.

Two years later, deployment remains significantly delayed.

The organization proposes revising the prediction to:

P2 — 65% probability that semi-autonomous delivery systems will achieve limited pilot deployment somewhere in Europe by 2030.

The numerical probability remains 65%.

But the proposed revision changes:

fully autonomous → semi-autonomous ; commercial deployment → limited pilot deployment ; Country A → Europe ; 2028 → 2030 .

The critical governance question is:

Is this still a revision of the same prediction?

PRVM determines that the proposed change materially alters:

Prediction Object; outcome definition; geographic boundary; deployment threshold; Prediction Horizon.

The correct state is therefore:

Prediction Reformation Required

The organization cannot preserve the appearance of predictive continuity by calling the new statement a revision.

Revision Must Not Be Used to Hide a New Prediction

Related Prediction Object ≠ Same Prediction Object

P1 remains historically preserved.

A new prediction must enter the appropriate earlier PGA™ governance stages.

89. Canonical Boundary

PRVM governs substantive revision of an existing Governed Prediction after formal review establishes a legitimate Revision Basis. It governs Revision Eligibility, Prediction Preservation, Revision Necessity, proportionality, scope, qualification changes, probability and uncertainty revision, assumption revision, Prediction Basis revision relationships, feasibility-related revision, Prediction Horizon and Predictive Boundary revision, collective prediction revision state, Operational Reality-related revision, Revision Timing, Revision Delta, Revision Rationale, Effective Revision Time, human, AI-assisted and autonomous revision, Prediction Lock Points, version creation, Version Lineage, historical prediction preservation, stale-version identification, and downstream reassessment signaling. It does not create the original prediction, independently recalculate probability, establish Operational Reality, determine final Prediction Supersession, determine Prediction Withdrawal, determine the eventual outcome, or assess predictive performance.

PCHM owns:

Prediction Change Monitoring

RTM owns:

Prediction Review Trigger Determination

PRVM owns:

Prediction Revision

PSSM owns:

Prediction Supersession

PWM owns:

Prediction Withdrawal

90. Canonical Closing Statement

Prediction revision is legitimate only when new conditions justify changing what can responsibly be predicted now — never when later knowledge creates an opportunity to improve what appears to have been predicted before.

PRVM allows predictive knowledge to evolve while preserving the exact historical states from which that evolution occurred.

The prediction may change. The record of what was predicted must not.
