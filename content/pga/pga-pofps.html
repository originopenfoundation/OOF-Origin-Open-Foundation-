Canonical Definition

The Prediction Outcome & Performance Standard (POFPS) governs how a historically identifiable Governed Prediction is associated with a sufficiently governed outcome reference and how predictive performance is subsequently determined, interpreted, compared, calibrated, and preserved without rewriting the prediction, manufacturing certainty after the fact, or confusing favorable outcomes with demonstrated predictive capability.

POFPS establishes the governance conditions under which a prediction may legitimately enter outcome comparison, how the relevant prediction version is selected, how prediction-to-outcome correspondence is determined, how error and calibration are represented, how repeated predictive performance is evaluated over time, and how informational position, intervention effects, withdrawal, revision, supersession, and other lifecycle conditions are preserved in performance interpretation.

The Standard governs the transition:

Governed Historical Prediction + Sufficiently Governed Outcome Reference → Prediction-to-Outcome Association → Prediction-to-Outcome Comparison → Predictive Performance Determination

POFPS governs the relationship between prediction and outcome .

It does not establish Operational Reality itself.

Operational Role

A prediction becomes meaningfully assessable only when there is sufficient governed information about what occurred.

Yet outcome availability alone does not automatically create legitimate performance assessment.

Before a prediction is judged, POFPS must establish:

which prediction version is relevant; what exactly the predicted outcome was; which Prediction Horizon applied; which Predictive Boundary applied; whether the prediction was revised; whether it was superseded; whether it was withdrawn; whether intervention materially changed the predicted environment; whether Predictor–Reality Proximity materially affected interpretation; whether the outcome reference is sufficiently governed; whether the prediction and outcome are actually comparable.

Only then may predictive performance be determined.

Its core responsibility is:

to prevent predictive success, failure, accuracy, calibration, or skill from being claimed without a governed prediction-to-outcome relationship.

Core Question

Once a sufficiently governed outcome becomes available, how should the prediction be associated with that outcome and its predictive performance legitimately determined over time?

Governance Flow

Governed Historical Prediction → Outcome Reference Qualification → Prediction-to-Outcome Association → Comparability Determination → Outcome Comparison → Error & Calibration Assessment → Performance Interpretation → Longitudinal Predictive Performance

Where multiple prediction versions existed:

Prediction Version History → Relevant Historical Prediction State → Outcome Association → Performance Determination

Where prediction use materially affected Operational Reality:

Prediction → Reliance / Use → Intervention → Changed Operational Reality → Outcome Context → Performance Interpretation

Outcome Reference Principle

Predictive performance must not be determined against an undefined or weakly established outcome.

POFPS requires a sufficiently governed Outcome Reference.

The Outcome Reference must be adequate to answer:

What actually occurred relative to the Prediction Object, Prediction Horizon, and Predictive Boundary?

The governing distinction is:

Prediction Outcome Association ≠ Outcome Determination

POFPS associates the prediction with an outcome reference.

It does not own the creation of that reality state.

Operational Reality Boundary

ORA™ or another appropriate reality-governance source may establish the relevant Operational Reality.

POFPS consumes that governed state.

Therefore:

Governed Outcome Reference → POFPS

not:

POFPS invents the outcome from predictive context

This boundary protects prediction assessment from circular evaluation.

Outcome Availability

Outcome availability may be:

Sufficiently Available

Partially Available

Conditionally Available

Disputed

Not Yet Available

Unable to Determine

Performance assessment should not exceed what the Outcome Reference legitimately supports.

Outcome Information Available ≠ Outcome Sufficiently Governed

Outcome Maturity

Some outcomes become known immediately.

Others emerge gradually.

Examples:

election result; project completion; product-market adoption; system failure; long-term economic outcome; climate condition.

POFPS must distinguish:

Early Outcome Signal

from:

Sufficiently Mature Outcome

Prediction Outcome Association

Prediction Outcome Association establishes which governed outcome corresponds to the prediction.

This relationship should preserve:

Prediction Object; predicted outcome; Prediction Horizon; Predictive Boundary; conditions; relevant prediction version.

A nearby or related event is not automatically the outcome of the prediction.

Related Event ≠ Predicted Outcome

Outcome Identity

The outcome should remain identifiable independently from the prediction.

This prevents performance evaluation from redefining the outcome to fit the prediction.

Prediction Definition Must Not Retroactively Define Reality

Prediction Version Selection

Where a prediction evolved through:

P1 → P2 → P3

POFPS must determine which historical state is relevant for which performance question.

Possible questions include:

What was predicted at first publication? What was current at Decision Time? What was the final prediction before the outcome? How did the prediction evolve over time? How well calibrated were all prediction states?

There may therefore be more than one legitimate performance view.

Original Prediction Performance

The original prediction state may be assessed independently.

This preserves:

What was originally predicted before subsequent information became available?

Original predictive performance must not be replaced by final-version performance.

Final Prediction Performance

The last legitimate prediction state before outcome may also be evaluated.

This answers a different question:

What did the governed prediction say closest to outcome resolution?

Original Prediction Performance ≠ Final Prediction Performance

Both may be important.

Decision-Time Prediction Performance

Where a decision relied upon a particular historical prediction version, POFPS may preserve performance relative to:

Prediction State Available at Decision Time

This prevents later revisions from being attributed to earlier decisions.

Version-Specific Performance

Every material prediction version may carry its own performance relationship.

Example:

P1 — 62%

P2 — 74%

P3 — 48%

Outcome occurs.

Performance analysis may evaluate all three without allowing P3 to erase P1 or P2.

Prediction Horizon Alignment

Outcome comparison must respect the Prediction Horizon.

A prediction that an event will occur:

within 12 months

cannot be treated as correct merely because the event occurs after 24 months.

Event Eventually Occurred ≠ Prediction Was Correct Within Horizon

Timing Performance

Where timing is part of predictive meaning, timing performance should be assessed separately from event occurrence.

A prediction may correctly predict:

what

while incorrectly predicting:

when

These are distinct performance dimensions.

Predictive Boundary Alignment

Outcome comparison must remain within the original Predictive Boundary.

A prediction concerning:

Germany

cannot be credited because the event occurred in:

France

unless the governed prediction legitimately included that geography.

Related Outcome Outside Boundary ≠ Predicted Outcome

Population Alignment

A prediction concerning one population must not be assessed against a different population.

Different Population Outcome ≠ Valid Prediction Comparison

Object Alignment

The Prediction Object must match the evaluated outcome object.

A prediction about:

regulatory approval

must not be treated as successful merely because:

commercial launch

occurred through a different legal route.

Condition-Aware Outcome Comparison

Conditional predictions require conditional outcome interpretation.

Example:

70% probability of project completion by 2028 provided regulatory approval occurs by March 2027

must not be evaluated as an unconditional prediction if the required condition never occurred.

Conditional Prediction ≠ Unconditional Performance Object

Assumption-Aware Performance

Material assumptions should remain visible during performance interpretation.

However, failed assumptions do not automatically excuse poor prediction.

POFPS preserves the assumption context so performance can be interpreted legitimately.

Probability Performance

Probability represents predictive uncertainty before outcome.

Once the outcome becomes known, the probability must not be treated as though it had been certainty.

A 70% prediction can legitimately be followed by non-occurrence.

70% Probability + Non-Occurrence ≠ Automatic Prediction Failure

Similarly:

30% Probability + Occurrence ≠ Automatic Prediction Success

Performance requires repeated and calibrated interpretation.

Single Prediction Accuracy Boundary

One probabilistic prediction does not establish predictive skill.

A single outcome may be consistent with many probability values.

Therefore:

Single Correct Prediction ≠ Demonstrated Predictive Skill

Single Incorrect Prediction ≠ Demonstrated Predictive Incompetence

Observed Accuracy

Observed Accuracy represents whether predicted events or states corresponded with governed outcomes under the applicable comparison structure.

But:

Observed Accuracy ≠ Demonstrated Predictive Skill

Accuracy alone may ignore:

base rates; probability quality; uncertainty; informational advantage; repeated performance; calibration; intervention; selection effects.

Prediction Error

Prediction Error represents a governed difference between prediction and outcome where such comparison is meaningful.

Error may concern:

categorical result; numerical value; range; timing; magnitude; direction; probability calibration.

There is no single universal error representation for every prediction type.

Categorical Prediction Error

Where prediction is categorical, outcome comparison may establish:

Matched

Partially Matched

Did Not Match

Not Comparable

Unable to Determine

The exact structure depends upon the Prediction Object.

Numerical Prediction Error

Numerical predictions may support measures such as:

absolute difference; proportional difference; signed difference; range deviation.

POFPS governs legitimacy of the comparison.

It does not require one universal mathematical error metric.

Range Performance

A predicted range may be evaluated against whether the governed outcome:

fell within range

fell outside range

and how far outside it fell.

Range width should remain visible.

A very broad range should not receive the same interpretation as a narrow, precise range merely because both contained the outcome.

Probability Calibration

Calibration concerns whether events assigned similar probabilities occur approximately at corresponding frequencies over a sufficiently meaningful prediction set.

Conceptually:

Predictions assigned approximately 70%

should resolve positively at approximately the expected rate across a sufficiently governed sample.

Calibration therefore requires repeated observations.

Calibration ≠ Single-Prediction Correctness

Calibration Population

Calibration results depend upon which predictions are grouped together.

A valid Calibration Population should consider:

Prediction Object type; horizon; methodology; probability range; environment; relevant predictive conditions.

Arbitrary aggregation may produce misleading calibration.

Calibration Segmentation

Calibration may need to be segmented by:

methodology; predictor; domain; horizon; probability band; geography; consequence; Prediction Object class.

Aggregate Calibration ≠ Calibration in Every Segment

Confidence Performance

Prediction confidence should not be judged as though it were probability.

Confidence may relate to:

basis quality; methodology stability; model applicability; support strength.

POFPS may assess whether higher-confidence predictions systematically demonstrate stronger governed performance, but:

Confidence ≠ Probability

remains preserved.

Uncertainty Performance

Uncertainty representation may be assessed by examining whether realized outcomes behaved consistently with stated uncertainty structures.

A system that consistently understates uncertainty may appear precise while being poorly governed.

Overconfidence

Overconfidence may be indicated where predictive systems systematically express stronger probability, confidence, or narrower uncertainty than outcomes justify across a governed prediction population.

Precision ≠ Calibration

Underconfidence

A predictor may also systematically understate predictive strength.

Underconfidence is distinct from prudent uncertainty.

Performance interpretation should preserve that distinction.

Predictive Resolution

A predictor that meaningfully differentiates between higher- and lower-probability situations may have greater predictive resolution than one that repeatedly outputs similar probabilities.

POFPS may govern performance representations that distinguish:

Calibration

from:

Resolution

and:

Discrimination

where relevant.

Base Rate Context

Predictive performance must be interpreted relative to the underlying event frequency where relevant.

If an event occurs 95% of the time, always predicting occurrence may achieve high observed accuracy without strong predictive capability.

Therefore:

High Accuracy ≠ High Predictive Skill by Default

Benchmark Comparison

Predictive performance may be compared against:

base-rate baseline; historical baseline; alternative methodology; competing predictor; market signal; model ensemble; random baseline where meaningful.

Benchmark legitimacy must remain explicit.

Benchmark Stability

A benchmark may itself change over time.

Performance conclusions should not silently rely upon unstable or retrospectively selected benchmarks.

Predictive Skill

Predictive Skill represents demonstrated ability to produce systematically informative predictions beyond an appropriate baseline under sufficiently governed conditions.

It generally requires repeated evidence.

Predictive Success ≠ Predictive Capability

Predictive Capability

Predictive Capability is broader than one performance outcome.

It may require consideration of:

calibration; discrimination; stability; methodology; repeatability; context; performance across time; information conditions.

POFPS should not assign capability from isolated success.

Longitudinal Performance

Longitudinal Performance evaluates predictive behavior across time.

It may reveal:

improvement; degradation; stable performance; regime sensitivity; calibration drift; methodology deterioration.

This is distinct from assessing one prediction.

Performance Window

Performance may be assessed across defined periods.

Examples:

one quarter; one year; one Prediction Horizon class; one model generation.

Window selection should remain explicit.

Performance Drift

Performance Drift occurs where predictive performance changes over time.

Possible causes include:

environment change; methodology degradation; Prediction Basis change; model change; participant change; operational regime change.

POFPS identifies performance change.

It does not automatically determine the cause.

Predictor-Level Performance

Where attribution is legitimate, performance may be evaluated for a predictor.

Predictor identity must remain stable enough to support comparison.

A predictor changing methodology or information access may require segmented performance interpretation.

Methodology-Level Performance

POFPS may evaluate performance across predictions produced under the same methodology.

This can support assessment of whether the methodology produces stable predictive value.

PMES remains owner of methodology governance.

Model-Level Performance

Where a prediction is materially attributable to a particular model, model-level performance may be evaluated.

But:

Model Output ≠ Complete Prediction Methodology by Default

Performance attribution should not erase orchestration, data, human input, or downstream transformation.

Human Predictor Performance

Human forecasters may be assessed using the same principles of historical preservation, calibration, comparability, and informational context.

Human status does not create special exemption from governed performance assessment.

AI Predictor Performance

AI-generated predictions may be evaluated across:

prediction versions; model versions; data conditions;

deployment contexts; calibration; uncertainty representation.

Model Upgrade ≠ Historical Performance Reset

Collective Prediction Performance

Collective predictions may be assessed against outcomes.

But performance must preserve CPGS conditions such as:

source diversity; independence; aggregation method; concentration; participant composition.

A collective result cannot be interpreted solely from headline accuracy.

Prediction Market Performance

Market-implied probabilities may be compared with outcomes where legitimately represented.

However:

Trading Profitability ≠ Predictive Reliability

and:

Market Profit ≠ Calibration

Market structure and information conditions remain relevant.

Information Advantage Context

A predictor may perform well because it has privileged or superior information.

This does not automatically invalidate performance.

But it changes what the performance demonstrates.

Information Advantage ≠ Predictive Capability

A system should not attribute superior methodology where performance primarily reflects superior access to information.

Outcome-Informed Prediction Context

Where a predictor already possessed information materially approaching outcome knowledge, apparent predictive success should be interpreted accordingly.

Outcome Knowledge ≠ Predictive Capability

Predictor–Reality Proximity Performance Context

POFPS preserves Predictor–Reality Proximity existing when predictions were made.

Possible states include:

Public-Information Predictor

Information-Advantaged Predictor

Decision-Proximate Predictor

Outcome-Informed Predictor

Outcome-Influencing Predictor

Outcome-Controlling Predictor

This context helps prevent performance from being misclassified as predictive skill.

Decision-Proximate Predictor

A predictor may know decisions likely to determine the outcome because of proximity to relevant decision-makers.

This may produce high apparent accuracy.

But:

Decision Proximity ≠ Predictive Skill

Outcome-Influencing Predictor

A predictor may influence the environment that determines the outcome.

Performance interpretation must therefore preserve that influence.

Outcome-Controlling Predictor

Where a predictor materially controls the outcome, the relation may no longer represent ordinary prediction.

Outcome Control ≠ Predictive Capability

The prediction may still be historically relevant, but performance meaning changes substantially.

Prediction Influence Context

Predictions may influence behavior.

Examples:

demand prediction changes inventory; failure prediction triggers maintenance; market prediction changes trading; shortage prediction changes purchasing.

POFPS must preserve where prediction use materially influenced the outcome.

Self-Fulfilling Prediction Performance

A prediction may contribute to producing the outcome it predicted.

The resulting match should not automatically be attributed wholly to predictive capability.

Self-Negating Prediction Performance

A prediction may trigger action that prevents the predicted event.

Example:

90% failure probability → maintenance → no failure

A simplistic score may classify the prediction as wrong.

But:

Prevented Outcome ≠ Automatically Failed Prediction

The intervention context must remain visible.

Intervention-Aware Performance

Where an intervention occurs after prediction but before outcome, POFPS should preserve:

prediction state before intervention; intervention; resulting changed conditions; eventual outcome.

Performance interpretation must reflect that the original predicted environment may no longer have remained intact.

Counterfactual Boundary

POFPS must not fabricate counterfactual certainty.

If intervention prevented observation of what would otherwise have occurred, POFPS may classify the performance relation as intervention-affected rather than pretending to know the counterfactual outcome.

Unobserved Counterfactual ≠ Known Outcome

Revision-Aware Performance

A prediction revised from:

P1 → P2 → P3

creates multiple historically valid prediction states.

POFPS may assess:

original performance; revision trajectory; final performance; timing of revision; responsiveness to new information.

Revision should not allow poor earlier predictions to disappear.

Revision Timing Performance

Repeated last-minute revisions may produce strong final accuracy while weak earlier predictive usefulness.

POFPS should preserve when revisions occurred relative to the Prediction Horizon.

Final Accuracy ≠ Full Lifecycle Predictive Performance

Supersession-Aware Performance

Superseded prediction states remain part of performance history.

Superseded ≠ Excluded from Performance History by Default

PSSM provides the state lineage.

POFPS interprets relevant predictive performance.

Withdrawal-Aware Performance

A withdrawn prediction remains part of Prediction History.

Withdrawal may affect performance interpretation.

It must not automatically remove the prediction from evaluation.

Withdrawal ≠ Performance Erasure

Strategic Withdrawal

Withdrawal immediately before adverse resolution may create performance-selection risk.

POFPS should preserve:

withdrawal timing; Withdrawal Basis; prior active duration; outcome.

It does not assume manipulation.

It preserves evidence needed to evaluate the pattern.

Survivorship Bias

Performance datasets that include only predictions remaining active until outcome may systematically exclude:

withdrawn predictions; superseded predictions; failed prediction systems; abandoned methodologies.

This can create inflated performance.

Visible Prediction Population ≠ Complete Prediction Population

Selection Bias

Predictors may selectively publish or retain only favorable predictions.

POFPS requires sufficient historical continuity to identify such selection where records permit.

Prediction Publication Bias

Performance should distinguish:

all governed predictions; published predictions; privately used predictions; selectively disclosed predictions.

Where appropriate.

Performance Population

Any performance claim should identify the prediction population from which it was derived.

Example:

82% accuracy

is incomplete without understanding:

how many predictions; which predictions; over what period; what horizons; what outcome types; what exclusions.

Performance Inclusion Rule

Inclusion and exclusion rules should be explicit.

A performance system should not retroactively remove unfavorable predictions merely because they were:

withdrawn; revised; superseded; inconvenient;

low confidence.

Performance Exclusion

Some predictions may legitimately be excluded from a specific metric because they are not comparable.

The exclusion should remain governed and traceable.

Not Comparable ≠ Prediction Failure

Performance Comparability

Before aggregating performance, POFPS should determine whether prediction cases are sufficiently comparable.

Relevant dimensions may include:

Prediction Object; horizon; probability representation; methodology; environment; outcome type; decision context.

Multiple Predictions ≠ Comparable Prediction Set

Cross-Domain Performance

Performance across different predictive domains should not be combined without clear methodological justification.

Forecasting:

weather

and:

technology adoption

may require fundamentally different performance structures.

Cross-Horizon Performance

A predictor may perform differently at:

one day; one month; one year; ten years.

Aggregate performance may conceal horizon-specific weakness.

Performance Segmentation

POFPS may segment performance by:

predictor; methodology; domain; Prediction Horizon; probability band; Prediction Object; geography; environment; information condition; Predictor–Reality Proximity.

Performance Aggregation

Aggregated performance should preserve what was aggregated.

Aggregation must not create false comparability.

Single Aggregate Score ≠ Complete Predictive Performance

Performance Score

POFPS permits performance metrics where appropriate.

It does not mandate a universal single score.

Different prediction types may require different performance representations.

The scoring methodology should remain documented.

Performance Metric Governance

A metric should identify:

what it measures; prediction population; outcome definition; weighting; exclusions; temporal window; interpretation limits.

Metric Value ≠ Self-Explanatory Performance

Accuracy Metric Boundary

Simple accuracy may be useful for categorical prediction.

It may be misleading for probabilistic prediction.

Accuracy ≠ Calibration

Accuracy ≠ Predictive Skill

Calibration Metric Boundary

Calibration may show whether probability estimates correspond to frequencies.

It does not by itself establish strong discriminatory ability.

Well-Calibrated ≠ Highly Informative by Default

Performance Benchmarking

Performance may be compared against other governed predictors or baselines.

Benchmark selection should not be retrospective simply because one comparator makes performance appear stronger.

Performance Attribution

POFPS should distinguish where performance can be legitimately attributed to:

methodology; data; predictor; model; human judgment; market structure; information advantage; intervention; outcome control.

Good Outcome Match ≠ Clear Performance Attribution

Causal Attribution Boundary

POFPS does not automatically determine why a prediction succeeded or failed.

Prediction-to-Outcome Association ≠ Causal Explanation

Causal governance may require other methodological structures.

Predictive Error Attribution

Error may arise from:

methodology; Prediction Basis; assumptions; probability representation; environmental change; feasibility change; unmonitored condition; outcome ambiguity.

POFPS may preserve error context without falsely assigning cause.

Performance Qualification

Performance results may require qualification.

Examples:

Strong Calibration — Limited Sample

High Accuracy — High Base Rate

Strong Final-Version Accuracy — Frequent Late Revision

High Success Rate — Outcome-Informed Predictor

Qualification prevents headline performance from exceeding evidence.

Performance Sufficiency

Performance Sufficiency concerns whether there is enough governed information to draw the proposed performance conclusion.

Outcome Known ≠ Performance Conclusion Sufficient

Sample Sufficiency

Repeated predictive skill claims require sufficient prediction population.

A small number of predictions may support descriptive results without supporting broad capability claims.

Small Sample Success ≠ Demonstrated Predictive Capability

Historical Performance Continuity

Performance records should remain linked to the original prediction records.

Later methodology or model changes must not alter earlier performance history.

Current Predictor State ≠ Historical Predictor Performance State

Model Upgrade Boundary

A new model version may improve performance.

It does not reset or erase historical performance of prior versions.

Model Upgrade ≠ Historical Performance Reset

Methodology Upgrade Boundary

A new methodology may deserve separate performance segmentation.

It must not inherit historical success from another methodology without justified comparability.

Predictor Identity Continuity

Performance claims require stable attribution.

If the predictor changes materially, the continuity of the performance series should be qualified.

Performance Revision

Performance assessment itself may need revision if:

outcome data is corrected;

outcome governance improves; prediction record is corrected; metric error is discovered.

Any revised performance state must preserve its earlier assessment history where material.

Outcome Correction

Where the governed outcome reference later changes, POFPS may reassess performance.

The original outcome interpretation and subsequent correction should remain reconstructable.

Performance Record Integrity

Performance results must not be silently changed after publication.

The same historical-integrity principle governing predictions applies to material performance records.

Performance Manipulation

Potential manipulation may include:

excluding failed predictions; changing outcome definitions; changing Prediction Horizon retrospectively; evaluating only final revisions; ignoring withdrawals; selecting favorable time windows; selecting favorable benchmarks; counting correlated predictions as independent successes.

POFPS must preserve controls capable of exposing these practices.

Correlation in Performance Samples

Multiple predictions may derive from the same underlying methodology, data, or event.

Treating them as fully independent may exaggerate sample strength.

Prediction Count ≠ Independent Prediction Count

Repeated Outcome Exposure

A predictor may issue many predictions about essentially the same underlying outcome.

Performance analysis should identify such clustering.

Performance and Decision Value

A prediction may be statistically well calibrated yet operationally low-value.

Conversely, an imperfect prediction may have high operational value.

POFPS governs predictive performance.

It does not equate performance with economic or decision value.

Predictive Performance ≠ Operational Value

Performance and Trading Profit

A trader may profit despite poorly calibrated predictions because of:

pricing; position sizing; timing; asymmetry; market structure.

Therefore:

Trading Profitability ≠ Predictive Reliability

Performance and Decision Quality

A good prediction can support a poor decision.

A poor prediction can occasionally be followed by a good outcome.

Predictive Performance ≠ Decision Quality

Decision governance remains separate.

Performance and Responsibility

POFPS does not assign legal, organizational, or moral responsibility for poor prediction outcomes.

AGA™ or other appropriate governance structures may govern accountability.

Performance and Validation

VALIDOS™ may validate applicable performance methodologies or assessment processes.

POFPS owns prediction performance governance within PGA™.

Performance and Evidence

ADIT® may govern evidence supporting outcome or performance records.

POFPS consumes appropriate evidence relationships without owning evidence governance.

Performance and Integrity

INTEGROS® may govern broader integrity concerns.

POFPS governs integrity of the prediction-to-outcome and performance assessment relationship.

Performance and Prediction Record

PRCS preserves the complete prediction history.

POFPS depends upon that history for legitimate longitudinal performance.

Without preserved historical predictions, performance can be easily distorted.

Governance Logic

POFPS applies the following Parent Standard-level logic:

Is there a sufficiently governed Outcome Reference?

If no:

→ performance remains Not Yet Assessable , Insufficient Outcome Governance , or Unable to Determine .

If yes:

→ identify the relevant Governed Prediction.

Which prediction version is being evaluated?

→ Original, Decision-Time, Current-at-Horizon, Final, or another explicitly governed historical state.

Do Prediction Object, Prediction Horizon, Predictive Boundary, conditions, population, and outcome reference align sufficiently for comparison?

If no:

→ classify Not Comparable or Conditionally Comparable .

If yes:

→ establish Prediction-to-Outcome Association.

Was the predicted environment materially altered by intervention, prediction use, or outcome-influencing behavior?

If yes:

→ preserve Intervention-Affected or other appropriate qualification.

What type of prediction is being evaluated?

→ apply appropriate categorical, numerical, range, timing, or probabilistic comparison logic.

Is a single prediction being used to establish predictive capability?

If yes:

→ restrict conclusion to the individual prediction outcome relationship.

Is repeated performance being assessed?

If yes:

→ establish Performance Population, comparability, inclusion rules, calibration context, benchmarks, and segmentation.

Does informational advantage, Predictor–Reality Proximity, or Outcome Control materially affect interpretation?

If yes:

→ preserve that context within Performance Qualification.

At every stage:

Observed Accuracy ≠ Demonstrated Predictive Skill

and:

Performance Without Informational Context ≠ Complete Predictive Performance Interpretation

Canonical Boundary

POFPS governs the association of historically identifiable Governed Predictions with sufficiently governed outcome references and the subsequent determination, interpretation, comparison, calibration, segmentation, aggregation, and preservation of predictive performance. It governs relevant prediction-version selection, Prediction Object, Horizon and Boundary alignment, outcome comparability, prediction error, probability calibration, confidence and uncertainty performance interpretation, repeated and longitudinal performance, predictor and methodology performance, collective prediction performance, Predictor–Reality Proximity context, information advantage, intervention-aware performance, revision-, supersession-, and withdrawal-aware performance, Performance Population, inclusion and exclusion rules, performance qualification, anti-selection controls, and historical performance continuity. It does not establish Operational Reality, create the governed outcome itself, form or revise predictions, determine Prediction Use, establish Decision Authority, assign legal or organizational accountability, determine causal responsibility, or erase historical prediction states.

PMRS governs the prediction while its predictive conditions continue to change.

POFPS governs the relationship between the prediction and what subsequently occurred.

PRCS preserves the resulting complete Prediction Record and historical continuity.

Canonical Closing Statement

A prediction does not become good because an outcome matched it, and it does not become bad merely because one uncertain outcome did not.

POFPS governs the harder question: what was actually predicted, under what conditions, with what probability and uncertainty, which historical prediction state was in force, what outcome was sufficiently established, and what that relationship legitimately demonstrates about predictive performance.

A prediction may be right by chance, wrong despite sound methodology, successful because it influenced reality, or apparently accurate because the predictor already knew more than others. Performance becomes meaningful only when those differences remain governed.
