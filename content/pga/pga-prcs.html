Governed Space

PRCS governs how a Governed Prediction and its complete historical lifecycle remain identifiable, versioned, reconstructable, temporally ordered, provenance-preserving, state-aware, and historically continuous across formation, communication, reliance, revision, supersession, withdrawal, outcome association, performance assessment, retirement, archival persistence, and legitimate lifecycle re-entry.

PRCS governs the final transition of PGA™:

Prediction Lifecycle → Historical Prediction Record → Continuity → Reconstructability

Its foundational principle is:

The current prediction state must not erase the historical prediction record from which it evolved.

Canonical Definition

The Prediction Record & Continuity Standard (PRCS) is the Parent Standard within PGA™ that governs how a prediction remains historically identifiable and reconstructable across its complete lifecycle, including every material prediction state, methodological context, Prediction Basis, assumption state, probability and uncertainty representation, formation event, communication state, reliance context, revision, supersession, withdrawal, outcome relationship, performance record, archival state, and subsequent re-entry into active governance.

PRCS ensures that a prediction can change without losing its history, be superseded without being deleted, be withdrawn without disappearing, be evaluated without retrospective rewriting, and be reactivated or reused without silently inheriting an altered historical identity.

Operational Role

PRCS closes the Prediction Lifecycle.

It does not create the prediction.

It does not determine its methodology.

It does not calculate probability.

It does not decide whether the prediction should be revised.

It does not determine whether an outcome occurred.

It does not determine predictive performance.

It governs whether all of those historically significant states remain reconstructable as one coherent Prediction Record.

The central governance requirement is:

Prediction Change Must Preserve Prediction History.

Core Question

How must a prediction and its complete history remain identifiable, reconstructable, versioned, and historically continuous across formation, revision, supersession, outcome, performance assessment, retirement, and lifecycle re-entry?

PGA™ Governance Flow

Prediction Object & Purpose → Prediction Methodology → Prediction Basis → Prediction Assumptions → Prediction Formation → Probability & Uncertainty → Prediction Feasibility → Collective Prediction where applicable → Communication & Reliance → Monitoring & Revision → Outcome & Performance → Prediction Record & Continuity

PRCS is the final Parent Standard.

It does not replace the preceding lifecycle stages.

It preserves their historically significant outputs.

1. Prediction Record

A Prediction Record is the governed historical representation of a prediction across its lifecycle.

It should preserve the prediction as:

an evolving governed object

rather than:

a replaceable current value.

A prediction may change many times.

Its historical identity should remain continuous.

2. Current State and Historical Record

The current prediction state represents:

what the prediction is now.

The Historical Prediction Record represents:

what the prediction has been.

These are not equivalent.

Current Prediction State ≠ Complete Prediction History

3. Historical Continuity

Historical Continuity means that material lifecycle transitions remain connected rather than becoming isolated records.

A prediction may evolve:

P1 → P2 → P3 → Superseded → Withdrawn → Outcome → Performance Assessment

PRCS preserves the continuity of that sequence.

4. Historical Reconstruction

A Governed Prediction should be reconstructable from the historical record sufficiently to determine:

what existed; when it existed; what its state was; what changed; why the change occurred where governed; what state preceded it; what state followed it; which methodologies, bases, assumptions, probabilities, conditions, or boundaries applied; what was communicated; what was relied upon; what outcome became associated; what performance assessment followed.

5. Prediction Identity

A prediction should retain a stable identity across material lifecycle states.

The prediction may evolve.

Its continuity should not depend upon preserving identical content.

Identity Continuity ≠ Content Immutability

6. Prediction Record Identity

A Prediction Record should remain distinguishable from:

predictor identity; model identity; methodology identity; event identity; outcome identity; performance-record identity.

These entities may be linked.

They should not be collapsed.

7. Prediction State

A Prediction State represents a governed lifecycle condition of the prediction.

Possible examples include:

Formed

Active

Communicated

Relied Upon

Under Review

Revised

Superseded

Withdrawn

Outcome-Associated

Performance-Assessed

Retired

Archived

Re-Entered

PRCS governs continuity among such states.

8. State Transition

A state change should remain identifiable as a transition.

Conceptually:

Prior Prediction State → Governed Transition → New Prediction State

The new state must not silently replace the old one.

9. Versioning

Material changes to a prediction should produce identifiable historical versions.

Versioning may preserve changes to:

prediction content; probability; uncertainty; Prediction Horizon; Predictive Boundary; assumptions; conditions; Prediction Basis; methodology; communication status; lifecycle status.

10. Material Change

Not every technical modification requires a new substantive prediction version.

Materiality depends on whether the change alters predictive meaning, interpretation, reliance, comparison, or historical assessment.

PRCS governs the continuity requirement.

It does not mandate one universal versioning mechanism.

11. Immutable Historical State Principle

Once a prediction state becomes historical, its historical content should not be silently rewritten.

Corrections may be appended or linked.

The original historical state must remain reconstructable where material.

Correction ≠ Historical Replacement

12. Revision Continuity

Revision changes the prediction.

It does not erase the prediction that existed before revision.

Revision ≠ Historical Replacement

13. Supersession Continuity

Supersession changes which prediction state is operationally current.

It does not delete the superseded prediction.

Supersession ≠ Deletion

14. Withdrawal Continuity

Withdrawal ends active predictive status.

It does not erase historical predictive existence.

Withdrawal ≠ Historical Erasure

15. Retirement Continuity

A prediction may reach a terminal lifecycle state.

Retirement should not sever its historical identity.

16. Archival Continuity

Archival status changes accessibility or operational use.

It should not alter historical meaning.

Archive ≠ Deletion

17. Re-Entry

A retired, archived, superseded, or withdrawn prediction may later become relevant again.

PRCS governs how such re-entry remains connected to the prior historical record.

18. Re-Entry Identity

Lifecycle re-entry should answer:

Is this the same prediction returning? Is this a new prediction derived from a historical one? Is this a historical prediction being reused? Is this a new prediction using old Prediction Basis? Is this a revived operational state?

The distinction must remain explicit.

19. Re-Entry ≠ New Origin by Default

A prediction returning to active consideration should not automatically receive a new historical origin that hides its past.

20. New Prediction vs Re-Entered Prediction

A materially new predictive claim may require new Prediction Identity.

A resumed historical prediction may retain the original identity with a new lifecycle state.

The distinction should be governed.

21. Temporal Ordering

Historical prediction states should remain temporally ordered.

This enables reconstruction of what was known and governed at each relevant time.

22. Event Time

PRCS may preserve relevant lifecycle timestamps including:

formation time; publication time; communication time; reliance time; revision time; supersession time; withdrawal time; outcome-association time; performance-assessment time; archival time;

re-entry time.

23. Temporal Sequence Integrity

A later state must not be presented as if it existed earlier.

Later Prediction State ≠ Earlier Historical State

24. Historical Timestamp Integrity

Timestamps should preserve actual lifecycle timing sufficiently to prevent retrospective backdating or forward-shifting of predictive states.

25. Decision-Time Historical State

Where a decision relied on a prediction, PRCS should preserve which prediction state existed at the decision time.

Later revisions must not replace that historical state.

Decision-Time Prediction ≠ Final Prediction

26. Communication-Time Historical State

Where a prediction was communicated externally, the communicated version should remain historically identifiable.

27. Reliance-Time Historical State

Where a prediction was relied upon, the prediction state relied upon should remain identifiable.

28. Outcome-Time Historical State

Where performance is later evaluated, PRCS should preserve the prediction state or states applicable before outcome knowledge.

29. Original Prediction State

The first governed prediction state should remain identifiable where relevant.

Original state history may be essential to evaluating:

early predictive capability; revision trajectory; prediction drift; decision usefulness.

30. Final Prediction State

The last active prediction state before outcome resolution may also remain identifiable.

But:

Final Prediction State ≠ Complete Prediction Lifecycle

31. Version Lineage

PRCS governs the lineage connecting prediction versions.

Conceptually:

P1 → P2 → P3 → P4

Each state should identify its relationship to the previous state.

32. Branching Prediction History

Prediction history may branch.

Example:

One prediction may be revised into separate scenario-specific predictions.

PRCS should preserve the branching relationship rather than flatten the history.

33. Merged Prediction History

Multiple prediction paths may later be consolidated.

Historical provenance should remain intact.

34. Derived Prediction

A new prediction may be derived from an earlier prediction.

Derivation should preserve the relationship without falsely representing the new prediction as the same predictive object.

35. Prediction Lineage ≠ Prediction Identity

Two predictions may belong to the same lineage while retaining different Prediction Identities.

36. Methodology Continuity

Historical Prediction Records should preserve which Prediction Methodology applied to each material prediction state.

A methodology change may materially alter interpretation.

37. Methodology Upgrade

Where methodology changes:

Methodology v1 → Methodology v2

historical predictions governed under v1 must not be retroactively represented as though v2 had applied.

Methodology Upgrade ≠ Historical Methodology Rewrite

38. Model Continuity

Where AI or computational models produce predictions, model version should remain associated with relevant historical prediction states where material.

39. Model Upgrade

A model upgrade should not erase performance or prediction history from earlier versions.

Model Upgrade ≠ Historical Performance Reset

40. Predictor Identity Continuity

Predictor identity may change across:

human teams; model versions; organizations; agents; collective systems.

PRCS should preserve who or what produced each historical prediction state where relevant.

41. Prediction Basis Continuity

Material changes in Prediction Basis should remain historically visible.

A revised prediction based on new evidence should not make it appear that the new basis existed at original formation.

42. Assumption Continuity

Assumptions applicable to each historical prediction state should remain reconstructable.

Later assumption changes must not rewrite earlier assumption states.

43. Probability Continuity

Historical probability values should remain preserved.

Example:

P1: 35%

P2: 61%

P3: 84%

The final 84% must not replace 35% and 61% in the historical record.

44. Uncertainty Continuity

Historical uncertainty representations should remain version-specific.

Outcome knowledge must not remove or simplify the uncertainty that genuinely existed beforehand.

45. Confidence Continuity

Confidence state should remain historically attributable to the prediction version that carried it.

46. Horizon Continuity

Prediction Horizon changes should remain explicit.

A prediction originally made for:

12 months

must not later appear to have always used:

24 months

because the event occurred later.

47. Boundary Continuity

Changes to Predictive Boundary should remain historically traceable.

Examples:

geography; population; product; market; technology; scenario.

48. Condition Continuity

Conditional prediction states should preserve the conditions applicable at each lifecycle stage.

49. Feasibility Continuity

Where feasibility state materially informs historical prediction interpretation, the applicable PFES state should remain linkable.

50. Collective Prediction Continuity

Where a prediction is collective, historical records may need to preserve:

participant population; aggregation method; prediction state; independence conditions; concentration; collective governance status.

A later collective state should not rewrite earlier collective composition.

51. Prediction Market Continuity

Prediction-market history may preserve:

market state; relevant implied probability; selected time point; resolution rule; material rule changes; outcome resolution.

Final Market Price ≠ Complete Market Prediction History

52. Communication Continuity

Material changes in how a prediction was represented should remain historically traceable where they affect interpretation.

53. Reliance Continuity

Reliance history may be material to understanding intervention, operational action, or performance.

PRCS preserves relevant references without taking ownership of PCRS governance.

54. Intervention Continuity

Where prediction use triggered intervention, the historical record should preserve the connection:

Prediction → Reliance → Intervention → Changed Operational Conditions

where sufficiently governed.

55. Outcome Association Continuity

POFPS-generated Prediction Outcome Associations should become part of the continuing Prediction Record.

56. Performance Continuity

Predictive Performance Assessments should remain linked to the historical prediction states they evaluate.

57. Performance Reassessment

Performance may later be reassessed because of:

corrected Outcome Reference; revised comparison method; improved calibration population; additional resolved predictions.

The earlier performance assessment should remain historically reconstructable.

Performance Reassessment ≠ Historical Performance Erasure

58. Outcome Correction

If a governed Outcome Reference changes, PRCS should preserve both:

prior Outcome Reference association; corrected Outcome Reference association.

59. Outcome Correction ≠ Prediction Rewrite

A corrected outcome may change performance interpretation.

It does not alter what the prediction originally said.

60. Record Completeness

A complete Prediction Record should preserve enough information to reconstruct all material lifecycle states relevant to governance.

Completeness does not require storing every transient technical event.

61. Material Historical Event

A Material Historical Event is one whose absence would materially impair reconstruction, interpretation, validation, accountability, performance analysis, or continuity.

62. Historical Granularity

Record granularity should be proportional to governance need.

Too little history destroys reconstruction.

Too much uncontrolled history can obscure materially relevant states.

63. Record Sufficiency

Possible states may include:

Historically Sufficient

Historically Sufficient with Qualification

Partially Reconstructable

Historical Gaps Present

Insufficient Historical Record

Unable to Determine

64. Historical Gap

A Historical Gap exists where one or more material lifecycle states cannot be reconstructed.

Examples:

missing original prediction; missing revision state; missing timestamp; missing Prediction Basis reference; missing probability history; missing withdrawal state.

65. Historical Gap ≠ Automatic Invalidity

A gap may weaken governance without automatically invalidating every remaining historical fact.

The effect should be proportional to the missing information.

66. Record Integrity

Prediction Record Integrity requires protection against:

silent deletion; untracked modification; retrospective version substitution; backdating; broken lineage; detached performance history; detached outcome history.

67. Historical Mutation

Historical Mutation occurs when a prior prediction state is changed after it has become historical without preserving the original.

This should be restricted.

68. Append-Not-Overwrite Principle

Where historical correction is necessary, governance should prefer:

Original Record + Corrective Record

over:

Original Record silently overwritten

where appropriate.

69. Retrospective Rewording

Prediction wording should not be improved after the outcome becomes known while still being presented as the original prediction.

Retrospective Rewording ≠ Historical Clarification

70. Historical Clarification

Clarification may be legitimate where it explains the existing record without altering its original meaning.

The distinction should remain traceable.

71. Record Provenance

Material Prediction Record elements should remain attributable to their source where appropriate.

Possible provenance dimensions include:

creator; system; methodology; timestamp; originating state; transformation.

72. Prediction Provenance

Prediction provenance should enable reconstruction of how a prediction state came to exist.

PRCS preserves lineage and record continuity.

It does not replace broader provenance governance where another architecture owns it.

73. Historical Attribution

Historical states should remain attributable to the predictor, model, methodology, or collective structure applicable at the time.

74. Ownership Boundary

Prediction Record governance does not automatically establish legal ownership of prediction intellectual property.

Ownership belongs to the appropriate governance or legal framework.

75. Record Access

Historical continuity does not require unrestricted public access.

Access may be governed separately.

The record must remain governably preservable even where access is restricted.

76. Confidentiality Boundary

Confidential or restricted predictions may still require complete internal historical continuity.

Confidentiality ≠ Permission to Destroy History

77. Data Minimization Boundary

PRCS governs historical sufficiency.

It does not require retention of irrelevant personal or sensitive information merely because it existed near the Prediction Record.

78. Retention

Prediction Record retention should remain sufficient for:

reconstruction; performance assessment;

auditability; accountability; methodology evaluation; legal or policy obligations where applicable.

PRCS does not prescribe jurisdiction-specific retention periods.

79. Retention Expiry

Where lawful retention expiry applies, governance should distinguish:

permitted record disposal

from:

retrospective historical rewriting.

80. Deletion Boundary

Deletion of a Prediction Record should not occur merely because:

the prediction failed; the prediction was withdrawn; a predictor changed methodology; a model was retired; a newer version exists.

81. Withdrawal ≠ Permission to Delete

Withdrawal changes lifecycle status.

It does not erase history.

82. Failure ≠ Permission to Delete

An incorrect prediction remains part of performance history.

83. Success ≠ Permission to Simplify History

A successful prediction should not have weak early states, revisions, or informational conditions removed simply because the final outcome matched.

84. Performance Selection Risk

Historical continuity protects against selective preservation of:

only correct predictions; only final versions; only favorable performance windows;

only resolved predictions.

85. Survivorship Protection

Prediction history should not be constructed solely from predictions that remained visible, active, or successful.

86. Prediction Record and Auditability

PRCS supports auditability by preserving enough historical structure to reconstruct:

what existed → what changed → what followed

ADIT® may govern broader evidence and audit structures.

PRCS owns Prediction Record continuity.

87. Prediction Record and Validation

VALIDOS™ may validate relevant methods or processes.

PRCS preserves the Prediction Record needed to determine which validated or non-validated state applied historically.

88. Prediction Record and Integrity

INTEGROS® may govern broader integrity structures.

PRCS owns integrity of the historical Prediction Record within PGA™.

89. Prediction Record and Operational Reality

ORA™ owns Operational Reality.

90. Prediction Record and Performance

POFPS owns Outcome & Predictive Performance.

PRCS preserves POFPS outputs as part of the continuing Prediction Record.

91. Historical Prediction State Reuse

A historical prediction may be reused for:

benchmarking; training;

methodology analysis; historical comparison; simulation; retrospective study.

Reuse should preserve its original historical identity and context.

92. Historical Record Recontextualization

A historical prediction may be examined under a new question.

The new analysis should remain distinguishable from the historical prediction's original purpose.

New Analysis ≠ Original Prediction Purpose

93. Training Data Boundary

A historical Prediction Record may later be used as training data.

Its use in training does not change its historical predictive meaning.

94. AI Memory Boundary

AI systems may retain or recall Prediction Records.

Memory representation should not collapse:

current prediction; historical prediction; corrected prediction; withdrawn prediction; performance record.

MGIA™ or another appropriate memory-governance architecture may own broader memory governance.

95. Machine-Readable Prediction Record

PRCS should support machine-readable representation of historical prediction states.

A machine-readable record should be capable of preserving:

Prediction Identity; version; lifecycle state; timestamps; lineage; relevant architecture references; outcome linkage; performance linkage; status history.

96. Machine Reconstruction

A machine should be able to reconstruct, where sufficient record exists:

What was the current prediction at time T?

What probability applied at time T?

What had been revised by time T?

What state had been superseded?

What was communicated?

What outcome later became associated?

What performance assessment followed?

97. Executable Historical Governance

Machine-readable continuity may support automated enforcement such as:

preventing deletion of historical states; preventing historical version substitution; preserving version lineage; identifying broken continuity; restricting performance claims to historically supported records.

98. Historical Record Completeness for AI

AI systems interpreting predictions should not be given only the latest state where historical context materially affects meaning.

Latest Prediction State ≠ Sufficient Historical Context by Default

99. Cross-System Continuity

Prediction Records may move across:

organizations; platforms; models; agents; databases; registries.

Historical identity and lineage should remain preservable across system boundaries.

100. Interoperability

Interoperable Prediction Record structures should preserve semantic meaning across systems.

A version number without common meaning is insufficient.

101. Migration

Prediction Record migration should preserve:

identity; versions; timestamps; lineage; lifecycle states; historical references.

102. Migration ≠ New Origin

Moving a Prediction Record into a new system does not create a new historical origin.

103. Format Change

Changing storage format should not change historical meaning.

Format Transformation ≠ Prediction Transformation

104. Record Duplication

Copies of a Prediction Record may exist.

Copies should remain distinguishable from independent prediction origins.

105. Canonical Record Reference

Where multiple copies exist, systems may maintain a Canonical Record Reference or equivalent mechanism.

PRCS does not mandate one implementation technology.

106. Historical Conflict

Different systems may contain conflicting historical versions.

PRCS requires that the conflict remain identifiable rather than silently resolved without trace.

107. Record Reconciliation

Reconciliation may establish a governed relationship among conflicting historical records.

The original conflicting records should remain reconstructable where material.

108. Historical Authenticity Boundary

PRCS governs continuity and reconstruction.

It does not by itself cryptographically prove authenticity.

ADIT®, OBIDENITY®, OPGA™, or another appropriate architecture may govern stronger provenance or authenticity requirements.

113. Governance Logic

PRCS applies the following architecture-level logic:

Does a historically identifiable Governed Prediction exist?

If no:

→ Prediction Record continuity cannot be established.

If yes:

→ establish Prediction Identity.

Are material lifecycle states identifiable?

If no:

→ identify Historical Gaps.

If yes:

→ preserve temporal sequence.

Are prediction versions historically distinguishable?

If no:

→ restrict Reconstruction State.

If yes:

→ establish Version Lineage.

Did revision, supersession, withdrawal, retirement, or re-entry occur?

If yes:

→ preserve each transition without historical replacement.

Did methodology, model, Prediction Basis, assumptions, probability, uncertainty, horizon, boundary, or conditions materially change?

If yes:

→ preserve version-specific context.

Was the prediction communicated or relied upon?

If yes:

→ preserve the applicable historical prediction state.

Did an outcome later become sufficiently governed?

If yes:

→ preserve Outcome Association reference.

Was Predictive Performance assessed?

If yes:

→ preserve performance record and applicable prediction-version relationship.

Was performance later reassessed?

If yes:

→ preserve both prior and revised performance states.

Was the Prediction Record migrated, copied, archived, retired, or re-entered?

If yes:

→ preserve origin, lineage, and continuity.

Then:

→ determine Record Sufficiency;

→ determine Reconstruction State;

→ determine Continuity State;

→ determine Record Integrity State;

→ preserve complete historical Prediction Record.

Canonical transition:

Prediction Lifecycle History → Structured Historical Record → Version Lineage → Continuity → Reconstructable Prediction Record

116. Architecture Boundaries

Prediction Record & Historical Continuity

It does not govern:

Prediction Object definition; Prediction Methodology formation; Prediction Basis selection; Prediction Assumption formation; Prediction Formation; Probability & Uncertainty creation; Prediction Feasibility; Collective Prediction composition; Communication & Reliance; Monitoring & Revision decisions; Outcome determination; Predictive Performance determination; legal ownership; general identity governance; general evidence governance; general memory governance; causal responsibility; liability.

Its role is to preserve the historical continuity of the governed prediction across those lifecycle domains.

121. Canonical Boundary

PRCS governs the identity, versioning, temporal ordering, state continuity, lineage, reconstruction, historical integrity, record sufficiency, lifecycle persistence, migration continuity, archival continuity, performance-record continuity, and legitimate re-entry of Governed Predictions across their complete historical lifecycle. It preserves what the prediction was, how it changed, which state existed at each material time, which methodology and predictive conditions applied, what was communicated and relied upon, what outcome later became associated, how performance was assessed, and how those states remain historically connected. It does not form predictions, revise predictions, establish Operational Reality, calculate predictive performance, determine liability, establish legal ownership, replace general evidence governance, replace general memory governance, or erase history when prediction status changes.

122. Canonical Closing Statement

Prediction governance is incomplete if the architecture can explain what a prediction says today but cannot reconstruct what that prediction said yesterday.

PRCS ensures that revision does not become rewriting, supersession does not become deletion, withdrawal does not become disappearance, model upgrades do not reset history, and outcome knowledge does not retroactively improve what was originally predicted.

A prediction may evolve, expire, fail, succeed, disappear from operational use, or return years later. Its history must remain governable.

The future may change the status of a prediction. It must not change its past.
