Canonical Definition

Feasibility State Determination Module (FSDM) defines the governance conditions under which the outputs of feasibility constraint identification, predictive constraint mapping, operational pathway assessment, and execution and outcome feasibility assessment are integrated into a governed determination of the feasibility state of a specific predicted outcome.

FSDM establishes whether the predicted outcome may legitimately be represented as:

Feasible

Conditionally Feasible

Feasibility Restricted

Feasibility Uncertain

Infeasible

or:

Unable to Determine

within the applicable Prediction Horizon, Predictive Boundary, Prediction Basis, Predictive Assumptions, constraint structure, operational pathway, execution conditions, and outcome requirements.

It transforms:

Governed Feasibility Constraint Set + Governed Predictive Constraint Map + Governed Operational Pathway Assessment + Governed Execution & Outcome Feasibility Assessment

into:

Governed Prediction Feasibility Determination

Its foundational principle is:

Feasibility is a governed conclusion derived from the complete feasibility structure — not a label inferred from one favorable condition.

Operational Role

FSDM is the fifth and final internal governance space of the Prediction Feasibility Standard (PFES) .

FCIM establishes:

what constraints exist.

PCMM establishes:

how those constraints are connected.

OPAM establishes:

whether a viable operational pathway exists.

EOFM establishes:

whether that pathway can actually deliver the required outcome.

FSDM establishes:

what feasibility state the prediction may legitimately carry.

The focused operational question is:

Given the complete governed feasibility structure, what feasibility state can legitimately be assigned to the predicted outcome?

The primary output is:

Governed Prediction Feasibility Determination

Internal Module Flow

Feasibility Constraint Identification → Predictive Constraint Mapping → Operational Pathway Assessment → Execution & Outcome Feasibility → Feasibility State Determination

Feasibility Determination

A Feasibility Determination is a governed conclusion concerning whether the predicted outcome is realistically achievable under the applicable conditions.

It must remain associated with:

a specific prediction; a specific predicted outcome; a specific Prediction Horizon; a specific Predictive Boundary; the feasibility information available at the time of determination.

Feasibility Determination ≠ Prediction Probability

FSDM does not determine how likely the outcome is.

It determines whether the outcome can realistically be achieved.

Feasibility Determination ≠ Prediction Validation

A prediction may be methodologically valid while the predicted outcome remains infeasible.

Feasibility State

A Feasibility State is the governed classification assigned to the predicted outcome after relevant feasibility conditions have been assessed.

Feasible

A predicted outcome may be classified as Feasible where:

no known material hard constraint prevents achievement;

a viable operational pathway exists; required execution capability is sufficiently available; required conditions can coexist; relevant dependencies can be satisfied within the applicable horizon; no unresolved feasibility condition materially prevents the outcome.

Feasible does not mean certain.

Feasible ≠ Certain

Conditionally Feasible

A predicted outcome is Conditionally Feasible where achievement is realistic only if one or more explicit conditions are satisfied.

Example:

Conditionally Feasible if regulatory approval is obtained by 30 June and required production capacity becomes operational by 1 September.

Conditional Feasibility Requires Explicit Conditions

A conditional state must not hide the conditions upon which feasibility depends.

Conditionally Feasible ≠ Feasible Without Qualification

Feasibility Restricted

A prediction may be Feasibility Restricted where the outcome remains achievable only with material restrictions affecting:

scale; scope; geography; timing; capacity; pathway; resources; operational conditions.

Example:

The predicted deployment is feasible for:

40 locations

but not for the predicted:

100 locations

within the same horizon.

Restricted Feasibility ≠ Full Feasibility

Feasibility Uncertain

A prediction may be classified as Feasibility Uncertain where material feasibility conditions are known but their future state or effect remains sufficiently uncertain to prevent an unqualified feasibility conclusion.

Feasibility Uncertainty ≠ Prediction Uncertainty

PPUS governs Predictive Uncertainty.

FSDM governs uncertainty concerning operational achievability.

Infeasible

A predicted outcome is Infeasible where one or more governed constraints, pathway limitations, execution limitations, or required conditions establish that the outcome cannot realistically be achieved under the applicable horizon and conditions.

Infeasible ≠ Impossible

The outcome may become feasible under:

a different horizon; different resources; different technology; different regulatory conditions; a different operational pathway; a changed Predictive Boundary.

Unable to Determine

A feasibility state of Unable to Determine applies where the governed information is insufficient, materially conflicting, or incomplete to support a legitimate determination.

Unable to Determine ≠ Feasible

Unable to Determine ≠ Infeasible

Insufficient information does not justify either conclusion.

Feasibility Qualification

Every feasibility state should carry any material qualification needed to interpret it correctly.

Qualified Feasible State

Example:

Feasible within the current infrastructure capacity and for deployment limited to Region A.

Unqualified Feasibility

An unqualified Feasible state should not be assigned where material unresolved conditions remain.

Feasibility Basis

The Feasibility Basis is the governed set of outputs and conditions supporting the determination.

It may include:

constraint set; constraint map; operational pathway; execution assessment; outcome thresholds; applicable assumptions; relevant operational conditions.

Feasibility Basis ≠ Prediction Basis

PBAS governs the Prediction Basis used to form the prediction.

FSDM governs the basis supporting the feasibility conclusion.

Feasibility Condition Set

A Feasibility Condition Set identifies the conditions that must hold for a particular feasibility state to remain legitimate.

Necessary Condition

A Necessary Condition must be satisfied for the relevant feasibility state to remain valid.

Supporting Condition

A Supporting Condition strengthens feasibility but is not independently required.

Necessary Condition ≠ Supporting Condition

Sufficient Condition

Where methodology supports it, a condition or combination of conditions may be sufficient to establish a particular feasibility conclusion.

Condition Interaction

Several individually satisfactory conditions may fail when combined.

Individually Satisfied Conditions ≠ Overall Feasibility by Default

Hard Constraint Determination

An active non-substitutable Hard Constraint may be sufficient to establish infeasibility under the applicable conditions.

Hard Constraint Exists ≠ Permanent Infeasibility

The state may change if the constraint changes.

Soft Constraint Composition

Multiple soft constraints may collectively prevent achievement.

No Single Hard Constraint ≠ Feasible

Constraint Resolution State

FSDM should distinguish whether material constraints are:

unresolved; mitigated; conditionally resolved; resolved; superseded; unable to determine.

Mitigated ≠ Resolved

Constraint Substitution State

Where an alternative removes one constraint but introduces another, the total feasibility structure must be assessed.

One Constraint Removed ≠ Feasibility Restored

Pathway State Integration

FSDM incorporates the state established by OPAM.

Relevant pathway states may include:

Viable

Conditionally Viable

Restricted

Fragile

Blocked

Incomplete

Unable to Determine

Viable Pathway ≠ Feasible Outcome by Default

Blocked Pathway

A blocked mandatory pathway may establish infeasibility unless a sufficiently viable alternative exists.

Alternative Pathway Requirement

Where the primary pathway is blocked, an alternative must be operationally available rather than merely imaginable.

Theoretical Alternative ≠ Feasibility Restoration

Execution State Integration

FSDM incorporates the state established by EOFM.

Relevant execution states may include:

Executable

Conditionally Executable

Restricted

Fragile

Infeasible

Unable to Determine

Executable Pathway ≠ Required Outcome Achievable by Default

Outcome State Integration

FSDM must account for whether execution can achieve the actual predicted outcome threshold.

Partial Achievement ≠ Full Feasibility

Scale Feasibility

A prediction may be feasible at one scale and infeasible at another.

Feasible at Scale A ≠ Feasible at Scale B

Scope Feasibility

Feasibility may depend upon the predicted scope.

Local Feasibility ≠ Full-Scope Feasibility

Temporal Feasibility

The predicted outcome must be achievable within the applicable Prediction Horizon.

Eventually Feasible ≠ Feasible Within Prediction Horizon

Horizon-Dependent State

A feasibility state is always horizon-relative where timing materially affects achievability.

Feasible at Horizon A ≠ Feasible at Horizon B

Boundary-Dependent State

Feasibility may differ across:

jurisdictions; organizations; systems; markets; populations; geographic regions.

Feasible in Boundary A ≠ Feasible in Boundary B

Resource Feasibility Integration

Resources must be available and accessible when required.

Available Somewhere ≠ Available to the Pathway

Capacity Feasibility Integration

Capacity must be sufficient for required execution.

Planned Capacity ≠ Feasibility-Creating Capacity Until Available

Authority Feasibility Integration

Required authority and permissions must remain accounted for.

Capability Without Authority ≠ Feasible Execution

Regulatory Feasibility Integration

A mandatory regulatory dependency may condition or prevent feasibility.

FSDM does not determine legal validity itself.

Technical Feasibility Integration

Technical capability is only one dimension of overall feasibility.

Technically Feasible ≠ Overall Feasible

Operational Feasibility Integration

The outcome must remain executable under actual operational requirements.

Technical Possibility ≠ Operational Achievability

Economic Feasibility Integration

Where financial or economic limits materially affect achievability, they become part of the feasibility determination.

Economic Feasibility ≠ Commercial Preference

Human Feasibility Integration

Where required human capability or behavior is materially necessary, the feasibility determination should preserve those dependencies.

Infrastructure Feasibility Integration

Required infrastructure must exist or become operational within the applicable pathway and horizon.

Infrastructure Planned ≠ Infrastructure Available

Interoperability Feasibility Integration

Where systems must work together, individual component capability is insufficient.

Components Feasible Individually ≠ Integrated Outcome Feasible

Feasibility Threshold

A Feasibility Threshold is a defined minimum condition required before a particular feasibility classification can be supported.

Threshold Satisfaction

Where a threshold is defined, the determination should preserve whether it is:

Satisfied

Conditionally Satisfied

Not Satisfied

Unable to Determine

Feasibility Margin

Feasibility Margin represents the operational distance between required conditions and limiting constraints or thresholds.

Positive Margin

Relevant capability exceeds the requirement.

Near-Zero Margin

The outcome may remain feasible but fragile.

Negative Margin

The requirement exceeds the governed capability.

Positive Margin in One Dimension ≠ Overall Feasibility Margin

Robust Feasibility

A predicted outcome is Robustly Feasible where reasonable variation in material operational conditions does not immediately cause the outcome to become infeasible.

Fragile Feasibility

Fragile Feasibility exists where the outcome remains achievable only with little tolerance for disruption or variation.

Feasible ≠ Robustly Feasible

Fragile ≠ Infeasible

Feasibility Sensitivity

Feasibility Sensitivity describes how strongly the feasibility state depends upon changes in material conditions.

High Sensitivity

Small changes may materially alter the feasibility state.

Low Sensitivity

Reasonable variation does not materially change the determination.

Feasibility Sensitivity ≠ Prediction Sensitivity

Dominant Feasibility Constraint

A Dominant Feasibility Constraint is the constraint or condition most materially limiting the feasibility state.

Dominant Constraint ≠ Only Constraint

Constraint Concentration

Where many feasibility dimensions depend upon one condition, the determination should preserve that concentration.

High Constraint Concentration ≠ Automatic Infeasibility

It may instead indicate fragility or dependency.

Feasibility Conflict

A Feasibility Conflict exists where sufficiently governed sources or assessments support materially different feasibility conclusions.

Conflict ≠ Average the Conclusions

Conflicting conclusions should remain attributable until reconciled or qualified.

Feasibility Evidence Limitation

Where feasibility depends upon insufficiently governed information, the limitation should remain visible.

Unknown Condition ≠ Satisfied Condition

Incomplete Feasibility Structure

Where a material component of feasibility governance is incomplete, FSDM should not manufacture a definitive conclusion.

Missing Input ≠ Positive Feasibility

Feasibility Determination Confidence

Where useful, the methodology may preserve confidence in the feasibility determination itself.

This must remain distinct from predictive confidence.

Feasibility Determination Confidence ≠ Prediction Confidence

Feasibility State Hierarchy

Feasibility states should not be treated as a simple linear ranking where context is lost.

For example:

Conditionally Feasible

may be stronger than:

Feasibility Uncertain

but only if its conditions are explicit and realistically satisfiable.

State Qualification Matters

The classification without its conditions may misrepresent the actual governed result.

Machine-Readable Feasibility State

The final state should be representable in machine-readable form where appropriate.

Example structure:

Prediction ID → Outcome → Horizon → Boundary → Feasibility State → Conditions → Dominant Constraints → Margin → Sensitivity → Historical Version

Feasibility State ≠ Free-Text Opinion

Historical Feasibility State

Every material feasibility determination should remain historically reconstructable.

Historical State Preservation

The record should preserve:

state; date; applicable horizon; applicable boundary; constraint conditions; pathway state; execution state; qualification; version.

Current Feasibility State ≠ Complete Feasibility History

Later Feasibility ≠ Earlier Feasibility

A predicted outcome that becomes feasible later must not be retroactively represented as feasible when the earlier prediction was made.

Later Capability ≠ Historical Feasibility

Feasibility State Change

A state may change because:

a constraint emerges; a constraint is removed; capacity changes; technology changes; authority is granted;

permission is withdrawn; infrastructure becomes available; an alternative pathway emerges; execution capability changes.

State Change Trigger

A material change may require reassessment.

Feasibility State Change ≠ Prediction Revision by Default

PMRS governs whether the prediction itself should be reviewed, revised, superseded, or withdrawn.

Feasibility Reassessment

A new feasibility determination should preserve the prior state rather than overwrite it.

Reassessment ≠ Historical Replacement

Superseded Feasibility State

A prior state may be superseded while remaining historically valid for its original context.

Current State ≠ Only Legitimate Historical State

Relationship to PFES

FSDM converts the preceding PFES module outputs into the final governed feasibility determination.

It is the decision layer of PFES.

Relationship to PPUS

PPUS governs Probability & Uncertainty.

FSDM does not modify probability because an outcome is feasible or infeasible.

Feasibility ≠ Probability Adjustment

A feasibility conclusion may trigger later prediction review through PMRS, but FSDM itself does not recalculate prediction probability.

Relationship to CPGS

Where Collective Prediction applies, the feasibility state may become an input to collective prediction governance.

Relationship to PCRS

The governed feasibility state should accompany the prediction into communication and reliance governance where material.

Relationship to PMRS

Material feasibility changes may create a trigger for prediction monitoring or revision.

PMRS owns that lifecycle decision.

Relationship to POFPS

Feasible Prediction ≠ Successful Prediction

Infeasible Prediction ≠ Automatically Incorrect Prediction Methodology

An infeasible outcome may reveal a feasibility problem without necessarily proving that every other prediction-governance layer failed.

Relationship to ORA™

FSDM consumes sufficiently governed operational conditions where necessary.

It does not establish reality itself.

Feasibility Determination ≠ Operational Reality Determination

Relationship to ADIT®

Relationship to VALIDOS™

Validated Components ≠ Validated Feasibility of the Whole Outcome

Enterprise Application

FSDM gives organizations a governed decision layer for converting complex feasibility analysis into a clear, traceable, and reusable result.

Without FSDM, teams may possess:

constraint lists; dependency maps; pathway analyses; capacity assessments; execution simulations;

while still having no disciplined method for determining what the final feasibility state actually is.

FSDM creates that final governed conclusion.

Instead of reducing feasibility to:

yes / no

it preserves:

what is feasible, under which conditions, at what scale, within what horizon, subject to which constraints, with what margin, and according to which historical state.

Use Case 1 — The Project Is Feasible, but Only at Half the Predicted Scale

A company predicts deployment of:

200 autonomous units across 20 facilities within twelve months.

FCIM identifies capacity, integration, workforce, and regulatory constraints.

PCMM maps the dependencies.

OPAM confirms that a viable deployment pathway exists.

EOFM finds that the available integration and commissioning teams can realistically deploy only:

100 units across 10 facilities

within the stated horizon.

The critical governance question is:

Should the original prediction be classified as feasible simply because a smaller version of the outcome can be achieved?

FSDM establishes:

Feasibility Restricted

with explicit qualification:

Feasible for approximately 100 units across 10 facilities within the current Prediction Horizon; the full predicted outcome of 200 units across 20 facilities is not feasible under current execution capacity.

Partial Achievement ≠ Full Feasibility

Use Case 2 — Outcome Is Achievable Only if Two Critical Conditions Occur on Time

An energy company predicts that a new facility will become operational by:

December 2028.

The operational pathway is viable.

Execution capacity is sufficient.

However, the project depends upon:

regulatory approval by March 2027

and:

grid connection availability by January 2028.

If either condition is delayed beyond its threshold, the Prediction Horizon can no longer be achieved.

The critical governance question is:

Can the outcome legitimately be called feasible when its achievability depends upon two unresolved time-critical conditions?

FSDM determines:

Conditionally Feasible

and preserves both conditions as part of the governed feasibility state.

Conditionally Feasible ≠ Feasible Without Qualification

Canonical Boundary

FSDM governs the integration of governed constraint, constraint-mapping, operational-pathway, execution, outcome, scale, scope, timing, authority, permission, infrastructure, resource, and other material feasibility conditions into a final governed feasibility state for a specific predicted outcome. It governs feasibility qualification, conditionality, restriction, uncertainty, infeasibility, inability to determine, margins, fragility, sensitivity, dominant constraints, historical state, reassessment, and state change. It does not form the prediction, calculate probability, govern Predictive Uncertainty, establish Operational Reality, validate evidence, govern Collective Prediction composition, determine actual outcomes, assess predictive performance, or decide prediction-level revision.

FSDM governs:

what feasibility state the predicted outcome may legitimately carry under the applicable conditions.

It does not determine:

whether the predicted outcome will actually occur.

Canonical Closing Statement

Feasibility is not established because nothing obvious appears impossible.

FSDM converts constraints, pathways, execution capability, conditions, scale, timing, and operational limits into a traceable determination of what the predicted outcome can legitimately be called.

A prediction may remain uncertain and still be feasible; it may appear likely and still be infeasible. Governance must know the difference.
