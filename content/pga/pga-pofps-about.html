About the Prediction Outcome & Performance Standard

What Is the Prediction Outcome & Performance Standard?

The Prediction Outcome & Performance Standard (POFPS) is the Parent Standard within PGA™ — Prediction Governance Architecture that governs what happens when a prediction can finally be compared with what subsequently occurred.

It establishes how a historically identifiable Governed Prediction is associated with a sufficiently governed Outcome Reference and how that relationship may legitimately support prediction comparison, error assessment, probability calibration, performance interpretation, and longitudinal predictive-performance analysis.

POFPS does not ask only:

Was the prediction right or wrong?

It asks the more rigorous questions:

What exactly was predicted?

Which prediction version is being evaluated?

What was the Prediction Horizon?

What Predictive Boundary applied?

What conditions and assumptions applied?

What outcome was sufficiently established?

Are the prediction and outcome actually comparable?

Did intervention or prediction use materially change the environment?

What does the observed result legitimately demonstrate about predictive performance?

This distinction is fundamental because:

Observed Accuracy ≠ Demonstrated Predictive Skill

Why Does POFPS Exist?

Prediction governance would be incomplete if it governed only how predictions are created and then treated the eventual outcome as a simple binary test.

A prediction can appear successful for many different reasons.

It may reflect:

strong predictive methodology; superior Prediction Basis; legitimate information advantage; chance; high underlying base rate; outcome proximity; late revision; selective reporting; prediction-induced intervention; direct influence over the predicted environment; partial knowledge of decisions that will determine the outcome.

Similarly, a prediction can appear unsuccessful even when the relationship is more complicated.

An intervention may prevent the predicted event.

The prediction may have been explicitly conditional.

The event may occur outside the Prediction Horizon.

The outcome may not yet be sufficiently established.

The prediction may have assigned only a probability rather than certainty.

Without governance, all of these situations can collapse into:

Correct

or:

Incorrect

POFPS exists to prevent that collapse.

From Prediction to Performance

POFPS governs the transition:

Governed Historical Prediction + Sufficiently Governed Outcome Reference → Prediction-to-Outcome Association → Prediction-to-Outcome Comparison → Predictive Performance Determination

The presence of an outcome does not automatically make the comparison legitimate.

The prediction and outcome must first be aligned.

The Outcome Must Be Sufficiently Governed

POFPS does not establish Operational Reality.

That responsibility belongs to ORA™ — Operational Reality Architecture or another appropriate reality-governance source.

POFPS receives a sufficiently governed Outcome Reference and determines how that outcome may legitimately be associated with the prediction.

This creates the critical boundary:

Prediction Outcome Association ≠ Outcome Determination

POFPS governs the relationship.

It does not manufacture the reality against which the prediction is judged.

Outcome Availability Is Not Enough

An outcome may appear obvious while still being insufficiently governed for the proposed performance claim.

Outcome status may be:

Sufficiently Available

Partially Available

Conditionally Available

Disputed

Not Yet Available

Unable to Determine

Performance conclusions must remain proportional to the quality and maturity of the available Outcome Reference.

Outcome Information Available ≠ Outcome Sufficiently Governed

Which Prediction Is Being Evaluated?

Predictions may change throughout their lifecycle.

For example:

P1 — 62%

→ P2 — 71%

→ P3 — 84%

→ Outcome

Which prediction should be evaluated?

There is no universal answer.

POFPS may distinguish:

Original Prediction Performance

Decision-Time Prediction Performance

Version-Specific Prediction Performance

Final Prediction Performance

Longitudinal Prediction Performance

Each answers a different question.

A final prediction cannot silently replace the original prediction when historical performance is evaluated.

Original Prediction Performance ≠ Final Prediction Performance

Historical Context Matters

Suppose a predictor originally assigns:

35% probability

to an event.

Immediately before the event, after substantial new information becomes public, the prediction is revised to:

92% probability.

The event occurs.

Reporting only:

“Final prediction: 92% — correct”

does not describe the complete predictive lifecycle.

POFPS preserves both prediction states and their timing.

This makes it possible to distinguish early predictive capability from late adaptation to information.

Final Accuracy ≠ Full Lifecycle Predictive Performance

Prediction Horizon Matters

A prediction is governed within time.

If a prediction states:

Project X will become operational within 12 months

and Project X becomes operational after 30 months, the eventual occurrence does not retroactively satisfy the original Prediction Horizon.

Event Eventually Occurred ≠ Prediction Was Correct Within Horizon

POFPS therefore preserves temporal alignment between prediction and outcome.

Predictive Boundary Matters

Predictions also operate within defined boundaries.

A prediction about:

one country; one population; one technology; one market; one organization; one scenario;

cannot automatically receive performance credit because a related event occurred somewhere else.

Related Outcome ≠ Predicted Outcome

Performance requires alignment between what was predicted and what actually occurred.

Conditional Predictions Require Conditional Evaluation

Consider:

75% probability that Project X becomes operational by 2028 provided regulatory authorization occurs by March 2027.

If regulatory authorization never occurs, evaluating the prediction as though it were unconditional would distort what was actually predicted.

POFPS preserves the governing conditions.

Conditional Prediction ≠ Unconditional Performance Object

Probability Is Not Binary Correctness

Probabilistic prediction is fundamentally different from certainty.

Suppose a prediction assigns:

70% probability that Event A occurs.

Event A does not occur.

That does not automatically prove the prediction was bad.

A legitimate 70% probability implies that non-occurrence remains possible.

Likewise, if a prediction assigns:

30% probability

and the event occurs, that does not automatically demonstrate superior predictive performance.

Therefore:

70% Probability + Non-Occurrence ≠ Automatic Prediction Failure

30% Probability + Occurrence ≠ Automatic Prediction Success

Probabilistic performance becomes more meaningful across repeated predictions.

Calibration

Calibration examines whether events assigned similar probabilities occur at approximately corresponding frequencies across a sufficiently governed prediction population.

Conceptually:

If a predictor repeatedly assigns approximately:

70% probability

to comparable events, approximately the expected proportion should eventually resolve positively over a sufficiently meaningful sample.

This makes calibration fundamentally different from asking whether one prediction was correct.

Calibration ≠ Single-Prediction Correctness

Calibration Requires Context

A predictor may be well calibrated in one domain and poorly calibrated in another.

Performance may therefore require segmentation by:

domain; Prediction Object; methodology; Prediction Horizon; probability band; geography; predictor; model; information environment.

Aggregate Calibration ≠ Calibration in Every Segment

Accuracy Is Not Predictive Skill

A predictor may achieve high accuracy by predicting an event that almost always occurs.

If an event occurs 95% of the time, repeatedly predicting occurrence can generate high observed accuracy without demonstrating substantial predictive capability.

POFPS therefore preserves:

base rates; benchmarks; calibration; discrimination; repeated performance; predictive conditions.

High Accuracy ≠ High Predictive Skill by Default

Predictive Success Is Not Predictive Capability

One successful prediction may result from:

methodology; information; chance; privileged access; late information; outcome influence.

Predictive capability requires stronger evidence.

It may require repeated performance across sufficiently comparable prediction cases.

Therefore:

Predictive Success ≠ Predictive Capability

and:

Single Correct Prediction ≠ Demonstrated Predictive Skill

Prediction Error

POFPS governs legitimate representation of prediction error where the Prediction Object permits meaningful comparison.

Error may concern:

categorical outcome; numerical difference; direction; magnitude; timing; predicted range; probability calibration.

There is no single universal error metric appropriate for every prediction.

The performance methodology must match the Prediction Object.

Performance Across Time

POFPS supports longitudinal performance governance.

This allows organizations to ask:

Is predictive performance improving?

Is calibration deteriorating?

Does the predictor perform differently across horizons?

Did performance change after a methodology update?

Did a model upgrade materially improve predictions?

Does performance degrade in particular operational regimes?

A single headline score cannot answer these questions.

Single Aggregate Score ≠ Complete Predictive Performance

Predictor–Reality Proximity

Performance cannot always be interpreted without understanding how close the predictor was to the reality producing the outcome.

PGA™ recognizes the Predictor–Reality Proximity continuum:

Public-Information Predictor

→ Information-Advantaged Predictor

→ Decision-Proximate Predictor

→ Outcome-Informed Predictor

→ Outcome-Influencing Predictor

→ Outcome-Controlling Predictor

This does not imply misconduct.

It provides performance context.

Information Advantage

A predictor may legitimately possess better information than others.

That may improve prediction accuracy.

But performance analysis should distinguish superior information from superior predictive methodology.

Information Advantage ≠ Predictive Capability

A highly accurate predictor with privileged information may demonstrate strong informational positioning without demonstrating a universally superior prediction methodology.

Outcome Knowledge

The distinction becomes stronger where the predictor already knows information materially determining the outcome.

Outcome Knowledge ≠ Predictive Capability

Knowing what will happen because the relevant decision is already known is different from predicting the outcome from uncertain information.

Decision Proximity

A predictor may have access to actors who will make the decision determining the outcome.

That proximity can materially improve apparent predictive performance.

But:

Decision Proximity ≠ Decision Authority

and:

Decision Proximity ≠ Predictive Skill

POFPS preserves this context rather than automatically treating it as methodological superiority.

Outcome Influence

Some predictions influence the reality they predict.

Examples include:

market forecasts influencing trading; shortage forecasts influencing purchasing; demand forecasts influencing production; failure predictions triggering maintenance; election forecasts affecting participation;

risk predictions changing organizational behavior.

Where this occurs:

Prediction Observation ≠ Prediction Influence

and:

Prediction Influence ≠ Predictive Accuracy

Self-Fulfilling Predictions

A prediction may contribute to producing the predicted outcome.

Example:

A widely adopted demand forecast causes organizations to increase production capacity, which then helps create the forecasted market expansion.

The eventual outcome may match the prediction.

But the prediction-to-outcome relationship now includes prediction influence.

POFPS preserves that influence as part of performance interpretation.

Self-Negating Predictions

The reverse can also occur.

A prediction may trigger action that prevents the predicted outcome.

Example:

90% probability of critical equipment failure

causes preventive maintenance.

The equipment does not fail.

A simplistic evaluation may classify the prediction as incorrect.

POFPS recognizes that intervention changed the environment.

Prevented Outcome ≠ Automatically Failed Prediction

Intervention-Aware Performance

Where prediction use materially changes Operational Reality, performance assessment should preserve:

Prediction → Reliance / Use → Intervention → Changed Conditions → Outcome

The original predicted environment may no longer exist.

This does not allow POFPS to invent the counterfactual outcome.

Unobserved Counterfactual ≠ Known Outcome

The appropriate performance state may instead be:

Intervention-Affected

Revision-Aware Performance

Predictions frequently evolve.

POFPS preserves the complete sequence:

P1 → P2 → P3 → Outcome

This allows evaluation of:

original prediction; revision timing; revision direction; final prediction; responsiveness to changing information.

A predictor should not be able to erase weak early predictions simply by updating shortly before resolution.

Superseded Predictions Still Matter

PSSM may establish that an older prediction was superseded.

That does not remove the older state from performance history.

Superseded ≠ Excluded from Performance History by Default

The older prediction was genuinely current during part of the lifecycle.

Withdrawn Predictions Still Matter

PWM may legitimately withdraw a prediction.

Withdrawal may be entirely justified.

But the prediction still existed before withdrawal.

It may have been:

communicated; relied upon; operationally active; part of historical predictive performance.

Therefore:

Withdrawal ≠ Performance Erasure

Preventing Survivorship Bias

Imagine a forecasting organization creates 1,000 predictions.

Before outcomes become known, it:

withdraws 150 weak predictions; deletes 100 abandoned predictions; preserves 750 predictions that performed better.

If performance is calculated only from the visible 750 predictions, the resulting score may substantially misrepresent actual predictive performance.

POFPS therefore requires preservation of the relevant Performance Population.

Visible Prediction Population ≠ Complete Prediction Population

Performance Population

A statement such as:

“Our predictions are 84% accurate.”

is methodologically incomplete without knowing:

how many predictions were evaluated; which predictions were included; which were excluded; which Prediction Horizons were used; what prediction types were included; what period was assessed; whether withdrawn predictions were included; whether superseded versions were included; what outcome definition was used.

POFPS makes the population behind the performance claim governable.

Performance Comparability

Not every prediction should be aggregated with every other prediction.

Predictions may differ fundamentally by:

object; methodology; horizon; probability structure; domain; environment; outcome type.

Multiple Predictions ≠ Comparable Prediction Set

Comparability must precede aggregation.

Performance Metrics

POFPS does not impose one universal performance score.

Different predictions may require different metrics.

Metrics may assess:

accuracy; error; calibration; range performance; timing performance; discrimination; stability; benchmark performance.

Every metric must preserve what it actually measures.

Metric Value ≠ Self-Explanatory Performance

Performance Qualification

Performance results may require contextual qualification.

Examples:

High Accuracy — High Base Rate

Strong Calibration — Limited Sample

Strong Final Accuracy — Frequent Late Revision

High Outcome Match — Information-Advantaged Predictor

Outcome Match — Material Prediction Influence

Performance Assessment — Intervention-Affected

These qualifications prevent a valid numerical result from becoming an invalid performance claim.

What POFPS Does Not Govern

POFPS does not:

establish Operational Reality; determine what actually occurred independently; create Outcome Evidence; form predictions; define prediction methodology; establish Prediction Basis; govern prediction assumptions; calculate probability; establish Prediction Feasibility; create Collective Predictions; determine Prediction Communication; determine Prediction Reliance; determine Prediction Use; make decisions; authorize execution; assign legal liability; assign organizational responsibility; determine causal responsibility; equate predictive performance with economic value.

Its domain is:

Prediction Outcome & Predictive Performance

Cross-Architecture Boundaries

ORA™ — Operational Reality Architecture

ORA™ governs Operational Reality.

POFPS consumes a sufficiently governed Outcome Reference.

Operational Reality Determination ≠ Prediction Outcome Association

ADIT®

ADIT® governs applicable evidence relationships and auditability.

POFPS does not become an evidence-governance architecture merely because performance depends upon evidence.

VALIDOS™

VALIDOS™ may govern validation of relevant methodologies, processes, or performance claims.

POFPS owns prediction-performance governance within PGA™.

INTEGROS®

INTEGROS® governs broader integrity structures.

POFPS preserves integrity of the prediction-to-outcome performance relationship.

PCRS

PCRS governs communication, interpretation, reliance, and use.

POFPS may consume relevant reliance or intervention history where prediction use affected the outcome.

PMRS

PMRS governs monitoring, review, revision, supersession, and withdrawal before and during predictive evolution.

POFPS preserves those lifecycle states when performance is later assessed.

PRCS

PRCS preserves complete Prediction Record and Historical Continuity.

POFPS generates performance records that become part of that continuing prediction history.

Position Within PGA™

POFPS is the eleventh Parent Standard within the Prediction Governance Lifecycle:

Prediction Object & Purpose → Prediction Methodology → Prediction Basis → Prediction Assumptions → Prediction Formation → Probability & Uncertainty → Prediction Feasibility → Collective Prediction where applicable → Communication & Reliance → Monitoring & Revision → Outcome & Performance → Prediction Record & Continuity

POFPS occupies the point where:

Prediction History meets governed outcome information.

It converts that relationship into governed predictive-performance information.

Why POFPS Matters

Prediction systems are increasingly evaluated through:

accuracy percentages; model leaderboards; market performance; calibration scores; forecast success rates; prediction-market histories; AI benchmark results; expert track records.

These numbers can appear objective while concealing critical methodological differences.

A performance claim may omit:

failed predictions; withdrawn predictions; early prediction versions; late revisions; information advantages; high base rates; outcome influence; intervention; incomparable prediction populations.

POFPS makes these hidden performance conditions governable.

What Organizations Gain

Organizations implementing POFPS can establish:

traceable prediction-to-outcome relationships; version-specific predictive performance; original-versus-final performance comparison; probability calibration governance; error governance; longitudinal predictive-performance records; predictor and methodology performance profiles; intervention-aware performance; Predictor–Reality Proximity context; explicit Performance Populations; governed inclusion and exclusion rules; protection against survivorship bias; protection against retrospective prediction rewriting; stronger AI and human predictor evaluation; auditable performance claims.

The result is not merely:

Was the prediction right?

It is:

What does the complete governed prediction-to-outcome relationship legitimately demonstrate?

Use Case 1 — A 90% Failure Prediction Prevents the Failure

An AI predictive-maintenance system generates:

90% probability that Machine A will suffer critical bearing failure within seven days.

The prediction is:

methodologically governed; sufficiently supported; uncertainty-qualified; operationally communicated.

Because of the prediction, the operator shuts down Machine A and replaces the bearing.

No failure occurs.

A conventional performance system records:

Prediction: Failure

Outcome: No Failure

Result: Incorrect Prediction

But the prediction itself materially changed Operational Reality.

The critical governance question is:

Can non-occurrence be treated as evidence that the prediction was wrong when reliance upon the prediction caused an intervention that prevented the predicted event?

POFPS reconstructs:

Governed Prediction

→ Reliance

→ Preventive Intervention

→ Changed Operational Conditions

→ No Failure

The appropriate performance relationship is therefore:

Intervention-Affected

rather than automatically:

Prediction Failed

POFPS does not claim that the bearing certainly would have failed without maintenance.

That would require knowledge of an unobserved counterfactual.

Instead it preserves:

Prevented Outcome ≠ Automatically Failed Prediction

and:

Unobserved Counterfactual ≠ Known Outcome

The result is a performance record that neither falsely credits nor falsely penalizes the prediction.

Use Case 2 — Forecasting Platform Reports 86% Accuracy After Removing Weak Predictions

A forecasting platform publishes thousands of predictions across:

politics; economics; technology; regulation; corporate events.

The platform advertises:

“86% Historical Prediction Accuracy.”

A performance review identifies that the published calculation includes only predictions that remained active until resolution.

During the same period:

some predictions were revised multiple times; some were superseded; some were withdrawn; some disappeared from the public interface; several final predictions were issued only shortly before outcomes became known.

The critical governance question is:

What prediction population does the 86% actually represent?

POFPS requires reconstruction of the governed Performance Population.

It distinguishes:

Original Predictions

Historical Prediction Versions

Final Predictions

Superseded Predictions

Withdrawn Predictions

Resolved Predictions

Excluded Predictions

It then requires any performance claim to identify its inclusion rules.

The resulting assessment may legitimately show:

Final-Version Accuracy: 86%

while also showing:

Original-Prediction Accuracy: materially different

Calibration: separately determined

Withdrawn Prediction Population: preserved

Late-Revision Frequency: disclosed

The original 86% number may therefore remain mathematically correct while being methodologically incomplete.

POFPS does not automatically accuse the platform of manipulation.

It makes the governance question visible:

What exactly does the number measure?

This preserves the foundational distinctions:

Visible Prediction Population ≠ Complete Prediction Population

Final Accuracy ≠ Full Lifecycle Predictive Performance

Observed Accuracy ≠ Demonstrated Predictive Skill

Canonical Boundary

POFPS governs the relationship between historically identifiable Governed Predictions and sufficiently governed Outcome References and the legitimate determination, interpretation, comparison, calibration, segmentation, aggregation, qualification, and preservation of predictive performance. It does not establish Operational Reality, manufacture the outcome, rewrite historical predictions, determine causal responsibility, authorize decisions or actions, or convert prediction performance into automatic claims of predictive capability.

Its boundary can be expressed simply:

Outcome governance establishes what occurred.

POFPS establishes what the relationship between that outcome and the governed prediction legitimately demonstrates.

Canonical Closing Statement

The future becoming known does not simplify prediction governance; it exposes whether the prediction was governed well enough to be judged without rewriting its history.

POFPS preserves the prediction that actually existed, the conditions under which it existed, the outcome that was sufficiently established, and the informational and operational context connecting the two.

A prediction should not be rewarded for knowing what was already effectively known, punished for an outcome it helped prevent, or allowed to improve its history after the future arrived.
