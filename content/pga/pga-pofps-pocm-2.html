Governed Space

POCM governs how an established Governed Prediction Outcome Association is transformed into a structured, traceable, and methodologically legitimate comparison between what was predicted and what subsequently occurred.

It governs the comparison layer between:

Prediction Outcome Association

and:

Prediction Error Measurement

POCM establishes:

what dimensions may be compared; what comparison structure applies; whether comparison is complete, partial, qualified, or restricted; whether timing, magnitude, direction, range, category, conditions, or other dimensions correspond; how unresolved, intervention-affected, partially matured, or conditionally comparable outcomes are represented; how comparison remains separate from error measurement and predictive-performance judgment.

The canonical transition is:

Governed Prediction Outcome Association → Governed Prediction Outcome Comparison

Canonical Definition

The Prediction Outcome Comparison Module (POCM) defines the operational governance requirements by which an established Governed Prediction Outcome Association is examined across the prediction dimensions necessary to determine how the predicted state corresponds to the sufficiently governed Outcome Reference.

POCM governs:

comparison eligibility; comparison object; comparison dimensions; categorical comparison; numerical comparison; directional comparison; magnitude comparison; timing comparison; range comparison; threshold comparison; conditional comparison; scenario comparison; partial comparison; composite comparison; intervention-aware comparison; version-specific comparison; comparison completeness; comparison sufficiency; comparison qualification; comparison state; comparison traceability.

Its foundational principle is:

Association establishes that the outcome belongs to the prediction. Comparison establishes how the prediction corresponds to that outcome.

Operational Role

POCM is the second operational module within POFPS.

Its focused operational question is:

Given a legitimate Prediction Outcome Association, in what governed way does the historical prediction correspond to the actual outcome across the dimensions that the prediction originally claimed?

POCM does not answer:

How large was the prediction error?

That belongs to PEMM.

POCM does not answer:

Was the predictor calibrated?

That belongs to PCALM.

POCM does not answer:

How good was the predictor overall?

That belongs to PPCM.

Its primary output is:

Governed Prediction Outcome Comparison

Internal Module Flow

Prediction Outcome Association → Prediction Outcome Comparison → Prediction Error Measurement → Prediction Calibration → Predictive Performance

Current Position:

Prediction Outcome Comparison

1. Comparison Eligibility

A prediction may enter POCM only after POAM establishes a sufficiently governed Prediction Outcome Association.

Possible upstream association states may include:

Association Established

Association Established with Qualification

Partial Association

A prediction should not proceed as fully comparable where POAM establishes:

Association Not Supported

or:

Outcome Not Yet Sufficient

2. Comparison Object

The Comparison Object is the specific aspect of the historical prediction being evaluated against the Outcome Reference.

Possible Comparison Objects include:

event occurrence; categorical state; numerical value; directional movement; magnitude; range; threshold; timing; duration; sequence; condition satisfaction; multi-part predicted state.

The Comparison Object must remain tied to the original prediction.

3. Comparison Dimension

A prediction may contain one or several Comparison Dimensions.

Example:

Company X revenue will grow 12–18% in 2028.

Comparison Dimensions include:

direction: growth; magnitude: 12–18%; timing: 2028.

A prediction may match one dimension and fail another.

Therefore:

Prediction Comparison ≠ Single Binary Result by Default

4. Comparison Structure

The Comparison Structure defines how the prediction and Outcome Reference are compared.

Possible structures include:

Categorical

Binary

Numerical

Directional

Range-Based

Threshold-Based

Temporal

Conditional

Scenario-Based

Composite

The comparison method must reflect the Prediction Object.

5. Binary Comparison

Binary comparison may be appropriate where the prediction legitimately concerns two resolution states.

Example:

Event occurs / Event does not occur

Even here, binary comparison must preserve probability and conditionality where applicable.

Binary Outcome ≠ Binary Probability Interpretation

6. Categorical Comparison

Categorical predictions may resolve into more than two states.

Possible comparison results include:

Exact Category Match

Partial Category Match

Related Category

Different Category

Category Not Comparable

7. Numerical Comparison

Numerical predictions may be compared to an observed numerical outcome.

Example:

Predicted revenue: CHF 120 million

Outcome: CHF 111 million

POCM establishes the compared values.

PEMM subsequently determines error magnitude.

8. Directional Comparison

A prediction may correctly identify direction even when magnitude is inaccurate.

Example:

Predicted: demand decreases

Outcome: demand decreases

Direction may match even if the predicted scale differs substantially.

Directional Match ≠ Magnitude Match

9. Magnitude Comparison

Magnitude comparison determines whether the scale of the predicted change corresponds to the observed scale.

Example:

Predicted decline: 5%

Observed decline: 22%

Direction matches.

Magnitude does not.

10. Range Comparison

Where the prediction specifies a range, POCM determines whether the Outcome Reference falls:

Within Range

At Boundary

Outside Range

Partially Comparable

Unable to Determine

Range width remains relevant for later performance interpretation.

11. Threshold Comparison

Some predictions concern crossing a threshold.

Example:

Inflation exceeds 4%

Outcome:

4.1%

The threshold was crossed.

POCM preserves that correspondence.

It does not yet determine how strong or useful the prediction was.

12. Timing Comparison

Timing Comparison determines how the actual event time corresponds to the predicted Prediction Horizon or predicted timing point.

Possible states include:

Within Predicted Time

Earlier Than Predicted

Later Than Predicted

Outside Prediction Horizon

Timing Not Comparable

13. Event Occurrence vs Timing Accuracy

A prediction may correctly predict that an event occurs while incorrectly predicting when it occurs.

Event Match ≠ Timing Match

Both dimensions should remain independently visible.

14. Duration Comparison

Where duration is part of the prediction, POCM may compare:

predicted duration; actual duration.

Example:

Supply disruption lasts 3–5 weeks

Outcome:

Supply disruption lasts 11 weeks

Occurrence may match.

Duration does not.

15. Sequence Comparison

Predictions may specify event order.

Example:

Approval → Financing → Construction

Outcome:

Financing → Approval → Construction

All events occurred.

The sequence differed.

Component Occurrence ≠ Sequence Match

16. Conditional Comparison

Conditional predictions require comparison under their stated conditions.

Example:

If interest rates remain below 3%, housing demand will increase.

POCM must preserve whether the condition was satisfied before evaluating predicted correspondence.

17. Condition-Satisfied Comparison

Where all governing conditions were satisfied, the prediction may be compared under the intended predictive structure.

18. Condition-Partially-Satisfied Comparison

Where a condition was only partly satisfied, comparison may require qualification.

Partial Condition Satisfaction ≠ Full Conditional Comparability

19. Condition-Not-Satisfied Comparison

If a governing condition did not occur, the prediction may not support ordinary outcome comparison.

Possible state:

Conditionally Not Comparable

20. Scenario Comparison

Scenario-based predictions must be evaluated against the applicable scenario.

A prediction for:

High-Energy-Price Scenario

should not be assessed as though it were the baseline scenario if the high-price condition never materialized.

21. Scenario Divergence

Where Operational Reality diverges materially from the governed scenario, POCM should preserve:

Scenario Divergence

rather than force a simplistic comparison.

22. Composite Prediction

A Composite Prediction contains multiple predictive claims.

Example:

Company X will enter Germany, achieve €50M revenue, and reach profitability by 2030.

Each component may require separate comparison.

23. Composite Comparison

A composite prediction may resolve as:

All Components Matched

Most Components Matched

Mixed Outcome

Partial Match

No Material Match

Not Fully Comparable

A single headline label should not hide material component differences.

24. Component-Level Comparison

Each material component should remain independently comparable where possible.

Example:

Geographic Expansion: Matched

Revenue Target: Not Matched

Profitability: Not Yet Resolved

25. Partial Comparison

A prediction may be only partially comparable because:

only some outcome components matured; part of the Prediction Horizon remains open; one condition remains unresolved; only part of the Outcome Reference is sufficiently governed.

POCM should represent partial comparison rather than forcing finality.

26. Comparison Completeness

Comparison Completeness may be:

Complete

Substantially Complete

Partial

Limited

Not Yet Complete

Unable to Determine

27. Comparison Sufficiency

Comparison Sufficiency asks whether the available information supports the proposed comparison conclusion.

This differs from Outcome Reference Sufficiency.

POAM may have a sufficient Outcome Reference while POCM still finds insufficient information for a particular comparison dimension.

28. Comparison Qualification

Comparison may require qualification because of:

provisional Outcome Reference; partial maturity; intervention; scenario divergence; semantic ambiguity; condition mismatch; incomplete timing; unavailable component outcome.

29. Exact Match

Exact Match may occur where the predicted state and governed outcome correspond across all material comparison dimensions.

Exact Match ≠ Demonstrated Predictive Skill

It is only a comparison result.

30. Partial Match

Partial Match means some material dimensions correspond and others do not.

This should not automatically be converted to:

Correct

or:

Incorrect

without further governance.

31. No Match

No Match may be appropriate where the outcome materially differs from the prediction across the relevant comparison dimensions.

This still does not by itself establish poor predictive capability.

32. Related but Non-Matching Outcome

A related event may occur without satisfying the prediction.

POCM must preserve:

Related Outcome

separately from:

Prediction Match

33. Temporal Partial Match

Example:

Prediction:

Product launches globally by Q2 2028.

Outcome:

Product launches in Europe in Q2 2028 and globally in Q4 2028.

Timing partially matches.

Scope does not fully match.

34. Boundary Partial Match

Example:

Prediction:

Demand increases across the EU.

Outcome:

Demand increases in 8 Member States but declines elsewhere.

POCM should not automatically classify this as complete match.

35. Population Partial Match

Prediction:

80% of surveyed enterprises adopt Tool X.

Outcome:

80% adoption among large enterprises but 35% overall.

The governed population matters.

36. Magnitude Partial Match

Direction may be correct while magnitude is materially different.

This should remain visible for PEMM.

37. Timing Partial Match

An event may occur close to, but outside, the Prediction Horizon.

POCM should preserve the actual timing relationship.

It should not silently extend the horizon.

38. Threshold Edge Case

A predicted threshold may be crossed by a negligible amount.

Example:

Prediction:

GDP growth exceeds 2%.

Outcome:

2.01%.

The threshold comparison may be a match.

PPCM may later qualify the practical meaning.

39. Range Edge Case

An outcome exactly on a stated range boundary should be governed consistently.

Example:

Prediction:

10–15%

Outcome:

15%

The implementation should define whether inclusive boundaries apply.

40. Rounding Effects

Outcome and prediction values may differ because of rounding.

POCM should preserve:

original precision; reported precision; actual precision where known.

Rounded Equality ≠ Exact Numerical Equality by Default

41. Measurement Scale Alignment

Predicted and outcome values must use compatible measurement scales.

Example:

Predicted growth: nominal

Outcome:

reported growth: real

These are not directly comparable without transformation.

42. Unit Alignment

Prediction:

500 MW

Outcome:

0.5 GW

Units differ, meaning may be equivalent.

POCM may allow normalized representation while preserving original units.

43. Currency Alignment

Predicted financial values may require governance of:

currency; nominal vs real; exchange rate basis; reporting date.

Without alignment, comparison may be misleading.

44. Inflation Adjustment Context

A nominal prediction should not be compared with an inflation-adjusted outcome without preserving the transformation.

45. Methodological Transformation

Where comparison requires normalization or transformation, that transformation should be documented.

Transformed Comparison ≠ Original Raw Comparison

46. Derived Outcome Comparison

Some comparisons require derived outcome values.

Example:

Prediction concerns:

year-over-year growth

but the source provides absolute values.

The calculation used to derive the comparison state should remain traceable.

47. Derived Prediction Comparison

A prediction may itself contain derived metrics.

POCM must compare like with like.

48. Comparison Reference Time

The reference time used for comparison should remain identifiable.

Example:

market capitalization at:

market close

versus:

intraday high

may create materially different results.

49. Resolution Window

Some prediction objects require a resolution window rather than one instant.

Example:

Average oil price during Q4

The outcome must correspond to that full window.

50. Event Persistence

A predicted state may need to persist for a defined duration.

Example:

System remains operational for 30 consecutive days.

One operational day does not satisfy the prediction.

51. Temporary Outcome

A temporarily achieved state should not automatically satisfy a prediction requiring sustained status.

Temporary State ≠ Sustained Predicted Outcome

52. Reversal After Outcome

Some events occur and are later reversed.

POCM should determine whether the Prediction Object concerned:

occurrence; persistence; final state; temporary state.

Example:

Approval granted then revoked.

The comparison depends on what was predicted.

53. Multi-Stage Outcome

A prediction may concern a process with multiple stages.

POCM may compare each stage separately.

54. Outcome Dependency

One predicted outcome may depend on another.

The comparison should preserve dependency rather than treating each event as fully independent.

55. Mutually Exclusive Outcomes

Where prediction categories are mutually exclusive, POCM should ensure comparison respects that structure.

56. Overlapping Outcomes

Where outcomes can overlap, a prediction may correspond to multiple states.

Comparison logic should not assume exclusivity by default.

57. Outcome Hierarchy

A prediction may refer to a parent category while the Outcome Reference identifies a more specific child state.

POCM should determine whether hierarchical equivalence is legitimate.

58. Semantic Equivalence

Different wording may represent the same operational meaning.

POCM may establish semantic equivalence where sufficiently governed.

Different Wording ≠ Different Outcome by Default

59. Semantic Non-Equivalence

Similar wording may still carry materially different operational meaning.

Similar Wording ≠ Same Outcome by Default

60. Comparison Freeze

Once the comparison basis is established, the historical prediction dimensions should remain fixed.

Later performance results must not alter the original comparison criteria.

61. Retrospective Comparison Drift

Comparison Drift occurs when evaluators subtly change:

threshold; scope; horizon; condition; outcome definition; category; measurement method;

after the outcome becomes known.

POCM must prevent this.

62. Comparison Cherry-Picking

A composite prediction may contain five components.

If only one matches, reporting only that component creates misleading comparison.

POCM should preserve all material governed components.

63. Favorable Dimension Selection

A predictor may emphasize direction because magnitude failed.

Or emphasize occurrence because timing failed.

POCM should preserve the full Comparison Profile.

64. Comparison Profile

The Comparison Profile is the complete set of material comparison dimensions and their states.

Example:

Occurrence: Matched

Timing: Did Not Match

Magnitude: Partial Match

Geography: Matched

Condition: Satisfied

This provides richer performance input than a single binary label.

65. Comparison Weighting

Some implementations may weight comparison dimensions.

Any weighting must be established before performance interpretation where possible.

Comparison Weight ≠ Prediction Importance by Default

66. Post-Outcome Weight Manipulation

Weights should not be retrospectively changed simply to improve the apparent performance result.

67. Material Dimension

A Material Comparison Dimension is one whose mismatch changes the substantive meaning of the prediction.

Not every textual detail needs equal weight.

68. Non-Material Difference

Minor formatting, rounding, or semantically equivalent wording may not create substantive mismatch.

POCM should preserve methodological proportionality.

69. Material Mismatch

A Material Mismatch occurs when a comparison dimension differs sufficiently to change predictive meaning.

Examples:

wrong geography; wrong horizon; wrong population; wrong magnitude class; wrong category; unmet condition.

70. Comparison Conflict

Different evaluation systems may produce conflicting comparison results.

Example:

System A: Match

System B: Partial Match

POCM should preserve the conflict and comparison method.

71. Comparison Method Identification

Every material comparison should remain associated with the method used to produce it.

This allows reconstruction and audit.

72. Human Comparison

Human evaluators may perform governed comparison.

The same requirements apply.

Human judgment should remain attributable where material.

73. AI-Assisted Comparison

AI may assist with:

semantic alignment; event classification; threshold detection; temporal comparison; composite decomposition.

But:

AI Comparison Suggestion ≠ Governed Comparison Determination

74. Autonomous Comparison

Automated comparison may be appropriate where:

prediction structures are machine-readable; Outcome References are machine-readable; rules are explicit; ambiguity is limited; versioning is preserved.

75. Intervention-Aware Comparison

A prediction may remain directly comparable with the observed outcome even if intervention affected the outcome.

The comparison should preserve:

Observed Outcome

plus:

Intervention-Affected Context

rather than inventing a counterfactual result.

76. Self-Fulfilling Comparison

If a prediction contributes to causing the predicted outcome, the observed outcome may still match.

POCM records the match.

PPCM later interprets what that match means.

77. Self-Negating Comparison

If a prediction contributes to preventing the predicted outcome, the observed outcome may be non-occurrence.

POCM records the observed correspondence and intervention context.

It does not conclude predictive failure.

78. Outcome-Controlled Comparison

Where the predictor controlled the outcome, POCM may still establish comparison.

But the Outcome-Control context must remain available downstream.

79. Predictor–Reality Proximity Context

POCM carries forward the applicable PRP state from POAM.

It does not convert proximity into comparison success or failure.

80. Revision-Aware Comparison

Every material prediction version may be compared separately with the same outcome.

Example:

P1: 30%

P2: 55%

P3: 80%

Outcome occurs.

POCM should preserve comparison for all three states if performance analysis requires it.

81. Supersession-Aware Comparison

Superseded predictions remain comparable historical objects.

Superseded ≠ Comparison-Ineligible

82. Withdrawal-Aware Comparison

Withdrawn predictions may remain comparable to later outcomes.

Withdrawn ≠ Comparison-Ineligible

Withdrawal status becomes part of performance context.

83. Original vs Final Comparison

A forecasting system may produce:

Original Prediction Comparison

and:

Final Prediction Comparison

without collapsing them.

84. Decision-Time Comparison

Where a decision was made using P2, later P3 should not be substituted into the historical decision comparison.

85. Collective Prediction Comparison

Collective predictions may be compared to outcomes while preserving:

aggregation state; participant concentration; prediction version; collective probability.

86. Prediction Market Comparison

Market-implied probabilities can be compared with outcomes.

POCM preserves the market state at the relevant time.

It does not equate outcome match with trading profit.

87. Comparison Across Similar Predictions

Different predictors may make comparable predictions about the same event.

POCM may support parallel comparison provided prediction definitions are sufficiently aligned.

88. Cross-Predictor Comparison Boundary

Two predictors may use different Prediction Objects or horizons even when their statements appear similar.

Same Topic ≠ Same Prediction

Comparability must be established first.

89. Cross-Version Comparison

The prediction itself may evolve.

Comparing P1 and P3 to the same outcome is legitimate.

Treating P3 as if it had existed at P1's timestamp is not.

90. Comparison History

Material comparison determinations should remain historically preserved.

Where an Outcome Reference changes, the comparison may be reassessed.

Earlier comparison states should remain reconstructable.

92. Governance Logic

POCM applies the following operational logic:

Has POAM established a sufficiently governed Prediction Outcome Association?

If no:

→ comparison cannot proceed as fully governed.

If yes:

→ identify the Comparison Object.

What did the historical prediction actually claim?

→ identify all material Comparison Dimensions.

Which Comparison Structure applies?

→ categorical, binary, numerical, directional, range-based, threshold-based, temporal, conditional, scenario-based, or composite.

Are all material dimensions sufficiently comparable?

If no:

→ classify:

Partial

Qualified

Not Yet Complete

or:

Not Comparable

If yes:

→ compare each dimension independently.

Did the event occur?

→ record occurrence relationship.

Did it occur within the Prediction Horizon?

→ record timing relationship.

Did magnitude correspond?

→ record magnitude relationship.

Did the outcome fall inside the predicted range?

→ record range relationship.

Were conditions satisfied?

→ record condition relationship.

Did scope, population, geography, or scenario correspond?

→ record applicable boundary relationships.

Did intervention materially affect the environment?

If yes:

→ preserve Intervention-Affected qualification.

Was the prediction composite?

If yes:

→ produce component-level comparison before aggregate representation.

Then:

→ construct the complete Comparison Profile;

→ preserve method, qualification, version, and history;

→ pass the Governed Prediction Outcome Comparison to PEMM.

Canonical transition:

Governed Prediction Outcome Association → Structured Comparison Across Material Prediction Dimensions → Governed Prediction Outcome Comparison

96. Enterprise Application

POCM can govern Prediction Outcome Comparison across:

financial forecasting; industrial forecasting; predictive maintenance; supply-chain forecasting; project delivery; technology forecasting; healthcare operations; insurance; investment analysis; public policy; elections; logistics; energy; climate and environmental forecasting; prediction markets; AI forecasting systems; model ensembles; Human-AI forecasting; autonomous agents; multi-agent prediction environments.

Its function remains constant:

compare what was historically predicted with what sufficiently governed reality shows occurred, without collapsing different predictive dimensions into an oversimplified right-or-wrong label.

97. Use Case 1 — Revenue Forecast Gets the Direction Right but the Magnitude Wrong

An enterprise forecast states:

Revenue will grow between 12% and 16% during FY2028.

The governed Outcome Reference establishes:

Revenue grew 3.2% during FY2028.

POAM has already established that:

the company is the same; the fiscal period is aligned; the revenue definition is aligned; the outcome is sufficiently governed.

POCM now evaluates:

Direction: Growth predicted → Growth observed Result: Matched

Range: 12–16% predicted → 3.2% observed Result: Did Not Match

Magnitude: Strong growth predicted → modest growth observed Result: Material Mismatch

Timing: FY2028 → FY2028 Result: Matched

The critical governance question is:

Should the prediction be called correct because it predicted growth, or incorrect because its predicted magnitude was materially wrong?

POCM does neither.

It produces:

Comparison Profile

Direction: Matched Magnitude: Did Not Match Range: Did Not Match Timing: Matched

PEMM can then measure the numerical error.

PPCM can later interpret the broader predictive performance.

Directional Match ≠ Magnitude Match

Prediction Comparison ≠ Single Binary Result by Default

98. Use Case 2 — Product Launch Occurs, but Outside the Predicted Geography and Time

A technology forecaster publishes:

“Product X will achieve full commercial launch across the European Union by Q2 2028.”

The Outcome Reference establishes:

pilot commercial launch in Germany in Q2 2028; broader EU launch in Q1 2029.

POAM confirms that the outcome events relate to the same Product X and establishes the relevant Outcome References.

POCM evaluates the historical prediction across three material dimensions:

Product Launch Occurrence: Occurred Result: Matched at general event level

Geographic Scope: EU-wide predicted → Germany-only in Q2 2028 Result: Did Not Match within Prediction Horizon

Timing: EU-wide launch by Q2 2028 → EU-wide launch Q1 2029 Result: Outside Prediction Horizon

The organization proposes reporting:

“Prediction Correct — Product X launched.”

POCM rejects that simplification.

The governed Comparison Profile is:

Occurrence: Partial Match

Scope: Did Not Match

Timing: Did Not Match

Overall Comparison State: Partial Match with Material Qualification

The later EU-wide launch remains historically relevant.

It does not retroactively move into Q2 2028.

Event Eventually Occurred ≠ Event Occurred Within Prediction Horizon

Related Outcome ≠ Complete Prediction Match

99. Canonical Boundary

POCM governs the structured comparison between an established Governed Prediction Outcome Association and the corresponding Outcome Reference across the material dimensions originally contained in the prediction. It governs categorical, binary, numerical, directional, magnitude, range, threshold, timing, duration, sequence, conditional, scenario, composite, partial, version-specific, intervention-aware, and machine-readable comparison; Comparison Completeness, Comparison Sufficiency, Comparison Qualification, Comparison Profile, Comparison Method, conflict, traceability, and historical continuity. It does not establish the Outcome Reference, determine Prediction Outcome Association, calculate prediction error, determine probability calibration, assign predictive skill, determine causal explanation, create counterfactual outcomes, or determine overall predictive performance.

100. Canonical Closing Statement

The future rarely answers a prediction with a simple yes or no. It may confirm the event while contradicting the timing, validate the direction while missing the magnitude, satisfy one condition while violating another, or reproduce only part of the predicted state.

POCM preserves those differences before error scores, calibration metrics, or performance claims compress them into a single result.

A prediction should be compared with reality across everything it actually claimed — not only across the dimension that makes it look right.
