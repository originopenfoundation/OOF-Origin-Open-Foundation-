Governed Space

PRFM governs how a sufficiently formed Governed Prediction becomes represented as an identifiable, historically anchored, reconstructable Prediction Record capable of preserving its lifecycle from initial governed state through later versioning, historical preservation, status transitions, retirement, and legitimate lifecycle re-entry.

It governs the transition:

Governed Prediction → Initial Historical Prediction State → Governed Prediction Record

Its foundational principle is:

A prediction cannot preserve a history that was never properly recorded at its origin.

Canonical Definition

The Prediction Record Formation Module (PRFM) defines the operational governance requirements by which a Governed Prediction is established as a persistent Prediction Record with sufficient identity, temporal anchoring, predictive content, methodology references, Prediction Basis references, assumption state, probability and uncertainty state, Prediction Horizon, Predictive Boundary, predictor context, lifecycle status, and provenance references to support subsequent historical continuity and reconstruction.

PRFM creates the historical anchor from which the prediction's governed lifecycle can subsequently be reconstructed.

Operational Role

PRFM is the first operational module within PRCS.

Its focused operational question is:

What minimum historical state must be established when a Governed Prediction enters the Prediction Record so that its later lifecycle can remain identifiable and reconstructable?

PRFM does not form the prediction itself.

PFOS owns Prediction Formation.

PRFM forms:

the historical record of the prediction that has been formed.

Prediction Formation ≠ Prediction Record Formation

Internal Module Flow

Prediction Record Formation → Prediction Version Continuity → Prediction History Preservation → Prediction Record State Transition → Prediction Record Re-Entry

Prediction Record Formation

1. Prediction Record Formation

Prediction Record Formation is the governed establishment of the first historically persistent representation of a Governed Prediction.

It converts:

Predictive State

into:

Historically Governable Predictive State

The prediction becomes capable of accumulating a reconstructable lifecycle.

2. Prediction vs Prediction Record

A prediction is the predictive claim.

A Prediction Record is the governed historical representation of that claim and its subsequent lifecycle.

Prediction ≠ Prediction Record

The distinction is essential.

3. Record Formation Trigger

A Prediction Record should be formed when a prediction reaches a state at which its historical existence becomes materially relevant.

Possible triggers include:

formal prediction formation; publication; communication; operational use; decision reliance; entry into a forecasting system; registration; automated execution eligibility; inclusion in a predictive population.

4. Record Formation Timing

The Prediction Record should be formed sufficiently early to prevent later reconstruction from depending solely on memory, retrospective reporting, or outcome knowledge.

Retrospective Reconstruction ≠ Original Record Formation

5. Initial Historical State

PRFM establishes the:

Initial Historical Prediction State

This represents the earliest governed state from which historical continuity begins.

6. Historical Anchor

The Initial Historical Prediction State becomes the Historical Anchor of the Prediction Record.

Conceptually:

Prediction Origin → Historical Anchor → Prediction Lifecycle

7. Prediction Record Identity

Every governed Prediction Record should possess sufficient identity to distinguish it from other Prediction Records.

The implementation may use:

unique identifiers; registry identifiers; persistent references; distributed identifiers; another governed mechanism.

PRFM does not mandate one technical identity system.

8. Stable Record Identity

The Prediction Record identity should remain sufficiently stable across later lifecycle changes.

Prediction Change ≠ New Record Identity by Default

9. Prediction Identity vs Record Identity

Prediction Identity and Prediction Record Identity may be operationally connected.

They should not be conceptually confused.

The Prediction Object identifies the prediction.

The Prediction Record identifies its governed historical record.

10. Record Origin

The Prediction Record should preserve when and under what governed context it originated.

Origin should not be recreated later merely because the record moves to another system.

Migration ≠ New Record Origin

11. Origin Timestamp

PRFM should preserve the time at which the Prediction Record entered governed historical existence.

Where relevant, separate timestamps may exist for:

prediction formation; record creation; publication; communication; registration; activation.

These should not be silently collapsed.

12. Formation Time vs Record Time

A prediction may be formed before its formal Prediction Record is created.

Where material:

Prediction Formation Time ≠ Prediction Record Creation Time

Both should remain visible.

13. Temporal Precision

Timestamps should carry sufficient precision for the governance purpose.

Examples:

year; date; hour; minute; second; machine event timestamp.

False precision should not be manufactured.

14. Time Source

Where timing is material, the applicable time source or system context should remain identifiable.

15. Prediction Content Snapshot

PRFM preserves the prediction content as it existed at record formation.

This becomes the first historical content snapshot.

16. Content Fidelity

The recorded prediction should faithfully represent what was actually predicted.

It should not be retrospectively improved for clarity after the outcome becomes known.

17. Prediction Object Reference

The record should identify the Prediction Object governed under POPS.

This preserves what exactly was being predicted.

18. Predictive Purpose Reference

Where material, the record should preserve the predictive purpose defined under POPS.

The same predictive statement used for different purposes may carry different governance implications.

19. Prediction Horizon Reference

The applicable Prediction Horizon should be recorded.

A later extension of the horizon must not alter the original record.

20. Predictive Boundary Reference

Material Predictive Boundaries should remain associated with the Initial Historical Prediction State.

Examples:

geography; population; market; technology; product; event; scenario.

21. Methodology Reference

The Prediction Record should identify the applicable PMES-governed Prediction Methodology or methodology version where available.

22. Methodology State at Formation

PRFM preserves the methodology state that actually applied when the prediction was formed.

A future methodology upgrade does not alter that historical state.

Future Methodology ≠ Historical Methodology

23. Prediction Basis Reference

The applicable PBAS-governed Prediction Basis should remain linkable to the Initial Historical Prediction State.

24. Basis Time Context

The record should preserve when relevant Prediction Basis information was available.

This helps prevent later information from being retrospectively attributed to the original prediction.

25. Assumption State

Material PAS-governed assumptions applicable at formation should remain identifiable.

26. Assumption Snapshot

Where assumptions are versioned separately, PRFM may preserve a reference to the applicable Assumption Snapshot rather than duplicating the entire assumption structure.

27. Formation Reference

The record should preserve the PFOS-governed Prediction Formation state or applicable reference.

PRFM does not reproduce PFOS mechanics.

28. Probability State

Where probability is present, the original probability should remain historically recorded.

Example:

Probability at Record Formation: 42%

This value must not later be replaced by:

Final Probability: 91%

29. Uncertainty State

Applicable uncertainty representation should remain associated with the initial prediction state.

30. Confidence State

Where confidence is separately represented, it should remain distinguishable from probability.

Probability ≠ Confidence

31. Range State

Where the prediction contains a range, the original range should remain recorded.

32. Conditionality State

Conditional predictions should preserve their original conditions.

Example:

The condition is part of the historical predictive meaning.

33. Scenario State

Scenario-specific predictions should preserve the scenario under which they were formed.

34. Feasibility Reference

Where PFES feasibility governance materially applies, the applicable feasibility state may be referenced.

35. Collective Prediction Reference

Retrospective Record ≠ Contemporaneous Prediction Record

Historical Freeze means that the initial state becomes an identifiable historical state once subsequent material change occurs.

92. Governance Logic

PRFM applies the following operational logic:

Has a sufficiently formed Governed Prediction reached a state requiring historical preservation?

If no:

→ Prediction Record Formation is not yet required.

If yes:

→ establish Prediction Record Identity.

Is the original predictive state sufficiently identifiable?

If no:

→ classify Record Formation as incomplete or retrospective.

If yes:

→ preserve Prediction Content Snapshot.

Can formation time and record creation time be established?

If yes:

→ preserve both where materially different.

If no:

→ preserve temporal qualification.

Are Prediction Object, Horizon, Boundary, Methodology, Basis, Assumptions, Probability, Uncertainty, and Predictor Context available where applicable?

→ preserve available governed references.

Are material elements missing?

If yes:

→ identify Historical Gaps.

Do not invent values.

Was the record created after outcome knowledge became available?

If yes:

→ classify as retrospective where appropriate.

Was the record imported from another system?

If yes:

→ preserve original origin and lineage.

Do not create false new origin.

Does a potentially identical Prediction Record already exist?

If yes:

→ evaluate duplication and linkage.

Is sufficient historical state available?

If yes:

→ establish:

Governed Initial Prediction Record

If partially:

→ establish qualified record state.

Then:

→ freeze the initial historical state against silent replacement;

→ enable subsequent version continuity.

Canonical transition:

Governed Prediction → Historical Anchoring → Initial Prediction Record → Version-Ready Prediction Record

97. Use Case 1 — A Company Claims It Predicted a Market Collapse Before It Happened

An investment firm publishes a report after a major market collapse stating:

“Our forecasting system predicted this event with 82% probability.”

The firm presents a database entry showing:

82% probability

but the entry was created after the market collapse.

Earlier internal documents contain several different forecasts, but it is unclear which one represented the actual prediction before the outcome became known.

The critical governance question is:

Did a contemporaneous Governed Prediction Record containing the 82% probability actually exist before the outcome, or was the record reconstructed retrospectively?

PRFM examines:

Prediction Record origin; Prediction Formation Time; Record Creation Time; source reference; probability state; methodology version; Prediction Basis timing; predictor identity; contemporaneous evidence.

If the record was formed after the outcome:

Retrospective Prediction Record

may be the legitimate classification.

The prediction may still have existed earlier.

But the later record must not automatically be treated as equivalent to a contemporaneous historical anchor.

Retrospective Record ≠ Contemporaneous Prediction Record

Outcome Knowledge ≠ Permission to Manufacture Prediction History

98. Use Case 2 — An AI Model Is Upgraded and the Original Prediction Context Disappears

An enterprise AI system predicts:

65% probability of a critical supply disruption within 90 days.

The prediction is used in operational planning.

Two weeks later, the company upgrades:

Model v4.1 → Model v5.0

The new system recalculates the event at:

38%

and replaces the previous value in the operational database.

Months later, management asks:

“What prediction was actually available when the supply decision was made?”

The database now contains only:

38% — Model v5.0

Without PRFM, the original predictive state may no longer be reconstructable.

A governed PRFM record would have preserved:

Prediction Record ID

Prediction: Supply disruption within 90 days

Probability: 65%

Model: v4.1

Prediction Horizon: 90 days

Prediction Formation Time

Record Creation Time

Methodology Reference

Prediction Basis Reference

Initial Lifecycle State

When Model v5.0 later creates a revised predictive state, the original record remains historically anchored.

The critical distinction is:

New Model Output ≠ Replacement of Historical Prediction State

The later PRCS modules can then preserve:

v4.1 Prediction State → v5.0 Prediction State → Subsequent History

rather than leaving only the latest database value.

99. Canonical Boundary

PRFM governs the formation of the initial historically persistent Prediction Record from a sufficiently formed Governed Prediction. It establishes the Historical Anchor, Prediction Record Identity, temporal origin, Prediction Content Snapshot, applicable methodology, Prediction Basis, assumption, probability, uncertainty, horizon, boundary, predictor, source, lifecycle, provenance, sufficiency, and integrity context necessary for subsequent historical continuity. It does not form the underlying prediction, determine predictive methodology, validate predictive quality, determine Operational Reality, assess Predictive Performance, establish legal ownership, or govern later version transitions, complete history preservation, record-state transitions, or lifecycle re-entry.

100. Canonical Closing Statement

Prediction history does not begin when someone later asks what was predicted. It begins when the prediction first becomes historically identifiable.

PRFM creates that anchor before revisions, new information, model upgrades, operational use, or outcome knowledge can blur the original predictive state.

A prediction can change a thousand times after formation. Governance must still be able to show exactly where its history began.
