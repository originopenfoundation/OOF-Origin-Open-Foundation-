1. Canonical Definition

The Prediction Object & Purpose Standard (POPS) governs the establishment of the object, purpose, target, horizon, and predictive boundary that define what a prediction is intended to address before predictive methodology, informational basis, assumptions, probability, uncertainty, feasibility, or prediction formation are applied.

POPS establishes the initial governed identity and scope of a Prediction Object so that subsequent predictive activity refers to a sufficiently defined future or unresolved outcome, state, condition, event, value, behavior, or development.

A prediction cannot become methodologically governable unless what is being predicted, why it is being predicted, what outcome or state is targeted, over what horizon it applies, and where its predictive boundary begins and ends are sufficiently established.

2. Operational Role

POPS establishes the entry point into the PGA™ Prediction Lifecycle.

Its role is to transform an intended subject of prediction into a sufficiently bounded Prediction Object capable of entering subsequent prediction governance.

POPS does not determine how the prediction will be made, what information will support it, what probability will be assigned, whether the predicted outcome is feasible, or whether the prediction will ultimately be correct.

It establishes what predictive governance is being applied to and for what defined purpose .

3. Core Question

What exactly is being predicted, for what purpose, over what horizon, and within what predictive boundary?

4. PGA™ Governance Flow

→ Prediction Object & Purpose → Prediction Methodology → Prediction Basis → Prediction Assumptions → Prediction Formation → Probability & Uncertainty → Prediction Feasibility → Collective Prediction where applicable → Communication & Reliance → Monitoring & Revision → Outcome & Performance → Prediction Record & Continuity

Current Position: Prediction Object & Purpose

POPS is the first Parent Standard in the PGA™ Governance Flow.

Its governed output establishes the object and predictive frame upon which subsequent PGA™ governance depends.

5. Governed Space

POPS governs the Prediction Object & Predictive Purpose space.

This includes governance of:

Prediction Object identity; predictive purpose; prediction target; prediction horizon; predictive boundary; relationship between the Prediction Object and the intended predictive outcome; distinction between the governed Prediction Object and adjacent or related objects; continuity of the defined predictive frame as the prediction proceeds into subsequent governance spaces.

The governed space begins when an unresolved or future condition becomes subject to intentional predictive treatment.

It ends when the Prediction Object, purpose, target, horizon, and boundary are sufficiently established for the prediction to enter Prediction Methodology governance.

6. Prediction Object

A Prediction Object is the specifically defined future or unresolved outcome, state, condition, event, value, behavior, development, or other predictive subject to which governed predictive activity is directed.

The Prediction Object must be sufficiently identifiable to distinguish it from related but materially different predictive objects.

A broad topic does not by itself establish a Prediction Object.

Examples such as a market, organization, technology, election, weather system, asset, population, or economy describe domains or subjects. A governed Prediction Object requires sufficient specification of what within that domain is actually being predicted.

Subject of Interest ≠ Prediction Object

7. Predictive Purpose

Every governed Prediction Object must have a defined predictive purpose.

Predictive purpose establishes why the prediction is being produced and provides the governance context necessary to interpret subsequent methodology, assumptions, uncertainty, communication, and reliance.

The same Prediction Object may legitimately support different predictions where the predictive purposes materially differ.

A prediction intended for exploratory analysis may not have the same governance requirements or reliance implications as a prediction intended to support operational action, resource allocation, automated execution, public communication, risk management, or consequential decision-making.

Same Prediction Object ≠ Same Predictive Purpose

8. Prediction Target

The Prediction Target establishes the specific outcome, state, condition, event, value, behavior, or development against which the prediction is directed.

The target must be sufficiently defined to preserve the meaning of the prediction throughout its lifecycle and to support subsequent association with an applicable governed outcome.

A prediction whose target materially changes without governance cannot be treated as an unchanged prediction.

Prediction Object ≠ Prediction Target

The Prediction Object identifies what is under predictive governance.

The Prediction Target identifies the specific predicted outcome or condition concerning that object.

9. Prediction Horizon

Every governed prediction must establish the temporal horizon to which the prediction applies where time is material to predictive meaning.

A prediction concerning the same object and target may represent a materially different prediction when its horizon changes.

The Prediction Horizon establishes the temporal frame within which the predicted outcome, state, condition, event, value, behavior, or development is expected, assessed, or otherwise governed.

Same Target + Different Horizon ≠ Same Prediction

Prediction Horizon does not determine probability, feasibility, timing evidence, or actual outcome occurrence.

Those matters belong to their applicable governance spaces.

10. Predictive Boundary

The Predictive Boundary establishes what is included within the governed predictive proposition and what remains outside it.

A sufficiently defined boundary prevents adjacent outcomes, conditions, populations, locations, variables, time periods, interpretations, or domains from being silently incorporated into or substituted for the original prediction.

Predictive boundaries must remain sufficiently stable to preserve the identity of the Prediction Object throughout subsequent PGA™ governance.

A material boundary change may require treatment as a changed Prediction Object or another governed prediction state rather than being represented as continuity of the original prediction.

Related Outcome ≠ Governed Prediction Target

Expanded Boundary ≠ Original Prediction

11. Prediction Object Identity

A governed Prediction Object must retain sufficient conceptual identity to remain distinguishable throughout the Prediction Lifecycle.

Prediction Object identity provides the reference point connecting:

Purpose → Methodology → Prediction Basis → Assumptions → Formation → Probability & Uncertainty → Feasibility → Communication → Revision → Outcome Association → Performance → Historical Record

Changes occurring later in the lifecycle must therefore remain attributable to the Prediction Object to which they apply.

Prediction Object identity does not establish identity governance for persons, organizations, systems, assets, or other external entities.

Such identity governance remains outside POPS.

12. Predictive Specificity

A Prediction Object must possess sufficient specificity for meaningful predictive governance.

Insufficient specificity may prevent legitimate determination of:

applicable methodology; relevant Prediction Basis; material assumptions; meaningful probability; uncertainty boundaries; feasibility; appropriate reliance; revision conditions; outcome association; predictive performance.

Specificity must be sufficient for the governed predictive purpose without artificially narrowing the Prediction Object beyond what the prediction legitimately claims.

Predictive Specificity ≠ Artificial Precision

13. Object–Purpose Alignment

The Prediction Object and Predictive Purpose must remain methodologically compatible.

A predictive purpose must not silently expand the meaning of a Prediction Object beyond what the object and target were established to support.

Likewise, a narrowly defined prediction must not later be relied upon as though it answered a materially broader predictive question.

Prediction Availability ≠ Fitness for Every Predictive Purpose

Object–Purpose Alignment establishes the governance foundation for later reliance boundaries without itself governing reliance.

14. Target–Horizon Alignment

The Prediction Target and Prediction Horizon must form a coherent predictive proposition.

Where the meaning, feasibility, or evaluability of the target materially depends upon time, the horizon becomes part of the governed predictive frame.

Changing the horizon may alter the predictive proposition even where the target wording remains unchanged.

The target and horizon must therefore remain sufficiently associated to allow subsequent predictive states to refer to the same governed proposition.

15. Boundary Stability

The Predictive Boundary must remain identifiable throughout the Prediction Lifecycle.

Boundary stability does not require that predictions remain immutable.

It requires that material changes to the predictive frame are not concealed as though the original Prediction Object remained unchanged.

Where the predictive boundary materially changes, subsequent PGA™ governance must be capable of distinguishing:

Original Predictive Frame → Changed Predictive Frame

Boundary stability therefore protects prediction continuity without preventing legitimate prediction evolution.

16. Entry Conditions

A Prediction Object may enter POPS governance when:

a future or unresolved outcome, state, condition, event, value, behavior, or development has become subject to predictive treatment; a prediction is intended to be methodologically formed; an existing predictive claim requires formal governance; a recurring predictive process requires a governed Prediction Object; a human, organizational, computational, AI, autonomous, market, or hybrid predictive process requires a defined object and purpose.

The existence of an expectation, belief, speculation, scenario, simulation, estimate, or opinion does not automatically establish a governed Prediction Object.

17. Exit Conditions

A Prediction Object may proceed from POPS into the Prediction Methodology Standard (PMES) when its:

Prediction Object; Predictive Purpose; Prediction Target; Prediction Horizon where applicable; and Predictive Boundary

are sufficiently established to support methodological selection and construction.

POPS does not establish that an appropriate predictive methodology exists.

It establishes the predictive frame against which methodology can subsequently be evaluated.

18. Governance Requirements

A governed Prediction Object must:

1. be sufficiently identifiable; 2. have a defined predictive purpose; 3. have a sufficiently specified Prediction Target; 4. establish an applicable Prediction Horizon where temporally relevant; 5. establish a Predictive Boundary; 6. distinguish the governed prediction from materially different adjacent predictions;

7. preserve sufficient object continuity for subsequent lifecycle governance; 8. prevent material changes in object, purpose, target, horizon, or boundary from being silently represented as an unchanged predictive proposition; 9. remain sufficiently defined to support subsequent methodology, basis, assumption, formation, probability, feasibility, reliance, revision, outcome, performance, and record governance; 10. preserve applicable cross-architecture boundaries.

20. Governance Outputs

POPS produces a governed predictive frame capable of supplying subsequent PGA™ standards with:

an identifiable Prediction Object; a defined Predictive Purpose; a specified Prediction Target; an applicable Prediction Horizon; a defined Predictive Boundary; an established relationship among these elements; a governed basis for Prediction Object continuity.

The principal architecture-level output is:

Governed Prediction Object & Purpose

This output becomes the primary input into PMES — Prediction Methodology Standard .

23. Cross-Architecture Boundaries

POPS governs the predictive object and predictive frame.

It does not assume governance ownership over the underlying object being predicted.

Where the Prediction Object concerns Operational Reality, ORA™ retains governance ownership of Operational Reality.

Where identity, origin, ownership, authority, or provenance are material, OBIDENITY® retains their governance ownership.

Where Value is predicted, VFM® retains governance ownership of the Value Lifecycle.

Where a Tax-Relevant Event is predicted, TREGA™ retains governance ownership of the Tax-Relevant Event Lifecycle.

Where simulation contributes to prediction formation, SIMULOS® retains governance ownership of simulation.

Where cognition or reasoning contributes to predictive activity, the applicable cognition architecture retains governance ownership of cognition.

POPS governs the prediction about the governed object , not the external governed object itself.

24. Standard Boundary

POPS begins when a future or unresolved outcome, state, condition, event, value, behavior, or development requires establishment as a governed Prediction Object.

It governs the establishment and coherence of the Prediction Object, Predictive Purpose, Prediction Target, Prediction Horizon, and Predictive Boundary.

POPS ends when that predictive frame is sufficiently established to enter Prediction Methodology governance.

POPS does not govern predictive methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, prediction construction, probability, uncertainty, confidence, feasibility, collective prediction, communication, reliance, monitoring, revision, outcome determination, predictive performance, or Prediction Record mechanics.

Canonical Closing Statement

A prediction cannot be governed until what is being predicted, why it is being predicted, what outcome it targets, when that prediction applies, and where its predictive boundary ends are sufficiently established.

POPS establishes the governed object and purpose from which the PGA™ Prediction Lifecycle can legitimately begin.
