Governed Space

PRSM governs the representation and signaling of a Governed Prediction when predictive meaning is converted into a form intended to be perceived, transmitted, displayed, exchanged, or consumed by humans, organizations, AI systems, autonomous agents, software systems, or machine-readable processes.

It governs whether the representation preserves the material predictive meaning of the underlying Governed Prediction.

Canonical Definition

The Prediction Representation & Signaling Module (PRSM) defines the operational governance requirements by which a Governed Prediction is converted into a communicable or machine-consumable representation without materially altering, overstating, suppressing, fragmenting, or detaching the predictive meaning established through the preceding PGA™ governance lifecycle.

PRSM governs the relationship between:

Underlying Governed Prediction → Predictive Representation → Predictive Signal

It ensures that the representation remains traceably associated with the prediction from which it originated and that material predictive qualifications survive the transformation into text, numbers, categories, visualizations, alerts, scores, rankings, API outputs, machine-readable objects, or other signaling forms.

Representation may simplify a prediction. It must not silently redefine it.

Operational Role

PRSM is the first operational module within PCRS.

Its role is to determine how the governed predictive object may be represented before downstream interpretation, contextualization, reliance, or publication occurs.

The focused operational question is:

How can a Governed Prediction be transformed into a human- or machine-consumable signal without materially changing what the prediction actually establishes?

PRSM does not determine whether a recipient interprets the representation correctly.

It governs whether the representation itself provides a legitimate basis from which correct interpretation remains possible.

Internal Module Flow

Prediction Representation & Signaling → Prediction-Led Decision Interpretation → Prediction Interpretation Boundary → Reliance Legitimacy & Context → Prediction Use Boundary

Module Operational Space

PRSM operates wherever predictive information is transformed into a representation.

This includes:

written forecasts; probability statements; prediction scores; dashboards; charts; risk indicators; traffic-light classifications; confidence displays; prediction rankings; alerts; notifications; executive summaries; market-implied prediction displays; model outputs; AI-generated summaries; agent messages; API responses; structured prediction records; machine-readable prediction objects; decision-support interfaces; automated system signals.

The representation medium does not remove the governance requirement.

Different Interface ≠ Different Prediction Meaning

Module Function

PRSM transforms:

Governed Prediction + Material Prediction Context + Applicable Governance Qualifications + Representation Method → Governed Predictive Representation

Its primary output is:

Governed Predictive Representation

The output establishes what predictive information has been represented, how it has been represented, what material context accompanies it, what information has been compressed or omitted, and whether the representation remains faithful to the underlying Governed Prediction.

1. Underlying Prediction Reference

Every governed representation must remain associated with an identifiable underlying Governed Prediction.

The representation should permit appropriate reconstruction of:

Prediction Object; predicted outcome; Prediction Horizon; Predictive Boundary; prediction version; prediction state; relevant methodology reference; applicable governance qualifications.

A screenshot, dashboard number, API value, or textual statement should not become an orphan predictive object detached from its governed origin.

Predictive Representation ≠ Independent Prediction

2. Representation Fidelity

Representation Fidelity describes the degree to which the communicated representation preserves the substantive meaning of the underlying prediction.

A representation is not faithful merely because its central number is correct.

For example:

Underlying prediction:

72% probability of commercial deployment within 18 months, conditional upon regulatory approval and infrastructure availability.

Representation:

72% chance of deployment.

The probability value has been preserved.

The predictive meaning has not necessarily been preserved.

PRSM therefore evaluates fidelity across the complete material predictive context.

Numerical Fidelity ≠ Meaning Fidelity

3. Material Predictive Context

Material Predictive Context consists of information whose omission could materially alter how the prediction is understood.

Depending on the prediction, this may include:

Prediction Object; predicted outcome; Prediction Horizon; Predictive Boundary; methodology context; Prediction Basis qualification; material assumptions; Probability & Uncertainty; confidence qualification; predictive range; conditionality; feasibility state; collective-prediction qualification; current prediction status; relevant revision state.

Not every representation must reproduce the complete prediction record.

But material meaning must remain preserved or reliably accessible.

Representation Compression ≠ Context Elimination

4. Representation Completeness

Representation Completeness evaluates whether sufficient predictive information is present for the intended representation context.

Completeness is contextual.

A detailed regulatory report may require substantially more predictive context than a compact operational dashboard.

A mobile alert may legitimately contain less information than the underlying prediction record.

However:

Limited Display Space ≠ Permission to Remove Material Meaning

Where information cannot reasonably be displayed directly, the representation may provide a traceable route to the governed context.

5. Prediction Compression

Predictions are frequently compressed.

Compression may transform:

Full Prediction Record → Executive Summary

or:

Probability Distribution → Single Percentage

or:

Detailed Forecast → Risk Category

or:

Prediction Record → API Field

Compression is not inherently improper.

PRSM governs whether compression changes predictive meaning.

A legitimate compression preserves the material relationship between the simplified representation and the underlying prediction.

6. Lossy Prediction Representation

A Lossy Prediction Representation occurs when predictive information is removed during representation.

Loss may be acceptable where removed information is not material to the intended use.

Loss becomes governance-relevant when it removes information necessary to understand what the prediction actually establishes.

Examples include removal of:

material conditions; Prediction Horizon; geographic boundary; uncertainty; feasibility restriction; relevant assumption; collective-prediction qualification.

Information Reduction ≠ Meaning Reduction by Default

But:

Material Information Removal → Potential Meaning Distortion

7. Probability Representation

Where probability is represented, PRSM requires the probability to remain associated with sufficient methodological meaning.

A number such as:

68%

must not automatically be treated as a complete predictive representation.

Its meaning may depend on:

predicted event; time horizon; conditions; probability methodology; uncertainty; prediction state.

PRSM preserves PPUS distinctions during representation.

Probability ≠ Certainty

Probability ≠ Confidence

Same Probability Value ≠ Same Predictive Meaning

8. Precision Signaling

Numerical precision can create perceived authority.

For example:

63.74%

may appear more reliable than:

approximately 64%

even where the underlying methodology does not justify that level of precision.

PRSM governs whether displayed precision exceeds the meaningful precision supported by the underlying prediction.

Numerical Precision ≠ Predictive Precision

More Decimal Places ≠ More Predictive Knowledge

9. Uncertainty Signaling

Material uncertainty should not disappear when a prediction becomes a representation.

Where relevant, uncertainty may be represented through:

ranges; intervals; categories; distributions; qualitative qualifications; scenario bands; uncertainty indicators.

The representation method may vary.

The governance requirement remains:

Uncertainty Must Not Disappear Merely Because the Interface Prefers a Single Number.

10. Confidence Signaling

Where confidence is represented, it must remain distinguishable from probability.

A prediction may have:

High Probability + Low Confidence

or:

Moderate Probability + High Confidence

depending on the underlying predictive conditions.

A representation that collapses these concepts may materially distort prediction meaning.

Confidence ≠ Probability

Confidence ≠ Certainty

11. Conditionality Signaling

Conditional predictions must remain identifiable as conditional.

Underlying prediction:

80% probability if regulatory authorization is granted by March 2027.

Representation:

80% probability.

The number remains identical.

The prediction does not.

PRSM therefore requires material predictive conditions to remain visible, attached, referenced, or otherwise reconstructable.

Conditional Prediction ≠ Unconditional Prediction

12. Prediction Horizon Representation

Prediction Horizon is a fundamental component of predictive meaning.

60% probability within 30 days

is not equivalent to:

60% probability within five years.

A representation that removes the horizon may create an ambiguous or materially different predictive claim.

Same Probability + Different Horizon ≠ Same Prediction

13. Predictive Boundary Representation

Predictions may apply only within specific:

geographic areas; populations; systems; organizations; markets; technologies; operational environments.

PRSM governs whether these boundaries remain sufficiently represented.

A prediction for one jurisdiction must not silently become a global prediction through representation.

Local Prediction ≠ Universal Prediction

14. Assumption Signaling

Material assumptions may determine whether a prediction remains meaningful.

Where removal of an assumption would materially change interpretation, the assumption must remain represented or accessible.

PRSM does not govern the assumption itself.

PAS owns Predictive Assumptions.

PRSM governs whether material assumption context survives representation.

Assumption Omission ≠ Assumption Removal from the Prediction

15. Feasibility Signaling

PFES may determine that a predicted outcome is:

Feasible; Conditionally Feasible; Feasibility Restricted; Feasibility Uncertain; Infeasible; Unable to Determine.

PRSM prevents representation from silently strengthening that state.

Conditionally Feasible → Feasible

is not legitimate simplification when the condition is material.

Feasibility Qualification ≠ Optional Decoration

16. Collective Prediction Signaling

Where the prediction originates through CPGS, its collective nature may require material qualification.

A representation such as:

“The market predicts 76%.”

may conceal:

influence concentration; source dependence; aggregation methodology; limited representativeness; effective predictor concentration.

PRSM does not redo CPGS analysis.

It preserves material CPGS qualifications during representation.

Collective Prediction ≠ Unqualified Crowd Wisdom

17. Market-Implied Prediction Representation

Prediction markets may generate market-implied probability signals.

These signals must not automatically be represented as:

population belief;

scientific probability; expert consensus; independent predictive consensus.

Market-Implied Probability ≠ Population Belief

Market Price ≠ Governed Probability Estimate by Default

The representation must preserve what the signal actually represents.

18. Categorical Representation

Predictions may be transformed into categories such as:

Low · Medium · High

or:

Green · Amber · Red

or:

Unlikely · Possible · Likely

Categorization can improve usability.

It can also destroy important predictive distinctions.

PRSM requires category thresholds and meanings to remain governed and traceable.

Category Label ≠ Self-Explanatory Predictive Meaning

19. Risk Labels

Prediction and risk are related but distinct concepts.

A prediction may contribute to risk assessment.

It does not automatically constitute a complete risk determination.

A prediction represented as:

High Risk

may therefore require governance clarification regarding how the prediction was transformed into that label.

Prediction ≠ Risk Determination

20. Visual Representation

Charts, gauges, maps, colors, icons, rankings, and other visual structures can materially influence interpretation.

Visual prominence can create predictive significance even when no explicit textual claim is made.

PRSM therefore governs:

scale selection; visual emphasis; comparative presentation;

threshold visibility; uncertainty display; qualification visibility; relative prominence.

Visual Emphasis ≠ Predictive Evidence

21. Qualification Visibility

A material qualification should not be technically present yet operationally invisible.

For example:

87%

may appear prominently at the top of a dashboard while:

“subject to substantial uncertainty and conditional infrastructure availability”

appears behind several interface layers.

PRSM evaluates whether material qualification is reasonably available relative to the predictive claim.

Qualification Existence ≠ Qualification Visibility

22. Representation Hierarchy

Interfaces naturally create hierarchy.

Some predictive elements become primary.

Others become secondary.

PRSM governs whether that hierarchy creates a materially misleading predictive impression.

Primary display elements should not systematically amplify certainty while relegating uncertainty and conditions to inaccessible positions.

23. Alert Signaling

Predictions may generate alerts such as:

Failure Likely

Flood Expected

Demand Surge Predicted

High Probability Event

Alerts frequently compress complex predictive structures.

PRSM requires that alert terminology remain proportional to the underlying prediction.

Alert Urgency ≠ Predictive Certainty

24. Ranking Representation

Predictions may be used to rank:

projects; opportunities; risks; individuals; locations; systems; scenarios.

Ranking creates relative predictive meaning.

PRSM governs whether ranking faithfully represents what the underlying predictions legitimately support.

Higher Rank ≠ Certain Superior Outcome

25. Comparative Prediction Representation

Two predictions should not be visually or numerically compared as equivalent unless their relevant predictive structures are sufficiently compatible.

Potential differences include:

Prediction Object; methodology; horizon; assumptions; uncertainty; feasibility; predictive boundary.

Comparable Numbers ≠ Comparable Predictions

26. Human-Readable Representation

Human-readable predictive communication should provide sufficient semantic context for a reasonable recipient to understand the prediction without silently acquiring stronger meaning.

This may involve:

clear terminology; visible conditions; understandable uncertainty; accessible prediction scope; explicit horizon; traceable qualification.

PRSM does not require one universal presentation format.

It requires preservation of governed meaning.

27. Machine-Readable Representation

Machine-readable predictions may be consumed without human interpretation.

PRSM therefore requires machine-readable representations to preserve sufficient structured context.

Potential fields may include:

Prediction ID; Prediction Object; outcome definition; probability; uncertainty; Prediction Horizon; Predictive Boundary; assumptions reference; feasibility state; methodology reference; version; governance state.

Machine Readability ≠ Machine Interpretability

28. API Prediction Signaling

An API returning only:

probability = 0.81

may technically transmit a prediction value while failing to transmit sufficient predictive meaning.

PRSM governs whether machine interfaces preserve the contextual structure required for legitimate downstream interpretation.

A prediction should not become a context-free numerical instruction merely because it crossed an API boundary.

29. AI-Generated Prediction Summaries

AI systems may summarize complex predictions into natural language.

The summary may inadvertently:

remove uncertainty; strengthen wording; eliminate conditions; generalize scope; change horizon; convert probability into expectation.

PRSM requires AI-generated summaries to remain traceably faithful to the governed prediction.

Language Fluency ≠ Predictive Fidelity

30. Agent-to-Agent Prediction Signaling

Autonomous agents may exchange predictions directly.

One agent may produce:

Probability = 0.64

and another may use it as a decision input.

PRSM governs the representation passed between agents.

Authority to act upon that signal belongs to the relevant downstream governance space.

Predictive Signal ≠ Execution Permission

31. Representation Transformation Chain

A prediction may pass through multiple representations:

Prediction Record → API → Dashboard → Executive Summary → Media Report → Social Post

Each transformation creates an opportunity for meaning drift.

PRSM requires material transformations to remain traceable where governance significance warrants it.

32. Representation Drift

Representation Drift occurs when predictive meaning changes through successive representation or redistribution.

The original prediction may remain unchanged while downstream representations gradually become stronger, broader, or more certain.

PRSM identifies and governs this transformation risk.

Repeated Representation ≠ Stable Meaning by Default

33. Prediction Version Signaling

Where a prediction has been revised or superseded, the representation should not silently continue presenting an obsolete prediction as current.

PRSM therefore requires appropriate prediction-version association.

Visible Prediction ≠ Current Prediction by Default

Superseded Prediction ≠ Deleted Prediction

Historical prediction representation remains legitimate when clearly identified as historical.

34. Representation State

PRSM may classify a predictive representation as:

Representation Ready

Conditionally Representation Ready

Representation Qualification Required

Representation Restricted

Materially Distorted

Insufficiently Contextualized

Unable to Determine

The state describes the governance condition of the representation.

It does not determine whether the underlying prediction is correct.

35. Representation Qualification

Where full predictive context cannot or should not be directly displayed, a representation may carry qualification.

Qualification may identify:

omitted material context; conditional applicability; uncertainty limitations; feasibility restrictions; collective-prediction conditions; representation limitations; requirement to consult the underlying prediction record.

Qualification allows controlled simplification without pretending that simplification is complete prediction meaning.

36. Representation Traceability

A Governed Predictive Representation should remain traceable to:

Representation → Prediction Version → Governed Prediction Record

Where applicable, traceability may also identify:

transformation method; representation version; responsible system; timestamp; omitted fields; qualification state.

37. Representation History

Material representation changes should remain reconstructable where required.

A prediction may remain unchanged while its public or operational representation changes.

That distinction matters.

Prediction Revision ≠ Representation Revision

Representation Revision ≠ Prediction Revision

38. Representation Governance Across Channels

The same prediction may appear in:

technical reports; executive dashboards;

mobile applications; public websites; machine APIs; internal AI systems.

PRSM permits channel-specific representation.

It does not permit channel-specific predictive truth.

Different Channel ≠ Different Underlying Prediction

39. Representation Proportionality

The level of representation detail may be proportional to:

audience; consequence; complexity; reliance potential; available interface; prediction materiality.

Higher-consequence environments may require stronger contextual preservation.

However, proportionality must not become a justification for material distortion.

41. Governance Logic

PRSM applies the following operational logic:

If a representation preserves the material predictive meaning required for its context → Representation Ready.

If the representation is materially faithful but requires explicit qualification → Conditionally Representation Ready.

If material context is absent but can be restored or referenced → Representation Qualification Required.

If the representation would materially exceed what the underlying prediction supports → Representation Restricted.

If the representation materially changes the meaning of the underlying prediction → Materially Distorted.

If sufficient context is unavailable to establish legitimate predictive meaning → Insufficiently Contextualized or Unable to Determine.

46. Use Case 1 — A 91.37% Failure Prediction on an Industrial Dashboard

An industrial prediction system estimates:

91.37% probability of component failure within 45 days, conditional upon continued operation above a specified thermal threshold.

The underlying prediction also carries substantial uncertainty because the system has limited observations under extreme operating conditions.

The operational dashboard displays:

FAILURE RISK — 91.37%

The Prediction Horizon, thermal condition, and uncertainty qualification are hidden behind a secondary information panel.

Operators begin interpreting the display as:

“The component has a 91.37% chance of failing regardless of operating conditions.”

The critical governance question is:

Does the dashboard faithfully represent the Governed Prediction, or has visual and numerical compression materially strengthened its meaning?

PRSM identifies three governance problems:

material conditionality has lost prominence; uncertainty has become operationally invisible; numerical precision creates stronger perceived predictive precision than the underlying conditions justify.

The representation may therefore require:

Representation Qualification Required

or redesign before becoming:

Representation Ready

The underlying prediction has not changed.

Its representation has.

Numerical Fidelity ≠ Meaning Fidelity

More Decimal Places ≠ More Predictive Knowledge

47. Use Case 2 — AI API Converts a Conditional Prediction into a Context-Free Number

A forecasting service produces:

68% probability that a logistics corridor will experience material disruption within six months, assuming current geopolitical restrictions remain in place and seasonal shipping volumes remain within the modeled range.

The full Governed Prediction contains:

Prediction Object; six-month Prediction Horizon; geopolitical assumptions; shipping-volume conditions; uncertainty range; methodology reference; current prediction version.

A downstream API sends another AI agent only:

disruption_probability: 0.68

The receiving agent interprets the value as a complete predictive signal and begins reallocating logistics capacity.

The critical governance question is:

Can a machine-consumable probability remain a governed predictive representation when the contextual structure necessary to interpret that probability has been removed?

PRSM determines that the numerical value alone is insufficient.

The machine-readable representation should preserve or reference the material predictive context required for legitimate interpretation.

Machine Readability ≠ Machine Interpretability

Probability Value ≠ Complete Prediction

Predictive Signal ≠ Execution Permission

PRSM therefore converts the context-free signal into a structured Governed Predictive Representation before it proceeds to downstream interpretation and reliance governance.

48. Canonical Boundary

PRSM governs how a Governed Prediction is transformed into a human-readable or machine-readable representation while preserving its material predictive meaning, context, qualifications, horizon, boundary, uncertainty, conditionality, feasibility state, collective-prediction conditions, version association, and traceability. It does not determine the underlying prediction, methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, probability, feasibility, collective legitimacy, downstream interpretation, reliance legitimacy, decision authority, execution authority, prediction revision, Operational Reality, or predictive performance.

PSDM governs source diversity.

PDVM governs Prediction Dependence & Variation.

PAGM governs Prediction Aggregation.

PICM governs Predictive Influence & Concentration.

CPRM governs Collective Prediction Representation within CPGS.

PRSM begins after the relevant Governed Prediction has been established and governs its transition into representation within PCRS.

Subsequent PCRS modules govern interpretation and reliance.

49. Canonical Closing Statement

A prediction can remain methodologically correct while its representation becomes methodologically misleading.

PRSM preserves the horizon, conditions, uncertainty, boundaries, qualifications, and predictive meaning that can disappear when a complex prediction becomes a number, chart, alert, sentence, or machine signal.

The form may become simpler. The prediction must not become stronger.
