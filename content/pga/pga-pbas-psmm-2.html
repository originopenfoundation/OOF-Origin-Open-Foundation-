Predictive Source Mapping

Canonical Definition

Predictive Source Mapping Module (PSMM) defines the governance conditions under which identified Prediction Inputs are associated with their material sources, origins, intermediaries, dependencies, informational relationships, and relevant proximity to the Operational Reality from which the predicted outcome may emerge.

PSMM establishes the governed source structure beneath Prediction Basis.

It enables PGA™ to distinguish:

multiple inputs

from:

multiple independent sources

and:

predictive capability

from:

informational advantage or proximity to the reality being predicted.

PSMM does not determine whether a source is evidentially valid, whether its information is predictively relevant, whether the Prediction Basis is sufficient, or whether source proximity constitutes misconduct.

Its function is to make the source relationships behind the prediction identifiable.

Operational Role

PSMM serves as the second internal governance space of the Prediction Basis Standard (PBAS) .

It receives:

Identified Prediction Inputs

from:

PIIM — Prediction Input Identification Module

and establishes the source relationships underlying those inputs.

The module asks:

Where did the identified Prediction Inputs actually come from, how are their sources related, and how close are those sources to the information, actors, decisions, processes, or conditions from which the predicted outcome may emerge?

The governed progression is:

Identified Prediction Inputs → Source Identification → Source-Origin Mapping → Dependency Mapping → Predictor–Reality Proximity Mapping → Governed Predictive Source Map

The resulting source map becomes an input to:

SRVM — Signal Relevance Module.

Internal Module Flow

Prediction Input Identification → → Predictive Source Mapping → Signal Relevance → Input Sufficiency → Prediction Basis Traceability

Module Function

PSMM prevents organizations from assuming that the number of visible Prediction Inputs represents the number or diversity of underlying information sources.

A predictive system may receive:

100 inputs

that ultimately originate from:

one source.

Five forecasting systems may appear independent while all relying on the same upstream dataset.

Ten analysts may appear to provide independent judgments while all relying upon the same private briefing.

A prediction market may appear to represent distributed forecasting while a small number of highly informed or capital- concentrated participants materially determine the signal.

PSMM makes these relationships visible before PGA™ evaluates relevance, sufficiency, collective prediction legitimacy, or predictive performance.

Multiple Inputs ≠ Independent Sources

Predictive Source

A Predictive Source is an identifiable person, system, organization, mechanism, information origin, or other source from which a material Prediction Input originates or through which it materially reaches the predictive process.

Sources may include:

individuals; expert groups; organizations; databases; sensors; operational systems; AI systems; analytical models; simulation systems; prediction markets; public records; private information systems; research institutions; external services; market infrastructures; other identifiable information origins.

Immediate Source

The Immediate Source is the source from which the predictive system directly receives the Prediction Input.

For example:

Original Dataset → Research Firm → Analyst Report → Prediction System

The analyst report may be the Immediate Source.

It is not necessarily the originating source.

Immediate Source ≠ Original Source

Originating Source

The Originating Source represents the materially relevant origin from which the information emerged.

Identifying originating sources helps prevent transformed, republished, summarized, or redistributed information from appearing independent merely because it entered the prediction through different channels.

Intermediary Source

Information may pass through one or more intermediaries before becoming Prediction Input.

An intermediary may:

transmit; summarize; transform; classify; aggregate; interpret; filter; enrich; redistribute

information.

PSMM preserves material intermediary relationships where they affect understanding of source structure.

Source Chain

Where information passes through multiple sources, PSMM may represent the relationship as:

Originating Source → Intermediary Source → Immediate Source → Prediction Input

More complex structures may contain multiple intermediary layers.

The purpose is not unlimited provenance reconstruction.

The purpose is sufficient source mapping for Prediction Basis governance.

Source Lineage

Source Lineage preserves the material path through which information moved from origin toward Prediction Input.

It allows later governance to distinguish independent information from information that merely appears independent because it was redistributed or transformed.

Shared-Source Relationship

Two or more Prediction Inputs possess a Shared-Source Relationship where they materially derive from the same underlying informational source.

For example:

Source A → Report 1

Source A → Report 2

Source A → AI Summary

may produce three Prediction Inputs while preserving one materially shared informational origin.

Multiple Representations ≠ Multiple Independent Sources

Source Dependency

Source Dependency exists where one predictive source materially depends upon another source for the information it provides.

Dependency may be:

direct; indirect; partial; hierarchical; informational; analytical; operational.

PSMM makes dependency visible.

It does not automatically determine that dependent information lacks value.

Source Independence

Source independence must not be inferred merely because sources possess different names, organizations, interfaces, or delivery channels.

Meaningful independence requires absence of material underlying dependence relevant to the Prediction Basis.

PSMM does not certify universal independence.

It establishes the source relationships necessary for independence claims to be assessed.

Different Source Labels ≠ Independent Sources

Source Concentration

Prediction Basis may depend heavily upon a small number of underlying sources.

Source Concentration describes the degree to which informational support originates from limited source origins.

High concentration does not automatically invalidate Prediction Basis.

It must remain identifiable where material.

Source Quantity ≠ Source Diversity

Informational Concentration

Informational Concentration may exist even where numerous sources are technically present.

For example, dozens of news reports may reproduce the same original announcement.

The number of documents therefore does not necessarily represent informational diversity.

Document Count ≠ Information Diversity

Human Sources

Human sources may include:

experts; analysts; operators; decision-makers; witnesses; researchers; market participants; domain specialists.

PSMM maps their informational role without determining the correctness of their statements or allocating accountability.

Organizational Sources

Organizations may function as predictive sources through:

reports; internal records; forecasts; operational information; research; disclosures; private communications.

Where multiple outputs originate from one organization, organizational relationships may be relevant to source dependency.

Machine and AI Sources

Machine systems may provide Prediction Inputs directly or through other systems.

PSMM maps relationships such as:

Underlying Information → AI System → AI Output → Prediction Input

or:

Model A → Model B → Prediction System

AI-generated outputs must not automatically be treated as independent sources merely because they are generated by different interfaces.

Different AI Systems ≠ Independent Information Sources by Default

Simulation Sources

Simulation systems may produce Prediction Inputs.

PSMM preserves the simulation source relationship.

It does not govern the simulation itself.

Simulation Source ≠ Operational Reality Source

SIMULOS® retains governance of simulation.

Market Sources

Markets may produce:

prices; implied probabilities; volume; liquidity information; order-book states; sentiment indicators; prediction-market outputs.

PSMM maps the market as a source and, where material, the informational structure underlying the market signal.

A market signal may represent many participants while remaining highly concentrated in actual influence.

Market Participation ≠ Information Independence

Public-Information Sources

Public information may form part of Prediction Basis.

PSMM identifies its source relationships where material.

Multiple predictors using the same public information may produce different predictions without possessing independent informational foundations.

Non-Public-Information Sources

Non-public information may also form Prediction Basis.

PSMM preserves its source relationship where material.

It does not determine whether access or use was lawful.

Non-Public Information ≠ Misconduct by Default

Legal and compliance determinations remain outside PGA™.

Information Access Relationship

PSMM may identify whether a predictor or predictive source possesses:

ordinary access; privileged access; earlier access; exclusive access; operational access; decision-proximate access

to information materially related to the predicted outcome.

Access characteristics can materially affect interpretation of predictive capability.

Information Timing

Two predictors may receive the same information at different times.

Earlier access may create predictive advantage.

PSMM therefore preserves information-timing relationships where material.

Earlier Information Access ≠ Superior Prediction Methodology

Information Exclusivity

A source may possess information unavailable to other predictors.

Information exclusivity may materially affect predictive performance.

PSMM identifies the condition without determining whether the exclusivity is appropriate.

Exclusive Information ≠ Predictive Skill

Predictor–Reality Proximity

Predictor–Reality Proximity (PRP) represents the governed relationship between a predictor or predictive source and the information, actors, processes, authorities, systems, decisions, or operational conditions from which the predicted outcome may emerge or by which that outcome may be materially determined or influenced.

PRP provides PGA™ with a structured method for distinguishing ordinary forecasting from prediction occurring close to the mechanisms that generate the outcome.

Predictor–Reality Proximity Dimensions

Where relevant, PRP may consider:

information access; information timing; information exclusivity; organizational proximity; operational proximity; decision proximity; authority proximity; non-public information exposure; outcome knowledge; influence capability; control capability; materially relevant conflicts.

Not every prediction requires every dimension.

The relevant dimensions depend upon the predicted outcome and predictive context.

Predictor–Reality Proximity Continuum

Where useful, PSMM may classify predictive relationships along the following governance continuum:

Public-Information Predictor

↓

Information-Advantaged Predictor

↓

Decision-Proximate Predictor

↓

Outcome-Informed Predictor

↓

Outcome-Influencing Predictor

↓

Outcome-Controlling Predictor

The continuum describes relationships.

It does not determine wrongdoing.

High Predictor–Reality Proximity ≠ Misconduct

Public-Information Predictor

A Public-Information Predictor operates primarily from information broadly available to other participants.

This does not imply low predictive capability.

It describes the informational relationship to Operational Reality.

Information-Advantaged Predictor

An Information-Advantaged Predictor possesses materially superior, earlier, richer, or more exclusive information than ordinary predictors.

Such advantage may improve observed predictive performance independently of methodological capability.

Information Advantage ≠ Predictive Capability

Decision-Proximate Predictor

A Decision-Proximate Predictor possesses material proximity to people, systems, or processes capable of influencing or determining the predicted outcome.

Decision proximity does not establish authority.

Decision Proximity ≠ Decision Authority

Outcome-Informed Predictor

An Outcome-Informed Predictor possesses information that materially indicates or establishes the likely or actual outcome beyond what ordinary prediction would reveal.

This creates a fundamentally different informational condition from ordinary forecasting.

Outcome Knowledge ≠ Predictive Capability

Outcome-Influencing Predictor

An Outcome-Influencing Predictor possesses some ability to materially affect the conditions from which the predicted outcome emerges.

Influence may be direct or indirect.

Prediction Influence ≠ Outcome Control

PSMM maps the relationship.

PCRS and PMRS govern later implications for communication, reliance, and changing predictive conditions.

Outcome-Controlling Predictor

An Outcome-Controlling Predictor possesses sufficient authority or operational capability to materially determine the outcome within the relevant context.

A prediction produced under such conditions must not automatically be interpreted as demonstrating ordinary forecasting capability.

Outcome Control ≠ Predictive Skill

Outcome Knowledge

Where a source already possesses material knowledge of the outcome, that condition must remain distinguishable from prediction.

Knowing an outcome before others is not the same as predicting it better than others.

PSMM records the informational relationship.

It does not determine legal or ethical consequences.

Outcome Influence and Control

Influence and control must remain separate governance states.

A source may influence an outcome without controlling it.

Another may possess authority without exercising it.

Another may participate operationally while lacking decision authority.

Therefore:

Outcome Influence ≠ Outcome Control

Decision Proximity ≠ Decision Authority

Authority ≠ Exercised Control

Conflict Relationships

A source may possess interests connected to the predicted outcome.

Where materially relevant to understanding the source relationship, PSMM may preserve such conflict context.

It does not determine misconduct or allocate responsibility.

Potential Conflict ≠ Established Misconduct

Source Map Change

Source relationships may change over time.

Examples include:

new source introduction; source removal; dependency change; ownership change; changed information access; changed operational role; changed decision proximity; new influence capability.

A historical source map must not automatically be replaced by the current source map.

Current Source Relationship ≠ Historical Source Relationship

Source Mapping Sufficiency

Predictive Source Mapping is sufficiently governed when:

material sources are identifiable; immediate and originating sources are distinguishable where necessary; material intermediary relationships are visible; shared-source relationships can be identified; material source dependencies remain visible;

source concentration can be recognized; information-access relationships are identifiable where material; Predictor–Reality Proximity can be represented where relevant; outcome knowledge, influence, and control remain distinguishable; historical source relationships remain reconstructable.

PSMM does not determine signal relevance or Prediction Basis sufficiency.

PSMM requires that:

1. material Prediction Inputs be associated with identifiable sources where possible; 2. immediate source not automatically be treated as originating source; 3. material intermediary relationships remain visible; 4. shared-source relationships remain identifiable; 5. multiple inputs not automatically be treated as independent sources; 6. source dependency remain governable; 7. source quantity not be mistaken for source diversity; 8. AI, model, simulation, market, human, organizational, and operational sources remain distinguishable where material; 9. public and non-public informational relationships remain identifiable where relevant; 10. information timing and exclusivity remain governable where material; 11. Predictor–Reality Proximity be assessed where materially relevant; 12. information advantage remain distinguishable from predictive capability; 13. decision proximity remain distinguishable from decision authority; 14. outcome knowledge remain distinguishable from predictive capability; 15. outcome influence remain distinguishable from outcome control; 16. high Predictor–Reality Proximity not automatically establish misconduct; 17. material conflicts remain identifiable without premature determination; 18. source-map changes remain historically traceable.

Enterprise Application

PSMM enables organizations to understand the real informational structure beneath predictive systems.

This becomes especially important where predictions are produced from large networks of:

data providers + AI systems + external models + research services + experts + markets + operational systems + public information.

Without source mapping, organizations may mistake information duplication for confirmation, dependency for diversity, privileged access for predictive skill, or outcome proximity for forecasting capability.

PSMM therefore creates a governance layer between:

“How many inputs or predictors do we have?”

and:

“How many materially distinct informational sources actually support the prediction?”

Use Case 1 — Twenty Independent Forecasts That All Depend on the Same Source

An organization aggregates twenty forecasts from different analysts, AI systems, research providers, and external forecasting services.

The apparent diversity creates strong confidence because many different predictors reach similar conclusions.

Source mapping later reveals that:

twelve use the same commercial dataset; four rely upon reports derived from that dataset; two AI systems retrieve summaries of those reports; several analysts reference one another's forecasts.

The twenty predictions therefore contain substantially less informational independence than their visible number suggests.

The critical governance question is:

Can agreement among many predictive sources legitimately be interpreted as independent confirmation when those sources materially depend upon the same underlying information?

PSMM maps the shared origins and dependencies before later PGA™ governance determines relevance, collective prediction legitimacy, or reliance.

Predictor Count ≠ Independent Information Count

Use Case 2 — A Predictor Appears Exceptionally Accurate Because It Is Close to the Outcome

A prediction market repeatedly shows one participant making unusually accurate predictions about whether major corporate transactions will close.

The participant appears to possess exceptional predictive skill.

Further source mapping shows that the participant maintains operational relationships with individuals close to the relevant transactions and frequently receives information earlier than the wider market.

In some cases, the participant may also interact with people capable of influencing the final outcome.

The critical governance question is:

Does the participant's observed accuracy demonstrate superior forecasting capability, or does it materially reflect informational and operational proximity to the reality being predicted?

PSMM does not assume misconduct.

It establishes the relevant Predictor–Reality Proximity and preserves the distinction between:

Prediction

Information Advantage

Outcome Knowledge

Outcome Influence

and:

Outcome Control

This allows later PGA™ performance governance to interpret predictive success within its actual informational context.

Knowing more about the emerging outcome is not automatically the same as predicting it better.

Canonical Boundary

PSMM governs the identification and mapping of predictive sources, source origins, intermediaries, source lineage, shared-source relationships, source dependency, source concentration, information-access relationships, information timing, information exclusivity, Predictor–Reality Proximity, and materially relevant outcome-knowledge, influence, and control relationships. It does not govern Prediction Input identification, signal relevance, Prediction Basis sufficiency, evidence validity, legal information access, misconduct determination, accountability allocation, collective prediction legitimacy, Operational Reality, or predictive performance.

PSMM establishes:

where the information behind the prediction came from and how its sources relate to the reality being predicted.

It does not determine:

whether those sources make the prediction correct.

Canonical Closing Statement

The number of inputs behind a prediction says little about informational diversity until their underlying sources and dependencies are known.

PSMM makes the source structure visible so that information advantage, source dependence, and proximity to reality are not mistaken for predictive capability.
