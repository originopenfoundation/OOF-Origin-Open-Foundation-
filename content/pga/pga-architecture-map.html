PGA™ serves as the official OOF® Prediction Governance reference map used to identify, define, classify, navigate, govern, evaluate, and understand governable Prediction spaces across human forecasting, AI systems, predictive models, prediction markets, autonomous agents, organizations, decision systems, collective intelligence environments, and future predictive systems.

The purpose of PGA™ is not to make predictions.

Core Architectural Principle

Every Prediction Governance space answers one primary governance question.

No Prediction Governance space duplicates the purpose of another Prediction Governance space.

Every Prediction Governance space has a defined beginning, a defined operational purpose, and a defined governance boundary.

Every Prediction Governance space begins where the preceding Prediction Governance requirement has been sufficiently established.

Together, these Prediction Governance spaces form the official Prediction Governance reference map of PGA™.

Official Prediction Governance Flow

Prediction Object & Purpose → Prediction Methodology → Prediction Basis → Prediction Assumptions → Prediction Formation → Probability & Uncertainty → Prediction Feasibility → Collective Prediction where applicable → Communication & Reliance → Monitoring & Revision → Outcome & Performance → Prediction Record & Continuity

1. Prediction Object & Purpose Standard (POPS)

Governed Space: Prediction Object & Predictive Purpose

Core Question

What exactly is being predicted, for what purpose, over what horizon, and within what predictive boundary?

Canonical Definition

Prediction Object & Purpose Standard (POPS) defines the governance conditions under which the Prediction Object, predictive purpose, prediction target, Prediction Horizon, and Predictive Boundary are formally identified, specified, bounded, and maintained as the foundational definition of a Governed Prediction.

Governance Boundary

Begins when a predictive activity, model output, forecast, judgment, signal, or anticipated future condition requires explicit definition of what is being predicted and why.

Ends when the Prediction Object, predictive purpose, target, horizon, and boundary have been sufficiently established to support Prediction Methodology Governance.

2. Prediction Methodology Standard (PMES)

Governed Space: Prediction Methodology

Core Question

By what documented and reproducible methodology is the prediction legitimately constructed?

Canonical Definition

Prediction Methodology Standard (PMES) defines the governance conditions under which the methodology used to construct a prediction is specified, structured, documented, applied, versioned, and maintained so that the prediction remains methodologically identifiable and reproducible.

Governance Boundary

Begins when the Prediction Object and predictive purpose have been sufficiently established and the method by which the prediction will be constructed must be explicitly governed.

Ends when the Prediction Methodology has been sufficiently specified, documented, structured, applied, and versioned to support Prediction Basis Governance.

A prediction without a documented methodology is an opinion, not a governed prediction.

3. Prediction Basis Standard (PBAS)

Governed Space: Prediction Basis

Core Question

What information, data, observations, evidence references, and signals legitimately form the basis of the prediction?

Canonical Definition

Prediction Basis Standard (PBAS) defines the governance conditions under which information, data, observations, evidence references, signals, sources, and materially relevant informational conditions are identified, selected, related, assessed, and maintained as the governed basis supporting a prediction.

Governance Boundary

Begins when a sufficiently defined Prediction Methodology requires an explicit informational basis from which predictive formation may proceed.

Ends when the materially relevant information, signals, sources, and basis relationships have been sufficiently identified, qualified, and traceably associated with the prediction to support Prediction Assumption Governance.

Prediction Basis ≠ Evidence Governance.

4. Prediction Assumption Standard (PAS)

Governed Space: Prediction Assumptions

Core Question

What assumptions must hold for the prediction and its stated probability, confidence, feasibility, or expected outcome to remain meaningful?

Canonical Definition

Prediction Assumption Standard (PAS) defines the governance conditions under which explicit and materially relevant assumptions supporting a prediction are identified, dependency-mapped, assessed for sensitivity and materiality, monitored, and governed through change.

Governance Boundary

Begins when the Prediction Basis has been sufficiently established and the assumptions necessary for predictive meaning must be explicitly governed.

Ends when materially relevant assumptions, dependencies, sensitivities, and change conditions have been sufficiently established to support Prediction Formation.

A hidden assumption cannot remain a governed assumption.

5. Prediction Formation Standard (PFOS)

Governed Space: Prediction Formation

Core Question

How are methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, and applicable conditions transformed into a specific governed prediction?

Canonical Definition

Prediction Formation Standard (PFOS) defines the governance conditions under which Prediction Methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, constraints, and applicable conditions are assembled into a specific, identifiable, traceable, and governable Prediction State.

Governance Boundary

Begins when methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, and applicable predictive conditions have been sufficiently governed.

Ends when a specific Governed Prediction has been sufficiently formed, represented, and traceably associated with the methodological inputs that produced it to support Probability & Uncertainty Governance.

6. Prediction Probability & Uncertainty Standard (PPUS)

Governed Space: Predictive Probability & Uncertainty

Core Question

How should probability, uncertainty, confidence, ranges, and conditionality be represented without overstating what the prediction actually establishes?

Canonical Definition

Prediction Probability & Uncertainty Standard (PPUS) defines the governance conditions under which probability, uncertainty, confidence, ranges, and conditionality associated with a Governed Prediction are represented, distinguished, qualified, and maintained without converting predictive estimates into overstated certainty.

Governance Boundary

Begins when a specific Governed Prediction has been sufficiently formed and its probabilistic or uncertainty-related meaning must be explicitly represented.

Ends when probability, uncertainty, confidence, ranges, and conditionality have been sufficiently governed to support Prediction Feasibility Governance.

Probability ≠ Confidence.

Probability ≠ Certainty.

7. Prediction Feasibility Standard (PFES)

Governed Space: Prediction Feasibility

Core Question

Is the predicted outcome realistically achievable under the stated assumptions, constraints, time horizon, Prediction Basis, and relevant conditions?

Canonical Definition

Prediction Feasibility Standard (PFES) defines the governance conditions under which the operational achievability of a predicted outcome is assessed against constraints, dependencies, time horizon, Prediction Basis, assumptions, execution conditions, and materially relevant real-world limitations.

Governance Boundary

Begins when a prediction and its probability and uncertainty representation have been sufficiently established and the predicted outcome requires explicit feasibility assessment.

Ends when feasibility conditions, operational constraints, plausibility, execution feasibility, and resulting Feasibility State have been sufficiently governed to support Collective Prediction Governance where applicable or direct Communication & Reliance Governance.

Possible ≠ Plausible ≠ Feasible ≠ Probable.

Feasible ≠ Probable.

8. Collective Prediction Governance Standard (CPGS)

Governed Space: Collective Prediction

Core Question

Under what conditions may predictions originating from multiple participants, models, agents, markets, or forecasting mechanisms be combined and represented as a legitimate collective prediction?

Canonical Definition

Collective Prediction Governance Standard (CPGS) defines the governance conditions under which predictions originating from multiple participants, models, agents, markets, or forecasting mechanisms may be evaluated for diversity, independence, concentration, aggregation legitimacy, and collective representation.

Governance Boundary

Begins when multiple sufficiently governed predictions are proposed for aggregation, combination, collective interpretation, or representation.

Ends when source diversity, independence, concentration, aggregation conditions, and collective representation have been sufficiently governed to support Communication & Reliance.

Multiple Predictors ≠ Independent Predictions.

A collective signal is not automatically a governed prediction.

9. Prediction Communication & Reliance Standard (PCRS)

Governed Space: Prediction Communication & Reliance

Core Question

How may a prediction be communicated, interpreted, and relied upon without exceeding what its methodology, uncertainty, feasibility, intended purpose, and governed conditions legitimately support?

Canonical Definition

Prediction Communication & Reliance Standard (PCRS) defines the governance conditions under which a Governed Prediction is represented, communicated, interpreted, and relied upon within legitimate methodological, contextual, uncertainty, feasibility, purpose, and use boundaries.

Governance Boundary

Begins when a Governed Prediction is sufficiently formed and ready for representation, communication, interpretation, or operational use.

Ends when communication, interpretation, reliance legitimacy, and use boundaries have been sufficiently governed to support continuing Monitoring & Revision.

A prediction must not become stronger in communication than it is in methodology.

Communicable ≠ Reliance-Eligible.

10. Prediction Monitoring & Revision Standard (PMRS)

Governed Space: Prediction Monitoring & Revision

Core Question

When changing information or conditions materially affect a prediction, when must it be preserved, reviewed, revised, superseded, or withdrawn?

Canonical Definition

Prediction Monitoring & Revision Standard (PMRS) defines the governance conditions under which changes in Prediction Basis, assumptions, operational conditions, predictive context, probability, uncertainty, feasibility, or other materially relevant factors trigger monitoring, review, revision, supersession, or withdrawal of a Governed Prediction.

Governance Boundary

Begins when a Governed Prediction enters continuing use, monitoring, communication, reliance, or exposure to changing predictive conditions.

Continues while materially relevant changes may alter predictive meaning and ends when required review, revision, supersession, or withdrawal actions have been sufficiently governed to support later Outcome & Performance Governance.

Revision ≠ Historical Replacement.

Supersession ≠ Deletion.

11. Prediction Outcome & Performance Standard (POFPS)

Governed Space: Prediction Outcome & Predictive Performance

Core Question

Once a sufficiently governed outcome becomes available, how should the prediction be associated with that outcome and its predictive performance legitimately determined over time?

Canonical Definition

Prediction Outcome & Performance Standard (POFPS) defines the governance conditions under which a historical Governed Prediction is associated with a sufficiently governed outcome, compared with that outcome, evaluated for error and calibration where applicable, and assessed for Predictive Performance within the informational and operational conditions under which the prediction existed.

Governance Boundary

Begins when a sufficiently governed outcome reference becomes available for a historical prediction.

Ends when prediction-to-outcome association, outcome comparison, error measurement, calibration where applicable, and Predictive Performance comparison have been sufficiently governed to support historical record continuity.

Prediction Outcome Association ≠ Outcome Determination.

Observed Accuracy ≠ Demonstrated Predictive Skill.

12. Prediction Record & Continuity Standard (PRCS)

Governed Space: Prediction Record & Historical Continuity

Core Question

How must a prediction and its complete history remain identifiable, reconstructable, versioned, and historically continuous across formation, revision, supersession, outcome, performance assessment, retirement, and lifecycle re-entry?

Canonical Definition

Prediction Record & Continuity Standard (PRCS) defines the governance conditions under which a Governed Prediction and its complete lifecycle history remain identifiable, versioned, preserved, historically reconstructable, state- continuous, and capable of governed lifecycle re-entry without allowing later predictive states or outcome knowledge to rewrite earlier prediction history.

Governance Boundary

Begins when a Governed Prediction requires persistent historical representation and continuity across its lifecycle.

Continues throughout formation, versioning, communication, reliance, revision, supersession, withdrawal, outcome association, Predictive Performance assessment, retirement, archival, and lifecycle re-entry.

Where material lifecycle return changes the predictive object, purpose, horizon, methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, or operational context beyond legitimate continuity, Prediction Record & Continuity Standard may refer the returning object to the appropriate earlier PGA™ governance space or require New Prediction Formation.

Current Prediction State ≠ Complete Prediction History.

Recursive Prediction Lifecycle

PGA™ does not end when a Governed Prediction is first formed.

Once a prediction remains in use, communication, reliance, monitoring, decision support, performance evaluation, or historical analysis, the architecture becomes recursive:

Governed Prediction → Monitoring → Review → Revision → Supersession / Withdrawal → Outcome Association → Predictive Performance → Prediction Record Continuity

Where materially relevant predictive conditions change:

Historical Prediction State → Change Review → Revised Prediction Version → Preserved Historical Continuity

Where a historical Prediction Record later becomes relevant:

Historical / Inactive Prediction Record → Re-Entry Evaluation → Re-Entry Classification → Re-Entered Prediction Record State or New Prediction Formation

Where Prediction Continuity becomes materially invalid:

Material Change → Continuity Reassessment → Continuity Break → Earlier PGA™ Governance Referral → New Prediction Formation where required

Lifecycle re-entry occurs at the earliest materially affected governance space.

A changed Prediction Object or predictive purpose may require re-entry into Prediction Object & Purpose Standard .

A materially changed predictive methodology may require re-entry into Prediction Methodology Standard .

A materially changed or stale Prediction Basis may require re-entry into Prediction Basis Standard .

Changed assumptions may require re-entry into Prediction Assumption Standard .

Material predictive reconstruction may require re-entry into Prediction Formation Standard .

Changed probability, uncertainty, confidence, range, or conditionality may require re-entry into Prediction Probability & Uncertainty Standard .

Changed operational achievability may require re-entry into Prediction Feasibility Standard .

Changed source diversity, aggregation conditions, independence, or concentration may require re-entry into Collective Prediction Governance Standard where applicable.

Changed communication or reliance conditions may require reconsideration under Prediction Communication & Reliance Standard .

Changing information or operational conditions may require reconsideration under Prediction Monitoring & Revision Standard .

A newly available or corrected outcome may require reconsideration under Prediction Outcome & Performance Standard .

The architecture therefore supports targeted governed Prediction lifecycle re-entry rather than uncontrolled reconstruction of the entire prediction.

The architecture progresses through:

Prediction Object & Purpose → Prediction Methodology → Prediction Basis → Prediction Assumptions → Prediction Formation → Probability & Uncertainty → Prediction Feasibility → Collective Prediction where applicable → Communication & Reliance → Monitoring & Revision → Outcome & Performance → Prediction Record & Continuity

Together, these Prediction Governance spaces govern what is being predicted and why, how the prediction is methodologically constructed, which information and assumptions support it, how it becomes a specific Governed Prediction, how probability and uncertainty are represented, whether the predicted outcome is operationally feasible, how multiple predictions may legitimately become collective prediction where applicable, how the prediction is communicated and relied upon, how it responds to changing conditions, how it is compared with governed outcomes, how Predictive Performance is assessed, and how the complete Prediction Record remains continuous, reconstructable, versioned, and historically governable throughout its lifecycle.

PGA™ therefore provides a complete governance path from the first identification of what is being predicted and for what purpose to the determination of how that prediction must remain methodologically interpretable, performance-assessable, and historically reconstructable throughout time .
