Governed Space

PLDIM governs the interpretation of a Governed Predictive Representation when its predictive meaning is translated into decision-relevant meaning.

It governs the transition from:

What does the prediction establish?

to:

What does the prediction mean for a contemplated decision?

PLDIM ensures that a prediction is not silently transformed into a recommendation, instruction, requirement, prohibition, authorization, or decision merely because it is decision-relevant.

Canonical Definition

The Prediction-Led Decision Interpretation Module (PLDIM) defines the operational governance requirements by which a Governed Predictive Representation is interpreted in relation to a contemplated decision without allowing the prediction to acquire decision authority, normative force, operational meaning, or prescriptive significance beyond what the underlying predictive structure legitimately supports.

PLDIM governs the relationship between:

Governed Predictive Representation → Decision Context → Prediction-Led Decision Interpretation

It establishes how predictive information may inform a decision while preserving the distinction between what is predicted and what should be done.

A prediction may inform a decision. It does not become the decision merely because it influences one.

Operational Role

PLDIM is the second operational module within PCRS.

PRSM governs whether the prediction has been faithfully represented.

PLDIM governs what that representation is interpreted to mean for a decision.

Its focused operational question is:

What decision-relevant meaning may legitimately be derived from a Governed Predictive Representation without converting predictive information into unsupported decision authority?

PLDIM does not determine whether reliance is ultimately legitimate.

It governs the interpretation from which downstream reliance may later be assessed.

Module Operational Space

PLDIM applies whenever a prediction becomes relevant to a contemplated human, organizational, algorithmic, AI- assisted, autonomous, financial, operational, strategic, or public decision.

Examples include:

investment decisions; capital allocation; hiring and workforce planning; insurance decisions; infrastructure planning; supply-chain decisions;

maintenance decisions; medical resource planning; emergency preparation; policy planning; procurement; market positioning; autonomous-agent decisions; AI-assisted decisions; operational scheduling; risk mitigation; strategic planning.

The presence of a prediction does not itself establish what decision should follow.

1. Prediction-to-Decision Transition

A prediction describes a possible future condition or outcome.

A decision selects, authorizes, rejects, delays, modifies, or prioritizes an action.

These are different governed objects.

PLDIM governs the transition:

Predictive Meaning → Decision-Relevant Meaning

It does not permit the uncontrolled transition:

Predictive Meaning → Required Action

Prediction ≠ Decision

2. Decision Context

A prediction acquires decision relevance only within a Decision Context.

Decision Context may include:

decision objective; available alternatives; timing; resource constraints; legal constraints; organizational authority; operational conditions; risk tolerance; reversibility; consequence severity; competing objectives; other available information.

The same prediction may legitimately produce different decision interpretations in different contexts.

Same Prediction ≠ Same Decision Meaning

3. Decision Object

The Decision Object identifies what decision is actually under consideration.

Examples:

whether to invest; whether to evacuate; whether to delay deployment; whether to increase inventory; whether to inspect equipment; whether to enter a market; whether to allocate additional resources.

Without an identifiable Decision Object, predictive information may become attached to vague or uncontrolled decision implications.

4. Prediction Relevance

PLDIM determines whether the prediction is materially relevant to the contemplated Decision Object.

A prediction may be accurate and methodologically governed while still being irrelevant to a particular decision.

Predictive Quality ≠ Decision Relevance

5. Decision-Relevant Prediction Scope

Only the portion of the prediction relevant to the contemplated decision should inform its interpretation.

A broad forecast may contain multiple predictive elements.

Not every element necessarily applies to every decision.

PLDIM establishes the relationship between:

Prediction Scope ↔ Decision Scope

6. Prediction Purpose Alignment

POPS establishes the predictive purpose.

PLDIM evaluates whether the contemplated decision interpretation remains compatible with that purpose.

A prediction created for:

strategic scenario planning

must not automatically be interpreted as:

real-time operational instruction.

Predictive Purpose ≠ Universal Decision Purpose

7. Prediction Horizon Alignment

Decision timing must be interpreted relative to Prediction Horizon.

A five-year forecast may be highly relevant to strategic infrastructure planning while being inappropriate as the sole basis for an immediate operational intervention.

Likewise, a seven-day prediction may have little legitimate meaning for a ten-year capital decision.

Prediction Horizon ≠ Decision Horizon

Alignment must be established rather than assumed.

8. Predictive Boundary Alignment

PLDIM evaluates whether the decision falls within the Predictive Boundary.

A prediction limited to:

one jurisdiction; one facility; one customer segment; one technology; one operating environment;

must not automatically govern interpretation outside that boundary.

Decision Within Related Domain ≠ Decision Within Predictive Boundary

9. Methodological Relevance

A prediction methodology may be appropriate for one predictive purpose but insufficient for another decision context.

PLDIM does not validate the methodology itself.

PMES owns Prediction Methodology.

PLDIM determines whether the methodological meaning available from the prediction supports the contemplated decision interpretation.

10. Prediction Basis Relevance

A prediction may be formed from information that is appropriate for prediction formation but incomplete for a particular decision.

PLDIM therefore distinguishes:

Sufficient Prediction Basis ≠ Sufficient Decision Basis

A prediction can be legitimately formed without containing every fact necessary for a downstream decision.

11. Assumption Relevance

Material assumptions may affect decision interpretation.

If a prediction assumes:

stable regulation

but the contemplated decision concerns an environment in which regulatory change is imminent, the assumption becomes materially relevant to decision interpretation.

PLDIM preserves applicable assumption conditions without taking ownership of PAS governance.

12. Probability Interpretation

Probability indicates predictive likelihood under governed predictive conditions.

It does not directly determine what action should be taken.

For example:

70% probability

does not independently establish:

Approve

Reject

Invest

Evacuate

Deploy

Do Not Deploy

Decision meaning depends on consequences and context.

Probability ≠ Decision Threshold

13. Probability Threshold Interpretation

Organizations may define decision thresholds.

For example:

If probability exceeds 80%, trigger additional inspection.

The threshold is a decision rule.

It is not part of the probability itself.

PLDIM requires this distinction to remain visible.

Prediction Probability ≠ Decision Rule

Decision Threshold ≠ Predictive Truth Boundary

14. Uncertainty Interpretation

A prediction with significant uncertainty may still be decision-relevant.

Likewise, a prediction with relatively low uncertainty may still be insufficient for a high-consequence decision.

PLDIM governs how uncertainty affects decision interpretation without automatically converting uncertainty into prohibition.

High Uncertainty ≠ No Decision Relevance

Low Uncertainty ≠ Automatic Decision Authority

15. Confidence Interpretation

Confidence may affect how strongly a prediction is considered.

It does not independently determine whether action is justified.

High Confidence ≠ Mandatory Action

Low Confidence ≠ Mandatory Inaction

16. Feasibility Interpretation

PFES determines Prediction Feasibility.

PLDIM governs what that feasibility state means within a Decision Context.

A predicted outcome may be:

Feasible but improbable

or:

Probable but subject to material feasibility restriction.

Neither condition independently determines the appropriate decision.

Feasible ≠ Advisable

Infeasible ≠ Irrelevant to Planning

17. Collective Prediction Interpretation

Where CPGS governs a collective prediction, PLDIM preserves material collective qualifications when deriving decision meaning.

A market, crowd, ensemble, or multi-agent consensus may inform a decision.

It does not automatically become superior decision evidence because it is collective.

Collective Prediction ≠ Collective Decision Authority

18. Prediction Strength

Prediction Strength represents how strongly the governed prediction supports the specific predictive proposition it makes.

Decision strength is different.

A strong prediction concerning a minor outcome may justify less action than a weaker prediction concerning a catastrophic outcome.

Prediction Strength ≠ Required Decision Strength

19. Consequence Context

Decision interpretation may depend materially on the consequences of:

acting; not acting; acting too early; acting too late; choosing the wrong alternative.

PLDIM allows predictive meaning to be interpreted within consequence context without converting the prediction into a risk-governance system.

20. Asymmetric Consequences

Decision consequences may be asymmetric.

A 10% probability of a catastrophic event may justify substantial preparation.

A 90% probability of a trivial event may justify almost none.

Therefore:

Higher Probability ≠ Greater Decision Importance by Default

21. Reversibility

Decision reversibility may affect legitimate prediction-led interpretation.

A low-cost reversible decision may tolerate greater predictive uncertainty than an irreversible high-consequence decision.

Same Prediction + Different Decision Reversibility ≠ Same Decision Interpretation

22. Decision Materiality

Decision Materiality describes the significance of the contemplated decision.

Materiality may depend on:

financial exposure; human impact; safety implications; legal consequences; operational disruption; strategic significance; reversibility; duration.

PLDIM uses materiality to determine how much predictive interpretation governance is required.

23. Prediction as One Decision Input

A prediction may be only one input among many.

Other inputs may include:

current operational facts;

legal requirements; contractual obligations; budgets; ethical constraints; safety controls; human judgment; organizational policy; alternative scenarios.

Prediction Input ≠ Complete Decision Basis

24. Multi-Prediction Decision Context

A decision may involve several predictions.

Examples:

demand forecast; cost forecast; regulatory forecast; supply forecast; technology forecast.

PLDIM prevents one predictive signal from silently becoming the complete predictive basis where several materially relevant predictions exist.

25. Conflicting Predictions

Different predictions may support different decision interpretations.

PLDIM requires conflict to remain visible where material.

Conflict must not be silently resolved by selecting whichever prediction supports the preferred decision.

Prediction Conflict ≠ Permission for Selective Interpretation

26. Decision Confirmation Bias

Decision-makers may already prefer an outcome before consulting predictive information.

PLDIM identifies the risk that a prediction is interpreted primarily to validate a pre-existing decision.

Prediction Supporting Preferred Action ≠ Independent Decision Justification

27. Prediction Cherry-Picking

Where multiple relevant predictions exist, selecting only favorable predictions may materially distort decision interpretation.

PLDIM requires material predictive plurality to remain identifiable where applicable.

Available Supporting Prediction ≠ Complete Predictive Picture

28. Decision Framing

The same prediction may be framed differently.

For example:

30% probability of failure

versus:

70% probability of success

may describe complementary aspects of the same predictive state while producing different psychological interpretations.

PLDIM governs whether framing materially changes decision meaning.

Equivalent Probability Expression ≠ Equivalent Decision Perception

29. Language Strength

Predictive language can become prescriptive through wording.

Examples:

Prediction:

Failure is likely.

Interpretation:

We should stop the project.

These statements are not equivalent.

PLDIM prevents linguistic escalation from silently converting descriptive predictive meaning into prescriptive decision meaning.

Likely ≠ Should

Unlikely ≠ Ignore

30. Recommendation Conversion

A prediction may be used by another methodology or governance process to generate a recommendation.

PLDIM requires the transition to remain explicit:

Prediction → Interpretation → Recommendation

not:

Prediction = Recommendation

The recommendation requires its own legitimate basis.

31. Decision Authority

The existence of predictive information does not establish who has authority to make the decision.

Decision authority may belong to:

an individual; an organization; a regulator; an automated control system; an authorized AI agent; another governance structure.

Predictive Authority ≠ Decision Authority

32. AI Decision Interpretation

AI systems may interpret predictions as decision inputs.

PLDIM requires the AI system to preserve:

prediction scope; probability meaning; uncertainty; conditions; feasibility; purpose; applicable boundaries; decision context.

An AI system must not convert:

0.82 probability

directly into:

execute action

unless a separately governed decision rule legitimately establishes that transition.

Prediction Score ≠ Execution Command

33. Autonomous-Agent Interpretation

Autonomous agents may consume predictions dynamically.

PLDIM governs the predictive interpretation stage before action authority is considered.

The agent may determine:

This prediction is materially relevant to the contemplated action.

That does not itself mean:

The action is authorized.

Interpretation Eligibility ≠ Execution Eligibility

34. Decision Rule Interaction

A governed Decision Rule may define how predictions affect decisions.

Example:

If flood probability exceeds 60% and water level exceeds threshold X, initiate preparation protocol Y.

PLDIM governs the prediction's role within the rule.

It does not establish the legitimacy of unrelated operational controls.

35. Automated Thresholds

Automated systems frequently use thresholds.

Threshold governance should preserve:

threshold source; threshold purpose; applicable prediction type; decision context; consequence context; revision history.

A threshold should not become an unexplained bridge between prediction and action.

36. Historical Performance Interpretation

Past predictive performance may affect how a prediction is considered.

However:

Past Accuracy ≠ Current Decision Authority

POFPS governs Prediction Outcome & Performance.

PLDIM may use governed performance information as contextual input without treating it as permanent authority.

37. Predictor Identity Interpretation

The identity, reputation, institutional status, or authority of a predictor may influence how recipients interpret a prediction.

PLDIM distinguishes source reputation from predictive meaning.

Authority of Source ≠ Certainty of Prediction

38. Predictor–Reality Proximity

A predictor may have unusually close access to the actors, information, systems, or decisions from which the predicted outcome may emerge.

High Predictor–Reality Proximity may materially affect interpretation.

But:

High Predictor–Reality Proximity ≠ Misconduct

Outcome Knowledge ≠ Predictive Capability

Decision Proximity ≠ Decision Authority

PLDIM preserves these distinctions when prediction-led decisions are considered.

39. Prediction Influence on Decision

A prediction may influence a decision.

PLDIM makes that influence identifiable.

Potential influence states may include:

Informational; Material; Significant; Dominant; Determinative.

A dominant predictive influence does not automatically mean the decision was improperly governed.

It means the relationship should be visible.

40. Prediction Influence on Reality

A prediction-led decision may alter the reality being predicted.

The chain may become:

Prediction → Decision Interpretation → Decision → Operational Action → Changed Operational Reality

Where this occurs, the predictive environment may change.

PMRS may subsequently govern whether the prediction requires review or revision.

Prediction Influence ≠ Predictive Accuracy

41. Decision Interpretation State

PLDIM may classify a Prediction-Led Decision Interpretation as:

Decision-Interpretation Ready

Conditionally Decision-Interpretation Ready

Additional Decision Context Required

Interpretation Restricted

Prediction-Decision Misaligned

Insufficiently Supported

Unable to Determine

The state concerns the legitimacy of prediction-led interpretation.

It does not determine the final decision.

42. Interpretation Qualification

Where prediction-led interpretation remains legitimate only under specific conditions, PLDIM may attach an Interpretation Qualification.

Examples:

applicable only to strategic planning; insufficient for autonomous execution; additional operational evidence required; dependent on specified assumptions; limited to stated jurisdiction; requires additional prediction comparison; high-consequence reliance requires escalation.

43. Interpretation Traceability

A governed interpretation should remain traceable through:

Governed Predictive Representation → Decision Context → Prediction-Led Decision Interpretation

Where material, the record should identify:

prediction reference; prediction version; representation reference; Decision Object; decision context; relevant prediction elements; applicable qualifications; interpretation state.

44. Interpretation Versioning

Decision context can change even when the prediction remains unchanged.

Therefore, interpretation may require revision without prediction revision.

Prediction Unchanged ≠ Interpretation Permanently Unchanged

Interpretation Revision ≠ Prediction Revision

46. Governance Logic

PLDIM applies the following operational logic:

If the Governed Predictive Representation is materially relevant and appropriately aligned with the Decision Context → Decision-Interpretation Ready.

If interpretation is legitimate only under explicit conditions or qualifications → Conditionally Decision- Interpretation Ready.

If material Decision Context is missing → Additional Decision Context Required.

If the contemplated interpretation exceeds predictive purpose, horizon, boundary, uncertainty, feasibility, or other governed conditions → Interpretation Restricted.

If the prediction and contemplated decision are materially incompatible → Prediction-Decision Misaligned.

If the available predictive structure cannot support the proposed decision meaning → Insufficiently Supported.

If sufficient information is unavailable to determine interpretation legitimacy → Unable to Determine.

50. Enterprise Application

PLDIM can be applied across:

executive decision-support systems; enterprise forecasting; investment decisions; financial planning; insurance; infrastructure planning; supply-chain management; predictive maintenance; emergency management; public-sector planning; healthcare resource planning; AI decision-support systems; autonomous systems; AI agents; multi-agent systems;

preserve the difference between what the prediction says and what the decision-maker decides that information means for action.

51. Use Case 1 — 15% Flood Probability and a High-Consequence Decision

A regional infrastructure authority receives a governed prediction:

15% probability that a major flood threshold will be exceeded within the next 72 hours.

The probability is relatively low.

However, failure to prepare could expose:

tens of thousands of residents; critical electricity infrastructure; transport networks; hospitals; emergency services.

A simplistic decision system applies:

Probability below 50% → No action.

The critical governance question is:

Does a prediction below 50% legitimately mean that preparation is unnecessary?

PLDIM determines that probability alone cannot establish the decision interpretation.

The Decision Context contains severe asymmetric consequences.

A 15% probability may therefore remain materially decision-relevant even though the predicted event is not the most probable outcome.

The prediction does not command evacuation.

It may legitimately support preparation, escalation, monitoring, or contingency activation when combined with appropriate decision governance.

Low Probability ≠ Low Decision Importance

Probability ≠ Decision Threshold

Prediction ≠ Decision

52. Use Case 2 — AI Turns an 82% Prediction into an Automatic Investment Decision

An enterprise AI system receives a Governed Predictive Representation:

82% probability that demand for a new industrial technology will exceed a defined market threshold within three years.

The prediction was created for:

strategic market planning.

Its assumptions include:

continued regulatory support; specified manufacturing-cost reductions; infrastructure availability.

An autonomous investment agent receives the prediction and interprets:

82% probability → Investment approved.

The critical governance question is:

Does a strong strategic prediction legitimately authorize an autonomous capital-allocation decision?

PLDIM identifies several governance gaps.

The prediction does not itself establish:

expected investment return; acceptable acquisition price; capital availability; portfolio exposure; regulatory permission; organizational investment authority; downside consequences; alternative investments.

The correct transition is:

Governed Prediction → Decision-Relevant Interpretation → Separate Decision Governance

not:

Governed Prediction → Automatic Investment Authorization

The prediction may be highly relevant.

It still does not possess investment authority.

High Probability ≠ Mandatory Action

Prediction Input ≠ Complete Decision Basis

Predictive Signal ≠ Execution Permission

53. Canonical Boundary

PLDIM governs how a Governed Predictive Representation is interpreted in relation to a contemplated decision, including Prediction Relevance, predictive-purpose alignment, horizon alignment, boundary alignment, probability and uncertainty interpretation, feasibility interpretation, consequence context, materiality, reversibility, Prediction Influence, Decision Context, interpretation qualification, and interpretation traceability. It does not establish the underlying prediction, determine Prediction Methodology, govern Prediction Basis or assumptions, establish probability, determine Prediction Feasibility, establish Collective Prediction legitimacy, make the decision, create Decision Authority, authorize execution, determine final Reliance Legitimacy, establish Operational Reality, or determine predictive performance.

PRSM governs Prediction Representation & Signaling.

PLDIM governs the transition from represented predictive meaning to decision-relevant meaning.

Subsequent PCRS modules govern Prediction Interpretation Boundary, Reliance Legitimacy & Context, and Prediction Use Boundary.

54. Canonical Closing Statement

The moment a prediction enters a decision process, its probability can easily be mistaken for permission, its confidence for authority, and its relevance for instruction.

PLDIM preserves the boundary between predictive knowledge and decision meaning by governing what a prediction legitimately contributes to a decision without allowing it to silently become the decision itself.

A prediction may change what a decision-maker knows. It does not, by itself, decide what the decision-maker must do.
