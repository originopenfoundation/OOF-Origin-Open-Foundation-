Governed Space

RLCM governs the legitimacy, degree, conditions, context, and limits of reliance placed upon a Governed Prediction.

It determines whether a prediction may legitimately contribute to a decision, action, allocation, escalation, prioritization, intervention, planning process, automated process, or other consequential use, and what level of reliance the prediction can support under the applicable conditions.

RLCM governs the transition:

Governed Prediction Interpretation Boundary → Governed Prediction Reliance Determination

Its purpose is to prevent predictive information from acquiring greater decision authority than its governed structure can legitimately support.

Canonical Definition

The Reliance Legitimacy & Context Module (RLCM) defines the operational governance requirements by which reliance upon a Governed Prediction is assessed, bounded, qualified, authorized for consideration, restricted, or rejected according to the prediction's methodological condition and the context in which reliance is contemplated.

RLCM evaluates whether the prediction's purpose, methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, probability, uncertainty, confidence, feasibility, collective-prediction qualifications, current lifecycle state, Interpretation Boundary, decision context, consequence severity, reversibility, dependency, and availability of independent safeguards support the proposed degree of reliance.

It does not determine whether the prediction is true.

It determines whether relying upon it in the contemplated context is methodologically legitimate.

A prediction can be legitimate without being sufficient to justify reliance.

Operational Role

RLCM is the fourth operational module within PCRS.

PRSM governs how the prediction is represented.

PLDIM governs how that prediction may be interpreted in relation to a contemplated decision.

PIBM establishes the legitimate Interpretation Boundary.

RLCM determines whether reliance within that boundary is legitimate in the applicable operational context.

Its focused operational question is:

Under what conditions, and to what degree, may a decision-maker, organization, AI system, autonomous agent, or other actor legitimately rely upon the prediction?

RLCM does not establish the final Prediction Use Boundary.

That responsibility belongs to PUBM.

Module Operational Space

RLCM applies whenever a prediction may materially influence:

human decisions; executive decisions; investment decisions; operational planning; resource allocation; scheduling;

procurement; infrastructure decisions; risk responses; safety interventions; insurance decisions; public-sector planning; AI recommendations; autonomous-agent behavior; machine prioritization; automated workflows; escalation; contingency activation; strategic planning.

The module governs reliance whether the prediction is used as:

contextual information; supporting information; one decision factor; a major decision factor; a primary decision basis; an automated trigger; a prerequisite for action.

1. Prediction Reliance

Prediction Reliance exists when a prediction materially influences a decision, action, allocation, prioritization, omission, escalation, or other consequential process.

Reliance may be:

direct; indirect; human; organizational; algorithmic; automated; autonomous; distributed across multiple decision stages.

Prediction Consideration ≠ Prediction Reliance

A prediction may be observed without materially affecting the resulting decision.

2. Reliance Legitimacy

Reliance Legitimacy describes whether the contemplated reliance is methodologically supportable under the applicable predictive and decision conditions.

A prediction may be validly formed yet unsuitable as the primary basis for a particular consequential decision.

Prediction Legitimacy ≠ Reliance Legitimacy

3. Reliance Context

Reliance cannot be evaluated independently of context.

Relevant contextual dimensions may include:

decision purpose; consequence severity; decision reversibility; time sensitivity; uncertainty tolerance; available alternatives; affected population; operational environment; safety criticality; financial exposure; dependency structure; availability of human review; availability of independent validation; ability to monitor outcomes.

Same Prediction + Different Context ≠ Same Reliance Legitimacy

4. Reliance Object

RLCM requires identification of what decision, action, or process would rely upon the prediction.

The Reliance Object may be:

strategic decision; operational decision; investment; deployment; resource allocation; hiring decision; maintenance action; intervention; escalation; automated workflow; autonomous action.

Reliance legitimacy must be assessed against the actual Reliance Object.

5. Reliance Degree

Reliance exists on a continuum.

RLCM distinguishes:

Informational Reliance

Supporting Reliance

Material Reliance

Primary Reliance

Determinative Reliance

The greater the reliance degree, the stronger the governance justification generally required.

6. Informational Reliance

The prediction contributes contextual information but does not materially determine the decision.

Example:

A five-year technology forecast is included in strategic discussion alongside numerous other inputs.

Informational Reliance generally carries lower dependence on predictive correctness.

7. Supporting Reliance

The prediction contributes materially to analysis but remains one of several independent decision inputs.

The decision can remain justified even if the prediction later proves incorrect.

Supporting Input ≠ Decision Authority

8. Material Reliance

The prediction materially affects the decision outcome.

Removing the prediction could reasonably change:

the selected action; resource allocation; timing; prioritization; escalation; risk posture.

Material Reliance requires stronger governance scrutiny.

9. Primary Reliance

The prediction constitutes the principal predictive basis for a decision.

Primary Reliance creates substantial dependency upon the prediction's legitimacy, current relevance, and applicable qualifications.

Primary Predictive Input ≠ Sufficient Decision Basis by Default

10. Determinative Reliance

Determinative Reliance exists where the prediction effectively determines the resulting decision or action.

Examples may include:

prediction threshold reached → action automatically executed

or:

prediction below threshold → service automatically denied

Determinative Reliance requires the strongest reliance governance.

Prediction Threshold ≠ Automatic Decision Authority

11. Reliance Proportionality

Reliance should be proportionate to:

predictive support; uncertainty; feasibility; current validity; consequence severity; reversibility; availability of safeguards.

Reliance Strength Should Not Exceed Predictive Support

12. Predictive Support

Predictive Support represents the governed structure available to justify contemplated reliance.

It may include:

documented methodology; sufficient Prediction Basis; explicit assumptions; probability representation; uncertainty representation; feasibility assessment; relevant collective qualifications; current monitoring state; applicable Interpretation Boundary.

Predictive Support does not itself authorize action.

13. Decision Consequence Severity

The potential consequences of the contemplated decision affect legitimate reliance.

RLCM may distinguish:

Low Consequence

Moderate Consequence

High Consequence

Critical Consequence

A prediction suitable for low-consequence planning may be insufficient for safety-critical automated action.

14. Consequence Asymmetry

Possible consequences of acting on a prediction may differ materially from consequences of not acting.

RLCM should evaluate:

Cost of Action

and:

Cost of Inaction

where relevant.

Same Probability ≠ Same Decision Significance

15. Reversibility

Reliance legitimacy depends partly on whether resulting actions can be reversed.

Possible classes:

Fully Reversible

Substantially Reversible

Partially Reversible

Difficult to Reverse

Irreversible

Greater irreversibility may require stronger predictive support and additional safeguards.

16. Recoverability

An action may technically be reversible but costly or operationally difficult to recover from.

Reversibility ≠ Recoverability

Both may affect reliance legitimacy.

17. Time Sensitivity

Some decisions permit extended review.

Others require immediate action.

Time pressure may affect governance procedures.

It does not create predictive authority that did not previously exist.

Urgency ≠ Predictive Legitimacy

18. Prediction Horizon Alignment

The Prediction Horizon must align sufficiently with the Reliance Object.

A 30-day prediction may not legitimately become the primary basis for a five-year irreversible investment without additional governed support.

Prediction Horizon ≠ Decision Horizon by Default

19. Purpose Alignment

The contemplated reliance should remain compatible with the prediction's governed purpose.

A prediction designed for research exploration may not automatically support operational deployment.

Predictive Purpose ≠ Universal Reliance Permission

20. Interpretation Boundary Alignment

Reliance must remain within the PIBM Interpretation Boundary.

If a decision depends upon an interpretation already classified as unsupported, reliance upon that interpretation cannot become legitimate merely because the decision-maker accepts it.

Unsupported Interpretation Cannot Become Legitimate Through Reliance

21. Probability Context

Probability alone does not determine legitimate reliance.

A 60% probability may justify action in one context and be insufficient in another.

Probability ≠ Reliance Authority

22. Probability Thresholds

Organizations may define predictive thresholds for certain actions.

A threshold must have a governed rationale.

Example:

Probability > 70% → Review

may be legitimate.

But:

Probability > 70% → Automatically Execute

requires separate reliance justification.

Threshold Crossing ≠ Automatic Action Legitimacy

23. Uncertainty Context

Reliance should preserve material predictive uncertainty.

High uncertainty may:

restrict reliance; require additional information; require human review;

require scenario-based decisions; require reversible action.

Probability Without Uncertainty Context ≠ Complete Reliance Basis

24. Confidence Context

Confidence must not be used as a substitute for probability, certainty, or decision authority.

High Confidence ≠ Permission for High Reliance by Default

25. Feasibility Context

PFES determines whether the predicted outcome is feasible.

RLCM determines how that feasibility state affects contemplated reliance.

Feasible ≠ Probable

Probable ≠ Operationally Actionable by Default

26. Assumption Dependence

Reliance must account for material assumptions.

Where a decision depends upon a prediction whose critical assumptions are unstable, reliance may require qualification or monitoring.

Reliance on Prediction = Reliance on Material Predictive Assumptions

where those assumptions materially determine predictive meaning.

27. Assumption Fragility

If small changes in an assumption materially change the prediction, reliance may be fragile.

Prediction Stability ≠ Assumption Stability

Fragile reliance should be identifiable.

28. Prediction Basis Sufficiency

RLCM does not govern Prediction Basis itself.

PBAS owns that governed space.

RLCM may consume its governed state when evaluating whether contemplated reliance is proportionate.

Available Prediction ≠ Sufficient Reliance Basis

29. Methodological Fitness for Reliance

A methodology may legitimately form a prediction while remaining unsuitable for a specific high-consequence reliance context.

Example:

An exploratory forecasting methodology may support strategic discussion without supporting autonomous execution.

Methodological Legitimacy ≠ Universal Decision Fitness

30. Current Predictive State

Reliance must consider whether the prediction remains current.

Relevant states may include:

Current; Review Required; Revised; Superseded; Withdrawn; Historical.

Historically Valid Prediction ≠ Currently Reliance-Eligible Prediction

31. Prediction Basis Decay

Prediction Basis may deteriorate as:

new information emerges; operating conditions change; assumptions weaken; source relevance declines.

Reliance must account for material decay.

Valid Initial Prediction ≠ Valid Continuing Reliance

32. Monitoring Dependency

Some reliance may remain legitimate only if predictive conditions are continuously or periodically monitored.

This creates:

Monitoring-Dependent Reliance

If monitoring stops, reliance legitimacy may change.

33. Revision Dependency

Where a prediction is subject to active revision, downstream reliance should identify which prediction version supports the decision.

Current Prediction ≠ Historical Prediction Version

34. Supersession

A superseded prediction should not silently continue to drive decisions where a newer governed prediction exists.

Superseded Prediction ≠ Current Reliance Basis

Historical preservation remains required.

35. Collective Prediction Context

Reliance on a collective prediction must preserve applicable CPGS qualifications.

Relevant factors may include:

source diversity; dependence; aggregation method; influence concentration; collective representation state.

Collective Prediction ≠ Automatically Stronger Reliance Basis

36. Collective Size

Large participation does not automatically justify stronger reliance.

More Participants ≠ More Reliance Legitimacy

37. Collective Independence

A collective containing many dependent predictors may provide less independent predictive information than nominal participant count suggests.

Nominal Predictor Count ≠ Effective Independent Predictive Support

38. Market-Derived Reliance

Market-derived predictions may contribute to decisions.

But market price or market-implied probability does not automatically establish sufficient predictive basis for consequential reliance.

Market Price ≠ Decision Authority

Trading Volume ≠ Predictive Reliability

39. Predictor–Reality Proximity

Predictor–Reality Proximity may affect interpretation of predictive information.

High proximity may reflect informational advantage.

It may also create contextual dependencies.

RLCM must not equate proximity with predictive capability.

Information Advantage ≠ Predictive Capability

Outcome Knowledge ≠ Predictive Skill

40. Independent Corroboration

High-consequence reliance may require independent predictive or non-predictive corroboration.

Independent corroboration may include:

separate forecast; operational data; human expert review; external validation; scenario analysis; safety assessment.

Single Governed Prediction ≠ Sufficient Basis for Every Decision

41. Corroboration Independence

Additional confirmation has limited value if it derives from the same predictive origin.

Repeated Information ≠ Independent Corroboration

42. Alternative Prediction Review

Where materially different governed predictions exist, reliance should not automatically select the preferred prediction without justification.

Relevant alternatives should be considered where they materially affect the decision.

Preferred Prediction ≠ Governed Decision Basis by Default

43. Predictive Conflict

Predictive Conflict exists where multiple relevant predictions materially disagree.

Conflict may require:

segmentation; additional analysis; reduced reliance; conditional action; escalation.

Prediction Disagreement ≠ Governance Failure

44. Decision Dependency

Decision Dependency measures how strongly the contemplated decision depends upon the prediction.

A decision may be:

Prediction-Independent

Prediction-Informed

Prediction-Sensitive

Prediction-Dependent

Prediction-Determined

This is distinct from the general Reliance Degree and may be used to expose structural dependency.

45. Reliance Concentration

A decision may rely upon multiple inputs while one prediction effectively dominates the result.

RLCM should identify such concentration.

Multiple Inputs ≠ Distributed Decision Basis

46. Cascading Reliance

One prediction may influence multiple downstream decisions.

Example:

Demand Forecast → Procurement → Hiring → Infrastructure Expansion → Financing

Reliance risk may therefore propagate.

One Prediction ≠ One Reliance Event

47. Reliance Chain

A Reliance Chain identifies downstream processes materially dependent upon the prediction.

Each link should preserve:

prediction identity; prediction version; interpretation boundary; reliance degree; relevant qualifications.

48. Reliance Propagation

Predictive reliance may propagate between systems.

Example:

Forecasting System → Planning AI → Procurement Agent → Supplier Commitment

The downstream system must not assume greater predictive authority than the upstream prediction supports.

Reliance Propagation ≠ Reliance Authority Expansion

49. Human Reliance

Human decision-makers may over-rely on predictions because of:

numerical precision; institutional reputation; AI presentation; confidence language; automation bias; perceived expertise.

RLCM requires reliance legitimacy to derive from governed conditions rather than perceived authority.

50. Automation Bias

Automation Bias occurs when users assign greater credibility or authority to predictive output because it was produced or presented by an automated system.

Automated Prediction ≠ Superior Prediction

51. AI-Assisted Reliance

AI systems may combine predictive information with other inputs.

The prediction must remain identifiable where it materially contributes to the AI-generated recommendation or decision.

AI Synthesis ≠ Prediction Independence

52. Autonomous Reliance

An autonomous system may rely upon a prediction to initiate action.

The level of autonomy does not increase the prediction's authority.

Autonomous Execution ≠ Autonomous Predictive Authority

53. Determinative Automated Reliance

Where a prediction automatically triggers consequential action, RLCM requires explicit governance of:

threshold; consequence; reversibility; override;

escalation; monitoring; prediction freshness; failure state.

54. Human Override

Where appropriate, reliance may require human override capability.

Human override does not automatically solve poor prediction governance.

Human-in-the-Loop ≠ Governed Reliance by Default

55. Escalation Requirement

Reliance may require escalation when:

consequence severity is high; uncertainty exceeds threshold; assumptions deteriorate; predictions conflict; Prediction Basis changes materially; interpretation is Boundary-Sensitive; reliance becomes determinative.

56. Reliance Safeguards

Safeguards may include:

independent review; secondary prediction; manual approval; reversible action; staged deployment; exposure limits; monitoring; stop conditions; override; escalation.

Safeguards can support legitimate reliance.

They do not repair an illegitimate prediction.

57. Staged Reliance

Instead of full commitment, a decision may use staged reliance.

Example:

Prediction → Pilot → Observation → Expanded Action

This may reduce consequence exposure while preserving decision utility.

Partial Reliance ≠ Weak Governance

58. Reversible Reliance

Where uncertainty is material, reversible actions may permit greater practical use of prediction without requiring determinative commitment.

RLCM distinguishes:

Prediction Reliance

from:

Irreversible Prediction Dependence

59. Conditional Reliance

Reliance may be legitimate only while specified conditions remain true.

Example:

Reliance permitted while assumption A remains valid and operational metric B remains within threshold.

If conditions fail, reliance must be reassessed.

60. Reliance Expiration

Reliance legitimacy may expire due to:

time; Prediction Horizon; new information; assumption change; revision; supersession; changed operational context.

Prediction Record Persistence ≠ Reliance Persistence

61. Reliance Reassessment Trigger

RLCM should identify triggers requiring reassessment.

Triggers may include:

material new evidence; Prediction Basis change; assumption change; probability revision; feasibility change; operational environment change; decision consequence change; prediction revision; supersession.

62. Reliance Conflict

Reliance Conflict occurs where different governance conditions point toward materially different reliance conclusions.

Example:

high probability + high uncertainty + irreversible critical consequence

The conflict should remain visible.

It must not be compressed into a simplistic single signal.

63. Reliance Qualification

A Reliance Qualification communicates the conditions under which reliance remains legitimate.

Examples:

Supporting Reliance Only

Human Review Required

Independent Corroboration Required

Reversible Action Only

Monitoring Required

Reliance Restricted Above Defined Exposure

64. Reliance Restriction

RLCM may restrict:

reliance degree; affected population; decision type; financial exposure; automation level; action duration; geographic scope; operational scope.

Restriction does not necessarily invalidate the prediction.

Reliance Restriction ≠ Prediction Invalidity

65. Reliance Prohibition

Where contemplated reliance materially exceeds what the governed prediction and context can support, RLCM may determine:

Reliance Not Supported

This does not establish that the prediction is false.

It establishes that the contemplated dependency is not methodologically justified.

66. Reliance Legitimacy States

RLCM may classify reliance as:

Reliance Supported

Reliance Supported with Qualification

Conditional Reliance

Supporting Reliance Only

Reliance Restricted

Reliance Not Supported

Unable to Determine

These states govern reliance legitimacy, not predictive correctness.

67. Reliance Sufficiency

Reliance Sufficiency describes whether enough governed information exists to determine an appropriate reliance state.

Reliance Sufficiency ≠ Prediction Confidence

Unable to Determine ≠ Reliance Supported

68. Reliance Documentation

Material reliance should preserve:

prediction reference; prediction version; Reliance Object; decision context; reliance degree; decision dependency; consequence severity; reversibility; safeguards; qualifications; reliance state; rationale.

69. Historical Reliance

The reliance conditions applicable at the time of a decision should remain reconstructable.

Later predictive performance must not rewrite the legitimacy of reliance as it existed when the decision was made.

Correct Outcome ≠ Legitimate Historical Reliance by Default

Incorrect Outcome ≠ Illegitimate Historical Reliance by Default

70. Outcome Bias

Outcome Bias occurs when the quality of a historical decision is judged solely by whether the prediction proved correct.

RLCM separates:

Reliance Legitimacy at Decision Time

from:

Prediction Outcome Known Later

This is essential for fair predictive governance.

71. Reliance Versioning

Where prediction, context, safeguards, or decision conditions change, the Reliance Determination should be versioned.

Reassessment ≠ Historical Replacement

73. Governance Logic

RLCM applies the following operational logic:

If contemplated reliance remains within the Interpretation Boundary, is proportionate to predictive support, aligned with purpose and horizon, and appropriate to consequence severity and reversibility → Reliance Supported.

If reliance is legitimate only when specific qualifications or safeguards remain in place → Reliance Supported with Qualification.

If reliance remains legitimate only while specified predictive, contextual, monitoring, or operational conditions remain true → Conditional Reliance.

If the prediction may legitimately inform the decision but should not become a material or primary decision basis → Supporting Reliance Only.

If contemplated reliance must be limited by exposure, scope, automation level, decision type, population, duration, or other boundary → Reliance Restricted.

If contemplated reliance materially exceeds what the governed prediction and context can support → Reliance Not Supported.

If sufficient information is unavailable to establish reliance legitimacy → Unable to Determine.

78. Use Case 1 — 85% Failure Prediction Triggers Automatic Shutdown

An industrial AI system predicts:

85% probability that a critical production unit will fail within the next 72 hours.

The prediction is methodologically governed and remains within its valid Prediction Horizon.

Management proposes:

Probability ≥ 80% → Automatically shut down the entire facility.

A shutdown would:

stop production for several days; affect contractual deliveries; create substantial financial loss; require a complex restart; affect connected production systems.

The critical governance question is:

Does an 85% governed probability itself justify determinative automated reliance?

RLCM identifies:

High Probability ≠ Automatic Decision Authority

The prediction may strongly support intervention.

But the proposed reliance is:

Determinative Reliance

with:

High Consequence

and:

Difficult-to-Reverse operational effects.

RLCM may therefore determine:

Reliance Supported with Qualification

subject to:

independent sensor verification;

engineering review; predefined shutdown criteria; human authorization; continuous monitoring.

The prediction remains legitimate.

What changes is the legitimate degree of reliance.

Prediction Legitimacy ≠ Reliance Legitimacy

Probability ≠ Reliance Authority

79. Use Case 2 — AI Investment Agent Relies on a Strong Collective Prediction

An autonomous investment system receives a collective prediction:

74% probability that Company X will receive regulatory approval within six months.

The collective contains:

12,000 market participants; several forecasting models; strong apparent agreement.

CPGS governance, however, identifies:

substantial concentration among several large market positions; common information sources across multiple forecasting models; significant Predictor–Reality Proximity among several contributors; limited independent corroboration.

The autonomous agent proposes allocating:

40% of the portfolio to Company X

based primarily on the 74% prediction.

The critical governance question is:

Can a legitimate collective prediction support this degree of autonomous financial reliance?

RLCM separates:

Collective Prediction Legitimacy

from:

Reliance Legitimacy

and evaluates:

concentration; independence; financial exposure; reversibility; portfolio dependency; uncertainty; alternative predictions; corroboration.

The prediction may remain a legitimate collective predictive input while the contemplated 40% allocation represents excessive reliance.

RLCM may determine:

Reliance Restricted

and require:

lower exposure; independent corroboration; human authorization above a defined allocation threshold; monitoring of regulatory conditions; reassessment if the prediction changes materially.

Collective Prediction ≠ Automatically Stronger Reliance Basis

Market Price ≠ Decision Authority

A prediction may legitimately inform a decision without legitimately determining it.

80. Canonical Boundary

RLCM governs whether, under what conditions, and to what degree a Governed Prediction may legitimately be relied upon within a defined decision or operational context. It governs Reliance Degree, Decision Dependency, predictive support, consequence severity, consequence asymmetry, reversibility, recoverability, purpose and horizon alignment, uncertainty context, feasibility context, assumption dependency, current predictive state, collective-prediction context, independent corroboration, Predictive Conflict, Reliance Concentration, Reliance Chains, AI-assisted and autonomous reliance, safeguards, Conditional Reliance, Reliance Restriction, Reliance Legitimacy State, reassessment, documentation, historical reliance, and Outcome Bias. It does not form or validate the prediction, determine Prediction Methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, probability, Prediction Feasibility, Collective Prediction legitimacy, establish Decision Authority, make the final decision, authorize execution, establish Operational Reality, or determine predictive performance.

PRSM governs Prediction Representation & Signaling.

PLDIM governs Prediction-Led Decision Interpretation.

PIBM governs Prediction Interpretation Boundary.

RLCM governs Reliance Legitimacy & Context.

PUBM governs the final Prediction Use Boundary.

81. Canonical Closing Statement

The existence of a well-governed prediction does not create an obligation to trust it, follow it, or build a consequential decision around it.

RLCM governs the point at which predictive information becomes operational dependence by testing whether the degree of reliance remains proportionate to predictive support, uncertainty, consequences, reversibility, safeguards, and the context in which the prediction is being used.

A prediction may inform a decision. It earns no automatic right to determine one.
