Prediction Communication & Reliance

Canonical Definition

Prediction Communication & Reliance Standard (PCRS) defines the governance conditions under which a Governed Prediction may be communicated, represented, interpreted, distributed, contextualized, and relied upon without exceeding what its methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, Probability & Uncertainty, feasibility, intended purpose, Predictive Boundary, Prediction Horizon, and applicable governance qualifications legitimately support.

PCRS governs the transition from:

Governed Prediction

to:

Governed Prediction Representation → Governed Interpretation → Governed Reliance

It establishes what a prediction may legitimately communicate, what meaning recipients may reasonably derive from it, what qualifications must remain attached to it, and what degree or type of reliance is justified by its governed state.

PCRS prevents prediction outputs from acquiring stronger authority through communication than they possessed when governed.

Its foundational principle is:

A prediction must not become stronger in communication than it is in methodology.

Core Question

How may a prediction be communicated, interpreted, and relied upon without exceeding what its methodology, uncertainty, feasibility, intended purpose, and governed conditions legitimately support?

Governance Position Within PGA™

PCRS governs the communication-and-reliance transition within the Prediction Lifecycle.

The PGA™ Governance Flow is:

Prediction Object & Purpose → Prediction Methodology → Prediction Basis → Prediction Assumptions → Prediction Formation → Prediction Probability & Uncertainty → Prediction Feasibility → Collective Prediction where applicable → Communication & Reliance → Monitoring & Revision → Outcome & Performance → Prediction Record & Continuity

PCRS receives a Governed Prediction after individual or, where applicable, collective prediction governance has been completed.

Its governance responsibility is not to change the prediction.

Its responsibility is to ensure that the prediction is:

represented accurately; interpreted within its governed meaning; relied upon only to the extent legitimately supported.

Prediction Communication

Prediction Communication is the act or process through which a prediction is made available to another human, organization, system, agent, decision process, platform, or public audience.

Communication may occur through:

reports; dashboards; APIs; alerts; recommendations; market interfaces; automated systems; decision-support systems; public statements; internal briefings; machine-readable outputs.

Communication ≠ Neutral Transmission

The way a prediction is communicated may materially change how it is perceived.

Prediction Representation

Prediction Representation is the form in which the governed prediction is presented.

It may include:

numerical probability; qualitative likelihood; scenario; range; confidence statement; risk category; ranking; recommendation context; visual indicator; machine-readable state.

Representation ≠ Underlying Prediction

A representation is an expression of the prediction.

It must not alter the governed meaning of the underlying predictive object.

Prediction Meaning Preservation

The meaning established through prediction governance should survive communication.

Communication Compression ≠ Permission to Remove Material Meaning

Prediction Object Preservation

The communicated prediction must continue to refer to the governed Prediction Object.

Communication ≠ Permission to Broaden the Prediction Object

Outcome Definition Preservation

The predicted outcome should remain consistent with the outcome originally governed.

Simplified Language ≠ Broader Outcome Claim

Prediction Horizon Preservation

The applicable Prediction Horizon should remain available where material.

Probability Without Horizon ≠ Complete Predictive Meaning

Predictive Boundary Preservation

Material boundaries should remain attached or accessible.

These may include:

geography; jurisdiction; population; organization; system; operational scope; market scope.

Prediction Without Boundary Context ≠ Universally Applicable Prediction

Methodology Context

A recipient should be able to determine, at a level appropriate to use, what methodology underlies the prediction.

PCRS does not require every communication to reproduce the full methodology.

It requires that communication not conceal or contradict the methodological basis relevant to interpretation.

Simplified Communication ≠ Methodology-Free Communication

Prediction Basis Context

Where material to reliance, the character of the Prediction Basis should remain accessible.

Prediction Basis Omission ≠ Prediction Basis Irrelevance

Assumption Context

Material assumptions may need to accompany or remain accessible from the communicated prediction.

Hidden Material Assumption ≠ Legitimate Reliance Condition

Probability Representation

Where a prediction contains probability, communication must preserve its governed meaning under PPUS.

Probability ≠ Certainty

Probability ≠ Confidence

Confidence ≠ Certainty

High Probability ≠ Guaranteed Outcome

Probability Number ≠ Complete Predictive Meaning

Uncertainty Communication

Material uncertainty should not be removed merely to make the prediction easier to communicate.

Uncertainty Omission ≠ Uncertainty Removal

Range Communication

Where prediction meaning depends upon a range, communicating only a point estimate may create false precision.

Point Estimate ≠ Complete Distribution by Default

Conditionality Communication

Predictions dependent upon explicit conditions should preserve those conditions where material.

Conditional Prediction ≠ Unconditional Prediction

Feasibility Communication

Prediction Feasibility should remain distinguishable from Prediction Probability.

Feasible ≠ Probable

Probable ≠ Feasible

Conditionally Feasible ≠ Unconditionally Feasible

Feasibility Restriction ≠ Full Feasibility

Collective Prediction Communication

Where CPGS applies, collective qualifications should survive communication.

These may concern:

source diversity; predictive dependence; aggregation methodology; influence concentration; representativeness; collective legitimacy.

Collective Prediction ≠ Population Belief by Default

Consensus ≠ Independent Consensus by Default

Market-Implied Probability ≠ Population Belief

Prediction Qualification

A Prediction Qualification is a material condition, limitation, uncertainty, restriction, methodological characteristic, or contextual statement necessary to prevent overinterpretation.

Qualification ≠ Disclaimer

A qualification defines the legitimate meaning of the prediction.

It should not be treated as peripheral legal language detached from the prediction itself.

Material Qualification

A qualification is material where its omission could reasonably change the interpretation, reliance, or decision significance of the prediction.

Material Qualification Omitted ≠ Equivalent Prediction Representation

Qualification Persistence

Material qualifications should remain attached or reliably accessible when a prediction is redistributed.

Prediction Redistribution ≠ Qualification Removal

Prediction Label

A Prediction Label may describe the governed status or nature of a prediction.

Examples may include:

preliminary; governed; conditional; restricted; market-derived;

collectively aggregated; model-generated; expert-generated.

Label ≠ Governance Substitute

A label does not replace underlying governance.

Prediction Status Representation

The lifecycle state of the prediction should remain distinguishable where relevant.

Examples include:

Current

Under Review

Revised

Superseded

Withdrawn

Superseded Prediction ≠ Current Prediction

Withdrawn Prediction ≠ Active Prediction

Interpretation

Interpretation is the meaning assigned to a prediction by a recipient, system, decision process, or downstream application.

PCRS governs conditions under which that interpretation remains consistent with the prediction's governed meaning.

Communication ≠ Interpretation

A prediction may be communicated correctly and still be interpreted incorrectly.

Interpretation ≠ Prediction Formation

The recipient does not recreate the prediction merely by interpreting it.

Interpretation Boundary

An Interpretation Boundary defines what the prediction legitimately supports.

Prediction Supports X ≠ Prediction Supports Every Decision Related to X

Interpretation Scope

A prediction may be appropriate for one decision context but inappropriate for another.

General Availability ≠ Universal Decision Applicability

Intended Purpose

The prediction's intended purpose should remain materially relevant to interpretation and reliance.

Use Beyond Intended Purpose ≠ Automatically Legitimate Use

Purpose Expansion

A prediction produced for one purpose may later be used for another.

Purpose Expansion Requires Governance

Prediction Recontextualization

Recontextualization occurs where a prediction is used in a context different from the one in which it was governed.

Same Prediction + New Context ≠ Same Reliance Meaning by Default

Audience Context

Different audiences may interpret identical predictive representations differently.

Relevant audiences may include:

experts; executives; regulators; public users; automated agents; operators; investors; consumers.

Same Communication ≠ Same Interpretation Across Audiences

Human Interpretation

Human recipients may overread precision, confidence, authority, or certainty.

Machine Interpretation

Machine-readable prediction outputs may be consumed automatically by downstream systems.

Machine Readability ≠ Machine Interpretability

Automated Interpretation

Automated systems should not infer stronger claims than the governed prediction supports.

Machine Consumption ≠ Permission to Remove Context

Prediction Reliance

Prediction Reliance is the degree to which a human, organization, AI system, autonomous agent, or decision process uses a prediction to inform, shape, trigger, constrain, prioritize, or justify action.

Reliance ≠ Mere Observation

A prediction may be viewed without being materially relied upon.

Prediction Use

Prediction Use may range from informational awareness to direct automated action.

Use ≠ Reliance at Equal Intensity

Reliance Intensity

Reliance may occur at different levels.

Examples include:

Informational Reliance

Advisory Reliance

Decision-Support Reliance

High-Consequence Reliance

Automated Operational Reliance

Same Prediction ≠ Same Legitimate Reliance Level in Every Context

Reliance Threshold

A Reliance Threshold defines the minimum governance conditions required before a prediction may legitimately support a specified type or level of reliance.

Availability ≠ Reliance Eligibility

Reliance Eligibility

Reliance Eligibility concerns whether the prediction's governed state sufficiently supports a particular intended use.

Prediction Exists ≠ Prediction Is Reliance- Eligible

Reliance Context

Legitimate reliance depends upon context.

Relevant factors may include:

decision consequence; reversibility; time sensitivity; operational criticality; affected population; automation level; availability of alternatives; uncertainty; feasibility; methodology quality; Prediction Horizon.

Low-Consequence Reliance ≠ High-Consequence Reliance

High-Consequence Reliance

Where a prediction materially influences safety-critical, financial, legal, medical, infrastructure, public-policy, or similarly consequential decisions, stronger governance conditions may be required.

High Probability ≠ Sufficient Basis for High- Consequence Reliance

Automated Reliance

A prediction may directly trigger automated action.

Prediction Output ≠ Automatic Execution Authority

Automated Action Threshold

Where a prediction is used to trigger autonomous or automated action, the relevant threshold should remain explicit.

Threshold Crossing ≠ Certainty

Decision Support

Predictions may support rather than determine decisions.

Prediction Input ≠ Decision

Prediction Recommendation

A prediction may be embedded within a recommendation.

Prediction ≠ Recommendation

A recommendation may involve:

objectives; values; preferences; constraints; risk tolerance; policy.

PCRS does not collapse these into prediction governance.

Reliance Authority

The fact that a prediction is governable does not itself grant authority to act upon it.

Governed Prediction ≠ Decision Authority

Reliance Responsibility

Responsibility for a downstream decision does not automatically transfer to the predictive system merely because the prediction influenced that decision.

Prediction Influence ≠ Decision Responsibility by Default

AGA™ and other applicable accountability architectures govern responsibility.

Overreliance

Overreliance occurs where a prediction is given more decision significance than its governed state legitimately supports.

Overreliance ≠ High Reliance Alone

High reliance may be legitimate where governance conditions support it.

Underreliance

Underreliance may occur where a well-governed prediction is systematically ignored despite legitimate relevance.

PCRS recognizes the concept but does not require reliance.

Governed Prediction ≠ Mandatory Reliance

Automation Bias

Users or systems may treat machine-generated predictions as inherently superior.

AI-Generated Prediction ≠ Superior Prediction by Default

Authority Bias

Predictions from prestigious institutions or experts may receive excessive weight.

Source Prestige ≠ Predictive Certainty

Consensus Bias

Collective agreement may be interpreted as certainty.

Consensus ≠ Certainty

Precision Bias

Precise numbers may appear more reliable than the underlying methodology warrants.

Numerical Precision ≠ Predictive Reliability

Recency Bias

A recently updated prediction may be preferred merely because it is newer.

Newer Prediction ≠ Better Prediction by Default

Reliance Escalation

As prediction reliance increases in consequence or automation, governance requirements should increase proportionally.

Greater Reliance ≠ Same Governance Requirement

Reliance Proportionality

Reliance should remain proportionate to:

prediction governance quality; uncertainty; feasibility; intended purpose; decision consequence; reversibility.

Reliance Proportionality ≠ Prediction Accuracy Guarantee

Prediction Misrepresentation

Misrepresentation occurs where communicated meaning materially exceeds the governed prediction.

Examples include:

probability represented as certainty; conditional prediction represented as unconditional;

collective market signal represented as population belief; limited-scope prediction represented as universal; feasibility-restricted prediction represented as fully achievable.

Prediction Misrepresentation ≠ Prediction Error

A prediction can be communicated incorrectly even if it later proves accurate.

Prediction Exaggeration

Prediction Exaggeration occurs where language strengthens the apparent predictive claim beyond its governed basis.

Stronger Wording ≠ Stronger Prediction

Prediction Minimization

Material limitations should not be buried or weakened so that the prediction appears simpler or stronger.

Visual Representation

Charts, colors, scores, rankings, and dashboards may influence interpretation.

Visual Simplicity ≠ Governance Neutrality

Visual Salience

A prominent headline probability may dominate less visible qualification.

Visible Number + Hidden Qualification ≠ Balanced Prediction Representation

Ranking Representation

Predictions may be ranked.

Higher Rank ≠ Greater Certainty by Default

Risk Category Representation

Low / Medium / High

Narrative Representation

Headline Representation

Reliance Traceability

Canonical Boundary

PCRS governs how a Governed Prediction may be represented, communicated, interpreted, distributed, contextualized, qualified, and relied upon without exceeding what its methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, Probability & Uncertainty, feasibility, intended purpose, Prediction Horizon, Predictive Boundary, collective governance state, and lifecycle status legitimately support. It governs Prediction Representation, material qualifications, interpretation boundaries, intended-purpose alignment, recontextualization, audience and machine interpretation, Reliance Eligibility, reliance intensity, high-consequence and automated reliance, overreliance, misrepresentation, escalation conditions, redistribution, communication states, reliance states, and material communication or reliance traceability. It does not form or revise predictions, determine Prediction Basis, govern Predictive Assumptions, calculate Probability & Uncertainty, determine Feasibility, establish Collective Prediction legitimacy, determine Operational Reality, assign decision authority or accountability, determine outcomes, or assess predictive performance.

PCRS governs:

what a prediction may legitimately communicate and how far that prediction may legitimately be relied upon.

It does not determine:

whether the predicted future will occur or what decision a recipient must ultimately make.

Canonical Closing Statement

A prediction can remain methodologically unchanged while becoming dangerously stronger through the way it is presented, interpreted, or acted upon.

PCRS preserves the boundary between what a prediction actually establishes and what humans, organizations, AI systems, and autonomous systems are entitled to infer or do because of it.

A governed prediction must carry its limits all the way from prediction to reliance.
