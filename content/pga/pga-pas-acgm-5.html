Assumption Change Governance

Canonical Definition

Assumption Change Governance Module (ACGM) defines the governance conditions under which changes in Predictive Assumptions are detected, classified, assessed, propagated through relevant dependency structures, associated with their sensitivity and materiality consequences, preserved historically, and translated into governed assumption-state outcomes and prediction-level review signals.

ACGM governs what happens when an assumption that supports a prediction no longer remains in the same governed state.

It transforms:

Governed Assumption Set + Dependency Map + Sensitivity Profile + Materiality Profile

into:

Governed Assumption Change State

and determines whether the change requires:

continued observation; assumption reassessment; qualification; restriction; escalation; prediction-level review trigger.

ACGM does not itself revise, supersede, or withdraw the prediction.

Those actions belong later to:

PMRS — Prediction Monitoring & Revision Standard.

Its foundational distinction is:

Assumption Change ≠ Automatic Prediction Revision

Operational Role

ACGM serves as the fifth and final internal governance space of the Prediction Assumption Standard (PAS) .

It receives:

Identified Predictive Assumptions

Governed Assumption Dependency Map

Governed Assumption Sensitivity Profile

Governed Assumption Materiality Profile

and governs material changes in those assumptions.

The module asks:

What changed in the assumption, how significant is that change, what does it affect, and does the prediction now require broader review?

The governed progression is:

Governed Assumption State → Change Detection → Change Classification → Materiality Reassessment → Dependency Propagation → Assumption State Transition → Historical Preservation → Prediction-Level Review Signal

The primary output is:

Governed Assumption Change Record

and, where applicable:

Prediction Review Trigger

This completes the internal PAS flow and provides the governed assumption structure required by:

PFOS — Prediction Formation Standard

and later by:

PMRS — Prediction Monitoring & Revision Standard.

Module Function

A prediction may be well governed at formation time.

Its assumptions may be explicit.

Their dependencies may be mapped.

Sensitivity may be understood.

Materiality may be determined.

But the world can change.

A supplier fails.

A regulation changes.

A market regime shifts.

A system configuration changes.

A historical relationship weakens.

A previously stable operating condition disappears.

At that point, assumption governance cannot remain static.

ACGM determines how the assumption state changes and what downstream governance consequences must become visible.

Assumption Change

An Assumption Change occurs when a condition represented by a Predictive Assumption changes materially relative to the governed state under which the prediction was formed or maintained.

Change may affect:

assumption truth state; assumption plausibility; assumption support; assumption scope; assumption boundary; assumption applicability; assumption timing; dependency structure; uncertainty;

sensitivity; materiality.

Change Detection

Change may be detected through:

new Prediction Basis information; Operational Reality updates; monitoring systems; human review; model signals; external events; policy updates; system-state changes; supplier changes; market changes; event occurrence; scheduled reassessment.

Detection does not automatically determine consequence.

Change Detection ≠ Material Change Determination

Assumption-State Transition

A Predictive Assumption may move between governed states.

Potential transitions include:

Holding → Conditionally Holding

Holding → Uncertain

Holding → Under Challenge

Holding → Breached

Conditionally Holding → Holding

Uncertain → Holding

Uncertain → Breached

Breached → Restored

Holding → No Longer Applicable

Unable to Determine → Determined

State transitions must remain historically identifiable.

Assumption Weakening

An assumption may weaken without fully failing.

Example:

A supplier expected to remain operational begins experiencing severe financial stress but continues delivering.

The assumption may move from:

Holding

to:

Conditionally Holding

or:

Under Challenge.

Assumption Weakening ≠ Assumption Breach

Assumption Strengthening

New information may strengthen an assumption.

Example:

A supplier expected to remain operational receives long-term financing and confirms secured production capacity.

Assumption strengthening may reduce uncertainty or change monitoring requirements.

Assumption Breach

An Assumption Breach occurs where the governed condition represented by the assumption no longer holds within its defined boundary.

Example:

Assumption:

The primary production line remains operational during the Prediction Horizon.

Operational Reality:

The production line is shut down indefinitely.

The assumption is breached.

Assumption Breach ≠ Prediction Error

The prediction may still happen to produce the correct outcome through another pathway.

Assumption Invalidation

An assumption may become invalid because its underlying basis, scope, methodology relationship, or governing conditions no longer support its use.

Invalidation may occur even before a clear real-world breach.

Example:

A methodology-dependent continuity assumption becomes unusable after a structural regime change.

Assumption Expiry

Some assumptions are valid only for defined periods.

When temporal applicability ends, the assumption may expire.

Assumption Expiry ≠ Assumption Breach

Expiry reflects the end of governed applicability rather than contradiction.

Assumption Contradiction

New information may directly challenge an assumption.

A contradiction may result in:

uncertainty; challenge state; breach review; materiality reassessment; prediction-review trigger.

Not every contradiction proves the assumption false.

Partial Assumption Change

A compound or bounded assumption may change only partially.

Example:

Supplier capacity remains between 90% and 100%.

Capacity falls to 82%.

The supplier remains operational, but the assumption no longer holds as stated.

ACGM preserves partial change rather than forcing false binary interpretation.

Threshold Crossing

Assumption change may cross a threshold defined under ASNM or AMAM.

Examples:

sensitivity threshold; materiality threshold; criticality threshold; operating-state threshold.

Threshold crossing may trigger stronger governance action.

Threshold Crossing ≠ Automatic Prediction Withdrawal

Material Assumption Change

A Material Assumption Change occurs where assumption-state change may materially alter:

predicted outcome; methodology applicability; probability; uncertainty; feasibility; interpretation; reliance conditions; prediction governability.

Such change requires explicit governance treatment.

Critical Assumption Change

A Critical Assumption Change occurs where a Critical Assumption:

materially weakens; is breached; becomes unable to determine; loses applicability; crosses a critical threshold.

Such changes may require immediate prediction-level review.

Change Classification

ACGM may classify assumption change as:

Immaterial

Minor

Material

Critical

Temporary

Persistent

Reversible

Irreversible

Conditionally Resolved

Unable to Determine

Multiple classifications may apply simultaneously.

Temporary Change

A temporary assumption change may be expected to revert.

Example:

A short-term infrastructure interruption.

Temporary does not automatically mean immaterial.

Temporary Change ≠ Immaterial Change

Persistent Change

A persistent change remains beyond the expected temporary window or establishes a new operating condition.

Persistent change may require assumption redefinition or supersession.

Reversible Change

A reversible change can return to a prior governed state.

Example:

A temporarily unavailable system is restored.

Irreversible Change

An irreversible change permanently alters the assumption context.

Examples:

legal authorization permanently revoked; facility destroyed; supplier permanently exits market; methodology permanently retired.

Irreversibility may materially affect future prediction continuation.

Change Source

Where material, ACGM identifies the source of assumption change.

Possible sources include:

Operational Reality; Prediction Basis update; policy change; human decision; system change; market change; model change; external event; new evidence reference; time expiry.

Change Source ≠ Change Consequence

Change Timing

The timing of assumption change matters.

A change:

before Prediction Formation

may prevent legitimate formation.

A change:

after formation but before reliance

may require re-evaluation.

A change:

after reliance

may affect monitoring and revision.

A change:

after outcome

may matter only for historical interpretation.

Pre-Formation Assumption Change

Where an assumption changes before Prediction Formation, PAS must update the governed assumption structure before PFOS proceeds.

The prediction should not be formed using an assumption state already known to be outdated.

Post-Formation Assumption Change

Where an assumption changes after formation, the original assumption state must remain preserved.

The new state becomes part of continuing governance.

Current Assumption State ≠ Original Assumption State

Dependency Propagation

Assumption change may affect dependent assumptions.

ACGM uses the dependency structure established by ADMM to identify downstream consequences.

Example:

Supplier Insolvency

↓

Supplier Continuity Assumption Breached

↓

Component Availability Assumption Weakens

↓

Production Continuity Assumption Weakens

↓

Output Prediction Requires Review

Propagation Depth

Change may propagate through multiple dependency layers.

A change should not be considered isolated merely because direct predictive impact is initially small.

Propagation Scope

ACGM determines which assumptions or predictive conditions may be affected by a change.

Potential propagation targets include:

other assumptions; Prediction Formation conditions; probability;

uncertainty; feasibility; reliance conditions.

Propagation Containment

Alternative pathways may contain assumption change.

Example:

Primary supplier fails, but qualified backup supplier fully replaces capacity.

The upstream assumption is breached.

Downstream prediction impact may remain limited.

Assumption Breach ≠ Unavoidable Downstream Failure

Sensitivity Reassessment

A changed assumption may alter its sensitivity relationship.

For example:

An assumption near a threshold may move into a region of much higher predictive sensitivity.

ACGM can trigger:

ASNM Reassessment

where necessary.

Materiality Reassessment

Assumption materiality may change as:

dependency structure changes; alternatives disappear; sensitivity changes; Prediction Purpose changes; reliance increases; Prediction Horizon shortens or extends.

ACGM can trigger:

AMAM Reassessment

where necessary.

Change Propagation vs Sensitivity

ADMM establishes:

where change can travel.

ASNM establishes:

how the prediction may respond.

AMAM establishes:

how important that consequence is.

ACGM governs:

what happens when the change actually occurs.

Assumption Reassessment Trigger

A reassessment trigger may include:

new contradictory information; threshold crossing; dependency change; elapsed review interval; operational disruption; methodology change; source deterioration; regulatory change; critical external event.

Monitoring Trigger

A material assumption may require continued observation after a change.

Monitoring may increase where:

uncertainty rises; critical threshold approaches; assumption remains conditionally holding; restoration is expected but unconfirmed.

Escalation Trigger

Assumption change may require escalation where:

critical assumptions are breached; multiple material assumptions change simultaneously; dependency propagation becomes extensive; assumption state cannot be determined; prediction remains in active reliance.

Escalation does not itself determine the final prediction action.

Prediction Review Trigger

A Prediction Review Trigger is a governed signal indicating that assumption change may be sufficiently significant to require broader prediction-level assessment under PMRS.

Potential triggers include:

Critical Assumption Breach; multiple Material Assumption Changes; methodology-applicability loss; feasibility deterioration; major uncertainty expansion; material reliance impact; severe dependency propagation.

Prediction Review Trigger ≠ Prediction Revision Decision

PMRS determines the prediction-level response.

Prediction Formation Relationship

Where material assumption change occurs before Prediction Formation, PFOS must receive the current governed assumption state.

PFOS must not silently consume superseded assumptions.

Probability & Uncertainty Relationship

Assumption change may alter:

probability; uncertainty; confidence; prediction ranges.

PPUS governs the resulting representation.

ACGM provides the relevant assumption-change signal.

Feasibility Relationship

Assumption change may materially affect whether the predicted outcome remains achievable.

PFES governs feasibility determination.

ACGM identifies the assumption change requiring that consideration.

Communication & Reliance Relationship

Where assumption changes materially alter permitted interpretation or reliance, PCRS may require updated communication or reliance conditions.

ACGM does not itself communicate the prediction.

Monitoring & Revision Relationship

PMRS governs:

prediction review; revision; supersession; withdrawal.

ACGM supplies governed assumption-change conditions that may trigger those processes.

Assumption Change Governance ≠ Prediction Revision Governance

Assumption Restoration

A breached or weakened assumption may later be restored.

Example:

A system outage ends and the system returns to its required operating state.

Restoration should create a new governed state.

It must not erase the prior breach.

Restoration ≠ Historical Erasure

Conditional Restoration

An assumption may appear restored but remain subject to conditions.

Example:

Supplier operations resume at only 60% capacity.

The assumption may move from:

Breached

to:

Conditionally Holding

rather than directly to:

Holding.

Assumption Supersession

Where an assumption is replaced by a materially different assumption, the original assumption should remain historically identifiable.

Example:

Original:

Primary supplier remains operational.

New:

Backup supplier provides at least 80% replacement capacity.

These are not the same assumption state.

A new predictive premise has emerged.

Assumption Re-entry

An assumption that ceased to apply may later become applicable again.

Re-entry should be explicitly governed.

Historical Applicability ≠ Current Applicability

Assumption Version Continuity

Material change may require a new assumption version or state record.

The history should preserve:

Initial State → Changed State → Breached State → Restored / Superseded / Retired State

where applicable.

Historical Change Integrity

Later information must not rewrite the assumption history.

A prediction formed under:

Assumption State A

must remain associated with State A even if the assumption later moves to:

State B.

Current Assumption State ≠ Historical Assumption State

Retrospective Change Discovery

A change may be discovered only later.

Example:

An organization learns after two weeks that a supplier had already lost a required authorization five days before the prediction was formed.

The governance record should distinguish:

Actual Change Time

from:

Change Discovery Time

This distinction is essential.

Change Time ≠ Discovery Time

A condition may change before anyone becomes aware of it.

ACGM preserves both times where material.

Unknown Change Time

The exact time of change may sometimes be unknown.

Where so:

Unknown Change Time ≠ No Change

The uncertainty must remain explicit.

Concurrent Assumption Change

Several assumptions may change simultaneously.

This may create:

cumulative effects; interaction effects; dependency cascades; systemic predictive change.

Concurrent changes should not be assessed only as isolated events where their combined effect is material.

Assumption Change Cascade

A change cascade occurs where one assumption change causes or contributes to several downstream state transitions.

Example:

Regulatory Ban

↓

Product Authorization Assumption Breached

↓

Sales Continuity Assumption Breached

↓

Revenue Assumption Breached

↓

Growth Prediction Requires Review

Change Contagion ≠ Independent Failure

Several assumptions changing together may reflect one upstream cause rather than independent failures.

This distinction helps prevent false interpretation of multiple signals as separate events.

Assumption Change Uncertainty

It may be unclear:

whether change occurred; when it occurred; whether it is temporary; whether it is material; whether restoration occurred.

Possible states may include:

Confirmed

Probable

Possible

Disputed

Unable to Determine

Uncertainty itself may justify heightened governance.

Unresolved Assumption Change

Where material change cannot be sufficiently determined, ACGM may produce:

Assumption Change Governance Gap

Possible responses include:

additional monitoring; clarification; restricted reliance; conditional prediction state; escalation; prediction review.

Assumption Change Closure

A change episode may be considered governed when:

change is identified; classification is established; affected dependencies are mapped; sensitivity/materiality consequences are considered; state transition is recorded; required triggers are issued; historical state is preserved.

Closure does not necessarily mean restoration.

Governance Requirements

ACGM requires that:

1. material assumption changes be identifiable; 2. assumption-state transitions remain explicit; 3. weakening remain distinguishable from breach; 4. breach remain distinguishable from prediction error; 5. expiry remain distinguishable from breach; 6. partial changes remain governable; 7. threshold crossing remain explicit; 8. temporary and persistent changes remain distinguishable; 9. reversible and irreversible changes remain distinguishable where material; 10. change source remain identifiable where relevant; 11. change time and discovery time remain distinguishable; 12. dependency propagation be assessed where material; 13. propagation containment remain visible; 14. sensitivity reassessment be triggered where necessary; 15. materiality reassessment be triggered where necessary; 16. material assumption changes remain distinguishable from immaterial changes; 17. Critical Assumption Changes receive appropriate escalation; 18. prediction-review triggers be generated where necessary; 19. prediction-review triggers remain distinguishable from revision decisions; 20. restoration not erase prior breach; 21. assumption supersession preserve prior assumption history; 22. concurrent changes be assessed collectively where necessary; 23. retrospective discoveries preserve actual and discovery timing; 24. unresolved change uncertainty remain explicit; 25. current and historical assumption states remain reconstructable.

Enterprise Application

ACGM enables organizations to maintain prediction governance after the moment of Prediction Formation.

Without Assumption Change Governance, a prediction may continue to be:

displayed; communicated; relied upon; automated; operationalized

even after one of its fundamental predictive conditions has materially changed.

This is particularly important in:

AI forecasting + supply-chain planning + finance + insurance + infrastructure + industrial systems + healthcare + strategic planning + market prediction + autonomous systems.

ACGM creates the bridge between:

assumption governance

and:

prediction monitoring and revision.

It ensures that prediction review can be triggered by changes in the conditions that made the prediction meaningful in the first place.

Use Case 1 — The Prediction Is Still Active After Its Critical Supplier Assumption Has Failed

A manufacturer predicts that output will remain above:

50,000 units per month

for the next six months.

One Critical Assumption states:

The sole supplier of a specialized component remains operational and capable of delivering minimum required volume.

Two months later, the supplier enters insolvency and stops production.

The prediction dashboard continues displaying the original forecast because no mechanism links supplier failure to the assumption structure.

The critical governance question is:

Can a prediction remain legitimately active when a Critical Assumption has already been breached but the prediction has no governed mechanism for detecting and propagating that change?

ACGM records:

Assumption State: Holding → Breached

maps the downstream dependency effects,

triggers sensitivity and materiality reassessment where necessary,

and produces:

Prediction Review Trigger: Required

for PMRS.

It does not itself rewrite the prediction.

Assumption Change ≠ Automatic Prediction Revision

Use Case 2 — The Market Forecast Looks Unchanged, but Its Historical Assumption No Longer Holds

An AI system predicts continued customer demand based on several years of stable purchasing behavior.

A core assumption states:

The historical relationship between price, customer behavior, and demand remains sufficiently applicable throughout the Prediction Horizon.

A new regulation fundamentally changes pricing incentives.

Demand has not yet visibly collapsed.

The central forecast remains nearly unchanged.

However, the continuity assumption underlying the predictive methodology is now materially under challenge.

The critical governance question is:

Should a prediction continue to be relied upon until its output visibly fails, or should material change in the assumptions supporting it trigger governance before the error appears?

ACGM moves the assumption from:

Holding

to:

Under Challenge

preserves the regulatory change and timing,

initiates relevant sensitivity and materiality reassessment,

and may generate a Prediction Review Trigger.

Prediction governance should react to material changes in predictive conditions before those changes become historical prediction errors.

Canonical Boundary

ACGM governs the detection, classification, state transition, breach, expiry, invalidation, restoration, dependency propagation, reassessment triggering, escalation, prediction-review signaling, version continuity, timing, and historical preservation of material changes in Predictive Assumptions. It does not govern initial assumption identification, dependency mapping itself, sensitivity assessment itself, materiality determination itself, Prediction Formation, probability representation, feasibility determination, communication and reliance, Operational Reality, or the prediction-level decision to revise, supersede, or withdraw a prediction.

ACGM establishes:

what has changed in the assumptions supporting the prediction and what broader governance must now be triggered.

It does not determine:

the final new prediction state.

Canonical Closing Statement

A governed assumption cannot become a permanent frozen condition inside a changing world.

ACGM ensures that when the conditions beneath a prediction change, the predictive governance structure changes with them — without erasing what was assumed before.

A prediction should not have to fail before governance notices that the assumptions supporting it already have.
