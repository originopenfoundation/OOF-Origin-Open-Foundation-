Canonical Language: UCL — Universal Canonical Language

1. Canonical Definition

The Predictive Purpose Definition Module (PPDM) governs the establishment, specification, differentiation, and maintenance of the purpose for which an identified Prediction Object is subjected to predictive treatment.

PPDM establishes why a prediction is intended to exist and what legitimate predictive function it is expected to serve before its target, horizon, boundary, methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, probability, uncertainty, feasibility, communication, or reliance are subsequently governed.

A Prediction Object may be identifiable without the purpose of predicting it being sufficiently defined.

2. Operational Role

PPDM is the second operational module within POPS.

It receives an identified Prediction Object from POIM — Prediction Object Identification Module and establishes the predictive purpose associated with that object.

Its principal transition is:

Identified Prediction Object → Defined Predictive Purpose

PPDM prevents predictions created for one purpose from being silently interpreted, communicated, or used as though they had been established for another.

3. Internal Module Flow

Prediction Object Identification → → Predictive Purpose Definition → Prediction Target Specification → Prediction Horizon Definition → Predictive Boundary Establishment

Current Position: Predictive Purpose Definition

PPDM receives the identified Prediction Object from POIM and produces the defined Predictive Purpose required by PTSM — Prediction Target Specification Module and subsequent POPS governance.

4. Operational Space

PPDM operates within the Predictive Purpose Definition space.

It governs:

purpose identification; purpose specification; purpose differentiation; intended predictive function; purpose–object alignment; primary and secondary predictive purposes; purpose ambiguity; purpose conflict; purpose change; purpose continuity.

PPDM governs why predictive activity is being undertaken.

It does not govern how the prediction will be produced or how it may ultimately be relied upon.

5. Operational Scope

PPDM applies whenever an identified Prediction Object is intended to support a defined predictive function.

Predictive purposes may include, where applicable:

planning; forecasting; operational preparation; risk anticipation; resource allocation; capacity planning; scenario-informed decision support; market analysis; research; public information; strategic analysis; automated decision support; autonomous-system preparation; early warning; performance forecasting; other sufficiently defined predictive functions.

The existence of a potential use does not automatically establish that use as the governed Predictive Purpose.

6. Predictive Purpose Identification

PPDM must identify why the Prediction Object is being subjected to predictive treatment.

The purpose must express the intended predictive function rather than merely restating the Prediction Object.

For example, identifying a future operational condition does not by itself establish whether the prediction exists for planning, warning, resource allocation, research, public communication, or another purpose.

Prediction Object ≠ Predictive Purpose

7. Purpose Specification

A Predictive Purpose must be sufficiently specified to support subsequent governance.

A generic statement such as “to know the future,” “to predict,” or “for analysis” may be insufficient where it does not establish the function the prediction is expected to perform.

Purpose specification must provide enough clarity for subsequent PGA™ governance to determine whether methodology, target, horizon, boundary, communication, and eventual use remain aligned with the original predictive intent.

General Interest ≠ Defined Predictive Purpose

8. Intended Predictive Function

PPDM establishes the functional role expected from the prediction.

The intended predictive function may influence what degree of specificity, uncertainty representation, methodological rigor, update frequency, communication, or downstream governance becomes relevant later in the Prediction Lifecycle.

PPDM does not itself determine those later requirements.

It establishes the purpose against which they can subsequently be evaluated.

Purpose Definition ≠ Method Selection

9. Object–Purpose Alignment

The Predictive Purpose must remain compatible with the identified Prediction Object.

PPDM must identify material mismatch where the stated purpose requires predictive meaning that the identified object cannot legitimately support.

A prediction concerning one object must not be assigned a purpose that implicitly requires prediction of a materially different object.

Where such mismatch exists, the Prediction Object or Predictive Purpose requires clarification before progression.

Identified Object + Unrelated Purpose ≠ Governed Predictive Alignment

10. Primary Predictive Purpose

Where a prediction may support multiple uses, PPDM should establish the primary Predictive Purpose where one exists.

The primary purpose provides the principal governance context for subsequent predictive treatment.

Secondary uses must not silently redefine the primary purpose or expand the predictive proposition beyond what was originally governed.

Potential Use ≠ Primary Predictive Purpose

11. Multiple Predictive Purposes

A Prediction Object may legitimately support multiple Predictive Purposes.

PPDM must determine whether those purposes:

remain compatible within one governed predictive context; require explicit differentiation; create materially different predictive requirements; or require separate predictive treatment.

Multiple purposes must not be collapsed into a single purpose where doing so obscures materially different predictive expectations.

Same Prediction Object ≠ Same Predictive Purpose

Multiple Purposes ≠ Automatically One Predictive Context

12. Purpose Compatibility

Where multiple purposes coexist, PPDM must preserve their compatibility or explicitly identify their differences.

A prediction intended for exploratory research may not automatically support operational execution.

A prediction intended for internal planning may not automatically support public representation.

A prediction intended for informational awareness may not automatically support consequential automated action.

PPDM establishes the distinction without governing the later reliance decision itself.

13. Purpose Ambiguity

PPDM must identify material ambiguity concerning why the prediction exists.

Purpose ambiguity may arise where:

multiple purposes are implied but not distinguished; stated purpose differs from apparent predictive function; purpose is too broad to guide subsequent governance; different actors assign different purposes to the same prediction; purpose changes without explicit governance; an intended use is represented as though it were the original predictive purpose.

A materially ambiguous purpose must not be treated as fully defined.

14. Purpose Conflict

Multiple predictive purposes may impose incompatible expectations on the same prediction.

PPDM must identify material purpose conflict where satisfying one purpose may undermine, distort, or materially alter the predictive requirements associated with another.

Purpose conflict does not automatically invalidate the prediction.

It requires differentiation, clarification, prioritization, or separate predictive treatment where necessary.

Multiple Legitimate Purposes ≠ Automatically Compatible Purposes

15. Purpose–Target Relationship

Predictive Purpose establishes why the prediction exists.

Prediction Target establishes what specific future outcome, state, condition, event, value, behavior, or development is being predicted.

PPDM must establish sufficient purpose clarity for the target to be specified coherently by PTSM — Prediction Target Specification Module .

PPDM does not itself determine the target.

Predictive Purpose ≠ Prediction Target

16. Purpose–Horizon Relationship

Predictive purpose may materially affect the relevance of the Prediction Horizon.

Short-term operational preparation and long-term strategic planning may concern the same Prediction Object while requiring materially different predictive horizons.

PPDM establishes the purpose context.

PHDM — Prediction Horizon Definition Module governs the actual horizon.

Purpose Context ≠ Prediction Horizon

17. Purpose–Boundary Relationship

Predictive purpose may affect what must ultimately be included within or excluded from the predictive proposition.

PPDM does not establish the Predictive Boundary.

It provides the purpose context necessary for PBEM — Predictive Boundary Establishment Module to determine the appropriate predictive limits.

Predictive Purpose ≠ Predictive Boundary

18. Purpose–Reliance Separation

Predictive Purpose and permitted reliance must remain distinct.

The reason a prediction was produced does not automatically determine every use for which it may legitimately be relied upon.

Reliance governance belongs to PCRS — Prediction Communication & Reliance Standard .

Therefore:

Intended Purpose ≠ Unlimited Permitted Reliance

and:

Prediction Availability ≠ Fitness for Every Purpose

19. Purpose Change

The purpose associated with a prediction may change over time.

PPDM must ensure that a material purpose change remains identifiable rather than being silently represented as continuity of the original predictive purpose.

A purpose change may require later reassessment of:

target; horizon; boundary; methodology; Prediction Basis; assumptions; uncertainty; feasibility; communication; reliance.

The applicable reassessment is governed by the relevant downstream PGA™ standards.

Changed Purpose ≠ Unchanged Predictive Context

20. Purpose Continuity

A Predictive Purpose must remain sufficiently traceable throughout the Prediction Lifecycle to allow later governance to determine whether the prediction continued to serve its original function.

Purpose continuity does not prohibit legitimate evolution.

It requires that material changes remain distinguishable from the original purpose.

This supports later interpretation, reliance, revision, performance assessment, and historical reconstruction.

21. Purpose Definition Sufficiency

Predictive Purpose is sufficiently defined when it:

identifies why predictive treatment is being undertaken; expresses a meaningful predictive function; remains associated with the identified Prediction Object; is sufficiently specific for subsequent target, horizon, and boundary governance; distinguishes materially different purposes where necessary; identifies material purpose conflicts; can remain traceable through subsequent predictive governance.

Purpose definition sufficiency does not establish that the prediction is methodologically valid or fit for every eventual use.

22. Governance Requirements

PPDM requires that:

1. every governed Predictive Purpose be explicitly identifiable; 2. the purpose state why the prediction is intended to exist; 3. the purpose remain distinguishable from the Prediction Object; 4. the purpose be sufficiently specific to support subsequent predictive governance; 5. object–purpose alignment be established; 6. primary and secondary purposes be distinguishable where materially relevant; 7. materially different purposes not be silently collapsed; 8. material purpose conflicts be identified; 9. material purpose changes remain traceable; 10. Predictive Purpose not be interpreted as unlimited authorization for reliance.

24. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum implementation of PPDM must establish:

A. Prediction Object Reference

The identified Prediction Object to which the purpose applies.

B. Predictive Purpose Statement

A clear statement describing why the prediction is being produced.

C. Intended Predictive Function

The principal function expected from the prediction.

D. Primary Purpose

The principal Predictive Purpose where multiple uses exist.

E. Secondary Purposes

Material secondary purposes where applicable.

F. Object–Purpose Alignment

Confirmation that the purpose is compatible with the identified Prediction Object.

G. Purpose Compatibility Status

Where multiple purposes exist, whether they remain compatible within the same predictive context.

H. Purpose Conflict Status

Identification of unresolved material purpose conflicts.

I. Purpose State

At minimum:

Undefined → Candidate → Clarification Required → Defined → Changed

A purpose treated as Defined must be sufficiently established for subsequent POPS governance.

25. Governance Outputs

PPDM may produce:

intended predictive function; primary purpose; secondary-purpose references; object–purpose alignment state; purpose compatibility state; purpose conflict state; purpose definition state; unresolved purpose requirements.

The principal governed output is:

Defined Predictive Purpose

This output proceeds with the identified Prediction Object into:

27. Enterprise Application

PPDM enables organizations and predictive systems to preserve why predictions were originally created.

This becomes particularly important where the same prediction is distributed across departments, platforms, models, decision systems, AI agents, customers, or external stakeholders.

A defined Predictive Purpose allows later governance to distinguish between a prediction produced for research, planning, warning, operational support, public information, automated decision support, or another function without allowing subsequent users to silently redefine its original predictive role.

28. Use Case 1 — Electricity Demand Forecasting

An electricity operator identifies regional electricity demand as the Prediction Object.

The same object could be predicted for several purposes: day-ahead generation planning, infrastructure investment, emergency preparedness, market analysis, or public reporting.

PPDM establishes which Predictive Purpose governs the prediction.

A forecast produced for long-term infrastructure planning must not automatically be treated as though it were created for hour-by-hour operational dispatch.

The object may remain the same while the predictive purpose materially differs.

29. Use Case 2 — Digital Asset Prediction

A predictive system produces a future price prediction for a digital asset.

The Prediction Object may be clearly identified, but the prediction could exist for research, market commentary, portfolio analysis, risk monitoring, trading support, or public communication.

PPDM establishes the intended Predictive Purpose before subsequent PGA™ governance evaluates the target, horizon, methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, probability, feasibility, communication, or reliance.

The existence of the prediction does not automatically make it suitable for every downstream decision involving the asset.

30. Canonical Boundary

PPDM governs the identification, specification, differentiation, alignment, compatibility, change, and continuity of Predictive Purpose associated with an identified Prediction Object. It does not govern Prediction Target specification, Prediction Horizon, Predictive Boundary establishment, methodology, Prediction Basis, assumptions, prediction formation, probability, uncertainty, feasibility, communication, permitted reliance, revision, outcome determination, performance evaluation, or Prediction Record continuity.

PPDM establishes why the prediction exists .

Subsequent PGA™ governance determines what it specifically predicts and how that prediction is constructed, represented, used, evaluated, and preserved.

Canonical Closing Statement

A prediction can be technically possible while remaining governably undefined in purpose.

PPDM establishes why a prediction exists so that its subsequent methodology, interpretation, and governance remain anchored to the function it was created to serve.
