Governed Space

POAM governs the controlled association between a historically identifiable Governed Prediction and a sufficiently governed Outcome Reference.

It establishes whether the outcome being presented for predictive-performance assessment actually corresponds to:

the same Prediction Object; the predicted outcome; the applicable Prediction Horizon; the Predictive Boundary; the relevant population; the relevant geography; the applicable conditions; the appropriate historical prediction version.

POAM governs the transition:

Governed Historical Prediction + Sufficiently Governed Outcome Reference → Governed Prediction Outcome Association

Its foundational principle is:

An outcome cannot legitimately evaluate a prediction until it has first been shown to be the outcome of that prediction.

Canonical Definition

The Prediction Outcome Association Module (POAM) defines the operational governance requirements by which a historically identifiable Governed Prediction is linked to a sufficiently governed Outcome Reference without allowing related events, retrospectively modified prediction definitions, incompatible horizons, mismatched populations, changed boundaries, or outcome knowledge to manufacture a false prediction-to-outcome relationship.

POAM establishes:

Prediction Reference; prediction-version identity; Outcome Reference; Outcome Reference sufficiency; Prediction Object alignment; predicted-outcome alignment; Prediction Horizon alignment; Predictive Boundary alignment; population alignment;

geographic alignment; condition alignment; outcome maturity; association strength; association qualification; association state; association traceability.

The canonical transition is:

Historically Governed Prediction + Qualified Outcome Reference + Alignment Conditions → Governed Prediction Outcome Association

POAM does not determine whether the prediction performed well.

It determines whether the prediction and outcome may legitimately be placed into the same governed comparison relationship.

Operational Role

POAM is the first operational module within POFPS.

Its focused operational question is:

Does the available Outcome Reference legitimately correspond to the prediction that is proposed for performance assessment?

Before error, calibration, or predictive performance can be determined, POAM establishes whether a legitimate prediction- to-outcome association exists.

Its primary output is:

Governed Prediction Outcome Association

Internal Module Flow

Prediction Outcome Association → Prediction Outcome Comparison → Prediction Error Measurement → Prediction Calibration → Predictive Performance

Current Position:

Prediction Outcome Association

1. Prediction Outcome Association

Prediction Outcome Association establishes the governed relationship between:

What was predicted

and:

What subsequently occurred

as represented by a sufficiently governed Outcome Reference.

Association must precede performance judgment.

2. Association Is Not Outcome Determination

POAM does not establish Operational Reality.

It consumes an Outcome Reference from an appropriate reality-governance source.

Therefore:

Prediction Outcome Association ≠ Outcome Determination

ORA™ or another legitimate source may establish what occurred.

POAM determines whether that outcome corresponds to the prediction.

3. Governed Historical Prediction

Outcome association must operate against an identifiable historical prediction state.

The prediction should preserve, where applicable:

Prediction ID; version; Prediction Object; predicted outcome; Prediction Horizon; Predictive Boundary; probability; uncertainty; assumptions; conditions; lifecycle status.

4. Prediction Version Identity

The exact prediction version being associated with the outcome must remain explicit.

Example:

P1 — 42%

P2 — 61%

P3 — 79%

An outcome cannot simply be associated with:

“the prediction”

if multiple materially different historical prediction states existed.

5. Relevant Prediction State

POAM may support association with:

Original Prediction

Decision-Time Prediction

Version-Specific Prediction

Final Prediction

Superseded Prediction

Withdrawn Prediction

provided the selected historical state remains legitimate for the intended performance question.

6. Outcome Reference

An Outcome Reference is the governed reference to the state, event, value, condition, or resolution against which the prediction is proposed to be evaluated.

It must remain distinguishable from the prediction itself.

7. Outcome Reference Independence

The outcome should not be defined retrospectively merely to fit the prediction.

Prediction Definition ≠ Outcome Definition

The prediction and outcome may be associated.

They must not become circular definitions of each other.

8. Outcome Reference Source

The source of the Outcome Reference should remain identifiable.

Possible sources may include:

governed Operational Reality; official event record; verified system state; validated measurement; authoritative resolution source; governed external outcome dataset.

POAM does not automatically validate the source itself.

9. Outcome Reference Sufficiency

The Outcome Reference must be sufficient for the proposed association.

Possible states:

Sufficient

Sufficient with Qualification

Partially Sufficient

Disputed

Insufficient

Not Yet Available

Unable to Determine

10. Outcome Availability

Outcome availability may differ from outcome sufficiency.

A news report may indicate that something happened.

That does not automatically establish a sufficiently governed Outcome Reference.

Outcome Information Available ≠ Outcome Sufficiently Governed

11. Outcome Maturity

Some outcomes mature gradually.

Example:

A prediction states:

Technology X will achieve commercial deployment by 2028.

A pilot installation in 2027 may not establish:

Commercial Deployment

POAM must determine whether the Outcome Reference has reached the state required by the Prediction Object.

12. Early Outcome Signal

An Early Outcome Signal may indicate movement toward resolution without constituting the final outcome.

Early Outcome Signal ≠ Mature Outcome

13. Provisional Outcome

An outcome may be provisional.

Examples:

preliminary election count; unaudited financial result; temporary operational status; provisional regulatory decision.

A provisional outcome may support qualified association but should not silently become final.

14. Final Outcome Reference

A Final Outcome Reference is sufficiently mature for the intended comparison under the relevant governance structure.

Final Outcome Reference ≠ Absolute Truth Claim

It means sufficient governance for the specified performance purpose.

15. Outcome Dispute

Multiple sources may disagree about what occurred.

POAM should preserve the dispute rather than arbitrarily selecting the outcome most favorable to the prediction.

Possible state:

Outcome Association Pending — Outcome Disputed

16. Prediction Object Alignment

The Prediction Object must correspond to the Outcome Reference.

Example:

Prediction:

Regulatory approval by December

Outcome:

Commercial launch in December

These may be related.

They are not necessarily the same object.

Related Object ≠ Predicted Object

17. Predicted Outcome Alignment

The outcome must correspond to what the prediction actually claimed.

Prediction:

Company will file for approval

is not automatically validated by:

Company receives approval.

The two events are distinct.

18. Outcome Granularity

Prediction and outcome should have sufficiently compatible granularity.

Prediction:

European demand increases

cannot automatically be evaluated against:

German demand increases

unless the predictive structure permits that relationship.

19. Outcome Specificity

A broad outcome should not automatically satisfy a narrow prediction.

Likewise, a narrow event should not automatically establish a broader predicted condition.

Outcome Overlap ≠ Outcome Equivalence

20. Prediction Horizon Alignment

POAM must determine whether the outcome occurred within the Prediction Horizon.

Example:

Prediction:

Event A by 31 December 2027

Outcome:

Event A on 15 March 2028

The event occurred.

But not within the governed Prediction Horizon.

Event Eventually Occurred ≠ Event Occurred Within Prediction Horizon

21. Horizon Start Alignment

Some predictions apply only after a specified starting point.

The outcome must be evaluated within the correct temporal interval.

22. Horizon End Alignment

Where the Prediction Horizon has an explicit endpoint, events after that endpoint must not be retrospectively moved inside it.

23. Open-Ended Prediction Horizon

Open-ended predictions require clear governance.

Without a meaningful horizon, eventual occurrence can create misleading apparent success.

Eventually Correct ≠ Temporally Useful Prediction

24. Timing Outcome

Where timing itself is predicted, the Outcome Reference should preserve actual timing sufficiently for later comparison.

25. Predictive Boundary Alignment

The Outcome Reference must remain inside the applicable Predictive Boundary.

Boundary dimensions may include:

geography; population; organization; product; technology; market; scenario; operational environment.

26. Geographic Alignment

Prediction:

Demand will decline in Switzerland.

Outcome:

Demand declines in Germany.

The outcome cannot be substituted merely because the markets are related.

Geographic Similarity ≠ Geographic Alignment

27. Population Alignment

Prediction:

Users aged 18–25 will adopt Product X.

Outcome:

Users aged 40–60 adopt Product X.

This does not establish the predicted population outcome.

28. Organizational Alignment

Prediction concerning one organization cannot automatically be evaluated using another organization's outcome.

29. Technology Alignment

Prediction concerning one technology generation should not automatically receive credit from a successor technology unless the Predictive Boundary legitimately included it.

30. Market Alignment

A prediction about one market segment should not automatically be evaluated using the broader market.

Related Market ≠ Same Predictive Market

31. Scenario Alignment

Scenario-dependent predictions must be evaluated against the scenario conditions under which they were formed.

32. Condition Alignment

Conditional predictions require condition-aware association.

Prediction:

80% probability of Outcome X if Condition C occurs

must preserve whether:

Condition C occurred.

33. Condition Satisfaction

Condition status may be:

Satisfied

Partially Satisfied

Not Satisfied

Disputed

Unable to Determine

This status affects the legitimacy of outcome association.

34. Condition Failure

Where the required condition did not occur, the prediction may not be directly comparable as though it were unconditional.

Condition Failure ≠ Prediction Failure

35. Assumption Context

Assumptions should remain visible during outcome association where they materially define predictive meaning.

However:

Assumption Failure ≠ Automatic Outcome Non-Association

The effect depends upon the structure of the prediction.

36. Feasibility Context

Feasibility status may help interpret the prediction-to-outcome relationship.

PFES owns Prediction Feasibility.

POAM preserves relevant feasibility context without recalculating it.

37. Probability Context

The original probability must remain attached to the relevant prediction version.

Outcome occurrence must not transform:

70%

into:

100% retrospectively

or non-occurrence into:

0% retrospectively.

Known Outcome ≠ Historical Probability Rewrite

38. Uncertainty Context

Historical uncertainty should remain associated with the prediction state that existed before outcome knowledge.

Outcome knowledge must not make prior uncertainty disappear from the record.

39. Confidence Context

Historical confidence should remain version-specific.

Later knowledge cannot retroactively increase the confidence that existed when the prediction was formed.

40. Outcome Resolution Time

The time at which the outcome becomes sufficiently established should remain identifiable where material.

This may differ from:

event occurrence time; observation time; publication time;

validation time.

41. Event Time vs Outcome Resolution Time

Example:

Event occurs:

1 June

Reliable confirmation becomes available:

3 June

Governed resolution is established:

5 June

These states may need to remain distinct.

Event Time ≠ Outcome Resolution Time

42. Outcome Observation Delay

Delayed observation should not change when the event actually occurred.

This is especially important for horizon assessment.

43. Outcome Correction

An Outcome Reference may later be corrected.

POAM should support reassociation where legitimate while preserving prior outcome-reference history.

44. Outcome Versioning

Where Outcome References evolve, versions should remain identifiable.

Example:

O1 — Provisional

→ O2 — Corrected

→ O3 — Final

Prediction association may therefore also evolve.

45. Outcome Reference Revision

A revised Outcome Reference may require reassessment of existing Prediction Outcome Association.

Outcome Revision ≠ Prediction Revision

46. Association Versioning

Prediction Outcome Associations should be versionable where material.

Example:

Association A1 — Based on Provisional Outcome

→

Association A2 — Based on Final Outcome

47. Association Strength

Association Strength represents how strongly the available governance supports that the Outcome Reference corresponds to the prediction.

Possible states:

Direct

Qualified

Partial

Weak

Unsupported

Unable to Determine

Association Strength is not predictive performance.

48. Direct Association

Direct Association exists where the Outcome Reference clearly corresponds to the Prediction Object, horizon, boundary, and applicable conditions.

49. Qualified Association

Qualified Association exists where association is legitimate but material qualifications remain.

Examples:

provisional outcome; partial condition satisfaction; limited outcome maturity.

50. Partial Association

A prediction may contain multiple components.

Some may resolve while others remain unresolved.

POAM may establish partial association without forcing a complete outcome state.

51. Unsupported Association

An association is unsupported where the proposed outcome does not sufficiently correspond to the prediction.

Examples:

wrong object; wrong geography; wrong population; wrong horizon; unrelated event.

52. Association Ambiguity

A prediction may be worded or structured in a way that allows multiple possible Outcome References.

POAM should preserve that ambiguity.

It should not select whichever interpretation makes the prediction appear more accurate.

53. Multiple Outcome Candidates

Several possible Outcome References may exist.

POAM should determine whether:

one is authoritative; multiple are complementary; the outcome is disputed; the prediction itself was insufficiently defined.

54. Outcome Substitution

Outcome Substitution occurs when a related but different outcome is used to evaluate the prediction.

Example:

Prediction:

Company reaches profitability.

Substituted outcome:

Company increases revenue.

Positive Related Event ≠ Predicted Outcome

55. Outcome Expansion

Outcome Expansion occurs when the original prediction is broadened after the fact.

Prediction:

Factory A becomes operational.

After Factory B becomes operational:

“The company predicted factory expansion.”

This is not legitimate association.

56. Outcome Narrowing

Outcome Narrowing occurs when a broad prediction is retrospectively reduced to the part that happened.

Prediction:

Three markets will enter recession.

Outcome:

One market enters recession.

Retrospective narrowing must not convert partial correspondence into complete success.

57. Semantic Outcome Drift

Terminology may change between prediction formation and outcome resolution.

POAM should determine whether the semantic meaning remains sufficiently equivalent.

Same Word ≠ Same Operational Meaning by Default

58. Prediction Semantic Freeze

The meaning of the historical prediction should be interpreted according to the governed definition existing when the prediction was formed.

Later outcome knowledge must not rewrite that meaning.

59. Outcome Semantic Governance

The outcome should be defined independently enough to support legitimate comparison.

Where the outcome term itself is ambiguous, association may require qualification.

60. Retrospective Prediction Reinterpretation

POAM must prevent the prediction from being reinterpreted after the outcome becomes known merely to improve apparent performance.

Outcome Knowledge ≠ Permission to Redefine Prediction Meaning

61. Outcome Knowledge Boundary

Outcome knowledge creates a strong retrospective bias.

Once the future is known, ambiguous prediction language can appear clearer than it actually was.

POAM preserves the historical prediction definition before outcome knowledge.

62. Hindsight Bias

Hindsight Bias may cause observers to believe the outcome was more predictable than it actually was.

POAM does not assess cognition itself but preserves the historical prediction state required to prevent retrospective reconstruction.

63. Outcome Selection Bias

Where multiple possible outcomes occurred, evaluators may select the one that best matches the prediction.

POAM requires explicit outcome-selection logic.

64. Positive Outcome Selection

A prediction must not receive credit simply because some related positive event occurred.

Favorable Event ≠ Predicted Event

65. Negative Outcome Selection

Likewise, an evaluator should not select an unrelated negative event merely to classify the prediction as failed.

Association must remain neutral to desired performance conclusion.

66. Outcome Association Neutrality

POAM should determine association before performance direction is assessed.

The desired conclusion must not determine the Outcome Reference.

Canonical order:

Association First → Comparison Second

not:

Desired Performance Conclusion → Select Supporting Outcome

67. Prediction Withdrawal Context

A withdrawn prediction may still be associated with an outcome for historical performance purposes.

Withdrawn ≠ Outcome-Ineligible

PWM owns withdrawal.

POAM preserves the historical prediction-to-outcome relationship.

68. Superseded Prediction Context

Superseded predictions may remain independently associable with the outcome.

Superseded ≠ Historically Irrelevant

69. Revised Prediction Context

Each material revision may maintain a separate association with the same eventual outcome.

Example:

P1 → Outcome O

P2 → Outcome O

P3 → Outcome O

This enables later version-specific performance assessment.

70. Decision-Time Prediction Context

Where performance is evaluated relative to a historical decision, POAM should associate the Outcome Reference with the prediction state that existed at the decision time.

Later predictions must not replace it.

71. Intervention Context

Prediction use may materially influence the eventual outcome.

POAM should preserve whether a material intervention occurred between prediction and outcome.

It does not determine the intervention's causal effect.

72. Intervention-Affected Association

Where the predicted environment changed materially because of intervention, the association may remain valid but require qualification:

Outcome Association — Intervention-Affected

73. Self-Fulfilling Context

A prediction may contribute to producing the outcome.

The prediction and outcome may still be associated.

The influence must remain visible for later performance interpretation.

74. Self-Negating Context

A prediction may trigger intervention preventing the predicted outcome.

The resulting non-occurrence remains an Outcome Reference.

But performance interpretation must preserve the intervention.

75. Counterfactual Boundary

POAM associates the prediction with observed governed outcomes.

It does not invent:

what would have happened without intervention.

Unobserved Counterfactual ≠ Governed Outcome Reference

76. Predictor–Reality Proximity Context

The predictor's relationship to the emerging outcome should remain associated where relevant.

Possible states:

Public-Information Predictor

Information-Advantaged Predictor

Decision-Proximate Predictor

Outcome-Informed Predictor

Outcome-Influencing Predictor

Outcome-Controlling Predictor

POAM preserves this context.

It does not judge predictive skill.

77. Outcome Control Context

Where the predictor materially controlled the outcome, the Outcome Association may remain valid.

However, later performance interpretation must not confuse control with predictive capability.

Outcome Control ≠ Prediction Outcome Non-Association

The association exists; its meaning changes.

78. Collective Prediction Context

Collective predictions may be associated with outcomes while preserving:

aggregation state; participant population; independence; concentration; collective representation.

CPGS owns collective prediction governance.

79. Prediction Market Outcome Association

A market-implied prediction may be associated with an outcome.

POAM preserves:

market state; prediction time; implied probability; resolution criterion; Outcome Reference.

It does not treat trading profitability as predictive performance.

80. Resolution Criterion

Where prediction systems or markets use predefined resolution criteria, the criterion should remain identifiable.

The criterion must not be retrospectively changed simply because the outcome became inconvenient.

81. Resolution Criterion vs Operational Reality

A platform's resolution rule may not always equal the complete Operational Reality.

POAM should preserve both where material.

Platform Resolution ≠ Complete Operational Reality by Default

82. Machine-Readable Outcome Association

Machine systems should be capable of representing:

Prediction ID; Prediction Version; Outcome Reference ID; Outcome Reference Version; association state; alignment states;

qualification; resolution time.

83. AI-Assisted Outcome Association

AI may assist in identifying candidate Outcome References.

But:

AI-Suggested Outcome Match ≠ Governed Prediction Outcome Association

The association must satisfy governance requirements.

84. Autonomous Outcome Association

Automated association may be permitted where:

Prediction Objects are structured; Outcome References are sufficiently governed; alignment logic is explicit; ambiguity is controlled; historical traceability is preserved.

Automation does not remove governance requirements.

89. Association Integrity

Later outcome information must not silently modify:

original prediction wording; Prediction Horizon; Predictive Boundary; probability; uncertainty; assumptions; conditions.

90. Association Gaming

Potential Association Gaming includes:

choosing favorable Outcome References; changing outcome definitions; shifting Prediction Horizon; broadening the Prediction Object; narrowing the prediction retrospectively; excluding inconvenient outcome components; changing resolution criteria after outcome knowledge.

POAM must preserve controls capable of exposing these practices.

92. Governance Logic

POAM applies the following operational logic:

Is there an identifiable historical Governed Prediction?

If no:

→ Prediction Outcome Association cannot be established.

If yes:

→ identify the exact Prediction Version.

Is there an identifiable Outcome Reference?

If no:

→ classify:

Outcome Not Yet Sufficient

or:

Unable to Determine

If yes:

→ identify Outcome Reference Source and version.

Is the Outcome Reference sufficiently governed for the proposed association?

If no:

→ classify:

Association Pending

Outcome Disputed

or:

Association Not Supported

If yes:

→ assess alignment.

Does the Outcome Reference correspond to the same Prediction Object?

If no:

→ Association Not Supported.

Does it correspond to the predicted outcome?

If no:

→ Association Not Supported.

Does the Outcome Reference align with the Prediction Horizon?

If no:

→ preserve temporal mismatch.

Does it align with the Predictive Boundary?

If no:

→ preserve boundary mismatch.

Do required conditions align?

If no:

→ determine whether association is conditional, partial, or unsupported.

Did intervention materially affect the predicted environment?

If yes:

→ preserve Intervention-Affected qualification.

Is semantic meaning sufficiently stable between prediction and outcome?

If no:

→ preserve ambiguity or restrict association.

If all material association requirements are satisfied:

→ Association Established

Where qualifications remain:

→ Association Established with Qualification

Then:

→ preserve Prediction Version;

→ preserve Outcome Reference Version;

→ preserve all alignment states;

→ preserve association rationale;

→ preserve Association History;

→ pass the governed association to POCM.

Canonical transition:

Governed Historical Prediction + Sufficiently Governed Outcome Reference → Governed Prediction Outcome Association

97. Use Case 1 — Technology Forecast Is Credited for a Different Milestone

A forecasting organization publishes:

Prediction P1

“75% probability that Technology X receives full commercial regulatory authorization in the European Union by 31 December 2028.”

In October 2028, Technology X receives authorization for:

a limited pilot deployment in one Member State.

The forecasting platform records:

Prediction Correct

because:

“Technology X was authorized in Europe before the deadline.”

POAM reconstructs the historical Prediction Object:

Full Commercial Regulatory Authorization

and compares it with the Outcome Reference:

Limited Pilot Authorization in One Member State

The critical governance question is:

Does a related regulatory milestone constitute the outcome that was actually predicted?

POAM determines:

Prediction Object Alignment: Partial

Predicted Outcome Alignment: Not Sufficient

Geographic / Scope Alignment: Not Sufficient

Association State: Association Not Supported for the claimed full-commercial outcome

The pilot authorization may be relevant historical information.

It is not automatically the predicted outcome.

Related Object ≠ Predicted Object

Outcome Overlap ≠ Outcome Equivalence

POAM prevents a related positive development from being retrospectively converted into predictive success.

98. Use Case 2 — Failure Prediction Triggers Maintenance and the Failure Never Occurs

An industrial prediction system generates:

Prediction P1

90% probability of critical bearing failure within seven days.

The prediction triggers immediate preventive maintenance.

The bearing is replaced.

No failure occurs.

The historical Outcome Reference is:

No bearing failure within the original seven-day horizon.

A simplistic performance system proposes:

Prediction Incorrect

POAM does not determine whether that conclusion is valid.

Instead it establishes the governed association:

Prediction: Critical bearing failure within seven days.

Observed Outcome: No critical bearing failure within seven days.

100. Canonical Closing Statement

Before asking whether a prediction was right, governance must first establish whether the event being used to judge it is actually the event that was predicted.

POAM freezes the historical meaning of the prediction, qualifies the outcome independently, and governs the relationship between them before hindsight, semantic drift, selective resolution, or retrospective reinterpretation can change the comparison.

The future may reveal what happened. It must not be allowed to rewrite what was predicted.
