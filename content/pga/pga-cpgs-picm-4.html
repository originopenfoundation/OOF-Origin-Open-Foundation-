Canonical Definition

Predictive Influence & Concentration Module (PICM) defines the governance conditions under which influence over an aggregated predictive representation is identified, attributed, measured or qualified, structurally interpreted, and assessed for material concentration before the aggregate may proceed toward final Collective Prediction Representation.

PICM determines whether apparent collective participation corresponds to materially distributed predictive influence or whether the resulting aggregate is disproportionately shaped by particular:

predictive sources; source families; dependence clusters; model families; institutions; information origins; weighting structures; capital positions; market participants; Predictor–Reality Proximity positions; decision-proximate or outcome-informed sources.

It transforms:

Governed Prediction Aggregation + Governed Predictive Source Diversity Profile + Governed Prediction Dependence & Variation Profile + Contribution Weights + Relevant Influence Relationships

into:

Governed Predictive Influence & Concentration Profile

Its foundational principle is:

Broad participation does not establish distributed predictive influence.

Operational Role

PICM is the fourth internal governance space of the Collective Prediction Governance Standard (CPGS) .

PSDM establishes:

what predictive source diversity exists.

PDVM establishes:

how dependent or independent the predictive contributions are and how they vary.

PAGM establishes:

how eligible predictive contributions are aggregated.

PICM establishes:

who or what materially determines the aggregated result.

The focused operational question is:

How is predictive influence distributed across the collective structure, where is it concentrated, and does the apparent collective result materially depend upon a limited number of sources, clusters, information origins, models, capital positions, or other influence structures?

The primary output is:

Governed Predictive Influence & Concentration Profile

Current Position: Predictive Influence & Concentration

Predictive Influence

Predictive Influence represents the material effect that a predictive contribution, source, cluster, methodology, information origin, weighting structure, or other predictive component has upon an aggregated predictive representation.

Participation ≠ Influence

A source may participate while having little effect on the final aggregate.

Another source may represent only one contribution while materially determining the result.

Nominal Participation

Nominal Participation represents presence within the collective prediction mechanism.

Effective Influence

Effective Influence represents the actual material contribution to the resulting aggregate.

Nominal Participation ≠ Effective Influence

Influence Distribution

Influence Distribution describes how predictive influence is allocated across the contributing structure.

Distributed Influence

Influence is relatively distributed where no small source, cluster, or structural origin disproportionately determines the aggregate.

Distributed Participation ≠ Distributed Influence

Influence Concentration

Influence Concentration exists where a limited number of predictive sources, source clusters, models, institutions, capital positions, information origins, or other structural components disproportionately shape the aggregate.

Large Collective ≠ Low Influence Concentration

Source-Level Influence

Influence may be attributed to individual predictive sources.

Source Count ≠ Source Influence

Contribution-Level Influence

A specific predictive contribution may exert material influence because of:

assigned weight; extreme predictive position; aggregation method; timing; market capital; methodological priority.

Contribution Weight ≠ Complete Influence

A nominal weight does not always capture actual effect on the aggregate.

Marginal Influence

Marginal Influence represents how materially the aggregated result changes when a contribution is modified, removed, or reduced.

High Weight ≠ High Marginal Influence by Default

Removal Sensitivity

Removal Sensitivity measures or qualifies how the aggregate changes when a predictive source or contribution is removed.

One Contribution Removed → Material Aggregate Change

may indicate concentrated influence.

Removal Sensitivity ≠ Prediction Error

Source-Family Influence

Multiple nominal sources may belong to one effective source family.

Their combined influence should remain governable.

Many Interfaces + One Model Family ≠ Distributed Model Influence

Model-Family Concentration

Model-Family Concentration exists where a collective prediction is materially shaped by one model family despite multiple model instances or services.

Multiple Models ≠ Distributed Model Influence

Agent Concentration

Multiple agents may share a common predictive origin.

Agent Count ≠ Distributed Agent Influence

Institutional Concentration

Predictive influence may be concentrated within one institution or organizational network.

Multiple Analysts ≠ Distributed Institutional Influence

Ownership Concentration

Different sources may share common ownership or control.

Different Legal Entities ≠ Distributed Ownership Influence

Methodological Concentration

A collective may contain many sources while most effective influence comes from one methodology.

Source Diversity ≠ Methodological Influence Diversity

Information-Origin Concentration

Many predictive contributions may ultimately depend upon one information origin.

Many Predictions ≠ Many Information Origins

Upstream Influence

An upstream information source may indirectly influence many downstream predictions.

Upstream Source Not Directly Included ≠ No Upstream Influence

Hidden Influence

Influence may exist even where the influential source does not directly participate in aggregation.

Direct Contribution ≠ Only Form of Predictive Influence

Dependence-Cluster Influence

Predictions grouped into a dependence cluster may collectively exert disproportionate influence.

Cluster Size ≠ Independent Influence

Cluster Weight

The combined effective influence of a dependence cluster should remain governable.

Nominal Contribution Weight ≠ Independent Cluster Weight

Influence Duplication

Influence Duplication occurs where one predictive origin enters the aggregate through multiple downstream contributions.

Repeated Pathways ≠ Independent Influence

Influence Amplification

Influence Amplification occurs where aggregation structure causes one predictive origin to receive greater effective influence than its underlying informational contribution would suggest.

Aggregation Weight ≠ Informational Contribution

Influence Attenuation

Influence may also be reduced through:

dependence adjustment; weight caps; segmentation; normalization; methodological rules.

Reduced Influence ≠ Invalid Prediction

Influence Transfer

Influence may move from one source to another through intermediate predictive systems.

Example:

Expert Forecast → AI Retrieval → Multiple AI Predictions → Aggregate

The expert may exert substantial indirect influence even though only AI predictions appear as direct contributors.

Intermediate Transformation ≠ Elimination of Upstream Influence

Influence Chain

An Influence Chain describes how predictive influence propagates across sources or systems.

Long Influence Chain ≠ Independent Influence

Influence Network

A collective predictive system may contain a network of direct and indirect influence relationships.

Influence Network ≠ Contribution List

Influence Centrality

Some sources may occupy structurally central positions within an influence network.

Centrality ≠ Predictive Accuracy

Dominant Predictive Source

A Dominant Predictive Source is a source whose effective influence materially shapes the collective result.

Dominant Source ≠ Correct Source

Dominant Source Cluster

A Dominant Source Cluster is a group of structurally related predictive contributions that collectively exert material influence over the aggregate.

Dominant Cluster ≠ Majority of Participants

Dominant Information Origin

A Dominant Information Origin exists where substantial collective influence traces back to one information source or environment.

Information-Origin Dominance ≠ Source-Count Dominance

Influence Concentration Across Dimensions

Concentration may exist across different dimensions:

source; institution; model; methodology; information origin; geography; capital; Predictor–Reality Proximity; decision authority.

Low Concentration in One Dimension ≠ Low Overall Concentration

Multi-Dimensional Concentration

A collective may appear distributed across one dimension while remaining concentrated across another.

Example:

100 traders may represent many individuals but most capital may belong to five accounts.

Participant Diversity ≠ Capital Diversity

Capital Influence

In market-based prediction systems, capital may determine contribution influence.

Capital ≠ Predictive Merit

Capital-Weighted Influence

A participant with greater capital may move a market-derived predictive signal more strongly than many smaller participants.

One Trader ≠ One Unit of Influence

Market Participation ≠ Equal Predictive Influence

Market Concentration

Market Concentration exists where a limited number of positions materially shape the market-derived predictive signal.

Number of Traders ≠ Distribution of Market Influence

Liquidity and Influence

Low liquidity may allow relatively small positions to materially change a market signal.

Small Trade ≠ Small Influence in Low-Liquidity Market

High Liquidity ≠ Distributed Influence by Default

Position Size

Position size may contribute to market influence.

Position Size ≠ Predictive Reliability

Trading Frequency

Repeated trading may increase apparent activity without increasing independent predictive participation.

Trade Count ≠ Predictor Count

Account Count

Multiple accounts may belong to one actor or coordinated group.

Account Count ≠ Independent Participant Count

Coordinated Influence

Multiple participants may coordinate their predictive actions.

Multiple Accounts ≠ Distributed Influence

Strategic Influence

Participants may intentionally attempt to shift the collective signal.

Signal Influence ≠ Predictive Accuracy

Market Manipulation Determination

PICM may identify influence patterns requiring further governance review.

It does not determine legal market manipulation.

Influence Pattern ≠ Legal Misconduct Determination

Predictor–Reality Proximity

Predictor–Reality Proximity may materially affect interpretation of influence.

Relevant positions may include:

Public-Information Predictor

Information-Advantaged Predictor

Decision-Proximate Predictor

Outcome-Informed Predictor

Outcome-Influencing Predictor

Outcome-Controlling Predictor

High Predictor–Reality Proximity ≠ Misconduct

Predictor–Reality Proximity ≠ Predictive Influence

A predictor may be close to the predicted reality while having little influence over the collective aggregate.

These dimensions must remain separate.

Outcome Influence

A predictive source may influence the real-world outcome itself.

Predictive Influence ≠ Outcome Influence

Predictive Influence concerns influence over the collective prediction.

Outcome Influence concerns influence over the predicted reality.

Outcome Influence ≠ Predictive Accuracy

Outcome Control

A predictor may possess authority capable of materially determining the outcome.

Outcome Control ≠ Predictive Skill

Decision Authority

Decision authority should remain distinguishable from predictive influence.

Decision Authority ≠ Collective Predictive Legitimacy

Outcome-Informed Influence

A source with privileged outcome information may strongly influence the collective prediction.

Outcome Knowledge ≠ Predictive Capability

Influence from Informational Advantage

Information advantage may legitimately cause a source to contribute valuable information.

However, the collective representation should preserve where a supposedly broad consensus is materially driven by one informationally advantaged source.

Information Advantage ≠ Distributed Collective Intelligence

Predictive Reputation Influence

Sources with strong reputations may influence other contributors.

Reputation ≠ Predictive Accuracy by Default

Authority Signaling

Participants may follow predictions because of perceived authority.

Authority Signaling ≠ Independent Confirmation

Consensus Influence

Existing consensus may influence later predictions.

Consensus as Input ≠ Independent Consensus

Feedback Influence

The aggregate itself may become an input into future predictive contributions.

Collective Prediction → Contributor Update → Collective Prediction

This can create feedback loops.

Feedback Loop ≠ New Independent Information

Reflexive Collective Influence

A collective prediction may influence both:

future predictions; the predicted reality.

Predictive Reflexivity

Predictive reflexivity occurs where the collective prediction alters subsequent predictive behavior.

Operational Reflexivity

Operational reflexivity occurs where the collective prediction contributes to changes in Operational Reality.

Predictive Reflexivity ≠ Operational Reflexivity

Self-Reinforcing Prediction Structure

A collective signal may become stronger because contributors repeatedly respond to the signal itself.

Growing Consensus ≠ Growing Independent Evidence

Self-Weakening Prediction Structure

A collective prediction may cause actors to take preventive action, reducing the probability of the predicted outcome.

Prediction Influence ≠ Prediction Failure

Influence Threshold

An Influence Threshold may define when a source, cluster, or structural origin becomes materially influential.

Threshold ≠ Universal Constant

The appropriate threshold depends upon the aggregation mechanism and governance purpose.

Concentration Threshold

A Concentration Threshold may define when influence concentration requires qualification or further governance treatment.

Concentration Threshold ≠ Automatic Illegitimacy

Influence Share

Influence Share represents the proportion of effective predictive influence attributable to a source or structure where measurable.

Influence Share ≠ Contribution Count Share

Effective Influence Share

Effective Influence Share may account for:

nominal weight; dependence; indirect influence; source-family relationships; aggregation sensitivity.

Nominal Weight Share ≠ Effective Influence Share

Effective Predictor Count

An Effective Predictor Count may estimate how many materially independent influence-bearing predictive sources exist after relevant dependence and concentration are considered.

Raw Predictor Count ≠ Effective Predictor Count

Effective Predictor Count ≠ Exact Number of Independent Minds

It is a governance representation, not necessarily a literal count.

Concentration Ratio

Where useful, concentration may be represented through defined ratios.

Concentration Ratio ≠ Complete Influence Structure

A single metric should not replace structural interpretation.

Influence Distribution Profile

An Influence Distribution Profile represents the distribution of effective predictive influence across relevant dimensions.

Influence Distribution Profile ≠ Predictive Performance Profile

Concentration State

PICM may classify concentration as:

Broadly Distributed

Moderately Concentrated

Materially Concentrated

Highly Concentrated

Dominantly Concentrated

Concentration Uncertain

Unable to Determine

Broadly Distributed

No limited source or cluster materially dominates the aggregate under the applicable methodology.

Moderately Concentrated

Some concentration exists but the aggregate retains substantial distributed influence.

Materially Concentrated

A limited set of sources or structures has substantial influence requiring explicit interpretation.

Highly Concentrated

A small number of sources or structures strongly determine the aggregate.

Dominantly Concentrated

The aggregate is effectively controlled or determined by one source, cluster, information origin, or equivalent influence structure.

Dominantly Concentrated ≠ Invalid Prediction by Default

The state requires transparent representation.

Concentration Uncertain

Material concentration may exist but cannot be sufficiently resolved.

Unable to Determine

Available information does not support a legitimate concentration determination.

Unable to Determine ≠ Distributed Influence

Influence Qualification

Where concentration is material, the aggregate should preserve an Influence Qualification.

Example:

Aggregate materially influenced by one model family representing approximately 64% of effective predictive influence.

Qualification ≠ Disqualification

Concentration Adjustment

Where methodology permits, aggregation may be reconsidered when excessive concentration is identified.

PICM identifies the concentration.

It does not silently rewrite PAGM methodology.

Concentration Identified ≠ Automatic Reweighting

Aggregation Reassessment Trigger

Material concentration may trigger reconsideration of aggregation methodology.

Reassessment Trigger ≠ Automatic Aggregation Change

Influence Conflict

Different influence-analysis methods may produce materially different concentration conclusions.

Influence Conflict ≠ Average the Conclusions

Influence Uncertainty

Influence may be difficult to determine where:

ownership is opaque; source relationships are incomplete; market positions are hidden; model provenance is unknown; indirect influence chains are unresolved.

Unknown Influence ≠ No Influence

Hidden Concentration

Hidden Concentration exists where apparent distributed participation conceals common ownership, common origin, coordinated activity, or indirect structural dominance.

Visible Diversity ≠ Distributed Influence

Influence History

Influence structures may change over time.

Current Influence ≠ Historical Influence

Historical Concentration

A collective prediction's historical interpretation should preserve the concentration structure existing when the aggregate was formed.

Later Distribution ≠ Historical Distribution

Influence Version

Material changes in influence structure should create a new governable version.

Influence Change ≠ Historical Replacement

Source Entry

A new source may materially change influence distribution.

New Source ≠ Material Influence Change by Default

Source Exit

Removing a dominant source may materially alter collective structure.

Source Removal ≠ Proportional Influence Reduction by Default

Weight Change

Changes in aggregation weights may alter effective influence.

Weight Change ≠ Predictive Source Change

Dependence Change

New information about predictive dependence may change the interpretation of effective influence.

Same Aggregate Number ≠ Same Influence Structure

Collective Stability

An aggregate may remain numerically stable while the sources determining it change substantially.

Numerical Stability ≠ Structural Stability

Prediction Dependence ≠ Predictive Influence

PAGM establishes aggregation method, contribution weights, and dependence adjustments.

PICM assesses the resulting effective influence structure.

Aggregation Weight ≠ Complete Influence Structure

Relationship to CPRM

PICM provides the influence and concentration structure required before final Collective Prediction Representation.

CPRM determines how the collective result may legitimately be represented.

Influence Assessment ≠ Final Collective Representation

Relationship to Predictor–Reality Proximity

Predictor–Reality Proximity may be preserved where material to understanding concentrated influence.

High Predictor–Reality Proximity ≠ Misconduct

Relationship to Prediction Markets

PICM governs predictive influence and concentration within market-derived collective signals.

It does not determine securities law, gambling law, market-abuse law, or other jurisdiction-specific legal conclusions.

Influence Pattern ≠ Legal Conclusion

Relationship to AI Ensembles

PICM identifies whether an apparent multi-model collective is materially dominated by:

one model family; one upstream model; one retrieval system; one orchestration layer; one information source.

Model Count ≠ Model Influence Distribution

Relationship to ORA™

ORA™ governs Operational Reality.

PICM does not establish whether a predictor actually controls or changes Operational Reality.

It governs the relevance of such relationships to collective predictive influence.

Enterprise Application

PICM enables organizations to see the real power structure behind a collective prediction.

A forecasting platform may display:

10,000 participants

while a small number of sources materially determine the result.

An AI ensemble may contain:

50 agents

while most effective influence originates from one base model.

A prediction market may show broad trading activity while a few capital positions dominate price formation.

An expert consensus may contain dozens of contributors while most forecasts inherit one upstream institutional forecast.

PICM makes these structures governable before the collective result is represented as broad predictive consensus.

Use Case 1 — 25,000 Traders, but Four Accounts Drive the Market Signal

A prediction market displays:

82%

for the probability of a regulatory approval occurring before the end of the year.

The market contains:

25,000 participant accounts.

At first sight, the 82% signal appears to represent broad collective belief.

PICM examines the influence structure.

Four large accounts represent a substantial share of active capital, frequently move the market price, and account for most of the marginal changes in the displayed probability.

Thousands of smaller participants contribute activity but have limited effect on the resulting market signal.

The critical governance question is:

Does participation by 25,000 accounts justify representing the 82% market signal as broadly distributed collective judgment when a small number of positions materially determine the price?

PICM establishes a:

Highly Concentrated

influence state and requires that the concentration remain visible to downstream collective representation.

Number of Traders ≠ Distribution of Market Influence

Broad Participation Does Not Establish Distributed Predictive Influence

Use Case 2 — Fifty AI Agents, One Upstream Predictive Origin

An organization deploys:

50 autonomous forecasting agents

to predict whether a major industrial project will reach commercial operation within two years.

The agents use different roles, prompts, and task descriptions.

Their outputs are aggregated and presented as:

consensus across 50 AI agents.

PICM traces the influence structure.

Most agents rely upon:

the same base model; the same retrieval infrastructure; the same project dataset; one upstream risk forecast repeatedly referenced across the system.

The aggregate therefore contains fifty visible contributions but is materially shaped by one narrow predictive infrastructure.

The critical governance question is:

Does a fifty-agent architecture create distributed predictive influence when the majority of those agents inherit their predictive structure from the same upstream system?

PICM identifies:

Model-Family Concentration

Information-Origin Concentration

and:

Dominant Upstream Influence

The collective representation must preserve that structure.

Agent Count ≠ Distributed Agent Influence

Many Predictions ≠ Many Information Origins

Canonical Boundary

PICM governs the identification, attribution, qualification, structural interpretation, concentration assessment, and historical preservation of predictive influence within an aggregated predictive structure. It governs direct and indirect influence, source-family and dependence-cluster influence, model, agent, institutional, ownership, methodological and information-origin concentration, capital and market influence, Influence Chains, Influence Networks, Effective Influence, marginal influence, removal sensitivity, Predictor–Reality Proximity context, feedback and reflexive influence, concentration states, Hidden Concentration, and influence history. It does not determine Predictive Source Diversity, establish Prediction Dependence, select the aggregation methodology, silently reweight predictive contributions, determine legal misconduct, establish Operational Reality, determine final Collective Prediction legitimacy, govern Prediction Communication & Reliance, or assess predictive performance.

PICM governs:

who or what materially influences the aggregated predictive result and how concentrated that influence actually is.

It does not determine:

whether the resulting collective structure may ultimately be represented as a legitimate Governed Collective Prediction.

Canonical Closing Statement

A collective prediction may have thousands of visible contributors while only a few sources carry the weight of the result.

PICM exposes the sources, models, information origins, capital positions, clusters, and indirect pathways through which predictive influence actually flows.

Collective participation becomes meaningful only when governance can distinguish who contributed from who effectively determined the signal.
