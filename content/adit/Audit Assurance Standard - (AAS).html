Governed Space

Audit Assurance

Core Question

How can the audit itself remain continuously trustworthy, complete, objective,
independent, and independently verifiable throughout its complete lifecycle?

Canonical Definition

Audit Assurance Standard defines the governance conditions required to
independently evaluate, validate, monitor, and continuously assure the
quality, integrity, completeness, independence, and methodological validity of
the audit process itself. It establishes assurance methodology, governance
controls, validation mechanisms, independence requirements, and continuous
assurance principles necessary to ensure that completed audits remain
objectively trustworthy, reproducible, and independently verifiable throughout
their complete lifecycle.

Governance Flow Position

Audit Observation → Evidence Formation → Evidence Integrity → Evidence
Correlation → Evidence Verification → Audit Execution → Audit Findings → Audit
Response → Audit Preservation → Audit Assurance

Standard Abstract

Completing and preserving an audit does not automatically guarantee that the
audit itself was trustworthy.

The audit methodology, evidence base, execution process, findings, responses,
preservation, and governance controls must themselves become subject to
independent assurance.

Audit Assurance Standard establishes the final governance layer of ADIT®,
ensuring that the complete audit lifecycle can be independently trusted,
evaluated, and continuously assured.

Operational Role

Audit Assurance Standard governs the assurance layer of audit governance by
establishing standardized methods for independently evaluating the quality,
completeness, integrity, independence, consistency, and methodological
validity of the entire audit lifecycle.

Operational Space

Audit Assurance Standard governs:

  • audit assurance
  • audit quality
  • audit independence
  • audit completeness
  • assurance validation
  • assurance review
  • assurance governance.


Why This Standard Exists

Organizations, regulators, and stakeholders require confidence not only in
audit findings but also in the audit process itself.

Without independent assurance, audits may be technically complete yet
methodologically weak, inconsistent, biased, or insufficiently reliable.

Audit Assurance Standard exists because Audit Assurance represents the tenth
and final governed space of ADIT® — Continuous Audit & Evidence Governance
Architecture™, completing the continuous audit governance lifecycle.
