Governed Space

Evidence Verification

Core Question

How can correlated evidence be objectively verified before it is accepted for
audit findings, governance decisions, or independent review?

Canonical Definition

Evidence Verification Standard defines the governance conditions required to
verify the accuracy, validity, reliability, authenticity, completeness,
consistency, and sufficiency of correlated evidence before it is used for
audit execution. It establishes verification principles, validation
requirements, verification controls, governance mechanisms, and continuous
verification assurance necessary to determine whether governed evidence
satisfies the required level of trust for audit, investigation, operational
reconstruction, and independent verification.

Governance Flow Position

Audit Observation → Evidence Formation → Evidence Integrity → Evidence
Correlation → Evidence Verification → Audit Execution → Audit Findings → Audit
Response → Audit Preservation → Audit Assurance

Standard Abstract

Trustworthy evidence must be verified before it can support audit conclusions
or governance decisions.

Even authentic, complete, preserved, and properly correlated evidence requires
objective verification to confirm that it satisfies the required governance
criteria.

Evidence Verification Standard establishes the methodological framework for
determining whether evidence is sufficiently reliable to proceed into formal
audit execution.

Operational Role

Evidence Verification Standard governs the verification layer of evidence
governance by establishing standardized methods for evaluating whether
correlated evidence satisfies defined governance requirements before audit
execution begins.

Operational Space

Evidence Verification Standard governs:

  • evidence verification
  • evidence validation
  • evidence reliability
  • evidence sufficiency
  • verification methodology
  • verification decisions
  • verification assurance.


Why This Standard Exists

Correlated evidence is not automatically accepted as verified evidence.

Organizations require an objective methodology for determining whether
evidence is accurate, reliable, complete, and suitable for audit, regulatory
review, legal proceedings, and governance decisions.

Evidence Verification Standard exists because Evidence Verification represents
the fifth governed space of ADIT® — Continuous Audit & Evidence Governance
Architecture™, providing the formal gateway between evidence governance and
audit execution.
