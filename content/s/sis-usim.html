Canonical Definition System

Urban Surface Integrity defines the conditions under which surfaces in public or shared environments
must remain free from unauthorized markings, alterations, or use as uncontrolled communication
channels.

A surface is considered integrity-valid only if it remains:
  • unmarked without authorization
  • structurally intact
  • visually unaltered
  • free from unauthorized communication
Any unauthorized marking constitutes surface degradation and system misuse.

A. Module Abstract

The Urban Surface Integrity Module establishes the rules required to protect surfaces in public
and shared environments from unauthorized use.

The objective is to ensure that:
  • surfaces are not used as uncontrolled communication channels
  • public and private assets remain intact
  • unauthorized markings are prevented and classified correctly
  • responsibility is attributable and enforceable
This module does not regulate art.
It defines the boundary between authorized use and unauthorized intervention.

B. Core Principle

A surface is not a communication channel unless explicitly authorized.

C. Structural Requirements

1. Authorization Condition
Any marking, application, or visual modification must be:
  • explicitly authorized
  • legally permitted
  • attributable to a responsible entity
Unauthorized marking is invalid.

2. Non-Use Condition
Surfaces must not be used for:
  • identity signaling
  • promotion
  • tagging
  • messaging
  • advertising
unless explicitly authorized.

3. Surface Protection Condition
Surfaces must remain:
  • structurally intact
  • visually clean
  • free from degradation
Any unauthorized marking is classified as damage.

4. Reversibility Condition
Any authorized marking must:
  • be removable
  • not damage the surface
  • preserve original state
Irreversible impact constitutes degradation.

5. Responsibility Attribution Condition
All markings must be:
  • attributable
  • traceable
  • linked to a responsible entity
Anonymous marking is prohibited.

6. Public Asset Protection Condition
Surfaces financed or maintained by public resources must:
  • maintain integrity
  • not be altered without authorization
Unauthorized marking constitutes public asset degradation.

D. Minimum Implementation Framework (MIF)

Step 1 — Surface Classification
Identify whether the surface is:
  • public
  • private
  • shared
 Step 2 — Authorization Definition

Define who has authority to approve markings.

Step 3 — Monitoring Condition

Ensure that surfaces are observable and controlled.

Step 4 — Detection Mechanism

Enable identification of unauthorized markings.

Step 5 — Response Logic

Define removal, restoration, and responsibility enforcement.

E. Validation Logic

A surface is valid only if:
  • no unauthorized marking exists
  • no degradation is present
  • no uncontrolled communication occurs

F. Prohibited Conditions

The system is non-compliant if:
  • surfaces are used without authorization
  • markings cannot be removed without damage
  • responsibility cannot be assigned
  • repeated unauthorized marking occurs

G. Enforcement Classification

Unauthorized marking is classified as:
  • surface degradation
  • unauthorized use of space
  • unauthorized communication

H. Operational Output

This module ensures:
  • protected public space
  • reduced repeated damage
  • enforceable responsibility
  • controlled communication boundaries

I. Integration Logic

This module operates with:
  • SIS — defines surface rules
  • INTEGROS® — ensures traceability
  • CGRM — ensures governance and enforcement

J. Case 1 — Repeated Tagging of Public Infrastructure

Scenario

A surface is repeatedly marked with identifiable tags.

Without Module
  • damage repeats
  • no accountability
  • cost accumulates
 With Module
  • marking classified as violation
  • responsibility traceable
  • enforcement possible
 Result

Repeated degradation is reduced.

K. Case 2 — Unauthorized Use of Surface for Promotion

Scenario

A surface is used to display names, symbols, or group identity.

Without Module
  • space becomes uncontrolled communication channel
  • visibility benefits unknown entities
 With Module
  • classified as unauthorized communication
  • responsibility extends to identifiable beneficiaries
 Result

Surface use becomes controlled and accountable.

Canonical Closing Statement

Public space is not an uncontrolled communication surface.

Urban Surface Integrity Module establishes a non-negotiable condition:

Unauthorized markings are not expression.
They are surface degradation and unauthorized use of shared space.

If communication requires consent,
then unauthorized marking is not communication — it is violation.
