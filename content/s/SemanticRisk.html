Semantic Risk™

The Risk Created by Unclear, Unvalidated, or
Ungoverned Meaning

Canonical Definition

Semantic Risk™ is the operational, regulatory, financial, legal, safety, or
AI-related risk created when critical meanings are unclear, undefined,
conflicting, unvalidated, unregistered, or interpreted differently by people,
organizations, systems, or AI environments.

Semantic Risk™ does not arise from the absence of information alone.

It arises when information is interpreted through unstable meaning.

Why Semantic Risk™ Exists

Organizations operate through language.

Instructions are language.

Contracts are language.

Policies are language.

Procedures are language.

Regulations are language.

AI prompts and system documentation are language.

But language is not always meaning.

The same word may be understood differently by:
  • departments
  • managers
  • employees
  • contractors
  • regulators
  • insurers
  • suppliers
  • AI systems
When meaning is not governed, risk enters the system silently.

The Nature of Semantic Risk™

Semantic Risk™ is dangerous because it is often invisible before failure
occurs.

A process may appear correct.

A procedure may appear complete.

A contract may appear clear.

An instruction may appear simple.

But if the underlying meaning is incomplete, assumed, or interpreted
differently, the system may still fail.

Semantic Risk™ often begins with phrases such as:
  • "I thought this meant..."
  • "That was not what I understood."
  • "Everyone knows what this means."
  • "It was obvious."
  • "Nobody told me that."
  • "The document did not say that."
  • "We use that term differently."
These statements reveal that the failure was not only technical.

It was semantic.
