About the Urban Surface Integrity
Module (USIM)

What This Module Defines


The Urban Surface Integrity Module (USIM) defines how surfaces in public and
shared environments must be protected from unauthorized marking, use, and degradation.

It establishes a clear boundary:
  • what is authorized use
  • what is unauthorized intervention
This module ensures that surfaces remain:
  • controlled
  • protected
  • accountable

Core Message

Expression is not the problem.
Unauthorized use of space is.

What This Module Solves

In current systems:
  • surfaces are repeatedly marked
  • damage is normalized
  • responsibility is unclear
  • public cost is continuous
Unauthorized markings act as:
  • uncontrolled communication
  • repeated identity exposure
  • indirect promotion
The result:
  • degraded infrastructure
  • increased maintenance costs
  • loss of control over public space

System Shift

USIM introduces a structural change:

Public and shared surfaces are not open communication channels.

They are:
  • owned
  • governed
  • protected
Any use requires authorization.

For Cities and Municipal Systems

Cities gain:
  • reduced cleaning and restoration costs
  • control over visual environment
  • ability to classify and enforce violations
  • reduction of repeated damage cycles
Instead of reacting to damage, cities operate:
  • proactively
  • systematically
  • with enforceable rules

For Sports Clubs and Organizations

Repeated tagging and identity marking often promotes:
  • club names
  • group identity
  • territorial presence
Without control:
  • visibility is gained without consent
  • cost is transferred to the public
  • responsibility remains unclear
With USIM:
  • unauthorized marking is classified as promotion
  • responsibility may extend beyond individuals
  • organizations are incentivized to prevent misuse
This creates:
  • internal responsibility
  • behavioral change
  • protection of brand integrity

For Companies and Infrastructure Owners

Companies operating:
  • transport systems
  • logistics infrastructure
  • public-facing assets
face:
  • repeated damage
  • operational disruption
  • increased maintenance costs
USIM enables:
  • classification of damage as system violation
  • traceability and accountability
  • reduction of repeated incidents
  • protection of asset value

Behavioral Outcome

With USIM:
  • individuals understand boundaries
  • groups become responsible
  • organizations become proactive
  • systems shift from reaction to control

Why This Will Be Adopted

Because it solves:
  • uncontrolled visual pollution
  • repeated public cost
  • lack of responsibility
  • system inefficiency
It replaces:
  • tolerance → with structure
  • reaction → with control
  • anonymity → with accountability

System Insight

Unauthorized marking is not neutral behavior.

It is:
  • use of space without consent
  • visual occupation
  • indirect promotion
  • structural degradation

Economic Reality

Every unauthorized marking creates:
  • cleaning costs
  • restoration costs
  • material degradation
  • repeated operational expense
Without structural control, the system absorbs these costs indefinitely.

What This Enables

USIM creates:
  • enforceable surface protection
  • reduced long-term costs
  • controlled public environments
  • clear responsibility mapping

Final Statement

A surface belongs to its owner or to the public — not to whoever writes on it.

If space is used without permission,
it is not expression.

It is unauthorized use, damage, and promotion combined.
