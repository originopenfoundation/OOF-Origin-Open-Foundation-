Canonical Definition

Remaining Disclosure Module defines the structural conditions
under which materially relevant remaining prize, reward, or
probabilistic availability information is disclosed in a traceable,
timely, non-manipulable, and operationally reviewable manner throughout
the lifecycle of a probabilistic system.

A system satisfies RDM only if:
  • materially relevant remaining-state information is disclosed
  • disclosure timing remains operationally consistent
  • disclosure outputs remain traceable to computed remaining-state reality
  • public visibility does not enable exploitability of individual outcomes
  • disclosure logic does not create false impressions of remaining availability


A system that withholds materially relevant remaining-state reality or
discloses it in a misleading, delayed, opaque, or manipulable manner
does not satisfy RDM.

Module Function

RDM defines the public transparency layer of probabilistic integrity.

It ensures that remaining-state reality does not remain hidden inside
internal operational systems, but becomes materially visible to
consumers, auditors, regulators, or other authorized participants under
governed disclosure conditions.

The module applies wherever probabilistic systems must publicly or
operationally disclose what still remains available.

Minimum Implementation Framework (MIF)

Step 1 — Define Disclosure Scope

The organization must define which remaining-state information is
disclosed.

Minimum requirement:
  • disclosed categories are explicit
  • materially relevant remaining-state information is identifiable
  • undefined disclosure scope is excluded from valid transparency logic


Step 2 — Define Disclosure Timing

The system must define when remaining-state information becomes visible.

Minimum requirement:
  • disclosure cadence is explicit
  • update timing remains operationally consistent
  • arbitrarily delayed or selectively hidden disclosure is excluded from valid operation


Step 3 — Define Disclosure Form

The system must define how remaining-state information is presented.

Minimum requirement:
  • disclosure outputs are understandable and reviewable
  • disclosure remains traceable to computed remaining-state reality
  • structurally misleading presentation is excluded from valid disclosure logic


Step 4 — Define Non-Manipulable Transparency Conditions

The system must define how disclosure remains informative without
enabling exploitation.

Minimum requirement:
  • disclosure remains sufficiently aggregated where necessary
  • individual winning outcomes cannot be inferred from disclosure outputs
  • transparency does not silently become exploit-enabling intelligence


Step 5 — Preserve Disclosure Integrity

The system must preserve consistency between computed remaining-state
reality and public or operational disclosure outputs.

Minimum requirement:
  • disclosed information remains aligned with computed state
  • omission or distortion is identifiable
  • disclosure integrity remains auditor-reviewable


Step 6 — Restrict Invalid Disclosure

The system must not be treated as valid if disclosure becomes materially
incomplete, misleading, manipulable, selectively hidden, or structurally
disconnected from remaining-state reality.

Minimum requirement:
  • invalid disclosure conditions are identifiable
  • materially misleading disclosure is blocked or invalidated
  • public visibility alone does not justify missing disclosure integrity


Use Case 1 — Lottery Remaining Prize Visibility

Scenario

A lottery operator must disclose current remaining prize availability
during lifecycle operation while ensuring that the disclosure remains
materially informative but non-exploitable.

Application

RDM is used to ensure:
  • remaining-state disclosure scope is explicit
  • update timing remains consistent
  • disclosure outputs reflect computed remaining-state reality
  • public visibility does not expose exploitable individual outcome patterns


Result

The operator gains a stronger basis for maintaining public transparency
without compromising operational integrity.

Use Case 2 — Digital Reward Platform Transparency

Scenario

A digital or Web3 reward system must publicly communicate remaining
reward availability while preserving trust, auditability, and
non-manipulable transparency conditions.

Application

RDM is used to ensure:
  • disclosure logic remains structurally governed
  • remaining-state outputs remain reviewable
  • public transparency remains aligned with actual computed reality
  • disclosure does not silently become misleading marketing representation


Result

The platform gains a structurally governed disclosure layer instead of
relying on symbolic or selectively visible transparency claims.

Canonical Closing Statement

If remaining reality is not materially disclosed, probabilistic
transparency does not operationally exist.
