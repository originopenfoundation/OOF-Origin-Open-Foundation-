Canonical Definition

Redemption Integrity Module defines the structural conditions
under which prize claims, reward claims, validation events, expired
claims, invalid claims, fraudulent claims, and completed redemptions
remain traceable, distinguishable, and auditor-verifiable throughout the
operational lifecycle of a probabilistic system.

A system satisfies RIM only if:
  • redemption events remain traceable
  • valid, invalid, expired, fraudulent, and completed claim states are distinguishable
  • reward-state changes caused by redemption remain reviewable
  • redemption records do not silently distort remaining-state reality
  • claim handling remains structurally aligned with lifecycle auditability conditions


A system that cannot distinguish and verify redemption states does not
satisfy RIM.

Module Function

RIM defines the redemption-state integrity layer of probabilistic
transparency.

It ensures that prize or reward availability is not reduced through
opaque, ambiguous, or unreviewable claim handling.

The module applies wherever probabilistic systems must process reward
claims and maintain a trustworthy distinction between what was redeemed,
what was merely claimed, what was invalid, and what remains available.

Minimum Implementation Framework (MIF)

Step 1 — Define Redemption States

The organization must define the operational states through which a
reward claim may pass.

Minimum requirement:
  • claim states are explicit
  • valid, invalid, expired, fraudulent, and completed conditions are distinguishable
  • undefined redemption states are excluded from valid operation


Step 2 — Define Claim Validation Conditions

The system must define how reward claims are validated.

Minimum requirement:
  • validation conditions are explicit
  • accepted and rejected claims are distinguishable
  • unverifiable or ambiguous validation is excluded from valid redemption logic


Step 3 — Define Redemption Event Recording

The system must define how redemption events are recorded and
reviewable.

Minimum requirement:
  • redemption events are traceable
  • reward tier and claim state are linked to recorded events
  • silent or missing redemption records are excluded from valid integrity conditions


Step 4 — Define Fraud and Expiry Distinction

The system must define how fraudulent, invalid, expired, or otherwise
non-completed claims are distinguished from valid redemption.

Minimum requirement:
  • fraud and expiry states are explicitly separated
  • invalid claims do not silently reduce valid remaining-state reality
  • claim-state distortion is excluded from valid operation


Step 5 — Preserve Redemption-State Consistency

The system must preserve consistency between redemption records and the
current remaining reward state.

Minimum requirement:
  • completed redemption affects remaining-state reality in a reviewable way
  • invalid or fraudulent claims do not create false depletion
  • redemption integrity remains auditor-verifiable


Step 6 — Restrict Invalid Redemption Handling

The system must not be treated as valid if redemption events are opaque,
ambiguous, untraceable, or structurally inconsistent with
remaining-state reality.

Minimum requirement:
  • invalid redemption conditions are identifiable
  • ambiguous or distorted claim handling is blocked or invalidated
  • claim processing alone does not justify missing redemption integrity


Use Case 1 — Lottery Prize Claim Verification

Scenario

A lottery operator receives prize claims during operation and must
preserve a trustworthy distinction between valid redemptions, invalid
submissions, expired entitlements, and fraudulent claims.

Application

RIM is used to ensure:
  • redemption states are explicitly defined
  • valid and invalid claims are structurally separated
  • redemption events remain traceable
  • remaining-state depletion reflects only valid completed redemption


Result

The operator gains a stronger basis for proving that remaining prize
reality reflects actual valid redemption rather than ambiguous or
distorted claim handling.

Use Case 2 — Digital Reward Claim Lifecycle

Scenario

A digital reward system processes claim attempts, verification states,
expirations, and fraud checks while needing to keep current remaining
availability materially accurate.

Application

RIM is used to ensure:
  • claim-state logic is explicit
  • fraudulent or expired claims remain distinguishable
  • completed redemption is properly recorded
  • remaining-state reality remains aligned with verified claim outcomes


Result

The platform gains a structurally governed redemption layer instead of
relying on opaque claim processing that may distort real remaining
availability.

Canonical Closing Statement

If redemption states cannot be distinguished and verified,
remaining-state reality cannot remain materially fair.
