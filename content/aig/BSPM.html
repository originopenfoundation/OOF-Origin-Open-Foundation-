Canonical Definition System

Canonical Definition

Behavioral Safety Preservation Module (BSPM) defines the structural conditions
under which AI behavioral safety is continuously preserved throughout
operational execution, behavioral evolution, environmental change, and
autonomous decision-making while maintaining governance integrity,
accountability, and operational trust.

BSPM governs behavioral safety preservation.

The module establishes the governance conditions required to ensure that safe
AI behavior remains continuously protected rather than temporarily achieved.

Safety is not established once.

Safety must be preserved continuously.

BSPM governs that preservation.

Module Operational Role

BSPM defines the behavioral-safety-preservation layer of BSS.

Its role is to preserve safe AI behavior throughout the complete behavioral
lifecycle.

Module Operational Space

BSPM governs:

  • behavioral safety preservation
  • continuous behavioral safety
  • governance safety preservation
  • operational safety continuity
  • behavioral protection
  • safety maintenance
  • trustworthy operation
  • safety governance


The module applies wherever organizations require AI behavior to remain
continuously safe despite changing operational conditions.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • safe behavior,
  • governance integrity,
  • operational reliability,
  • behavioral predictability,
  • accountable operation,
  • long-term safety.


Its function is to ensure that behavioral safety remains an ongoing governance
responsibility rather than a one-time validation.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Behavioral Safety Preservation Object

The organization must define which AI behaviors require continuous safety
preservation.

2. Define Preservation Conditions

The system must define governance conditions including:

  • behavioral predictability,
  • governance compliance,
  • operational reliability,
  • accountability,
  • behavioral stability,
  • continuous safety.


3. Define Preservation Failure Detection Logic

The system must identify:

  • declining behavioral safety,
  • unsafe behavioral patterns,
  • governance failures,
  • operational instability,
  • emerging safety risks,
  • governance-invalid safety conditions.


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

Governance response may include:

  • safety review,
  • governance assessment,
  • preventive intervention,
  • operational restriction,
  • escalation,
  • corrective actions.


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • safety assessments,
  • governance reviews,
  • behavioral observations,
  • preventive actions,
  • supporting evidence,
  • resulting governance outcomes.


An AI behavioral environment must not be considered behaviorally safe unless
behavioral safety can be continuously preserved, reviewed, validated,
monitored, and governed.

Use Case 1 — Autonomous Surgical Assistant

Scenario

An AI-assisted robotic surgery platform continuously supports complex medical
procedures.

Application

BSPM preserves behavioral safety throughout continuous operation despite
changing patient conditions and clinical environments.

Result

The healthcare organization strengthens patient protection, clinical
confidence, and long-term governance assurance.

Use Case 2 — Autonomous Railway Network

Scenario

AI systems continuously coordinate autonomous train operations across a

Application

BSPM governs continuous behavioral safety preservation despite operational

Result

The railway operator maintains safe autonomous operations, strengthens public

Canonical Closing Statement

Safety endures only when it is continuously protected. Behavioral Safety
Preservation ensures that trustworthy AI remains worthy of trust every moment
it operates, not only at the moment it is approved.
