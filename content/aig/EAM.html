Canonical Definition System

Canonical Definition

Escalation Authority Module (EAM) defines the structural conditions under
which AI behavioral escalation is transferred to the appropriate authority
according to predefined governance roles, delegated responsibilities,
operational hierarchies, authorization rules, and escalation policies
throughout the complete behavioral lifecycle.

EAM governs escalation authority.

The module establishes the foundational conditions required to ensure that
every escalation reaches the correct decision-maker capable of assuming
responsibility and exercising legitimate governance authority.

Escalation without authority creates uncertainty.

Escalation must always reach the appropriate authority.

EAM governs that authority.

Module Operational Role

EAM defines the escalation-authority layer of BES.

Its role is to ensure that AI behavioral escalation reaches the correct
governance authority.

Module Operational Space

EAM governs:

  • escalation authority
  • authority transfer
  • governance authority
  • delegated authority
  • escalation routing
  • Human-AI authority transition
  • escalation authorization
  • authority validation


The module applies wherever AI behavioral escalation requires transfer of
authority.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • legitimate escalation authority
  • correct authority assignment
  • traceable authority transfer
  • governance-valid intervention
  • accountable escalation
  • trustworthy governance execution


Its function is to ensure that escalation is always directed to the person,
role, organization, or AI governance entity with legitimate authority to act.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define Escalation Authorities

The organization must define all authorities authorized to receive AI
behavioral escalations.
This may include:

  • human operators
  • supervisors
  • governance officers
  • safety managers
  • incident response teams
  • executive authorities
  • regulatory authorities
  • authorized autonomous supervisory systems


2. Define Authority Assignment Rules

The system must define which authority is responsible for each escalation
category.

3. Define Authority Selection Logic

The system must determine the appropriate escalation authority based on
operational context, severity, governance policy, and delegated
responsibilities.

4. Define Governance Response

The organization must define governance actions following successful authority
transfer.

5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • escalation authority assignments
  • authority transfer events
  • governance reviews
  • intervention decisions
  • resulting behavioral states


An AI behavioral environment must not remain escalation-valid if materially
significant authority-transfer activities cannot be reconstructed, reviewed,
validated, preserved, or governed.

Use Case 1 — Autonomous Hospital AI

Scenario

An AI clinical assistant identifies a life-threatening condition beyond its
delegated authority.

Application

EAM governs the transfer of escalation to the responsible emergency physician
according to predefined governance rules.

Result

The hospital preserves accountability, accelerates critical response,
strengthens governance, and improves patient safety.

Use Case 2 — Autonomous Manufacturing System

Scenario

An industrial AI detects abnormal production behavior requiring immediate
operational intervention.

Application

EAM governs escalation routing to the authorized production supervisor
responsible for operational decisions.

Result

The organization reduces operational downtime, preserves governance authority,
improves accountability, and strengthens Human-AI operational coordination.
