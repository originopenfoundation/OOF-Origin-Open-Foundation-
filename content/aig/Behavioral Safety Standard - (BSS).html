Canonical Definition System

Canonical Definition

Behavioral Safety Standard (BSS) defines the structural conditions under which
AI behavior remains safe, governable, predictable, controllable, accountable,
and operationally trustworthy while minimizing unacceptable behavioral risks
throughout the complete behavioral lifecycle.

BSS governs AI behavioral safety.

The standard establishes the governance conditions required to ensure that
behavioral safety is continuously preserved despite operational uncertainty,
environmental change, autonomous decision-making, or evolving system behavior.

Safety is not created by technology alone.

Safety emerges when behavior remains continuously governed.

BSS governs that behavioral safety.

Governance Flow Position

Behavioral Integrity → Behavioral Boundaries → Behavioral Escalation →
Behavioral Auditability → Behavioral Evidence → Behavioral Trust → Behavioral
Correction → Behavioral Continuity → Behavioral Interoperability → Behavioral
Safety

Standard Abstract

Every previous behavioral space ultimately supports one final objective:

Safe AI behavior.

Integrity, boundaries, escalation, auditability, evidence, trust, correction,
continuity, and interoperability all contribute to behavioral safety.

BSS integrates these governance conditions into the final behavioral
governance objective.

Operational Role

Behavioral Safety Standard provides the governance layer responsible for
preserving safe AI behavior across all operational conditions.

Its role is to ensure that behavioral safety remains continuously governed
rather than assumed.

Operational Space

BSS governs the architecture space responsible for AI behavioral safety.

The space includes:

  • behavioral safety
  • safe behavioral operation
  • behavioral risk prevention
  • safe Human-AI interaction
  • safe AI collaboration
  • operational behavioral protection
  • governance safety
  • behavioral harm prevention


This space exists because trustworthy AI ultimately depends on behavior that
remains safe under real operational conditions.

Why This Standard Exists

Without governed behavioral safety:

  • people may be harmed,
  • organizations lose trust,
  • operational risks increase,
  • autonomous failures escalate,
  • governance loses credibility,
  • AI adoption declines.


BSS exists because behavioral safety is the final governance outcome toward
which every preceding AIG® standard contributes.

Behavioral Safety Logic

Behavioral safety remains governance-valid when AI behavior is:

  • predictable,
  • controllable,
  • accountable,
  • explainable,
  • continuously monitored,
  • governance-aligned.


Behavioral Safety Failure Rule

Behavioral safety fails when AI behavior creates unacceptable risk that can no
longer be governed, controlled, justified, or safely managed.
