Canonical Definition System

Canonical Definition

Behavioral Legitimacy Module (BLM) defines the structural conditions under
which AI behavior remains authorized, policy-compliant, governance-aligned,
explainable, traceable, governable, observable, and operationally legitimate
throughout the behavioral lifecycle.

BLM governs behavioral legitimacy.

The module establishes the foundational conditions required to ensure that AI
behavior remains within approved governance objectives, organizational
policies, legal requirements, ethical constraints, and authorized operational
boundaries.

AI behavior may be technically correct.

AI behavior must also be legitimately authorized.

BLM governs that legitimacy.

Module Operational Role

BLM defines the behavioral-legitimacy layer of BIS.

Its role is to preserve the legitimacy of AI behavior throughout operational
execution.

Module Operational Space

BLM governs:

  • behavioral legitimacy
  • behavioral authorization
  • policy-compliant behavior
  • governance-aligned behavior
  • behavioral conformity
  • behavioral authorization validation
  • behavioral legitimacy monitoring
  • behavioral governance assurance


The module applies wherever AI behavior must remain legitimate under
established governance rules.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • authorized AI behavior
  • governance-compliant behavior
  • policy-aligned behavior
  • traceable behavioral authorization
  • governance-valid behavioral legitimacy
  • operational behavioral trust


Its function is to ensure that AI behavior remains both operationally
effective and governance-legitimate.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Behavioral Legitimacy Object

The organization must define which AI behaviors require legitimacy governance.
This may include:

  • autonomous decisions
  • conversational behavior
  • recommendations
  • robotic actions
  • Human-AI interactions
  • multi-agent coordination
  • automated workflows
  • adaptive operational behavior


2. Define Behavioral Legitimacy Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which behavioral legitimacy
remains valid.
This includes:

  • authorization requirements
  • policy requirements
  • governance requirements
  • traceability requirements
  • validation requirements
  • governance-valid legitimacy conditions


3. Define Behavioral Legitimacy Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how behavioral illegitimacy is identified.
This may include:

  • unauthorized behavior
  • policy violations
  • governance deviations
  • behavioral boundary violations
  • unauthorized autonomous adaptation
  • governance-invalid behavioral activities


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for behavioral-legitimacy failures.
Governance response may include:

  • legitimacy review
  • policy validation
  • behavioral correction
  • governance intervention
  • authorization reassessment
  • operational suspension
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • behavioral authorizations
  • governance reviews
  • policy validations
  • legitimacy assessments
  • governance interventions
  • resulting behavioral states


An AI behavioral environment must not remain integrity-valid if materially
significant behavioral-legitimacy activities cannot be reconstructed,
reviewed, validated, preserved, or governed.

Canonical Closing Statement

Behavioral Legitimacy Module (BLM) defines the structural conditions under
which AI behavior remains authorized, policy-compliant, governance-aligned,
explainable, traceable, governable, observable, and operationally legitimate
