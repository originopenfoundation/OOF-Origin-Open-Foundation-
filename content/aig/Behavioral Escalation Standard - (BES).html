Canonical Definition System

Canonical Definition

Behavioral Escalation Standard (BES) defines the structural conditions under
which AI behavior triggers appropriate escalation, intervention, notification,
authority transfer, operational restriction, or human oversight when
predefined behavioral conditions, governance thresholds, or operational risks
are exceeded throughout the complete behavioral lifecycle.

BES governs AI behavioral escalation.

The standard establishes the foundational conditions required to ensure that
AI systems never continue autonomous behavior beyond approved governance
thresholds without appropriate escalation to higher authority or alternative
governance mechanisms.

AI behavior cannot govern every situation autonomously.

Some situations require escalation.

BES governs that escalation.

A. Standard Abstract

No AI system should operate indefinitely without recognizing situations that
exceed its authority, capability, confidence, operational limits, or
governance permissions.

Behavioral escalation ensures that AI knows when autonomous behavior must stop
and governance must take over.

Escalation transforms uncertainty into governable action.

BES establishes the governance architecture for that transition.

B. Core Principle

Trustworthy AI must know not only how to act, but also when to escalate.

C. Scope

This standard may apply to:

  • AI systems
  • autonomous agents
  • Human-AI environments
  • multi-agent ecosystems
  • robotics
  • industrial AI
  • enterprise AI
  • governmental AI
  • future autonomous intelligence ecosystems


BES applies wherever AI behavior requires governed escalation mechanisms.

D. Why This Standard Exists

Many AI failures occur because escalation never happens.

Examples include:

  • failure to request human intervention
  • continued execution despite uncertainty
  • autonomous operation beyond authority
  • ignored governance thresholds
  • delayed emergency response
  • missing operational intervention
  • escalation-chain failure
  • excessive autonomous confidence


BES exists because behavioral escalation forms a distinct governance space.

E. Behavioral Escalation Logic

Behavioral escalation exists only when the following remain materially
preservable: 1. Escalation Trigger Integrity

Escalation conditions remain clearly defined. 2. Escalation Timing Integrity

Escalation occurs at the appropriate moment. 3. Escalation Authority Integrity

Escalation reaches the correct authority. 4. Escalation Traceability

Escalation activities remain observable and reconstructable. 5. Escalation
Continuity

Escalation remains governable throughout the complete response process.

F. Operational Architecture Space

BES defines the operational architecture space for:

  • behavioral escalation
  • behavioral intervention
  • Human-AI escalation
  • autonomous escalation
  • governance escalation
  • escalation authority
  • escalation governance
  • operational escalation management


This space exists because trustworthy AI must recognize when autonomous
behavior is no longer sufficient.

BES governs AI behavioral escalation.

G. Runtime Position

BES operates as the third behavioral governance standard of AIG®.

It follows Behavioral Boundaries because escalation begins when behavioral
boundaries, permissions, operational conditions, or governance thresholds can
no longer be safely maintained.

H. Behavioral Escalation Failure Rule

Behavioral-escalation failure occurs when AI behavior continues despite
conditions requiring intervention, authority transfer, operational
restriction, or governance escalation.

Examples include:

  • missing escalation
  • delayed escalation
  • incorrect escalation authority
  • ignored governance thresholds
  • continued unauthorized execution
  • escalation process failure


BES exists to identify and govern these conditions.

I. Validity Logic

An AI behavioral environment is valid under BES when:

  • escalation triggers remain defined
  • escalation timing remains appropriate
  • escalation authority remains correct
  • escalation activities remain traceable
  • governance continuously supervises escalation


An AI behavioral environment becomes invalid when:

  • escalation does not occur
  • escalation occurs too late
  • escalation reaches the wrong authority
  • governance loses escalation control
  • autonomous execution continues beyond approved limits

which AI behavior triggers appropriate escalation, intervention, notification,
authority transfer, operational restriction, or human oversight when
