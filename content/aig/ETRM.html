Canonical Definition System

Canonical Definition

Escalation Traceability Module (ETRM) defines the structural conditions under
which every AI behavioral escalation remains completely observable,
reconstructable, attributable, explainable, verifiable, governable, and
operationally auditable throughout the complete behavioral lifecycle.

ETRM governs escalation traceability.

The module establishes the foundational conditions required to ensure that
every escalation event can be reconstructed from its initial trigger through
authority transfer, intervention, decision-making, and final resolution.

An escalation that cannot be reconstructed cannot be fully governed.

ETRM governs that traceability.

Module Operational Role

ETRM defines the escalation-traceability layer of BES.

Its role is to preserve complete governance visibility across the entire
escalation process.

Module Operational Space

ETRM governs:

  • escalation traceability
  • escalation event reconstruction
  • escalation audit trail
  • authority traceability
  • intervention traceability
  • governance evidence
  • escalation documentation
  • escalation lifecycle visibility


The module applies wherever AI behavioral escalation must remain fully
reconstructable.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • complete escalation history
  • traceable escalation decisions
  • attributable authority actions
  • governance-valid audit records
  • explainable intervention history
  • trustworthy escalation evidence


Its function is to ensure that every escalation event remains transparent and
independently verifiable.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define Escalation Traceability Objects

The organization must define which escalation events require complete
traceability.
This may include:

  • escalation triggers
  • authority transfers
  • intervention actions
  • governance decisions
  • behavioral restrictions
  • operational recoveries
  • Human-AI interactions
  • autonomous escalation events


2. Define Traceability Conditions

The system must define the information required to preserve escalation
traceability.
This includes:

  • timestamps
  • trigger conditions
  • responsible authorities
  • intervention records
  • governance decisions
  • outcome documentation


3. Define Traceability Validation Logic

The system must continuously validate that escalation events remain complete
and reconstructable.

4. Define Governance Response

The organization must define governance actions when escalation traceability
becomes incomplete or unreliable.

5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • escalation initiation
  • authority transitions
  • intervention activities
  • governance evaluations
  • final escalation outcomes
  • resulting behavioral states


An AI behavioral environment must not remain escalation-valid if materially
significant escalation activities cannot be reconstructed, reviewed,
validated, preserved, or governed.

Use Case 1 — AI Banking Platform

Scenario

An AI fraud-detection system escalates suspicious financial activity for
manual investigation.

Application

ETRM governs complete traceability of escalation triggers, authority
transfers, investigator actions, governance decisions, and case resolution.

Result

The financial institution strengthens auditability, regulatory compliance,
governance transparency, and operational trust.

Use Case 2 — Autonomous Air Traffic Support AI

Scenario

An AI air traffic support system escalates an abnormal flight condition to
human controllers.

Application

ETRM governs complete reconstruction of the escalation process from initial
detection through operational resolution.

Result

The aviation authority improves operational accountability, strengthens safety
investigations, preserves governance evidence, and increases trust in
AI-supported operations.
