Methodology Governance

Managing Methodologies Throughout Their Complete Lifecycle

Every methodology should be governed. Without governance,
methodologies may evolve inconsistently, lose structural
integrity, accumulate conflicting changes, or gradually diverge
from the purpose and principles upon which they
were originally established.

Methodology Governance establishes the principles,
responsibilities, and governance mechanisms required to manage
methodologies throughout their complete lifecycle while preserving
their identity, consistency, quality, and long-term reliability.
Governance ensures that methodologies remain controlled assets
rather than collections of independently changing documents.

Effective governance provides structured processes for reviewing,
maintaining, updating, extending, versioning, approving, and, when
appropriate, retiring methodologies. It also establishes
accountability by ensuring that significant methodological
decisions and changes remain documented, understandable,
traceable, and aligned with the
methodology's governing principles.

Well-governed methodologies are better equipped to adapt to
changing technologies, operational environments, and
organizational needs without compromising their structural
integrity or methodological identity. Governance supports
long-term confidence by ensuring that evolution occurs
deliberately, transparently, and under clearly defined
methodological control.

Canonical Closing Statement

Methodologies are created through design, but they remain
trustworthy through governance. Governance preserves their
identity, integrity, and long-term value
throughout their entire lifecycle.
