MIF v6.0 — Dual-Direction Workplace & Supply Chain Verification

Parent methodology: VERICYCLE™ — Verification & Accountability Cycle. The framework is a voluntary, progressive, risk-based and technology-neutral implementation reference, not a regulatory or certification requirement.

It starts with a simple declaration, establishes operational visibility, enables verification, and corrects what the evidence establishes. A declaration is not a compliance certificate, institutional finding, or guarantee of lawful performance.

PART I — IMPLEMENTATION PURPOSE

1. Canonical Definition

The VERICYCLE™ Minimum Implementation Framework establishes the smallest practical sequence of organizational actions through which employers can begin identifying, declaring, verifying and correcting qualification-dependent workplace authorization conditions.

The framework implements the VERICYCLE™ canonical cycle:

IDENTIFY → DECLARE → DISCLOSE → VERIFY → CORRECT

Its implementation is initiated through two complementary directions:

Bottom-Up Workplace Verification Top-Down Product & Supply Chain Verification Both directions may initiate the same basic employer declaration.

The framework does not require the creation of a new permanent inspection structure, proprietary software platform, centralized personnel database or additional statutory reporting duty.

Its primary purpose is to encourage employers to identify their own relevant operational requirements, recognize authorization gaps and correct them before an external intervention becomes necessary.

Canonical Implementation Principle Start With a Declaration. Establish Operational Visibility. Enable Verification. Correct What the Evidence Establishes.

2. Why Minimum Implementation Is Necessary

Organizations may already maintain qualification records, personnel files, production documentation and safety procedures.

The difficulty is not necessarily the absence of those records.

It is the potential absence of a reliable connection between:

Restricted Activity → Actual Performer → Required Qualification → Legitimate Assignment Authority → Operational Permission → Actual Execution A complete compliance audit may be expensive and unnecessarily burdensome as the first step.

VERICYCLE™ therefore begins with a much smaller intervention.

A company is asked to identify the relevant activities it performs and provide basic aggregate information about the workers and authorizations associated with those activities.

This first declaration creates an identifiable starting point.

It is not a compliance certificate.

It is not an institutional finding.

It is not a guarantee that all relevant work was performed lawfully.

Foundational Distinction

Initial Declaration ≠ Verified Compliance

3. Implementation Objective

The framework has five primary objectives.

First — Organizational Self-Identification Encourage companies to recognize which activities require specific competence or authorization.

Second — Actual Operator Awareness Encourage companies to distinguish the persons formally listed as qualified from the persons actually performing the relevant work.

Third — Legitimate Assignment Control Ensure that restricted work is assigned by persons acting within appropriate authority boundaries.

Fourth — Practical Verification Make relevant authorization information accessible to those who legitimately need it.

Fifth — Continuous Internal Correction Create a repeatable process through which identified discrepancies can be corrected without waiting for an accident or external inspection.

Primary Objective

Companies Should Begin Verifying Their Own Operational Reality.

PART II — TWO VERIFICATION DIRECTIONS

4. Dual-Direction Implementation Model

The framework introduces two independent verification directions.

Direction A — Bottom-Up Verification begins with actual workplace activities, employer obligations, relevant workers and workplace evidence.

The initial declaration may be requested or encouraged through appropriate occupational safety, employer, contractor or competent institutional arrangements.

Direction B — Top-Down Verification begins with relevant final-product, customer, production-process or supplier assurance requirements.

A final-product manufacturer or principal customer requests a basic declaration from a supplier whose work affects the relevant requirements.

Common Response The receiving company begins the same internal identification and verification process.

Two Independent Verification Directions. One Continuous Correction Cycle. Embedded in VERICYCLE™.

Essential Boundary

Neither direction automatically creates new legal powers.

Requests must originate from legitimate authority, applicable requirements, voluntary participation or appropriate contractual arrangements.

5. Bottom-Up Initiation

5.1 Purpose

Bottom-up initiation encourages an employer to identify qualification-dependent activities and establish a basic internal authorization picture.

5.2 Potential Initiators

Depending on legal competence and organizational arrangements:

The employer itself.

A responsible workplace safety function.

A principal contractor.

A host organization.

A relevant industry or employer association.

A competent occupational safety institution.

A competent labor-related authority.

5.3 Initial Action

A short official communication invites or, where an existing lawful basis permits, requests the company to complete a basic internal declaration.

The message identifies the purpose and relevant scope.

It does not require the immediate submission of all employee certificates.

5.4 Institutional Relevance

Suva and other competent Swiss occupational safety institutions may possess valuable practical knowledge concerning hazardous activities, workplace conditions, accident prevention and the difference between written procedures and actual execution.

Their potential contribution is particularly relevant to assessing whether a proposed verification mechanism reflects operational reality.

Participation, endorsement and implementation remain subject to their independent decisions and applicable mandates.

6. Top-Down Initiation

6.1 Purpose

Top-down initiation encourages suppliers to examine relevant competence and authorization conditions because a final- product manufacturer or principal customer requires appropriate production-process assurance.

6.2 Potential Initiators

Final-product manufacturers.

Principal customers.

Main contractors.

Relevant procurement functions.

Authorized supplier quality functions.

Other competent actors operating within applicable product or contractual frameworks.

6.3 Initial Action

A short communication requests the supplier to identify relevant qualification-dependent production activities and provide a basic declaration.

6.4 Proportionality

The request must relate to applicable product, process, technical or contractual requirements.

It must not assume that every production process requires a formally certified worker.

6.5 Preventive Function

A supplier that expects proportionate customer verification has an additional reason to maintain accurate internal authorization records.

Canonical Principle

Customer Verification Should Encourage Supplier Self-Verification, Not Automatically Require Customer Inspection.

PART III — PHASE ONE: SIMPLE DECLARATION

7. Phase One — One Email, One Basic Declaration

Indicative Period: Weeks 1–2

7.1 Objective

Establish an initial, dated organizational declaration concerning relevant qualification-dependent activities.

7.2 Communication Method

Use existing official email channels or another already available organizational communication method.

No new digital platform is required.

7.3 Basic Declaration Content

The company identifies:

Company Name Company Identifier, such as the Swiss UID where relevant and available.

Operating Site Relevant Restricted Activity or Production Process Applicable Qualification or Authorization Requirement Number of Persons Declared as Qualified or Authorized for the Relevant Activity Responsible Organizational Contact Declaration Date Responsible Confirmation Where the actual number of performers is already known, the company may also state it separately.

Where that number is not yet established, it must not be invented or presented as verified.

7.4 Scope

The first declaration concerns relevant qualification-dependent activities.

It does not require a complete list of all employees.

It does not require the publication of individual personnel data.

It does not require a certificate attachment for every worker.

7.5 Initial Evidence Status

The declaration is classified as:

Employer-Declared — Not Yet Independently Verified

7.6 Document Retention

The employer retains the declaration within its existing records.

Routine submission to a public inspectorate is not required by VERICYCLE™ itself.

A legitimate requesting party may receive the agreed or lawfully required information within the applicable scope.

7.7 Governance Output

Initial Restricted Activity & Aggregate Authorization Declaration

7.8 Canonical Principle

The First Step Is Not to Inspect the Company. It Is to Require the Company to Identify What It Claims to Control.

8. Phase One — Bottom-Up Email Template

Dear Sir or Madam, As part of a proposed workplace competence and authorization verification initiative, your organization is invited to complete a basic internal declaration concerning activities that require specific professional qualifications, technical approvals or operational authorizations.

The initial declaration should identify the relevant activities, applicable requirements, general numbers of qualified or authorized persons and the responsible organizational contact.

At this initial stage, no complete personnel lists, individual certificates or new software systems are requested.

The purpose is to establish a clear starting point for organizational self-verification and, where necessary, subsequent internal correction.

Participation and any information submission remain subject to the applicable legal or organizational basis of the requesting body.

Kind regards, [Authorized Requesting Organization]

9. Phase One — Top-Down Email Template

Dear Sir or Madam, To support verification of relevant production-process requirements associated with our products or supplied components, we request a basic declaration of qualification-dependent activities within the agreed supply scope.

Please identify the relevant production processes, applicable qualification or authorization requirements, general numbers of qualified or authorized personnel and the responsible organizational contact.

The initial declaration does not require complete personnel records or copies of every individual certificate.

Its purpose is to establish a proportionate starting point for supplier self-verification and further clarification where necessary.

The scope and form of the declaration are subject to our applicable contractual and technical requirements.

Kind regards, [Authorized Customer or Manufacturer]

10. Why Phase One Must Remain Simple

The first phase is designed to produce an initial governance signal with minimal disruption.

It should not be converted into a complete audit.

Requiring extensive records immediately may discourage participation, create unnecessary administrative work and obscure the central purpose.

The initial question is deliberately limited:

Which relevant activities does the company perform, and what general authorization coverage does it declare?

A company may discover inconsistencies while preparing the declaration.

That discovery is already a useful internal governance outcome.

Canonical Principle

A Simple Declaration Can Trigger a Serious Internal Review Without Becoming a Complex External Inspection.

PART IV — PHASE TWO: ACTUAL OPERATORS & ASSIGNMENT AUTHORITY

11. Phase Two — Internal Population Verification

Indicative Period: Weeks 3–4

11.1 Objective

Determine whether the initial declared qualification coverage corresponds to the actual persons performing the relevant activities.

11.2 Required Internal Questions

Which persons actually perform the restricted activity?

Which qualifications or instructions apply?

Which persons satisfy those requirements?

Which authorizations are currently valid?

Who may legitimately assign the work?

Is separate technical approval required?

Are there unresolved authorization gaps?

11.3 Core Distinction

Declared Qualified Persons ≠ Actual Operator Population

11.4 Illustrative Example

A company declares two certified crane operators.

An internal review establishes that ten persons operated the relevant crane during the period.

The company must examine the qualification and authorization status of the actual operator population.

The eight additional persons are not automatically classified as unqualified.

Their status is initially unresolved until assessed.

11.5 Governance Output

Actual Operator & Assignment Authority Verification Record

12. Phase Two — Assignment Authority Verification

12.1 Objective

Identify the persons who may legitimately assign qualification-dependent work.

12.2 Required Distinctions

Organizational Management Authority Work Assignment Authority Technical Approval Authority Worker Operational Authorization

12.3 Core Requirement

A person assigning restricted work must not rely solely on organizational seniority as justification for disregarding applicable competence requirements.

12.4 Internal Correction

Where an assignment authority boundary is unclear, the employer must clarify the relevant responsibility, delegation or technical approval arrangement.

12.5 Governance Output

Declared & Verified Assignment Authority Boundaries

PART V — PHASE THREE: INDIVIDUAL AUTHORIZATION

13. Phase Three — Operational Authorization Record

Indicative Period: Weeks 5–6

13.1 Objective

Establish practical evidence of the relevant worker's permitted operational scope.

13.2 Minimum Content

Worker Identity Employer or Responsible Organization Authorized Activity Relevant Qualification or Competence Basis Applicable Equipment or Process Scope Required Supervision or Technical Approval Restrictions Validity Conditions Legitimate Granting Authority Authorization Date Verification Contact or Mechanism

13.3 Format

The record may be:

Paper-based.

An existing employer authorization document.

A controlled digital record.

A suitable authorization card.

Another reliable format.

13.4 Copies

Where appropriate, a worker-accessible version and employer-controlled record may be maintained.

13.5 Limitation

An employer authorization card does not replace a legally required professional certificate.

13.6 Governance Output

Individual Operational Authorization Evidence

14. Phase Three — Cross-Site Verification

14.1 Objective

Ensure relevant qualification and authorization evidence remains practically verifiable when the worker performs restricted activities at another operating site.

14.2 Receiving-Site Verification

The responsible receiving-site actor examines:

Identity Relevant Qualification

Current Validity Activity Scope Employer Assignment Site-Specific Conditions Required Technical Approval

14.3 Evidence Options

Depending on applicable requirements, evidence may include original documents, accepted certified copies, verifiable digital credentials or other trusted confirmation.

14.4 Important Boundary

VERICYCLE™ does not impose universal notarization or a new statutory document-carrying requirement.

14.5 Governance Output

Cross-Site Authorization Verification State

PART VI — PHASE FOUR: WORKPLACE VISIBILITY & SELF-CONTROL

15. Phase Four — Mandatory Workplace Visibility Within the VERICYCLE™

Implementation

15.1 Minimum Requirement

An organization implementing the VERICYCLE™ Minimum Implementation Framework shall establish and maintain a clearly visible, accessible workplace information notice at an appropriate location where relevant workers can reasonably consult it.

The notice shall identify:

Company name and relevant operating site.

Company identification number, where applicable.

Qualification-dependent or restricted activities performed at the site.

Applicable competence and authorization requirements.

The rule that restricted work may only be assigned and performed under valid authorization conditions.

The responsible person or function through which relevant qualifications and authorizations can be verified.

The available channel for reporting potential discrepancies.

The date of the most recent update.

15.2 Placement

The information shall be placed at an appropriate visible workplace location, such as an existing safety information board, workshop entrance, site information point or another location accessible to relevant workers.

Where a physical noticeboard is unsuitable, an equally practical and reliably accessible alternative may be used.

15.3 Privacy Boundary

The notice shall not require the public display of individual certificates, complete personnel records or unnecessary personal information.

15.4 Maintenance

The employer shall update the notice when relevant restricted activities, verification contacts or authorization procedures materially change.

15.5 Verification Function

The workplace notice must provide an actual means of obtaining authorization confirmation.

A statement that the company complies with all requirements is not sufficient.

Canonical Principle

Restricted Work Must Be Identifiable. Relevant Authorization Must Be Verifiable. The Verification Route Must Be Visible at the Workplace.

16. Phase Four — Mutual Qualification Verification

16.1 Objective

Allow workers whose safety depends on another person's restricted activity to obtain appropriate confirmation of relevant competence and authorization.

16.2 Example — Crane Operation

A worker assisting with a heavy lift must have a practical way to verify the relevant crane operator's authorization.

The crane operator must likewise be able to establish the competence and responsibilities of relevant supporting personnel.

16.3 Scope

Verification concerns relevant operational permissions and safety-critical roles.

It does not require unrestricted access to employment files.

16.4 Governance Output

Mutual Operational Qualification Visibility

17. Phase Four — Workplace Self-Control Activation

17.1 Objective

Enable relevant workers to recognize and communicate possible authorization discrepancies.

17.2 Minimum Conditions

Workers must know:

Which Activities Are Restricted How Relevant Authorization Can Be Verified Who Can Clarify an Uncertain Assignment How a Concern Can Be Reported

17.3 Reporting Boundary

A reported concern is a signal.

It is not automatic proof of a violation.

17.4 Institutional Boundary

Workers do not acquire statutory inspection powers through VERICYCLE™.

17.5 Governance Output

Operational Self-Control & Reporting Readiness

PART VII — CONTINUOUS DUAL-DIRECTION VERIFICATION

18. Bottom-Up Continuing Verification

Following the initial implementation, workplace verification may be activated by:

New Restricted Activities New Actual Operators Changed Qualifications Changed Assignment Authorities New Equipment Relevant Workplace Concerns Accidents or Safety-Critical Events Competent Institutional Requests Each event is assessed according to its relevance.

The entire implementation process need not restart when a limited update is sufficient.

19. Top-Down Continuing Verification

Supplier verification may be activated by:

New Customer Requirements New Components New Production Processes Supplier Changes Relevant Qualification Requirements Customer Evidence Requests Identified Process Gaps Relevant Product-Related Findings The customer request should be limited to the applicable technical, contractual or legal scope.

The supplier performs appropriate internal verification.

20. Shared Correction Mechanism

Both directions use the same canonical process.

IDENTIFY → DECLARE → DISCLOSE → VERIFY → CORRECT

A verified discrepancy may require:

Authorization Clarification Qualification Verification Additional Training or Instruction Work Reassignment Technical Approval Correction Supplier Record Correction Operational Restriction Where Required Competent Institutional Referral Where Appropriate Renewed Verification

Canonical Principle

Two Independent Verification Directions. One Continuous Correction Cycle. Embedded in VERICYCLE™.

PART VIII — INSTITUTIONAL RESPONSE & GOVERNANCE BOUNDARIES

21. Institutional Verification Trigger

A competent institution may receive information suggesting a relevant workplace discrepancy.

The institution determines its response under its existing legal mandate.

Potential actions may include:

Clarification Evidence Request Risk Assessment Targeted Inspection Referral Corrective Requirement Where Legally Authorized Reasoned Closure VERICYCLE™ does not require every concern to result in a physical inspection.

22. Reporting & Evidence Classification

The framework distinguishes:

Signal Allegation Evidence Validated Finding Corrective Response The relevant institution or responsible organization must not automatically treat a reported concern as established fact.

Likewise, an unsupported managerial assurance must not automatically override credible contrary evidence.

Canonical Principle

Evidence Must Determine the Finding. Organizational Position Must Not Substitute for Evidence.

23. Institutional Role Separation

Employers Maintain relevant competence, assignment and authorization controls under applicable obligations.

Workers Follow applicable requirements and use legitimate verification or reporting routes.

Customers Request relevant supplier assurance within legitimate technical or contractual boundaries.

Suppliers Maintain appropriate evidence concerning the production activities they perform.

Occupational Safety Institutions Exercise their own lawful responsibilities concerning workplace safety and relevant operational conditions.

Product-Related Institutions Exercise applicable conformity, market surveillance or technical oversight functions within their mandates.

OOF® Provides the independent methodology and implementation reference.

OOF® Does Not Replace the Competent Institution.

24. No Mandatory New Technology

The minimum framework must be capable of initial application using existing organizational resources where sufficient.

Potential means include:

Official Email Existing Company Records Paper Declarations

Authorization Cards Existing Noticeboards Responsible Contact Persons Established Reporting Channels Digital systems may support later scaling.

They are not a prerequisite for the minimum implementation.

Foundation Before Automation.

25. Privacy & Proportionality

The framework requires only information relevant to the verification purpose.

The initial declaration is aggregate and organizational.

Individual worker information becomes relevant only when the corresponding operational authorization must be established.

Disclosure must remain proportionate and legally justified.

Supplier verification must not become unrestricted access to employee records.

Verification Need ≠ Unlimited Data Access

PART IX — BENEFITS & VALIDATION

26. Potential Institutional Benefits

26.1 Workplace Safety

Earlier recognition of authorization gaps may reduce exposure to improperly assigned qualification-dependent work.

26.2 Accident Prevention

Better alignment between actual performers, competence and operating conditions may contribute to preventing certain accidents.

26.3 Institutional Efficiency

Improved employer self-verification may provide competent institutions with more specific and actionable information.

26.4 Operational Reality Awareness

Workplace-originated evidence may help distinguish formal compliance arrangements from actual operating practices.

26.5 Supplier Assurance

Customer requests may encourage suppliers to maintain more reliable production-process evidence.

26.6 Organizational Accountability

Relevant assignment decisions become more identifiable and examinable.

These benefits are proposed and require independent validation.

27. Potential Economic & Workforce Benefits

27.1 Qualification Investment

Where companies identify unresolved competence gaps, they may invest in relevant training, qualification and authorization processes.

27.2 Workforce Retention

Investment in employee competence may increase the value of retaining experienced, qualified workers.

However, lower employee turnover is a potential secondary effect, not a guaranteed result.

27.3 Reduced Operational Disruption

Better authorization planning may reduce disruption caused by unavailable specialists or unclear assignment boundaries.

27.4 Accident-Related Costs

If the methodology contributes to fewer relevant accidents, organizations and insurance systems may benefit from reduced accident-related costs.

27.5 Supplier Reliability

Improved process evidence may reduce uncertainty, repeated clarification and certain forms of supplier-related rework.

27.6 Economic Incentive Alignment

The framework seeks to make qualification investment, accurate authorization records and responsible work assignment operationally valuable.

Canonical Research Hypothesis When Competence Becomes Operationally Verifiable, Organizations May Have Stronger Incentives to Invest in and Retain Qualified Personnel.

This hypothesis must be tested against actual organizational behavior.

28. Minimum Evaluation Indicators

An institutional pilot should evaluate:

Participating Organizations Declared Restricted Activities Declared Qualified Persons Actual Operator Coverage Verified Authorization Coverage Unresolved Qualification Gaps Assignment Authority Gaps Corrective Actions Relevant Customer Verification Requests Supplier Self-Assessment Activity Reporting Accuracy

Administrative Burden Worker Access to Verification Relevant Safety Outcomes Qualification Investment Workforce Retention Trends Results must be interpreted against suitable baselines and relevant external factors.

More detected discrepancies may indicate improved verification rather than deteriorating compliance.

Fewer reported discrepancies do not independently establish improved safety.

29. Independent Impact Validation

System-wide impact claims should be examined using appropriately reconciled evidence from:

Source A — Qualification, Training & Certification Providers Source B — Participating Companies & Relevant Suppliers Source C — Competent Occupational Safety or Other Relevant Institutions The evaluation must consider:

Scope Time Period Geography Representativeness Data Quality Evidence Independence Double Counting Causal Limitations Alternative Explanations The existence of three sources does not automatically prove causation.

Canonical Principle

A Claimed Improvement Is Not a Validated Improvement.

31. Minimum Implementation Requirements

An organization applying the minimum framework should be able to establish:

What Restricted Activities It Performs Which Requirements Apply How Many Persons It Initially Declares as Qualified or Authorized Who Actually Performs the Relevant Work Who May Legitimately Assign It How Individual Authorization Is Established How Relevant Workers Can Verify Authorization How External Sites Can Verify Relevant Qualifications How Customer Requirements Are Addressed How Discrepancies Are Corrected The initial phase does not require every item to be completed.

The implementation sequence progressively establishes the necessary relationships.

32. Canonical Implementation Boundaries

The VERICYCLE™ Minimum Implementation Framework does not:

Create new statutory qualification requirements.

Require immediate full workforce certification audits.

Require all employee certificates to be emailed to institutions.

Require mandatory proprietary software.

Create a centralized personal-data registry.

Require continuous physical inspections.

Grant inspection powers to coworkers.

Grant unrestricted personnel-data access to customers.

Establish product conformity automatically.

Replace competent institutional judgment.

Guarantee accident reduction.

Guarantee workforce retention.

Guarantee certification growth.

Create new enforcement powers.

Its application must remain consistent with applicable law, technical requirements and legitimate organizational authority.

33. Institutional Evaluation Statement

OOF® — OriginOpen® Foundation proposes the VERICYCLE™ Minimum Implementation Framework as a proportionate means of translating the VERICYCLE™ methodology into practical workplace and supply chain governance.

Its first intervention is deliberately simple:

One Official Communication. One Basic Employer Declaration.

The declaration initiates organizational self-identification.

Subsequent phases establish actual operator coverage, legitimate assignment authority, individual authorization evidence and practical workplace visibility.

The framework integrates two verification directions.

Bottom-up verification connects actual work with relevant competence and assignment authority.

Top-down verification connects final-product and customer requirements with relevant supplier processes and execution evidence.

Both directions may encourage companies to review and correct their own operational arrangements.

The framework does not presume that every organization requires a new external audit.

It seeks to strengthen internal verification through legitimate, proportionate and recurring operational incentives.

Independent institutions may evaluate whether this approach can improve safety-relevant authorization control without disproportionate administrative or technological burden.

34. Canonical Closing Statement

The central problem is not necessarily the absence of certificates, rules or institutions.

It is the potential absence of a verifiable relationship between those requirements and the work actually performed.

VERICYCLE™ does not begin by demanding another complex system.

It begins by asking the organization to identify the relevant activities and declare its initial authorization coverage.

The organization then examines the actual performers, the legitimate assignment authorities and the conditions under which work is carried out.

Relevant workers gain practical means of verifying safety-critical authorization.

Relevant customers gain proportionate means of requesting production-process assurance.

Competent institutions retain their existing authority to examine substantiated concerns.

The intended result is a decentralized system of organizational self-verification supported by legitimate workplace, customer and institutional mechanisms.

The objective is not more inspections for their own sake.

The objective is fewer unresolved authorization gaps, earlier correction and stronger operational accountability.

Two Independent Verification Directions. One Continuous Correction Cycle. Embedded in VERICYCLE™.
