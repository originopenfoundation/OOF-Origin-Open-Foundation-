VERICYCLE™ — Verification & Accountability Cycle

A Continuous Methodology for Verifying Declared Authority Against Operational Reality

Canonical Definition

VERICYCLE™ — Verification & Accountability Cycle is an OOF® governance methodology establishing a continuous, structured process for identifying governed activities, declaring legitimately established authority and responsibilities, disclosing relevant verification information, verifying declared conditions against operational reality, and correcting identified discrepancies.

The methodology operates through five interconnected stages:

IDENTIFY → DECLARE → DISCLOSE → VERIFY → CORRECT

VERICYCLE™ provides a common verification and accountability logic that can be applied, through appropriate domain-specific implementation, to humans, organizations, AI agents, robots, autonomous systems, and other authority-dependent operational environments.

Its purpose is not to grant authority, issue qualifications, or replace competent oversight. It makes relevant declarations, authorizations, and operational conditions examinable, verifiable, and correctable.

What Problem Does VERICYCLE™ Solve?

VERICYCLE™ addresses the gap between declared operational conditions and verifiable operational reality. Records or declarations alone do not establish that conditions remain valid during actual operations.

An organization may declare that qualified personnel perform restricted work without adequate evidence of who actually performs it. An AI agent may have a legitimate objective while specific actions lack independently established permissions. A robot may have technical capability without operational authorization for a given environment. A supplier may declare conformity while execution conditions remain insufficiently traceable.

A declared condition may be accepted or relied upon without sufficient verification of its actual operational validity. VERICYCLE™ responds with continuous verification and correction rather than treating an initial declaration as permanent proof.

The Five-Stage Verification & Accountability Cycle

IDENTIFY — What must be governed?

Identify relevant actors, activities, responsibilities, dependencies, risks, and boundaries of the governed operational environment.

DECLARE — Who is responsible and authorized?

Establish and document relevant responsibilities and legitimately established permissions or authorizations. A declaration records an asserted governance condition; it does not independently create legitimate authority.

DISCLOSE — What information must be accessible?

Make information needed for appropriate verification available to relevant parties while respecting confidentiality, privacy, security, and legitimate access boundaries.

VERIFY — Do declared conditions match reality?

Examine whether identities, responsibilities, authorizations, evidence, and operating conditions correspond to actual activities and applicable requirements. Verification must be evidence-based and proportionate.

CORRECT — How are discrepancies resolved?

Address identified inconsistencies, correct invalid or outdated declarations, restore appropriate operating conditions, and feed findings into the next verification cycle.

Five interconnected stages. One continuous governance cycle.

Universal Methodological Application

  • Humans: qualifications, work assignments, permissions, and restricted activities.
  • Organizations: responsibilities, delegated authority, operational declarations, and verification obligations.
  • AI agents: assigned objectives, tool permissions, delegated authority, and execution evidence.
  • Robots: operational permissions, safety boundaries, oversight, and authorized actions.
  • Autonomous systems: system authority, operational constraints, supervision, and corrective intervention.

Universal applicability does not mean identical evidence, legal requirements, technical controls, or procedures in every domain. The verification logic is common; implementation follows the governed reality.

Relationship to the OOF® Authority Governance Reference

The Authority Governance Reference addresses the legitimate basis, scope, permission, evidence, and operating conditions of authority. VERICYCLE™ addresses how declarations and operating conditions remain continuously verifiable and correctable. They serve complementary purposes.

Authority must have a legitimate basis. VERICYCLE™ does not create that basis through declaration. A role is not automatically proof of authority; a certificate is not automatically proof of permission; and an instruction is not automatically proof of legitimacy.

Methodological Boundaries

VERICYCLE™ is not a regulatory authority, certification body, inspection agency, or replacement for applicable law. It does not independently qualify personnel, authorize restricted work, grant AI permissions, or certify autonomous systems.

It does not require unrestricted disclosure of personal or confidential records, and it does not presume that every discrepancy proves unlawful conduct or product nonconformity. Implementations must respect applicable legal, institutional, technical, privacy, and evidentiary boundaries. Intended benefits are not guaranteed outcomes.

Authority on paper is not proof of authority in reality. Declaration is not verification.
