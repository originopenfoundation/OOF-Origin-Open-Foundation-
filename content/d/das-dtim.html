Canonical Definition

Decision Traceability Integrity Module (DTIM) defines the structural
conditions under which decisions, decision pathways, decision
approvals, decision modifications, decision records, decision
inputs, decision reviews, decision outcomes, and decision-governance
activities remain traceable, reconstructable, reviewable, auditable,
governable, enforceable, and operationally valid throughout
the decision lifecycle.

DTIM governs decision traceability.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine how a decision evolved, who participated in the decision
process, what information influenced the decision, how approvals
occurred, and whether the complete decision history can
be reconstructed.

Module Operational Role

DTIM defines the decision-traceability governance layer of DAS.

Its role is to preserve visibility and reconstructability of
decision history throughout governance environments.

Module Operational Space

DTIM governs:

  • decision traceability
  • decision reconstruction
  • decision auditability
  • decision-history governance
  • approval traceability
  • decision-pathway traceability
  • decision lifecycle traceability
  • governance traceability


The module applies wherever governance requires reconstruction
of decision history.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • traceable decision pathways
  • reconstructable decision history
  • auditable decision records
  • accountable decision transitions
  • governance-valid decision documentation
  • operational decision transparency


Its function is to ensure that governance can reconstruct how
decisions were created, reviewed, approved, modified, and executed.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Decision Traceability Object

The organization must define which decision environments
require traceability governance.

This may include:

  • organizational structures
  • boards
  • management systems
  • governance frameworks
  • compliance environments
  • AI governance systems
  • Human-AI decision environments
  • operational decision systems


2. Define Decision Traceability Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which decision
traceability remains valid.

This includes:

  • traceability requirements
  • reconstruction requirements
  • auditability requirements
  • decision-history requirements
  • governance requirements
  • traceability-valid decision conditions


3. Define Traceability Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how decision-traceability failures
are identified.

This may include:

  • missing decision records
  • undocumented approvals
  • incomplete decision history
  • decision-pathway gaps
  • governance-blind decision activities
  • unreconstructable decision chains


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for
decision-traceability failures.

Governance response may include:

  • decision review
  • audit procedures
  • governance intervention
  • decision reconstruction
  • corrective actions
  • traceability verification
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • decision creation
  • decision approvals
  • decision modifications
  • governance reviews
  • validation activities
  • resulting decision states


A decision-accountability environment must not remain
traceability-valid if materially significant decision activities
cannot be reconstructed, reviewed, audited, validated, enforced,
or governed.

Use Case 1 — Executive Decision Investigation

Scenario

A strategic decision creates major operational consequences and
governance must reconstruct how the decision evolved.

Application

DTIM reconstructs decision pathways, approvals, modifications,
reviews, and governance actions throughout the decision lifecycle.

Result

Governance gains visibility into the complete
decision-history pathway.

Use Case 2 — Human-AI Decision Environment

Scenario

An AI-assisted decision platform contributes recommendations which
influence operational decision-making.

Application

DTIM reconstructs recommendation pathways, approval chains,
governance decisions, and decision-history evolution.

Result

Governance gains visibility into decision traceability across
intelligent operational ecosystems.

Canonical Closing Statement

Decision Traceability Integrity Module (DTIM) defines the structural
conditions under which decisions, decision pathways, decision
approvals, decision modifications, decision records, decision
inputs, decision reviews, decision

outcomes, and decision-governance activities remain traceable,
reconstructable, reviewable, auditable, governable, enforceable, and
operationally valid throughout the decision lifecycle.

A decision cannot be governed if its history cannot be
reconstructed. Decision Traceability Integrity therefore becomes a
foundational condition of trustworthy decision accountability
governance and trustworthy governance transparency.
