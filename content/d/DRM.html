Canonical Definition System

Canonical Definition

Delegation Revocation Module (DRM) defines
the structural conditions under which delegated
governance authority may be suspended, withdrawn,
terminated, restricted, revoked, reviewed, governed,
traceable, auditable, and operationally valid
throughout the governance lifecycle.

DRM governs delegation revocation.

The module establishes the foundational conditions
required to determine when delegated authority
may be removed, how delegated authority is revoked,
who may revoke delegated authority, and whether
delegation revocation remains reconstructable
throughout operational reality.

Delegated authority may be assigned.

Delegated authority may be validated.

Delegated authority may be revoked.

DRM governs that revocation.

Module Operational Role

DRM defines the delegation-revocation
governance layer of GDS.

Its role is to establish governance-valid
delegated-authority termination throughout
governance environments.

Module Operational Space

DRM governs:

  • delegation revocation
  • delegated-authority withdrawal
  • delegated-authority suspension
  • delegated-authority termination
  • delegation restriction
  • delegation auditability
  • delegation traceability
  • delegation reconstruction


The module applies wherever delegated authority
must be removed, suspended, restricted, or terminated.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • legitimate delegation revocations
  • traceable authority withdrawals
  • reconstructable revocation history
  • attributable revocation decisions
  • governance-valid revocation records
  • operational governance control


Its function is to ensure that governance can
reconstruct when, why, and how delegated
authority was revoked.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Delegation Revocation Object

The organization must define which governance
environments require delegation-revocation governance.
This may include:

  • organizations
  • institutions
  • governments
  • governance committees
  • corporate governance structures
  • AI governance systems
  • autonomous-agent ecosystems
  • Human-AI governance environments


2. Define Delegation Revocation Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which
delegation revocation remains valid.
This includes:

  • revocation requirements
  • authorization requirements
  • legitimacy requirements
  • traceability requirements
  • validation requirements
  • governance-valid revocation conditions


3. Define Revocation Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how delegation-revocation
failures are identified.
This may include:

  • unauthorized revocations
  • hidden authority removals
  • undocumented revocation activities
  • illegitimate authority withdrawals
  • unverifiable revocation records
  • governance-invalid revocation actions


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for
delegation-revocation failures.
Governance response may include:

  • revocation review
  • revocation validation
  • governance intervention
  • corrective actions
  • revocation reconstruction
  • authorization verification
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • delegation revocations
  • revocation decisions
  • governance reviews
  • intervention procedures
  • validation activities
  • resulting delegation states


A governance environment must not remain revocation-valid
if materially significant delegation revocations cannot be
reconstructed, reviewed, validated, attributed,
preserved, or governed.

Canonical Closing Statement

Delegation Revocation Module (DRM) defines the structural
conditions under which delegated governance authority may be
suspended, withdrawn, terminated, restricted, revoked,
reviewed, governed, traceable, auditable, and operationally
valid throughout the governance lifecycle.

Delegated authority that cannot be revoked cannot remain
governable. Delegation Revocation therefore becomes a
foundational condition of trustworthy governance
delegation governance.
