Canonical Definition System

Canonical Definition

Delegation Accountability Module (DAM-A) defines
the structural conditions under which accountability
relationships associated with delegated authority
remain identifiable, attributable, traceable, reviewable,
governable, enforceable, auditable, and operationally
valid throughout the governance lifecycle.

DAM-A governs delegation accountability.

The module establishes the foundational conditions
required to determine who remains accountable after
authority has been delegated, how accountability is
preserved, whether accountability follows delegated
authority, and whether accountability relationships
remain reconstructable throughout operational reality.

Authority may be delegated.

Accountability must not disappear.

DAM-A governs that accountability.

Module Operational Role

DAM-A defines the delegation-accountability
governance layer of GDS.

Its role is to preserve accountability visibility
throughout delegated-governance environments.

Module Operational Space

DAM-A governs:

  • delegation accountability
  • delegated-authority accountability
  • accountability preservation
  • accountability attribution
  • delegation-accountability relationships
  • accountability auditability
  • accountability traceability
  • accountability reconstruction


The module applies wherever governance authority
is delegated and accountability must remain identifiable.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • accountable delegated authority
  • traceable accountability ownership
  • reconstructable accountability history
  • attributable governance decisions
  • governance-valid accountability records
  • operational accountability visibility


Its function is to ensure that governance can
reconstruct who remained accountable throughout
delegated-authority activities.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Delegation Accountability Object

The organization must define which governance
environments require delegation-accountability governance.
This may include:

  • organizations
  • institutions
  • governments
  • governance committees
  • corporate governance structures
  • AI governance systems
  • autonomous-agent ecosystems
  • Human-AI governance environments


2. Define Delegation Accountability Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which
delegation accountability remains valid.
This includes:

  • accountability requirements
  • attribution requirements
  • preservation requirements
  • traceability requirements
  • validation requirements
  • governance-valid accountability conditions


3. Define Accountability Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how delegation-accountability
failures are identified.
This may include:

  • accountability gaps
  • hidden accountability ownership
  • undocumented accountability transfers
  • accountability ambiguity
  • missing accountability records
  • governance-invalid accountability activities


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for
delegation-accountability failures.
Governance response may include:

  • accountability review
  • accountability verification
  • governance intervention
  • corrective actions
  • accountability reconstruction
  • attribution validation
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • accountability relationships
  • delegated-authority ownership
  • governance reviews
  • intervention procedures
  • validation activities
  • resulting accountability states


A governance environment must not remain accountability-valid
if materially significant accountability relationships cannot be
reconstructed, reviewed, attributed, validated,
preserved, or governed.

Canonical Closing Statement

Delegation Accountability Module (DAM-A) defines the structural
conditions under which accountability relationships associated
with delegated authority remain identifiable, attributable,
traceable, reviewable, governable, enforceable, auditable,
and operationally valid throughout the governance lifecycle.

Delegated authority that loses accountability cannot remain
governance-valid. Delegation Accountability therefore becomes
a foundational condition of trustworthy governance
delegation governance.
