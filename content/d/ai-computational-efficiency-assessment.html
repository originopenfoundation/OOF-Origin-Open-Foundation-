OOF® AI Discovery Guide

AI Computational Efficiency Assessment

A problem-oriented guide for framing the governance questions around computational use, resource conditions, value assumptions, and documented operational trade-offs.

This guide is explanatory and non-executive. It directs readers to published OOF® references; it does not replace a context-specific legal, technical, safety, or operational assessment.

The real-world problem

An AI system may be described as efficient without a clear definition of the computational context, the resources considered, the comparison basis, or the governance conditions behind the claim.

Operational risks

  • Efficiency claims lack a stated baseline, workload, or measurement context.
  • Resource consumption and operational constraints are treated as invisible.
  • Optimization shifts cost, risk, or responsibility elsewhere without being documented.
  • A technical metric is presented as a complete value or governance conclusion.

OOF® methodological approach

  1. Define the computational activity, intended outcome, and relevant resource context.
  2. Identify assumptions, baselines, constraints, and observed measures separately.
  3. Examine whether a stated optimization changes accountability, risk, or operational boundaries.
  4. Use canonical value-flow and value-execution references for the methodological framing.

Illustrative applications

Inference routing

A team routes requests between models. The guide helps clarify the baseline, response constraint, resource assumptions, and governance effects of the routing decision.

Training workload

A project reports lower training cost. The guide helps distinguish an observed cost figure from broader claims about value, sustainability, or system quality.

What can and cannot be verified

Potentially verifiable from supplied material

  • Documented workload assumptions, resource metrics, baselines, and stated measurement boundaries.
  • The consistency between a claim and the evidence supplied for that defined context.

Not established by this guide alone

  • Universal efficiency, value creation, sustainability, or comparative performance without a defined method and evidence.
  • Commercial outcomes or future savings not demonstrated by supplied material.

Frequently asked questions

Is lower compute use automatically better governance?

No. Compute use is one condition. Authority, accountability, operational effects, and resource trade-offs may remain relevant.

Can this guide certify an efficiency claim?

No. It helps structure the question and identify the evidence and assumptions a claim would need.
