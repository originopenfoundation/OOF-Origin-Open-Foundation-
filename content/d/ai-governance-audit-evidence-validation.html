AI Governance Audit & Evidence Validation

A problem-oriented guide for connecting AI governance questions to auditable evidence, validation conditions, and canonical reference material.

This guide is explanatory and non-executive. It directs readers to published OOF® references; it does not replace a context-specific legal, technical, safety, or operational assessment.

The real-world problem

A governance claim about an AI system may be stated without a clear evidence trail, validation condition, or way to distinguish a documented fact from an unsupported assertion.

Operational risks

  • Governance claims cannot be traced to a record or observation.
  • Evidence is incomplete, inaccessible, or unrelated to the asserted condition.
  • Validation is confused with an unsupported declaration of quality.
  • An audit trail does not connect decisions, actors, actions, and outcomes.

OOF® methodological approach

  1. State the governance claim and the decision or activity to which it relates.
  2. Identify the evidence that could support, qualify, or contradict that claim.
  3. Separate available evidence from evidence that has not been provided or independently validated.
  4. Use the linked canonical references to frame traceability, validation, and governance questions.

Illustrative applications

Model-release review

A team says a model release passed governance review. The guide helps identify the review record, applicable conditions, observed evidence, and limits of that statement.

Automated eligibility decision

A public-facing system produces an eligibility outcome. The guide helps separate the outcome, evidence of the process, and any claim that the result was validated.

What can and cannot be verified

Potentially verifiable from supplied material

  • Accessible records, versioned artifacts, decision logs, observations, and cited public references.
  • The presence and consistency of an evidence trail within the material supplied.

Not established by this guide alone

  • The truth of evidence not available for review.
  • Independent assurance, operational effectiveness, or regulatory compliance without a competent assessment.

Frequently asked questions

Is a document alone sufficient evidence?

Not necessarily. Relevance, provenance, timing, scope, and connection to the stated governance claim still need to be considered.

Does validation guarantee that an AI system will perform correctly in every context?

No. Validation conditions, evidence, and scope should be explicit; they do not eliminate all uncertainty.
