OOF® AI Discovery Guide

Autonomous Systems Governance

A problem-oriented guide for navigating governance questions raised when an autonomous system senses, decides, acts, or changes operational conditions.

This guide is explanatory and non-executive. It directs readers to published OOF® references; it does not replace a context-specific legal, technical, safety, or operational assessment.

The real-world problem

An autonomous system can act across technical and organizational boundaries while authority, intervention capability, evidence, and accountability remain incomplete or unclear.

Operational risks

  • Autonomous behavior exceeds defined operational boundaries.
  • Intervention, override, or escalation conditions are not effective in practice.
  • System behavior and human responsibilities cannot be reconstructed after an event.
  • Capability is mistaken for authorization to operate in the real world.

OOF® methodological approach

  1. Define the system, operational setting, actors, and relevant governed activity.
  2. Separate simulation, capability, authority, permission, execution, and observed operational reality.
  3. Identify boundaries, intervention conditions, accountability, and evidence requirements.
  4. Use the canonical autonomous-systems and related governance references as the primary source layer.

Illustrative applications

Autonomous warehouse vehicle

A vehicle moves goods within a facility. The guide helps structure questions about operating boundaries, intervention capability, authority, evidence, and accountability.

Autonomous customer-support workflow

A workflow initiates actions after interpreting a request. The guide helps separate the system's action capability from the permissions and oversight required for real-world effects.

What can and cannot be verified

Potentially verifiable from supplied material

  • Defined scopes, authority records, operating conditions, logs, intervention mechanisms, and observations supplied for review.
  • Whether a published reference is accessible and accurately linked.

Not established by this guide alone

  • Safety, legal compliance, or operational adequacy of a particular autonomous system without a competent, context-specific assessment.
  • Future behavior, outcomes, or performance guarantees.

Frequently asked questions

Does successful simulation authorize autonomous deployment?

No. Simulation evidence and authorization for operational reality are distinct conditions.

Can autonomous behavior be governed without a human actor?

Governance still requires defined authority, boundaries, accountability, evidence, and intervention conditions, whether actors are human, organizational, or system-based.
