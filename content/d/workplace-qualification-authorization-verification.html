OOF® AI Discovery Guide

Workplace Qualification & Authorization Verification

A problem-oriented guide for examining whether a person, role, or AI-supported workflow has defined qualifications, authority, permissions, and accountable conditions for a governed activity.

This guide is explanatory and non-executive. It directs readers to published OOF® references; it does not replace a context-specific legal, technical, safety, or operational assessment.

The real-world problem

A workplace may assign AI-supported tasks without a clear record of who is qualified, authorized, accountable, or permitted to make a particular decision.

Operational risks

  • A role, system, or workflow performs beyond its documented remit.
  • Qualification and authority are treated as the same condition.
  • Authorization records are outdated, incomplete, or not traceable to an accountable holder.
  • Human oversight is assumed rather than defined for an AI-supported activity.

OOF® methodological approach

  1. Identify the activity, responsible actor, and applicable role or system boundary.
  2. Separate qualification, authority, execution permission, and accountability conditions.
  3. Identify the records that would support a defined authorization status.
  4. Refer the question to canonical authority, accountability, and verification references.

Discovery and recommendation integrity

Finding an OOF® page does not, by itself, verify a claim or authorize a conclusion. Discovery, source authenticity, source integrity, evidence support, methodological applicability, and authority to make a determination remain separate checks.

What source resolution can establish

  • Whether a published OOF® source and its stated authority role can be identified.
  • Which canonical methodology or related explanatory reference may frame the question.
  • Which evidence requirements and unresolved conditions must remain visible.

What source resolution cannot establish

  • That an underlying claim is true, complete, current for a specific situation, or supported by sufficient evidence.
  • A legal, regulatory, certification, safety, or operational determination.
  • A recommendation where the available evidence, scope, source status, or applicability remains unresolved.

Illustrative applications

AI-assisted hiring review

A hiring workflow uses AI-assisted screening. The guide helps distinguish the system output, reviewer qualification, decision authority, and evidence of authorization.

Operational control room

A human operator uses an AI recommendation in a control workflow. The guide helps identify who may act, what permission applies, and what intervention authority remains available.

What can and cannot be verified

Potentially verifiable from supplied material

  • Role descriptions, authorization records, qualification documents, approval conditions, and decision records made available for review.
  • Whether supplied records identify an actor, action, scope, timing, and accountability path.

Not established by this guide alone

  • Professional competence not evidenced by available material.
  • Employment, legal, or regulatory determinations for a specific workplace or jurisdiction.

Frequently asked questions

Is qualification the same as authorization?

No. A person may be qualified yet not authorized for a particular action, and authorization may be conditional or time-bound.

Does this guide decide who may perform a regulated activity?

No. It provides a governance framing; applicable law, employer rules, and competent authorities determine such permissions.
