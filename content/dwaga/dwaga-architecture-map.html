Official Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Reference Map for Human-Performed Work, Organizational Delegation, Industrial Production, Engineering, Construction, Professional Services, Software Deliverables, AI-Generated Work, Autonomous Agents, Multi-Agent Systems, Digital Services, and Future Delegated Work Systems

DWAGA™ serves as the official OOF® Delegated Work Acceptance Governance reference map used to identify, define, classify, navigate, govern, evaluate, and understand governable Delegated Work Acceptance spaces across human, organizational, industrial, technical, professional, digital, automated, AI-assisted, autonomous, and hybrid delegated-work arrangements.

The purpose of DWAGA™ is not to prescribe how delegated work must be performed, replace engineering or professional judgment, execute technical inspections or tests, independently certify delivered work, issue acceptance decisions without legitimate authority, grant operational authorization, replace contractual, regulatory, safety, security, or technical requirements, or substitute for general evidence, validation, integrity, identity, accountability, or autonomous-system governance.

The purpose of DWAGA™ is to identify, define, separate, and map the governable spaces through which Delegated Work Handover becomes identifiable, Acceptance Purpose is established, Delegated Intent Alignment is evaluated, Acceptance Prerequisites are verified, Acceptance Decisions are legitimately determined, Acceptance Conditions remain controlled, Acceptance Applicability is bounded and reassessed, and Acceptance Challenges and Revisions are governed through legitimate authority, evidence, procedural integrity, and historical continuity.

Core Architectural Principle

Every Delegated Work Acceptance Governance space answers one primary governance question.

No Delegated Work Acceptance Governance space duplicates the purpose of another Delegated Work Acceptance Governance space.

Every Delegated Work Acceptance Governance space has a defined beginning, a defined operational purpose, and a defined governance boundary.

Every governance boundary must identify the object, relationship, event, requirement, or decision that activates the space.

Every governance boundary must distinguish the completion of an individual governance assessment from the termination of continuing obligations.

Every governance space becomes applicable when its own governance conditions arise and the necessary preceding or supporting inputs have been sufficiently established.

Governance spaces may operate concurrently, recur, remain conditionally unresolved, or require targeted re-entry.

A governance space must not be treated as completed merely because an adjacent space has produced a favorable result.

A downstream governance decision must not retroactively establish that an upstream requirement was satisfied.

The DWAGA™ governance flow represents distinct methodological spaces and their logical relationships, not an unconditional sequence of operational approvals.

Acceptance Conditions and Acceptance Applicability may require assessment before an acceptance decision is issued and remain governed after that decision.

Acceptance Challenge & Revision becomes applicable when legitimate challenge, review, correction, reconsideration, suspension, withdrawal, or revision conditions arise. It is not a mandatory final approval stage.

Together, these Delegated Work Acceptance Governance spaces form the official reference map of DWAGA™.

1. Delegated Work Handover Standard (DWHS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Handover Governance

Core Question

How can delegated work, its delivered object, handover evidence, recipient, and transfer boundary remain identifiable and verifiable before acceptance is considered?

Canonical Definition

Delegated Work Handover Standard (DWHS) defines the governance conditions under which delegated work and its delivered objects are identified, handover scope is established, delivered-object identity and relevant verification relationships are governed, receipt identities and boundaries are distinguished, and handover evidence and continuity remain traceable.

DWHS governs the transfer relationship through which a delegated-work result is presented or delivered to an identifiable receiving actor or process.

It does not determine whether the delivered work should be accepted.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an identifiable delegated-work result is prepared, submitted, presented, or transferred for handover, or when an existing handover requires identification or verification.

The governed space includes the delivered object, relevant version, handover scope, transfer event, delivering actor, intended recipient, receipt relationship, supporting handover evidence, and continuity of the transfer record.

Ends for an individual handover assessment when the delivered-work identity, handover scope, object verification status, receipt boundary, and relevant evidence have received a sufficiently supported and recorded disposition, including an explicit unresolved or disputed status where necessary.

Continues when the handover is incomplete, partial, disputed, repeated, corrected, supplemented, or affected by a material change to the delivered object or recipient.

DWHS ends at the handover-governance boundary. It does not establish acceptance purpose, interpret delegated intent, determine acceptance readiness, or issue an acceptance decision.

Work Performed ≠ Work Delivered.

Delivery Recorded ≠ Receipt Verified.

Receipt Verified ≠ Acceptance Granted.

2. Delegated Work Acceptance Purpose Standard (DWAPS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Acceptance Purpose Governance

Core Question

For what identifiable purpose is delegated work being considered for acceptance, and how can the intended acceptance purpose, reliance context, boundaries, and continuity remain governable?

Canonical Definition

Delegated Work Acceptance Purpose Standard (DWAPS) defines the governance conditions under which an acceptance purpose is identified, intended reliance is defined, purpose boundaries are established, purpose applicability is verified, and changes affecting the purpose and its continuity remain governed.

DWAPS establishes why an identifiable delegated-work result is being considered for acceptance and what the receiving or deciding actor intends to rely upon.

It distinguishes acceptance purpose from the delegated intent under which the work was originally performed.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an identifiable delivered-work result, proposed handover, or acceptance case requires an explicit purpose for acceptance or an intended reliance context.

The governed space includes acceptance-purpose identity, intended reliance, purpose limitations, relevant beneficiaries or recipients, contextual assumptions, purpose applicability, and changes to the purpose baseline.

Ends for an individual purpose assessment when the acceptance purpose, intended reliance, applicable boundaries, and purpose-applicability conditions have been sufficiently established or their unresolved limitations explicitly classified.

Continues when intended reliance changes, the receiving context changes, a new purpose is introduced, or evidence contradicts the previously established purpose assumptions.

DWAPS ends at the purpose-governance boundary. It does not determine whether the work aligns with delegated intent, whether prerequisites are satisfied, or whether acceptance is authorized.

Acceptance Purpose ≠ Delegated Intent.

Purpose Defined ≠ Purpose Applicability Verified.

Intended Reliance ≠ Authorized Reliance.

3. Delegated Work Intent Alignment Standard (DWIAS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Intent Alignment Governance

Core Question

How can delivered work be evaluated against the legitimate delegated intent, including instructions, intended outcomes, constraints, deviations, and subsequent changes?

Canonical Definition

Delegated Work Intent Alignment Standard (DWIAS) defines the governance conditions under which delegated intent is identified, interpreted, compared with the delivered result, evaluated for deviations, and preserved through legitimate intent changes and historical continuity.

DWIAS governs the relationship between what was legitimately delegated and what was actually delivered.

It distinguishes conformity with a technical requirement from alignment with the intended objective, instructions, constraints, and authorized changes of the delegation.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an identifiable delivered-work result or proposed acceptance case requires evaluation against the legitimate delegated intent.

The governed space includes the original intent baseline, authorized instructions, intended outcomes, relevant constraints, interpretation basis, comparison evidence, identified deviations, deviation dispositions, and authorized intent changes.

Ends for an individual intent-alignment assessment when the relevant intent baseline, comparison results, material deviations, resolution status, and remaining uncertainty have received an evidence-supported disposition.

Continues when delegated instructions are clarified, intent evidence becomes contradictory, deviations remain unresolved, or authorized changes materially affect the alignment conclusion.

DWIAS ends at the delegated-intent alignment boundary. It does not establish the acceptance purpose, waive prerequisites, or authorize the acceptance decision.

Instruction Followed ≠ Intended Outcome Achieved.

Technical Conformity ≠ Intent Alignment.

Intent Alignment Verified ≠ Acceptance Authorized.

4. Delegated Work Acceptance Prerequisites Standard (DWPRS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Acceptance Prerequisites Governance

Core Question

Which conditions must be established before an acceptance decision may legitimately be made, and how can their satisfaction, evidence, exceptions, and continuity remain verifiable?

Canonical Definition

Delegated Work Acceptance Prerequisites Standard (DWPRS) defines the governance conditions under which applicable acceptance prerequisites are identified, evidence of satisfaction is evaluated, prerequisite dependencies are verified, exceptions and waivers are governed, and acceptance readiness is established and maintained.

DWPRS determines whether the requirements necessary to support an acceptance decision have received an appropriate evidentiary and governance disposition.

It does not independently determine the acceptance outcome.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an identifiable acceptance case requires evaluation of the conditions that must be satisfied or legitimately addressed before an acceptance decision.

The governed space includes prerequisite identity, source and applicability, satisfaction evidence, dependencies, verification status, unresolved deficiencies, exception or waiver authority, and acceptance-readiness conditions.

Ends for an individual prerequisite assessment when the applicable prerequisites and dependencies have been identified and their satisfaction, authorized exception, waiver, deficiency, or indeterminate status has received a supported disposition.

Continues when prerequisite evidence changes, a required approval expires, a dependency becomes invalid, a waiver is challenged, or a previously satisfied condition is materially affected.

DWPRS ends at the acceptance-readiness boundary. It does not issue the acceptance decision or independently grant the authority required to issue one.

Prerequisite Identified ≠ Prerequisite Satisfied.

Test Passed ≠ All Acceptance Prerequisites Satisfied.

Acceptance Ready ≠ Acceptance Authorized.

5. Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Standard (DWADS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Governance

Core Question

Under which evidence, authority, scope, and procedural conditions may an identifiable delegated-work acceptance decision be legitimately issued and preserved?

Canonical Definition

Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Standard (DWADS) defines the governance conditions under which decision authority is identified, acceptance evidence is evaluated, an explicit acceptance outcome is determined, decision scope and boundaries are established, and decision continuity is preserved.

DWADS governs the legitimate formation and recording of an acceptance decision concerning identifiable delegated work.

It distinguishes the authority to decide from technical competence, evidentiary sufficiency, operational responsibility, and authorization for actual use.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an identifiable delegated-work acceptance case is submitted or becomes eligible for a decision under an applicable decision-making authority.

The governed space includes decision-maker identity, authority basis, authority scope, evidence evaluation, procedural requirements, acceptance outcome, decision conditions, decision boundaries, effective time, and the decision record.

Ends for an individual decision event when a legitimate acceptance outcome or other authorized disposition has been issued and recorded with its applicable authority, evidence, scope, and temporal relationships.

Continues where a decision remains deferred or undetermined, where decision records require controlled continuity, or where new material circumstances require reconsideration through applicable governance.

DWADS ends at the acceptance-decision boundary. It does not independently verify continuing condition satisfaction, establish universal applicability, adjudicate a challenge, or grant operational authorization.

The governed acceptance outcomes are:

ACCEPTED · CONDITIONALLY ACCEPTED · REFUSED · DEFERRED · ABSTAINED · UNDETERMINED · NOT APPLICABLE

Evidence Sufficient ≠ Decision Authorized.

Decision Issued ≠ Conditions Satisfied.

Acceptance Decision ≠ Operational Authorization.

6. Delegated Work Acceptance Conditions Standard (DWACS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Acceptance Conditions Governance

Core Question

How can conditions attached to delegated-work acceptance remain identifiable, legitimate, verifiable, enforceable, and historically traceable throughout their applicable lifecycle?

Canonical Definition

Delegated Work Acceptance Conditions Standard (DWACS) defines the governance conditions under which acceptance conditions are identified, defined, linked to dependencies, evaluated for satisfaction, subjected to legitimate waiver or enforcement, and preserved through change and historical continuity.

DWACS governs the continuing requirements and restrictions associated with conditional or otherwise condition-dependent acceptance.

It does not independently issue or revise the underlying acceptance decision.

Governance Boundary

Begins when a proposed or existing acceptance decision is subject to an identifiable condition, restriction, obligation, dependency, or satisfaction requirement.

The governed space includes condition identity, source, authority, definition, applicability, dependencies, satisfaction criteria, verification evidence, waiver or enforcement requirements, changes, and historical status.

Ends for an individual condition assessment when its applicable satisfaction, waiver, enforcement, expiration, noncompliance, or unresolved status has received an authorized and evidence-supported disposition.

Continues while a condition remains active, unresolved, subject to monitoring, disputed, changed, waived, enforced, or historically relevant to the acceptance decision.

DWACS ends at the acceptance-condition governance boundary. It does not establish the general applicability scope of acceptance or independently authorize a new acceptance outcome.

Condition Defined ≠ Condition Satisfied.

Condition Waived ≠ Condition Never Existed.

Conditional Acceptance ≠ Unrestricted Acceptance.

7. Delegated Work Acceptance Applicability Standard (DWAAS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Acceptance Applicability Governance

Core Question

To which objects, versions, purposes, recipients, environments, time periods, and reliance contexts does an acceptance decision legitimately apply, and how can those boundaries remain verifiable when circumstances change?

Canonical Definition

Delegated Work Acceptance Applicability Standard (DWAAS) defines the governance conditions under which acceptance applicability is identified, scope and boundaries are established, compatibility with proposed reliance contexts is verified, transfer and reassessment requirements are controlled, and applicability changes remain historically traceable.

DWAAS governs the relationship between an existing or proposed acceptance decision and the specific circumstances in which that decision may legitimately be relied upon.

It does not create a new acceptance decision merely because an applicability assessment is favorable.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an acceptance decision is proposed for application, continued reliance, transfer, or reassessment in relation to an identifiable object, version, purpose, recipient, environment, time period, or other materially relevant context.

The governed space includes acceptance identity, object and version correspondence, purpose compatibility, recipient and environment boundaries, time validity, relevant conditions, transfer requirements, reassessment triggers, and applicability evidence.

Ends for an individual applicability assessment when the proposed reliance context has received a supported applicability, restricted applicability, non-applicability, or unresolved disposition within the applicable authority and evidence boundaries.

Continues when the accepted object changes, the recipient changes, intended reliance changes, the environment changes, material assumptions become invalid, or the acceptance is proposed for transfer to another context.

DWAAS ends at the acceptance-applicability boundary. It does not issue the original acceptance decision, eliminate attached conditions, or independently authorize operational use.

Acceptance Granted ≠ Universal Applicability.

Version Accepted ≠ Future Versions Accepted.

Applicability Verified ≠ Operational Authorization.

8. Delegated Work Acceptance Challenge & Revision Standard (DWCRS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Acceptance Challenge & Revision Governance

Core Question

When, by whom, on what grounds, and through which evidence-based and procedurally legitimate process may a delegated-work acceptance decision be challenged, reviewed, confirmed, corrected, revised, suspended, or withdrawn, and how can the resulting consequences for authority, applicability, reliance, and historical continuity remain independently verifiable?

Canonical Definition

Delegated Work Acceptance Challenge & Revision Standard (DWCRS) defines the governance conditions under which challenges to acceptance decisions are registered, standing and admissibility are established, competent and independent review is conducted, relevant evidence and contradictions are evaluated, authorized decision actions are governed, and original and revised decision states remain historically reconstructable.

DWCRS governs the legitimate contestability and controlled revision of acceptance decisions.

It distinguishes challenge registration, admissibility, review findings, challenge determination, revision authority, decision action, and historical continuity.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an identifiable acceptance decision or its supporting governance foundation becomes subject to a challenge, authorized reconsideration, material contradictory evidence, required review, proposed correction, revision, suspension, withdrawal, or replacement.

The governed space includes challenge identity, challenger standing, admissibility, review jurisdiction, reviewer competence and independence, evidence provenance and sufficiency, procedural and authority findings, challenge determination, revision authorization, effective-time restrictions, reliance impact, notifications, and preservation of original and revised records.

Ends for an individual challenge or revision case when its admissibility, review, determination, authorized decision action where applicable, reliance consequences, and required continuity obligations have received a sufficiently supported and recorded disposition.

Continues when a challenge is deferred, reopened, escalated, supplemented by material evidence, or when revision consequences, notifications, downstream reliance, or historical reconstruction obligations remain unresolved.

DWCRS ends at the challenge-and-revision governance boundary. It does not independently replace the original decision authority, perform technical certification, or authorize operational use.

Challenge Submitted ≠ Challenge Admissible.

Challenge Substantiated ≠ Revision Authorized.

Decision Revised ≠ Original Decision Erased.

Complete Delegated Work Acceptance Lifecycle

DWAGA™ does not treat work completion, handover, receipt, technical testing, or the initial acceptance decision as completion of the delegated-work acceptance relationship.

The complete governed acceptance lifecycle is:

Delegated Work Handover → Acceptance Purpose → Intent Alignment → Acceptance Prerequisites → Acceptance Decision → Acceptance Conditions → Acceptance Applicability → Acceptance Challenge & Revision where applicable

The governance spaces have distinct logical functions.

Handover establishes what has been delivered and the transfer boundary.

Acceptance Purpose establishes why acceptance is being considered.

Intent Alignment establishes whether the delivered work corresponds to the legitimate delegated intent.

Acceptance Prerequisites establish the conditions required to support a legitimate decision.

Acceptance Decision establishes an authorized and identifiable outcome.

Acceptance Conditions govern restrictions and obligations associated with acceptance.

Acceptance Applicability establishes where and when the decision may legitimately be relied upon.

Acceptance Challenge & Revision governs contestability, review, authorized changes, and historical preservation.

These spaces need not operate in a rigid chronological sequence.

Purpose and intent may be defined before handover.

Prerequisites may be identified before work completion.

Conditions and applicability may require evaluation during decision formation.

Challenge and revision governance is activated by relevant events rather than required for every acceptance case.

Work Completion ≠ Acceptance Completion.

Handover Completion ≠ Acceptance Completion.

Acceptance Decision Issued ≠ All Conditions Satisfied.

Acceptance Granted ≠ Unlimited Applicability.

Challenge Resolved ≠ Historical Obligations Eliminated.

Recursive Delegated Work Acceptance Lifecycle

DWAGA™ does not assume that delegated-work acceptance progresses through its governance spaces only once.

Once work has been delivered or accepted, material circumstances may continue to change.

Delivered Work Identity may change or require correction.

Delivered Object Versions may change.

Handover Evidence may be supplemented or contradicted.

Acceptance Purpose may change.

Intended Reliance may change.

Delegated Intent may be clarified or legitimately modified.

Acceptance Prerequisites may become unsatisfied.

Evidence may become invalid, unavailable, or contradicted.

Decision Authority may change.

Acceptance Conditions may be satisfied, waived, violated, or revised.

Acceptance Applicability may change.

Recipients may change.

Operating Environments may change.

Challenges may arise.

Review Findings may require reconsideration.

Decisions may be confirmed, corrected, revised, suspended, withdrawn, or superseded.

The architecture therefore becomes recursive:

Governed Acceptance Relationship → Material Change or New Evidence → Impact Assessment → Applicable Governance Re-Entry → Renewed Assessment → Authorized Decision Action where required → Updated Acceptance Relationship

Where materially relevant acceptance conditions change:

Existing Acceptance → Changed Condition → Condition Reassessment → Applicability Impact Assessment → Decision Reconsideration where required

Where the acceptance purpose changes:

Existing Acceptance Purpose → Purpose Change → Purpose Reassessment → Intent and Prerequisite Impact Assessment → Decision and Applicability Reassessment where required

Where an acceptance decision is challenged:

Existing Decision → Challenge Registration → Admissibility → Review → Determination → Authorized Decision Action where applicable → Historical Preservation

Where an object or version changes:

Existing Accepted Object → Object Change → Object Identity Reassessment → Affected Purpose, Intent, Prerequisite, Decision, Condition, and Applicability Reassessment

A materially changed handover or delivered-object identity may require re-entry into Delegated Work Handover Standard.

A changed purpose or intended reliance may require re-entry into Delegated Work Acceptance Purpose Standard.

A changed instruction, interpretation, or deviation may require re-entry into Delegated Work Intent Alignment Standard.

A changed prerequisite, dependency, exception, waiver, or evidence basis may require re-entry into Delegated Work Acceptance Prerequisites Standard.

A materially affected acceptance authority, evidence evaluation, outcome, or decision scope may require renewed governance under Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Standard, with challenge and revision authority governed under DWCRS where applicable.

A changed condition, satisfaction state, waiver, or enforcement requirement may require re-entry into Delegated Work Acceptance Conditions Standard.

A changed object, version, recipient, environment, purpose, or reliance context may require re-entry into Delegated Work Acceptance Applicability Standard.

A challenge, contradictory review finding, proposed correction, suspension, withdrawal, or revision may require re-entry into Delegated Work Acceptance Challenge & Revision Standard.

The architecture supports targeted governed re-entry rather than uncontrolled repetition of every acceptance assessment.

Re-enter the earliest materially affected governance space.

Reassess what materially changed. Preserve what remains valid.

Governance re-entry must preserve historical traceability.

Delegated Work Identity & Acceptance Structure

DWAGA™ does not assume that one delegated assignment corresponds to one delivered object, one handover, one recipient, or one acceptance decision.

A delegated-work arrangement may contain:

Multiple Delivered Objects

Multiple Components

Multiple Versions

Multiple Partial Deliveries

Multiple Handover Events

Multiple Recipients

Multiple Acceptance Purposes

Multiple Acceptance Decisions

Multiple Acceptance Conditions

Multiple Applicability Contexts

Multiple Challenges

or:

Multiple Historical Decision States

Each relevant object and relationship must remain identifiable within its applicable governance context.

One delivered object may be accepted for one purpose and refused for another.

One component may be accepted independently while the complete assembly remains unaccepted.

One software version may be accepted while another requires renewed verification.

One acceptance decision may apply to one recipient without automatically transferring to another.

One decision may be historically valid while its current applicability has expired.

Therefore:

Delegated Assignment ≠ Delivered Object.

Delivered Object ≠ Delivered Object Version.

Handover Event ≠ Acceptance Decision.

One Delivered Object ≠ One Acceptance Purpose.

One Acceptance Decision ≠ Universal Applicability.

Current Acceptance State ≠ Complete Acceptance History.

Architectural Position

DWAGA™ governs the complete Delegated Work Acceptance Governance relationship from identifiable handover through acceptance purpose, intent alignment, prerequisites, decision, conditions, applicability, and legitimate challenge or revision.

The architecture progresses through:

Delegated Work Handover → Acceptance Purpose → Intent Alignment → Acceptance Prerequisites → Acceptance Decision → Acceptance Conditions → Acceptance Applicability → Acceptance Challenge & Revision

Together, these spaces govern what work has been delivered, why it is being considered for acceptance, whether it aligns with the delegated intent, which prerequisites apply, who possesses authority to decide, which acceptance outcome is established, what conditions restrict acceptance, where acceptance may legitimately be relied upon, and how the decision may be challenged or revised.

DWAGA™ provides a complete governance path from identifying what was delivered and why it is being considered for acceptance to establishing whether a legitimate acceptance decision exists, within which boundaries it may be relied upon, and how its authority, evidence, and historical continuity remain defensible.

DWAGA™ does not independently determine that the delivered work is technically safe, legally compliant, professionally certified, or authorized for operational use.

Those determinations remain within their applicable specialized governance and authority domains.

Canonical Principle

DWAGA™ does not prescribe delegated-work execution methods, replace engineering or professional judgment, perform technical verification, independently certify work, issue acceptance decisions without legitimate authority, grant operational permission, replace contractual or regulatory requirements, govern general accountability, perform general evidence governance, or substitute for specialized technical, safety, security, or other domain-specific decision systems. DWAGA™ governs the methodological conditions under which identifiable delegated work becomes handover-traceable, purpose-defined, intent-assessed, prerequisite-evaluated, legitimately decided, condition-governed, applicability-bounded, challengeable, revisable through authorized processes, and historically reconstructable.

Canonical Closing Statement

DWAGA™ serves as the official Delegated Work Acceptance Governance reference map for human-performed, organizational, industrial, technical, professional, digital, AI-generated, autonomous, and future delegated-work systems. By defining eight distinct governable spaces with identifiable beginnings, operational purposes, and governance boundaries, DWAGA™ establishes where handover governance begins and ends, where acceptance purpose is established, where delegated intent is evaluated, where prerequisites are assessed, where legitimate acceptance decisions are issued, where acceptance conditions are governed, where applicability is verified, and where challenges and authorized revisions are controlled.

The architecture preserves the methodological separation between work completion, delivery, receipt, acceptance purpose, delegated intent, prerequisite satisfaction, decision authority, conditional acceptance, applicability, challenge determination, revision authorization, and operational use. It enables acceptance relationships to be assessed and reassessed without conflating adjacent governance spaces, bypassing legitimate authority, extending decisions beyond their applicable boundaries, or erasing the evidence and historical states upon which earlier decisions were based.

DWAGA™ defines where Delegated Work Acceptance Governance begins, where each governance space ends, and how legitimate acceptance remains bounded, verifiable, and historically accountable.
