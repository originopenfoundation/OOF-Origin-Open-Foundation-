1. Canonical Definition

DWPRS — Delegated Work Acceptance Prerequisites Standard is the fourth Parent Standard within DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

DWPRS establishes the methodological requirements for identifying, defining, evaluating, verifying, and historically preserving the prerequisites that must be satisfied before a delegated-work result can be considered ready for an acceptance decision.

It governs the conditions of readiness that precede acceptance, including the availability and applicability of required evidence, satisfaction of material dependencies, completion of mandatory verification activities, relevant qualification and competence references, safety and security prerequisites, and applicable authority or compliance references.

DWPRS distinguishes the existence of a delivered result from the satisfaction of the prerequisites required to consider accepting that result.

A delivered object may be identifiable and complete while mandatory evidence remains unavailable.

A result may correspond to the original delegated intent while required verification remains incomplete.

A technical test may pass while the evidence applies to a different object version.

A qualified actor may perform the work while a separately required inspection or approval remains outstanding.

An acceptance prerequisite may be waived only where an applicable governance basis permits the waiver and the relevant authority, scope, conditions, and evidence can be established.

DWPRS requires these states to remain distinguishable.

It does not assume that every prerequisite applies to every delegation.

It does not require identical evidence or verification methods across unrelated industries.

It does not independently establish technical conformity, legal compliance, professional qualification, or the validity of a third-party certificate.

It does not grant acceptance or authorize operational use.

Its principal governance sequence is:

Delivered Work & Acceptance Purpose → Applicable Prerequisite Identification → Requirement Classification → Dependency & Evidence Assessment → Verification → Exception Assessment → Readiness Determination → Historical Preservation

Its principal output is the Delegated Work Acceptance Prerequisite & Readiness Profile.

3. Core Question

Which identifiable, applicable, and evidence-supported prerequisites must be satisfied before a specific delegated-work result can be considered ready for acceptance for a declared purpose, which prerequisites have actually been satisfied or remain unresolved, and how can that readiness determination be verified without confusing delivery, technical success, qualification claims, waiver requests, or recipient willingness with legitimate acceptance?

4. Foundational Principle

Acceptance readiness must be demonstrated through satisfaction of applicable prerequisites, not inferred from the existence, completion, usefulness, or apparent correctness of delivered work.

The following distinctions apply:

Work Delivered ≠ Acceptance Ready.

Work Complete ≠ Prerequisites Satisfied.

Technical Test Passed ≠ All Required Evidence Available.

Qualification Claimed ≠ Qualification Evidence Verified.

Evidence Present ≠ Evidence Applicable.

Prerequisite Identified ≠ Prerequisite Satisfied.

Exception Requested ≠ Exception Authorized.

Prerequisites Satisfied ≠ Work Accepted.

Acceptance Ready ≠ Authorized for Reality.

5. Governance Objective

DWPRS establishes a defensible methodological basis for determining whether the conditions necessary to proceed toward an acceptance decision have been satisfied.

Its objectives are to:

  1. Identify applicable acceptance prerequisites.
  2. Distinguish mandatory, conditional, optional, and non-applicable requirements.
  3. Establish prerequisite sources and applicability.
  4. Preserve dependencies between prerequisites.
  5. Identify required evidence.
  6. Evaluate evidence applicability and sufficiency.
  7. Preserve required verification references.
  8. Distinguish qualification claims from verified qualification evidence.
  9. Identify missing, failed, expired, or conflicting prerequisites.
  10. Govern waiver and exception references.
  11. Prevent unsupported readiness claims.
  12. Preserve historical readiness determinations.
  13. Support downstream acceptance decisions without replacing them.

71. Acceptance Readiness States

DWPRS recognizes the following operational readiness states:

READY — All applicable readiness requirements are satisfied within the assessed scope.

CONDITIONALLY READY — A conditional readiness state is explicitly permitted and its limitations are supported.

NOT READY — One or more applicable prerequisites demonstrably prevent readiness.

UNDETERMINED — Available evidence is insufficient to establish readiness.

NOT APPLICABLE — The readiness assessment does not apply to the defined object or context.

These operational states must not be confused with acceptance decisions.

72. Readiness Versus Acceptance

Acceptance establishes whether the work is accepted through the relevant decision-governance process.

READY ≠ ACCEPTED.

DWPRS applies:

83. Governance Determination

DWPRS applies:

PASS — Applicable prerequisite-identification, satisfaction, evidence, verification, and readiness-governance requirements are satisfied within the assessed scope.

CONDITIONAL — The assessment is supportable only under explicit, permissible conditions or limitations.

FAIL — A material prerequisite-governance requirement is demonstrably violated or contradicted.

INDETERMINATE — Evidence is insufficient for a defensible determination.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed requirement does not apply.

A demonstrated failure of a non-waivable mandatory prerequisite may justify FAIL.

An unresolved evidence requirement may justify INDETERMINATE.

A properly supported, explicitly permitted conditional prerequisite state may justify CONDITIONAL.

DWPRS PASS establishes only the bounded acceptance-prerequisite governance determination.

It does not independently grant acceptance or operational authorization.

1. What Is DWPRS?

DWPRS — Delegated Work Acceptance Prerequisites Standard is the fourth Parent Standard within DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

DWPRS establishes the methodological requirements for determining whether the prerequisites necessary to consider accepting delegated work have been identified, satisfied, and supported by applicable evidence.

It governs the transition from a delivered-work result to an evidence-supported state of acceptance readiness.

A completed task does not necessarily mean that the result is ready for acceptance.

A delivered product may still require inspection.

A software system may still require security verification.

An engineering deliverable may require evidence of applicable technical testing.

Work performed by a qualified person may still require separate quality-control evidence.

An AI-generated output may require verification before a recipient can reasonably rely upon it for a declared purpose.

DWPRS provides a structured method for identifying these requirements and determining whether they have been satisfied.

Its purpose is not to create unnecessary approval layers.

Its purpose is to prevent an acceptance decision from being based on an incomplete or unsupported understanding of the prerequisites that apply.

2. Why DWPRS Exists

Delegated work frequently passes through several stages before acceptance.

The work is performed.

A result is produced.

The result is delivered.

The recipient reviews it.

An acceptance decision may follow.

However, the existence of a delivered result does not establish that every necessary prerequisite has been satisfied.

Important requirements may remain incomplete, undocumented, expired, unverified, or incorrectly classified as non-applicable.

A favorable technical result may be presented without evidence that the test applies to the delivered version.

A qualification certificate may exist without demonstrating that its scope covers the actual work.

A mandatory verification may be replaced by an informal statement that the result appears correct.

An exception may be requested and then incorrectly treated as an authorized waiver.

DWPRS addresses these governance gaps by requiring prerequisites to remain identifiable, assessable, evidence-supported, and historically traceable.

3. Core Question

Which identifiable, applicable, and evidence-supported prerequisites must be satisfied before a specific delegated-work result can be considered ready for acceptance for a declared purpose, which prerequisites have actually been satisfied or remain unresolved, and how can that readiness determination be verified without confusing delivery, technical success, qualification claims, waiver requests, or recipient willingness with legitimate acceptance?

This question establishes the central boundary of DWPRS.

The standard evaluates readiness to proceed toward an acceptance decision.

It does not make that decision.

4. Foundational Principle

Acceptance readiness must be demonstrated through satisfaction of applicable prerequisites, not inferred from the existence, completion, usefulness, or apparent correctness of delivered work.

The following distinctions are essential:

Work Delivered ≠ Acceptance Ready.

Technical Test Passed ≠ All Prerequisites Satisfied.

Qualification Claimed ≠ Qualification Evidence Verified.

Evidence Available ≠ Evidence Applicable.

Exception Requested ≠ Exception Authorized.

Acceptance Ready ≠ Work Accepted.

These distinctions prevent one successful activity or favorable record from being treated as proof that the entire prerequisite framework has been satisfied.

5. What DWPRS Governs

DWPRS governs the methodological assessment of prerequisites associated with a specific delivered result and its proposed acceptance purpose.

Its principal governance areas include:

  • Prerequisite identification and source traceability.
  • Mandatory, conditional, optional, and non-applicable requirements.
  • Prerequisite hierarchy and dependencies.
  • Technical, qualification, competence, safety, and security evidence.
  • Evidence authenticity, applicability, validity, and sufficiency.
  • Verification requirements and independence references.
  • Missing, failed, expired, and contradictory prerequisites.
  • Exception requests, authorized waivers, and alternative satisfaction methods.
  • Prerequisite aggregation and readiness determination.
  • Reassessment, temporal integrity, and historical continuity.

Each prerequisite must be evaluated within its applicable scope rather than assumed to apply universally.

6. What DWPRS Does Not Govern

DWPRS does not independently:

  • Certify the technical correctness of delivered work.
  • Issue professional qualifications or competence certificates.
  • Determine legal or regulatory compliance.
  • Grant authority to waive a requirement.
  • Approve or accept delivered work.
  • Authorize deployment, installation, operation, or autonomous execution.
  • Determine legal liability or professional misconduct.

These responsibilities remain with the applicable authorities, qualified parties, specialized governance processes, and other DWAGA™ Parent Standards.

DWPRS determines whether acceptance prerequisites have been defensibly assessed and satisfied. It does not replace the acceptance decision.

15. Missing, Failed & Unverified Prerequisites

DWPRS distinguishes different forms of incomplete prerequisite satisfaction.

Missing means a required activity or record is absent.

Failed means evidence demonstrates that the applicable criteria were not met.

Not Verified means sufficient verification has not been established.

Expired means evidence may no longer satisfy its applicable validity requirements.

Conflicted means material evidence supports incompatible conclusions.

These states require different governance responses.

Missing evidence must not automatically be interpreted as proof that the underlying work was never performed.

A demonstrated failure must not be hidden by classifying it merely as missing documentation.

16. Exceptions & Waivers

Some governance frameworks permit defined exceptions or alternative satisfaction methods.

DWPRS does not prohibit legitimate flexibility.

It requires that flexibility to be governed.

A waiver must have an applicable basis, identifiable scope, relevant authority reference, conditions, and supporting evidence.

A requested exception is not automatically an approved exception.

An authorized waiver for one prerequisite does not automatically apply to other requirements.

Non-waivable requirements must remain non-waivable.

Administrative Convenience ≠ Authority to Waive.

17. Prerequisite Aggregation

Acceptance readiness may depend on numerous prerequisites.

DWPRS establishes a method for combining their individual states without losing material information.

A high percentage of completed requirements does not establish readiness when a mandatory prerequisite remains unsatisfied.

For example, an aggregate score must not override a non-waivable requirement.

The aggregation method must preserve mandatory failures, unresolved dependencies, conditional limitations, and applicable exceptions.

A Favorable Aggregate Result Must Not Conceal a Failed Mandatory Requirement.

18. Acceptance Readiness Determination

DWPRS recognizes five operational readiness states.

READY — Applicable readiness requirements are satisfied within the assessed scope.

CONDITIONALLY READY — A bounded conditional state is expressly permitted and supported.

NOT READY — One or more applicable prerequisites demonstrably prevent readiness.

UNDETERMINED — Evidence is insufficient to establish readiness.

NOT APPLICABLE — The readiness assessment does not apply to the defined context.

These readiness states are distinct from the standard's governance determinations of PASS, CONDITIONAL, FAIL, INDETERMINATE, and NOT APPLICABLE.

A READY state does not constitute acceptance.

19. Readiness Versus Acceptance Decision

DWPRS and DWADS govern different questions.

DWPRS asks:

Have the applicable prerequisites for considering acceptance been satisfied?

DWADS asks:

What acceptance decision is legitimate and supportable for the delivered work under the applicable governance conditions?

A result may be ready for an acceptance decision without being accepted.

A recipient may also wish to accept work that is not ready.

Recipient willingness does not independently satisfy mandatory prerequisites.

The two governance states must remain distinguishable.

1. Canonical Definition

APIM — Acceptance Prerequisite Identification Module is the first Core Module within DWPRS — Delegated Work Acceptance Prerequisites Standard, operating under DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

APIM establishes the methodological requirements for discovering, identifying, classifying, registering, and historically preserving the prerequisites applicable to a specific delegated-work result and its proposed acceptance purpose.

It governs the relationship between an identifiable acceptance prerequisite, its originating requirement source, its applicability criteria, the delivered-work object, the relevant delegation, and the intended form of reliance.

APIM recognizes that acceptance prerequisites may originate from different sources and operate under different conditions.

Some prerequisites are mandatory.

Some apply only when a defined condition is met.

Some are optional.

Others are not applicable to the assessed object or purpose.

A requirement may apply to one delivered-object version but not another.

A requirement may become applicable when the acceptance purpose changes.

A requirement may depend on the type of work, its operating environment, the actors involved, or the consequences of intended reliance.

APIM requires these distinctions to be established through identifiable criteria and supporting evidence.

A prerequisite must not be omitted merely because it is inconvenient to satisfy.

A requirement must not be classified as optional solely because its evidence is unavailable.

A conditional prerequisite must not be treated as non-applicable without evaluating its triggering condition.

An administrative checklist must not automatically be considered a complete representation of all applicable requirements.

APIM also distinguishes prerequisite identification from prerequisite satisfaction.

The presence of a requirement in a register does not demonstrate that it has been fulfilled.

The existence of a certificate does not establish that a qualification prerequisite applies or has been satisfied.

The identification of a required test does not establish that the test was performed.

APIM provides the structured prerequisite foundation required by subsequent DWPRS modules.

It does not independently verify technical correctness, determine evidence sufficiency, resolve dependency satisfaction, authorize exceptions, establish acceptance readiness, or issue acceptance decisions.

Its principal governance sequence is:

Delivered Work & Acceptance Purpose → Requirement Source Discovery → Prerequisite Identification → Applicability Assessment → Requirement Classification → Prerequisite Set Registration → Coverage Verification → Historical Preservation

75. Governance Determination

APIM applies:

PASS — Applicable prerequisite-identification, source, applicability, classification, coverage, and evidence requirements are satisfied within the assessed scope.

CONDITIONAL — Identification is supportable only under explicit limitations or unresolved conditions.

FAIL — A material identification or classification requirement is demonstrably violated or contradicted.

INDETERMINATE — Evidence is insufficient for a defensible determination.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed requirement does not apply.

A demonstrated omission of a material mandatory prerequisite may justify FAIL.

An unresolved requirement source may justify INDETERMINATE.

A defensibly classified non-applicable requirement may support PASS within the assessed identification scope.

APIM PASS establishes only the bounded prerequisite-identification determination.

It does not establish prerequisite satisfaction, acceptance readiness, or acceptance.

1. Canonical Definition

PESM — Prerequisite Evidence & Sufficiency Module is the second Core Module within DWPRS — Delegated Work Acceptance Prerequisites Standard, operating under DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

PESM establishes the methodological requirements for identifying, collecting, associating, evaluating, verifying, and historically preserving the evidence required to demonstrate satisfaction of applicable delegated-work acceptance prerequisites.

It governs the relationship between a prerequisite, its required evidence, the evidence actually available, the claims that evidence supports, and the delivered-work result for which acceptance readiness is being assessed.

PESM recognizes that the existence of a document, certificate, test result, inspection record, digital artifact, or automated verification output does not automatically establish that an applicable prerequisite has been satisfied.

Evidence may be authentic but irrelevant.

Evidence may be technically correct but apply to a different object version.

A qualification certificate may be valid but not cover the work performed.

A test report may demonstrate a successful test while failing to establish that the required testing scope was completed.

A verification record may exist without sufficient information about the verified object, method, criteria, or responsible actor.

Evidence may be complete in appearance but insufficient for the specific acceptance purpose.

PESM requires these distinctions to remain explicit.

It evaluates evidence according to the prerequisite's applicable satisfaction criteria rather than according to document quantity, presentation quality, administrative status, or the desired acceptance outcome.

PESM also recognizes that evidence requirements may legitimately differ between industries, professional activities, risk contexts, and acceptance purposes.

It does not impose universal certification or documentation requirements where no applicable source establishes them.

It does not independently certify professional qualifications, perform technical inspections, establish legal compliance, grant exceptions, determine overall acceptance readiness, or authorize acceptance.

PDVM establishes the methodological requirements for identifying, structuring, evaluating, verifying, and historically preserving dependencies between acceptance prerequisites and the verification obligations necessary to demonstrate their satisfaction.

It governs the relationships through which one prerequisite depends on another requirement, activity, evidence object, qualification, competence condition, verification result, authority reference, or explicitly defined triggering condition.

PDVM recognizes that acceptance prerequisites rarely operate as isolated checklist items.

A verification result may depend on the identity and version of the object tested.

An inspection may require a defined procedure and an appropriately competent inspector.

A qualification prerequisite may depend on the relevant work activity, credential scope, and validity period.

An approval may depend on prior completion of a mandatory review.

A readiness requirement may depend on multiple independent verification findings.

A conditional prerequisite may become mandatory when its triggering condition is satisfied.

PDVM requires these relationships to remain identifiable, evidence-supported, and independently assessable.

A dependent prerequisite must not be declared satisfied when a mandatory dependency remains unresolved.

A verification activity must not be considered valid merely because an administrative status indicates completion.

A test result must not be treated as applicable to an unverified object version.

A verifier's professional title must not automatically establish the competence or independence required by the applicable verification criteria.

A failed mandatory dependency must not be concealed by a favorable aggregate score.

PDVM distinguishes dependency identification from dependency satisfaction, verification execution from verification validity, and successful verification from acceptance authorization.

It does not independently perform technical certification, issue professional qualifications, grant verification authority, authorize exceptions, determine overall acceptance readiness, or issue acceptance decisions.

Its principal governance sequence is:

Applicable Prerequisite Set → Dependency Identification → Dependency Classification → Verification Requirement Identification → Dependency & Verification Applicability → Verification Evidence Assessment → Dependency Satisfaction Determination → Conflict & Propagation Assessment → Historical Preservation

Its principal output is the Prerequisite Dependency & Verification Profile.

3. Operational Question

Which acceptance prerequisites depend on other requirements, activities, evidence, qualifications, authority references, or verification results, and how can those dependencies and their verification obligations be evaluated and demonstrated without treating an incomplete dependency chain, unqualified verification, favorable administrative status, or isolated successful test as proof that the dependent prerequisite has been satisfied?

8. Foundational Principle

A dependent prerequisite cannot be defensibly satisfied while a mandatory dependency remains unresolved, and a verification result cannot establish satisfaction beyond the object, criteria, competence, independence, scope, and time for which it is supported.

The following distinctions apply:

Dependency Identified ≠ Dependency Satisfied.

Verification Scheduled ≠ Verification Performed.

Verification Performed ≠ Verification Valid.

Test Passed ≠ All Dependencies Satisfied.

Verifier Identified ≠ Verifier Competent.

Verifier Competent ≠ Verifier Independent.

Verification Evidence Available ≠ Verification Applicable.

Dependent Prerequisite Satisfied ≠ Acceptance Ready.

73. Confidence Classification

PDVM applies:

Confirmed — Sufficient applicable evidence supports the dependency or verification determination.

Reconciled — The determination is supported through documented, justified reconciliation of material conflicts.

Estimated — The determination depends materially on assumptions or indirect evidence.

Conflicted — Material evidence supports incompatible conclusions.

Unknown — Available evidence is insufficient.

Confidence classification does not replace the governance determination.

74. Governance Determination

PDVM applies:

PASS — Applicable dependency-identification, satisfaction, verification, competence, independence, scope, evidence, and continuity requirements are satisfied within the assessed scope.

CONDITIONAL — The assessment is supportable only under explicit, permissible conditions or limitations.

FAIL — A material dependency or verification-governance requirement is demonstrably violated or contradicted.

INDETERMINATE — Evidence is insufficient for a defensible determination.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed requirement does not apply.

A failed mandatory dependency may justify FAIL.

An unresolved verifier competence requirement may justify INDETERMINATE.

A properly supported conditional dependency may justify CONDITIONAL when permitted by the applicable criteria.

PDVM PASS establishes only the bounded dependency-and-verification governance determination.

It does not independently establish overall acceptance readiness, acceptance, or operational authorization.

78. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum PDVM implementation establishes eight governance layers.

Layer 1 — Prerequisite & Dependency Intake

Receive applicable prerequisites, dependency references, evidence requirements, and acceptance-context identifiers.

Layer 2 — Dependency Identification & Graph Construction

Identify dependency nodes, edges, directions, types, sources, and versions.

Layer 3 — Applicability & Satisfaction Criteria

Evaluate conditional triggers, dependency applicability, and satisfaction criteria.

Layer 4 — Verification Requirement Definition

Identify verification obligations, methods, criteria, scope, competence, independence, and authority references.

Layer 5 — Verification Evidence & Result Assessment

Evaluate verification activities, object binding, version applicability, evidence, and results.

Layer 6 — Dependency Propagation & Conflict Assessment

Evaluate failed, missing, circular, contradictory, and unresolved dependencies.

Layer 7 — Governance Determination

Issue a bounded dependency-and-verification determination with confidence, limitations, and unresolved findings.

Layer 8 — Historical Continuity & Interoperability

Preserve dependency graphs, verification results, versions, reassessments, and historical determinations in reconstructable form.

The framework may be implemented manually, through software, or through hybrid governance processes.

Conformance depends on demonstrated governance behavior and supporting evidence.

1. Canonical Definition

PEWM — Prerequisite Exception & Waiver Module is the fourth Core Module within DWPRS — Delegated Work Acceptance Prerequisites Standard, operating under DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

PEWM establishes the methodological requirements for identifying, evaluating, verifying, and historically preserving exceptions, waivers, conditional satisfaction arrangements, and alternative satisfaction methods associated with delegated-work acceptance prerequisites.

It governs whether an applicable prerequisite may legitimately be modified, waived, conditionally satisfied, or fulfilled through an alternative method under the governing requirement framework.

PEWM recognizes that not every acceptance prerequisite operates as an absolute, unchangeable obligation.

Some requirements expressly permit alternative verification methods.

Some allow defined exceptions.

Some permit conditional satisfaction.

Some may be waived by a specifically authorized decision-maker.

Other requirements are mandatory and non-waivable within the applicable governance context.

These distinctions must be established through identifiable requirement sources, authority references, eligibility conditions, and supporting evidence.

A waiver request does not establish waiver approval.

A manager's willingness to proceed does not independently establish authority to waive a requirement.

A technical workaround does not automatically qualify as an authorized alternative satisfaction method.

A compensating measure does not independently eliminate the original obligation.

A prerequisite cannot be classified as non-applicable merely because an exception is desired.

A non-waivable requirement must not be bypassed through administrative reclassification.

PEWM also distinguishes the authority to approve an exception from the authority to accept delegated work.

A person or system may possess limited authority to approve a specific deviation without possessing authority to accept the entire delivered result.

Conversely, an acceptance decision-maker may lack authority to waive a mandatory prerequisite.

PEWM preserves these authority boundaries.

Its principal governance sequence is:

Applicable Prerequisite → Exception Eligibility → Exception Request → Authority Verification → Evidence & Alternative Method Assessment → Exception Decision → Conditions & Limitations → Residual Obligation Assessment → Historical Preservation

Its principal output is the Prerequisite Exception & Waiver Profile.

PEWM does not independently grant waivers, issue legal exemptions, certify technical equivalence, establish overall acceptance readiness, authorize acceptance, or permit operational execution.

8. Foundational Principle

Acceptance readiness exists only within the demonstrated satisfaction of applicable prerequisites and must remain bounded by the evidence, conditions, object version, purpose, and time for which it was established.

The following distinctions apply:

Work Delivered ≠ Acceptance Ready.

Prerequisite Identified ≠ Prerequisite Satisfied.

Evidence Sufficient ≠ Every Requirement Fulfilled.

Verification Passed ≠ All Dependencies Satisfied.

Exception Authorized ≠ All Conditions Fulfilled.

Majority of Prerequisites Satisfied ≠ Mandatory Readiness Established.

Conditionally Ready ≠ Unconditionally Ready.

Ready at Time A ≠ Ready at Time B.

Acceptance Ready ≠ Work Accepted.

Work Accepted ≠ Authorized for Reality.

58. Readiness State Precedence

ARCM applies the following decision principles:

  1. Establish the assessment's applicability.
  2. Verify the applicable prerequisite set and coverage.
  3. Identify demonstrated blocking failures.
  4. Identify unresolved mandatory conditions and material uncertainty.
  5. Evaluate authorized exceptions and permitted conditional arrangements.
  6. Determine whether all requirements for unconditional readiness are satisfied.
  7. Issue the supported readiness state.

Where a demonstrated blocking failure exists, the result must not be classified as READY.

Where material uncertainty prevents a definitive conclusion and no demonstrated blocking failure establishes NOT READY, the result must remain UNDETERMINED unless a specifically permitted conditional state is supported.

A conditional state must not be used to conceal a prohibited deferral.

92. Governance Determination

ARCM applies:

PASS — Applicable readiness-governance requirements, including assessment integrity, prerequisite aggregation, evidence support, scope, and continuity, are satisfied.

CONDITIONAL — The governance assessment is supportable only under explicit, permissible conditions or limitations.

FAIL — A material readiness-governance requirement is demonstrably violated or contradicted.

INDETERMINATE — Evidence is insufficient for a defensible governance determination.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed governance requirement does not apply.

The governance determination evaluates whether ARCM's methodological requirements were fulfilled.

The readiness state evaluates whether the delegated-work result is ready for acceptance consideration.

These must remain distinct.

A correctly performed assessment may produce:

Governance PASS + Readiness NOT READY

or:

Governance PASS + Readiness UNDETERMINED

A methodological PASS must not be misrepresented as an unconditional READY state.

96. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum ARCM implementation establishes eight governance layers.

Layer 1 — Readiness Context Intake

Receive delivered-object identity, version, handover, acceptance purpose, intended reliance, and delegated-intent references.

Layer 2 — Prerequisite Set & Coverage Validation

Validate applicable prerequisite identity, classification, coverage, source versions, and unresolved identification limitations.

Layer 3 — Evidence & Verification Integration

Integrate PESM evidence findings and PDVM dependency and verification determinations.

Layer 4 — Exception & Boundary Integration

Evaluate PEWM exception decisions, waiver applicability, conditions, residual obligations, and non-waivable boundaries.

Layer 5 — Prerequisite Aggregation

Evaluate mandatory, conditional, optional, and non-applicable prerequisites without concealing blocking failures.

Layer 6 — Readiness Determination

Determine READY, CONDITIONALLY READY, NOT READY, UNDETERMINED, or NOT APPLICABLE according to explicit criteria.

Layer 7 — Change & Reassessment Governance

Identify material changes, evaluate their effect, and issue reassessments where required.

Layer 8 — Historical Continuity & Interoperability

Preserve readiness assessments, prerequisite findings, evidence references, change events, and historical determinations in reconstructable form.

The framework may be implemented manually, through software, or through hybrid governance processes.

Conformance depends on demonstrated governance behavior and supporting evidence.

109. Canonical Closing Statement

Acceptance readiness is not the sum of favorable indicators. It is the defensible conclusion that every applicable condition required to consider acceptance has been addressed within its governing boundaries.

A result may be technically successful while a mandatory prerequisite remains unsatisfied.

A verification may pass while its supporting evidence applies to another version.

An authorized waiver may be valid while an unrelated mandatory requirement remains unresolved.

A readiness assessment may be correct at one point in time and no longer applicable after a material change.

A conditional readiness state may be legitimate without establishing unconditional readiness.

ARCM preserves these distinctions.

As the fifth and final Core Module of DWPRS — Delegated Work Acceptance Prerequisites Standard, ARCM completes the methodological transition from prerequisite identification to evidence-supported acceptance readiness.

It integrates the findings of APIM, PESM, PDVM, and PEWM without replacing their specialized governance responsibilities.

It ensures that readiness is determined through explicit criteria, applicable evidence, mandatory satisfaction, verified dependencies, legitimate exceptions, and historically traceable assessments.

ARCM also establishes the boundary through which DWPRS findings may inform DWADS — Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Standard.

The purpose of ARCM is not to make acceptance easier or harder. Its purpose is to ensure that the conditions required before acceptance can be considered are visible, evaluated, and defensibly satisfied.

Readiness Is a Verified State of Prerequisite Satisfaction—Not a Decision to Accept.
