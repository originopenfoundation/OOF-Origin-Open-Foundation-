About DWAGA™ · Governance Layer · Architecture Map · Standards & Modules Index

Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Layer

Official Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture Map for Human-Performed Work, Organizational Delegation, Industrial Production, Engineering, Construction, Professional Services, Software Deliverables, AI-Generated Work, Autonomous Agents, Multi-Agent Systems, Digital Services, and Future Delegated Work Systems

This document serves as the official OOF® Delegated Work Acceptance Governance architecture map and structural index used for Delegated Work Acceptance Governance design, acceptance lifecycle navigation, governance diagnostics, architecture development, acceptance governance assessment, acceptance decision analysis, condition and applicability governance, challenge and revision governance, historical decision reconstruction, and interoperability across human, organizational, automated, AI-assisted, autonomous, and hybrid delegated-work arrangements.

DWAGA™ consists of 8 Parent Standards and 40 Core Modules.

The Parent Standard and Core Module names, acronyms, and sequence in this index follow the established DWAGA™ canonical architecture. The index does not introduce replacement modules or rename existing components.

Additional compatible standards and modules may be introduced when new governable Delegated Work Acceptance spaces are identified. However, the standards and modules listed below form the official core structural backbone of DWAGA™.

1. Delegated Work Handover Standard (DWHS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Handover Governance

Core Question: How can delegated work, its delivered object, handover evidence, recipient, and transfer boundary remain identifiable and verifiable before acceptance is considered?

Modules:

  • DWIDM — Delivered Work Identification Module
  • HSDM — Handover Scope Definition Module
  • DOVIM — Delivered Object Version Identification Module
  • RIBM — Representation & Interpretation Binding Module
  • HCEM — Handover Completeness & Exceptions Module

Governance Function: Establishes the identity, scope, evidence, recipient, receipt boundary, and historical continuity of delegated-work handover.

Primary Boundary: Delivery and receipt do not independently establish acceptance.

2. Delegated Work Acceptance Purpose Standard (DWAPS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Acceptance Purpose Governance

Core Question: For what identifiable purpose is delegated work being considered for acceptance, and how can the intended acceptance purpose, reliance context, boundaries, and continuity remain governable?

Modules:

  • APIDM — Acceptance Purpose Identification Module
  • IRDM — Intended Reliance Definition Module
  • APBM — Acceptance Purpose Boundary Module
  • PAVM — Purpose Authority & Verification Module
  • PCCM — Purpose Change & Continuity Module

Governance Function: Establishes why acceptance is being considered, what intended reliance it supports, and which purpose boundaries govern subsequent acceptance assessments.

Primary Boundary: A delivered result does not independently establish a legitimate acceptance purpose.

3. Delegated Work Intent Alignment Standard (DWIAS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Intent Alignment Governance

Core Question: How can delivered work be evaluated against the legitimate delegated intent, including instructions, intended outcomes, constraints, deviations, and subsequent changes?

Modules:

  • DIIDM — Delegated Intent Identification Module
  • DIIM — Delegated Intent Interpretation Module
  • IACM — Intent Alignment & Correspondence Module
  • IDRM — Intent Deviation & Reconciliation Module
  • ICCM — Intent Change & Continuity Module

Governance Function: Establishes the relationship between delegated intent and the delivered result, including interpretation, comparison, deviation, authorized resolution, and continuity.

Primary Boundary: Technical conformity does not automatically establish alignment with delegated intent.

4. Delegated Work Acceptance Prerequisites Standard (DWPRS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Acceptance Prerequisites Governance

Core Question: Which conditions must be established before an acceptance decision may legitimately be made, and how can their satisfaction, evidence, exceptions, and continuity remain verifiable?

Modules:

  • APIM — Acceptance Prerequisite Identification Module
  • PESM — Prerequisite Evidence & Sufficiency Module
  • PDVM — Prerequisite Dependency & Verification Module
  • PEWM — Prerequisite Exception & Waiver Module
  • ARCM — Acceptance Readiness & Continuity Module

Governance Function: Establishes applicable acceptance prerequisites, satisfaction evidence, dependencies, exceptions, waivers, and readiness continuity.

Primary Boundary: Successful testing does not independently establish satisfaction of all acceptance prerequisites.

5. Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Standard (DWADS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Governance

Core Question: Under which evidence, authority, scope, and procedural conditions may an identifiable delegated-work acceptance decision be legitimately issued and preserved?

Modules:

  • DAIM — Decision Authority & Identity Module
  • DEEM — Decision Eligibility & Evidence Module
  • DODM — Decision Outcome & Determination Module
  • DSBM — Decision Scope & Boundary Module
  • DCCM — Decision Change & Continuity Module

Governance Function: Establishes legitimate decision authority, evidence evaluation, acceptance outcome determination, decision scope, and decision continuity.

Acceptance Outcomes:

ACCEPTED · CONDITIONALLY ACCEPTED · REFUSED · DEFERRED · ABSTAINED · UNDETERMINED · NOT APPLICABLE

Primary Boundary: Evidence supporting an acceptance outcome does not independently grant authority to issue that outcome.

6. Delegated Work Acceptance Conditions Standard (DWACS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Acceptance Conditions Governance

Core Question: How can conditions attached to delegated-work acceptance remain identifiable, legitimate, verifiable, enforceable, and historically traceable throughout their applicable lifecycle?

Modules:

  • ACIM — Acceptance Condition Identification Module
  • CDDM — Condition Dependency & Definition Module
  • CSVM — Condition Satisfaction & Verification Module
  • CWEM — Condition Waiver & Enforcement Module
  • CCCM — Condition Change & Continuity Module

Governance Function: Establishes acceptance-condition identity, definitions, dependencies, satisfaction evidence, legitimate waiver or enforcement, and condition continuity.

Primary Boundary: Conditional acceptance must not be represented as unrestricted acceptance.

7. Delegated Work Acceptance Applicability Standard (DWAAS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Acceptance Applicability Governance

Core Question: To which objects, versions, purposes, recipients, environments, time periods, and reliance contexts does an acceptance decision legitimately apply, and how can those boundaries remain verifiable when circumstances change?

Modules:

  • AAIM — Acceptance Applicability Identification Module
  • ASBM — Applicability Scope & Boundary Module
  • ACVM — Applicability Compatibility & Verification Module
  • ATRM — Applicability Transfer & Restriction Module
  • ACCM — Applicability Change & Continuity Module

Governance Function: Establishes the applicable scope of acceptance, compatibility of reliance contexts, transfer and reassessment requirements, and historical applicability continuity.

Primary Boundary: Acceptance granted for one object, version, purpose, recipient, or context does not automatically apply to another.

8. Delegated Work Acceptance Challenge & Revision Standard (DWCRS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Acceptance Challenge & Revision Governance

Core Question: When, by whom, on what grounds, and through which evidence-based and procedurally legitimate process may a delegated-work acceptance decision be challenged, reviewed, confirmed, corrected, revised, suspended, or withdrawn, and how can the resulting consequences for authority, applicability, reliance, and historical continuity remain independently verifiable?

Modules:

  • CRIM — Challenge Registration & Identification Module
  • CAAM — Challenge Admissibility & Authority Module
  • CERM — Challenge Evidence & Review Module
  • DRAM — Decision Revision & Authorization Module
  • CRCM — Challenge & Revision Continuity Module

Governance Function: Establishes legitimate challenge registration, standing and admissibility, evidence-based review, authorized decision revision, and preservation of challenge-and-revision history.

Challenge Outcomes:

SUBSTANTIATED · PARTIALLY SUBSTANTIATED · NOT SUBSTANTIATED · INADMISSIBLE · WITHDRAWN · DEFERRED · UNDETERMINED · NOT APPLICABLE

Primary Boundary: A substantiated challenge does not automatically authorize revision of the original acceptance decision.

Architectural Position

DWAGA™ governs the complete Delegated Work Acceptance Governance lifecycle from identifiable handover through acceptance purpose, intent alignment, prerequisites, acceptance decision, conditions, applicability, and legitimate challenge or revision.

The architecture progresses:

Delegated Work Handover → Acceptance Purpose → Intent Alignment → Acceptance Prerequisites → Acceptance Decision → Acceptance Conditions → Acceptance Applicability → Acceptance Challenge & Revision

Together these standards govern what work was delivered, which object and version are involved, why acceptance is required, whether the work aligns with delegated intent, which prerequisites apply, who may issue an acceptance decision, which outcome is established, what conditions govern reliance, where acceptance legitimately applies, and how acceptance decisions may be challenged or revised without destroying historical accountability.

The complete acceptance relationship progresses:

Delegated Assignment → Work Performance → Delivered Work Identification → Handover & Receipt → Acceptance Purpose Definition → Intent Alignment Assessment → Prerequisite Verification → Acceptance Decision → Condition Governance → Applicability Verification → Governed Reliance → Challenge & Revision where applicable

Work performance and operational use are contextual activities surrounding DWAGA™.

They are not independently governed as technical execution functions by DWAGA™.

Work Completion ≠ Acceptance Governance Completion.

Handover Completion ≠ Acceptance Governance Completion.

Acceptance Decision Issued ≠ Acceptance Governance Completion.

Challenge Resolved ≠ Historical Governance Completion.

An acceptance relationship may remain governed while the delivered object is used, modified, transferred, challenged, or subjected to subsequent reassessment.

Delegated Work Acceptance Lifecycle Structure

The acceptance lifecycle establishes distinct governance boundaries.

1. Handover Boundary

Delivered Work → Delivered Object Identity → Handover Scope → Object Verification → Receipt Boundary → Handover Evidence

The handover stage establishes what was transferred and whether the relevant receipt relationship can be identified.

Delivery ≠ Receipt.

Receipt ≠ Acceptance.

2. Acceptance Purpose Boundary

Acceptance Purpose → Intended Reliance → Purpose Scope → Purpose Applicability → Purpose Continuity

The acceptance purpose establishes the context in which acceptance is being considered.

Purpose Defined ≠ Purpose Verified.

3. Intent Alignment Boundary

Delegated Intent → Intent Interpretation → Alignment Comparison → Deviation Resolution → Intent Continuity

Intent alignment establishes whether the delivered result corresponds to the legitimate delegated assignment.

Instruction Followed ≠ Intended Outcome Achieved.

4. Prerequisite Boundary

Prerequisite Identification → Satisfaction Evidence → Dependency Verification → Exception or Waiver Governance → Acceptance Readiness

The prerequisite stage establishes whether the conditions necessary for legitimate acceptance have been satisfied or otherwise legitimately addressed.

Test Passed ≠ Acceptance Ready.

5. Acceptance Decision Boundary

Decision Authority → Evidence Evaluation → Outcome Determination → Decision Scope → Decision Continuity

The acceptance decision establishes an explicit outcome through an applicable authority.

Evidence Sufficient ≠ Decision Authorized.

6. Acceptance Conditions Boundary

Condition Identity → Condition Definition → Satisfaction Verification → Waiver or Enforcement → Condition Continuity

Acceptance conditions govern restrictions and requirements attached to acceptance.

Conditionally Accepted ≠ Conditions Satisfied.

7. Acceptance Applicability Boundary

Applicability Identity → Scope & Boundary → Compatibility Verification → Transfer & Reassessment → Applicability Continuity

Applicability governs the contexts within which acceptance may legitimately be relied upon.

Acceptance Granted ≠ Universal Applicability.

8. Challenge & Revision Boundary

Challenge Registration → Admissibility & Review Authority → Evidence Review → Authorized Decision Revision → Historical Continuity

Challenge and revision governance preserves legitimate contestability and controlled decision changes.

Challenge Substantiated ≠ Revision Authorized.

Where a material change occurs:

Existing Acceptance Relationship → Material Change or New Evidence → Impact Assessment → Earliest Materially Affected Governance Space → Applicable Reassessment → Authorized Decision Action where required → Updated Acceptance State

Examples include:

Object Modification → Handover or Object Verification Reassessment → Dependent Acceptance Reassessment

Purpose Change → Acceptance Purpose Reassessment → Intent and Prerequisite Impact Assessment

New Technical Evidence → Evidence and Prerequisite Reassessment → Decision Reconsideration where required

Condition Change → Condition Governance → Applicability Reassessment

Recipient Transfer → Applicability Transfer Assessment → Required Authority and Reliance Verification

Substantiated Challenge → Revision Eligibility → Revision Authority → Controlled Decision Action

The canonical re-entry rule is:

Re-enter the earliest materially affected Delegated Work Acceptance Governance space.

The canonical reassessment rule is:

Reassess what materially changed. Preserve what remains valid.

Acceptance Reassessment ≠ Complete Acceptance Restart by default.

Decision Revision ≠ New Delivered Object by default.

Changed Applicability ≠ New Acceptance Purpose by default.

Re-entry must preserve original acceptance identity and historical traceability.

Acceptance Purpose & Intent Structure

DWAGA™ distinguishes the purpose of acceptance from the intent of the delegated assignment.

DWAPS governs the acceptance purpose and intended reliance.

DWIAS governs the delegated intent and alignment of delivered work.

A delivered result may align with a technical instruction while failing the intended acceptance purpose.

A recipient may intend to rely upon the delivered work in a context not covered by the original delegation.

A purpose change may require renewed evaluation without changing the delivered object.

Therefore:

Delegated Intent ≠ Acceptance Purpose.

Intent Alignment ≠ Purpose Applicability.

Purpose Established ≠ Acceptance Authorized.

Purpose Changed ≠ Prior Evidence Automatically Applicable.

Acceptance Prerequisite & Decision Structure

DWPRS and DWADS establish separate governance boundaries.

DWPRS determines whether applicable prerequisites have been identified, assessed, and satisfied or legitimately addressed.

DWADS governs whether a legitimate acceptance decision may be issued on the basis of applicable evidence and authority.

A prerequisite may be satisfied without establishing authority to accept.

An authorized decision-maker may lack sufficient evidence to issue an acceptance outcome.

A technical verification may pass while a required document, approval, or dependency remains unresolved.

Therefore:

Prerequisite Satisfied ≠ Acceptance Decision Issued.

Decision Authority Established ≠ Evidence Sufficient.

Evidence Sufficient ≠ Acceptance Automatically Granted.

Acceptance Outcome ≠ Governance Determination.

Acceptance Conditions & Applicability Structure

DWACS and DWAAS govern different aspects of acceptance.

DWACS governs conditions attached to acceptance.

DWAAS governs the scope within which acceptance applies.

A condition may restrict the circumstances under which reliance is permitted.

A condition may be satisfied without expanding the original applicability scope.

A transfer may require reassessment even when all existing conditions remain satisfied.

A change in applicability may require modification of acceptance conditions.

Therefore:

Condition Satisfied ≠ Applicability Expanded.

Condition Waived ≠ Acceptance Scope Unlimited.

Acceptance Applicable ≠ Conditions Eliminated.

Acceptance Transfer ≠ Automatic Acceptance Continuity.

Challenge & Revision Structure

DWCRS establishes five distinct governance functions.

Challenge Registration & Identification

CRIM — Challenge Registration & Identification Module

→ identifies the challenge, challenged decision, challenger, grounds, scope, submission time, and evidence references.

Challenge Admissibility & Authority

CAAM — Challenge Admissibility & Authority Module

→ evaluates standing, admissibility, review jurisdiction, authority, competence, independence, and procedural legitimacy.

Challenge Evidence & Review

CERM — Challenge Evidence & Review Module

→ evaluates evidence, contradictions, procedural findings, authority findings, technical findings, and challenge determinations.

Decision Revision & Authorization

DRAM — Decision Revision & Authorization Module

→ governs confirmation, correction, revision, suspension, withdrawal, replacement, revision authority, effective time, and reliance restrictions.

Challenge & Revision Continuity

CRCM — Challenge & Revision Continuity Module

→ preserves the challenge lifecycle, review history, original and revised decisions, effective-time integrity, reliance consequences, notifications, and historical reconstruction.

The governance sequence is:

Original Acceptance Decision → Challenge → Admissibility → Review → Determination → Revision Authorization → Decision Action → Historical Preservation

Challenge Submitted ≠ Challenge Admissible.

Challenge Admissible ≠ Challenge Substantiated.

Review Finding ≠ Revision Authorization.

Decision Revised ≠ Original Decision Erased.

Acceptance Governance Determination Structure

DWAGA™ distinguishes acceptance outcomes from governance determinations.

The architecture-wide governance determination vocabulary is:

PASS

CONDITIONAL

FAIL

INDETERMINATE

NOT APPLICABLE

These values describe the status of applicable governance requirements.

They do not replace the substantive acceptance outcome.

For example:

An authorized REFUSED acceptance outcome may be associated with governance PASS.

An ACCEPTED outcome issued without legitimate authority may be associated with governance FAIL.

An unresolved evidence gap may produce governance INDETERMINATE.

A correctly rejected inadmissible challenge may satisfy applicable challenge-governance requirements.

Acceptance ACCEPTED ≠ Governance PASS by default.

Acceptance REFUSED ≠ Governance FAIL by default.

Challenge NOT SUBSTANTIATED ≠ Review Governance FAIL.

Governance PASS ≠ Operational Authorization.

Canonical Principle

DWAGA™ does not prescribe delegated-work execution methods, replace engineering or professional judgment, perform technical verification, issue acceptance decisions independently of legitimate authority, certify work, grant operational permission, replace contractual or regulatory requirements, govern general accountability, perform general evidence governance, or substitute for specialized domain-specific decision systems. DWAGA™ governs the methodological conditions under which identifiable delegated work may be handed over, considered for a defined acceptance purpose, assessed for alignment with delegated intent, evaluated against applicable prerequisites, subjected to a legitimate acceptance decision, governed through acceptance conditions, relied upon within verified applicability boundaries, and challenged or revised through authorized, evidence-supported, and historically accountable processes.

Delegated work may be performed by humans, organizations, automated systems, AI systems, autonomous agents, or hybrid arrangements. It may contain multiple deliverables, versions, recipients, purposes, technical contexts, acceptance conditions, and downstream reliance relationships. It may be modified after acceptance, transferred to another context, challenged through new evidence, or subjected to authorized correction, suspension, withdrawal, or replacement. DWAGA™ provides the governance architecture for that complete acceptance relationship while preserving the distinction between work completion, technical verification, legitimate acceptance, applicable reliance, and operational authorization.

DWAGA™ establishes the complete structural map of Delegated Work Acceptance Governance — from identifiable handover to legitimate acceptance, bounded reliance, authorized revision, and preserved historical accountability.
