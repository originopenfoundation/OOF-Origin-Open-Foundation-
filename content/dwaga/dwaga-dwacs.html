1. Canonical Definition

DWACS — Delegated Work Acceptance Conditions Standard is the sixth Parent Standard within DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

DWACS establishes the methodological governance requirements for identifying, defining, authorizing, evaluating, verifying, enforcing, and historically preserving conditions associated with the acceptance of delegated-work results.

It governs the conditions under which an acceptance decision may be issued, remain applicable, permit defined reliance, require additional obligations, or become subject to restriction, reassessment, suspension, or other consequences established by an applicable governing framework.

DWACS recognizes that acceptance conditions are not merely descriptive notes attached to an acceptance record.

They may define mandatory prerequisites, outstanding obligations, restrictions, evidence requirements, permitted reliance boundaries, corrective actions, time limits, or events affecting the continuing applicability of an acceptance decision.

A condition may exist before acceptance.

A condition may become applicable through a conditional acceptance decision.

A condition may require fulfillment before a particular form of reliance is permitted.

A condition may remain outstanding after acceptance.

A condition may be satisfied, partially satisfied, violated, waived where permitted, superseded, or rendered inapplicable.

A condition may affect only a defined component, object version, purpose, or acceptance scope.

A condition may be technically satisfied while remaining unverified.

A condition may be declared fulfilled without sufficient supporting evidence.

A condition may be waived by an actor who lacks authority to grant the waiver.

A condition may expire without its underlying obligation necessarily being discharged.

DWACS requires these distinctions to remain explicit, evidence-supported, authority-bounded, and historically traceable.

It separates the existence of an acceptance condition from its applicability, satisfaction, verification, enforcement, and consequences.

DWACS does not independently establish acceptance decisions, technical conformity, legal obligations, contractual remedies, or operational authorization.

It provides a governance methodology for evaluating conditions established by applicable authorities, agreements, procedures, or decision mechanisms.

Its principal governance sequence is:

Condition Identification → Source & Authority Verification → Applicability Determination → Condition Structure & Dependencies → Satisfaction Evidence → Verification → Consequence & Enforcement Assessment → Change & Continuity

Its principal output is the Delegated Work Acceptance Conditions Governance Profile.

2. Core Question

Which conditions legitimately govern the acceptance of a specific delegated-work result, who has authority to establish or modify those conditions, what evidence demonstrates their applicability and satisfaction, which restrictions or consequences follow from their status, and how can their complete lifecycle remain independently verifiable without confusing conditional acceptance with unconditional acceptance or technical completion with authorized reliance?

3. Foundational Principle

Acceptance conditions must remain independently identifiable, authority-bounded, evidence-verifiable, and enforceable only within their applicable governance scope.

The following distinctions apply:

Condition Identified ≠ Condition Applicable.

Condition Applicable ≠ Condition Satisfied.

Condition Satisfied ≠ Condition Verified.

Condition Verified ≠ Acceptance Automatically Granted.

Conditional Acceptance ≠ Unconditional Acceptance.

Condition Waived ≠ Condition Never Existed.

Condition Expired ≠ Obligation Automatically Discharged.

Condition Violated ≠ Acceptance Automatically Revoked.

Condition Fulfilled ≠ Operational Authorization Granted.

Test Passed ≠ Authorized for Reality.

41. SATISFIED

SATISFIED means the applicable condition criteria have been met.

Where independent verification is required, satisfaction must not be represented as verified before that verification occurs.

42. CONDITIONALLY SATISFIED

CONDITIONALLY SATISFIED means the condition meets an explicitly permitted conditional satisfaction arrangement.

The remaining limitations must remain identifiable.

43. PARTIALLY SATISFIED

PARTIALLY SATISFIED means only a defined portion of the condition has been fulfilled.

Partial satisfaction must not automatically be treated as full satisfaction.

44. NOT SATISFIED

NOT SATISFIED means applicable evidence demonstrates that the required condition has not been met.

The unmet requirement must remain identifiable.

45. NOT VERIFIED

NOT VERIFIED means available evidence is insufficient to confirm satisfaction.

Absence of verification must not automatically be treated as proof of non-satisfaction.

46. WAIVED

WAIVED means an applicable authority has permitted non-fulfillment or alternative treatment of a condition within a defined scope.

Waiver validity must be independently evaluated.

A waived condition must remain historically identifiable.

47. NOT APPLICABLE

NOT APPLICABLE means the condition does not govern the assessed context.

The basis must remain explicit.

Non-applicability must not be used to conceal an unmet mandatory condition.

84. Confidence Classification

DWACS applies:

Confirmed — Sufficient applicable evidence supports the assessment.

Reconciled — The assessment is supported through documented reconciliation of material conflicts.

Estimated — The assessment depends materially on assumptions or indirect evidence.

Conflicted — Material evidence supports incompatible conclusions.

Unknown — Available evidence is insufficient.

Confidence classification does not replace condition satisfaction, verification, or governance determination.

85. Governance Determination

DWACS applies:

PASS — Applicable condition-governance requirements are satisfied.

CONDITIONAL — Governance is supportable only under explicit, permissible limitations.

FAIL — A material governance requirement is demonstrably violated or contradicted.

INDETERMINATE — Evidence is insufficient for a defensible determination.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed requirement does not apply.

A governance PASS does not establish that every condition is SATISFIED.

A correctly governed outstanding condition may coexist with a governance PASS where the applicable framework permits it.

An unauthorized waiver may produce a governance FAIL even where the underlying acceptance decision is favorable.

1. What Is DWACS?

DWACS — Delegated Work Acceptance Conditions Standard is the sixth Parent Standard within DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

DWACS governs the conditions associated with accepting delegated-work results.

It establishes how acceptance conditions are identified, authorized, defined, evaluated, verified, enforced, modified, and historically preserved.

Its central purpose is to ensure that conditions affecting acceptance remain explicit and independently governable rather than being reduced to administrative notes, ambiguous obligations, or unverified completion claims.

A delegated-work result may be accepted subject to outstanding requirements.

Those requirements may concern additional evidence, corrective actions, technical verification, restricted reliance, defined deadlines, or continuing obligations.

DWACS establishes the methodological structure through which such conditions can be evaluated without confusing conditional acceptance with unconditional acceptance.

2. Why DWACS Exists

Acceptance decisions do not always produce an unrestricted or final state.

An acceptance decision may contain conditions.

Some conditions must be satisfied before acceptance.

Others may remain outstanding after a conditional acceptance decision.

Certain conditions restrict how a delivered result may be relied upon.

Others require additional verification or corrective action.

Without explicit condition governance, organizations may treat a favorable acceptance status as evidence that all obligations have been fulfilled.

This creates a governance gap between what was accepted and what remains required.

DWACS addresses that gap.

It requires conditions to retain their identity, authority, applicability, requirements, evidence, verification status, and historical continuity.

3. The Core Problem

The central problem addressed by DWACS is:

An acceptance decision may be identifiable and authorized while the conditions governing its application, fulfillment, and consequences remain unclear, unverified, or incorrectly interpreted.

This problem can arise when:

  • Conditions are recorded without stable identifiers.
  • The authority establishing a condition is unclear.
  • Conditions are applied outside their intended scope.
  • Mandatory prerequisites are reclassified as optional obligations.
  • Dependencies between conditions are ignored.
  • Completion claims are treated as verified satisfaction.
  • Partial fulfillment is represented as full satisfaction.
  • Conditions are waived without proper authority.
  • Violations automatically trigger unsupported consequences.
  • Changes overwrite the historical condition record.

DWACS establishes a structured methodology for preventing these failures.

4. Core Question

Which conditions legitimately govern the acceptance of a specific delegated-work result, who has authority to establish or modify those conditions, what evidence demonstrates their applicability and satisfaction, which restrictions or consequences follow from their status, and how can their complete lifecycle remain independently verifiable?

Acceptance conditions must remain independently identifiable, authority-bounded, evidence-verifiable, and enforceable only within their applicable governance scope.

8. Foundational Principle

An acceptance condition cannot be defensibly governed unless its identity, source, authority, and applicable context remain distinguishable and traceable.

The following distinctions apply:

Condition Mentioned ≠ Condition Established.

Condition Established ≠ Condition Applicable.

Condition Identified ≠ Condition Satisfied.

Condition Registered ≠ Condition Authorized.

Condition Source ≠ Condition Authority.

Condition Applicable to One Version ≠ Applicable to Every Version.

Condition Applicable to One Purpose ≠ Applicable to Every Purpose.

Condition Classified as Mandatory ≠ Independently Verified as Non-Waivable.

Condition Identity Preserved ≠ Condition Currently Effective.

86. Governance Determination

ACIM applies:

PASS — Applicable condition-identification governance requirements are satisfied.

CONDITIONAL — The governance assessment is supportable only under explicit, permissible limitations.

FAIL — A material identification-governance requirement is demonstrably violated or contradicted.

INDETERMINATE — Evidence is insufficient for a defensible governance determination.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed governance requirement does not apply.

A governance PASS does not establish that the identified condition is satisfied.

An accurately registered condition with an unresolved satisfaction state may receive an identification-governance PASS.

An unauthorized condition represented as legitimately established may receive a governance FAIL.

It governs the distinction between performing an action, claiming that a condition has been fulfilled, demonstrating satisfaction through applicable evidence, and obtaining verification from an appropriately authorized actor or mechanism.

A verified violation may require corrective action.

A violation may justify a restriction on reliance.

A violation may trigger reassessment of an acceptance decision.

However, a violation does not automatically revoke acceptance.

An enforcement actor may possess authority to require corrective action but not to revoke an acceptance decision.

An automated workflow may detect a potential violation without possessing authority to impose consequences.

An AI system may recommend a waiver or enforcement response without possessing authority to approve or execute it.

A condition may be waived without ever having been satisfied.

A waiver may expire.

An enforcement action may later be reversed or revised.

Historical records must preserve the original condition, waiver, violation, consequence, and authority relationships.

CWEM requires these distinctions to remain explicit.

It establishes a governance framework through which exceptions and enforcement responses remain authority-bounded, evidence-supported, proportionate where required, and historically reconstructable.

CWEM does not independently establish condition identity, define condition requirements, verify ordinary satisfaction, issue acceptance decisions, determine legal liability, or authorize operational execution.

Its principal governance sequence is:

Identified Condition → Applicable Requirement → Exception or Violation Event → Authority Assessment → Eligibility Assessment → Evidence Verification → Waiver or Violation Determination → Authorized Consequence → Enforcement Boundary → Historical Preservation

Its principal output is the Condition Waiver & Enforcement Profile.

102. Governance Determination

CWEM applies:

PASS — Applicable waiver, violation, consequence, and enforcement-governance requirements are satisfied.

CONDITIONAL — Governance is supportable only under explicit, permissible limitations.

FAIL — A material waiver or enforcement-governance requirement is demonstrably violated or contradicted.

INDETERMINATE — Evidence is insufficient for a defensible governance determination.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed requirement does not apply.

A governance PASS does not require that a waiver be approved.

A properly governed waiver refusal may receive PASS.

A properly documented violation with an authorized and procedurally compliant response may receive PASS.

An unauthorized waiver or enforcement action may receive FAIL even when the underlying condition is otherwise legitimate.

144. Non-Bypassable Governance Rules

The following rules apply throughout CCCM:

R1. A proposed change must not be treated as an authorized change.

R2. Change authority must not exceed the applicable governing scope.

R3. An amendment must not silently overwrite the original condition.

R4. Suspension must not be represented as satisfaction or waiver.

R5. Expiration must not automatically discharge historical obligations.

R6. Supersession must not erase the historical identity of the superseded condition.

R7. A new object version must not automatically inherit earlier satisfaction or verification findings.

R8. Evidence created later must not be represented as historically available earlier.

R9. A waiver change must not bypass non-waivable requirements.

R10. A condition change must not automatically revise an acceptance decision.

R11. Material change impacts and required reassessments must not be silently omitted.

R12. No condition change or favorable continuity determination may independently grant operational authorization.

These rules implement the DWACS continuity boundary and do not replace the architecture-wide normative rules of DWAGA™.

146. Canonical Closing Statement

A condition may change its requirements, status, applicability, or consequences—but it must never lose the evidence of what it was, who governed it, and when its changes became legitimate.

An amendment must preserve the original requirement.

A suspension must preserve the condition's historical existence.

An expiration must preserve earlier obligations.

A supersession must preserve the superseded condition.

A reassessment must preserve the original finding.

An evidence correction must preserve the original evidence record.

An authority change must preserve the authority applicable at the relevant time.

A waiver change must preserve the original waiver decision.

A violation reversal must preserve the original allegation and determination.

An enforcement change must preserve the action that was previously authorized or executed.

A condition change must not silently alter an acceptance decision.

CCCM preserves these distinctions.

As the fifth Core Module of DWACS — Delegated Work Acceptance Conditions Standard, CCCM establishes the historical and lifecycle governance boundary through which acceptance conditions remain traceable, authority-bounded, evidence-supported, and independently reconstructable across every material change.

It ensures that current condition records do not erase historical obligations, that later evidence does not rewrite earlier knowledge, and that changing conditions cannot silently create permissions or acceptance effects beyond the applicable governing framework.

The purpose of CCCM is not to prevent acceptance conditions from changing. Its purpose is to ensure that every material change remains legitimate, traceable, verifiable, and historically accountable.

A Condition May Change Its State—but Never Erase the Governance History That Gives That State Meaning.
