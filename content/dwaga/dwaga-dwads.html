1. Canonical Definition

DWADS — Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Standard is the fifth Parent Standard within DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

DWADS establishes the methodological requirements for identifying, evaluating, authorizing, recording, communicating, and historically preserving decisions concerning the acceptance of delegated-work results.

It governs the transition from demonstrated acceptance readiness to an identifiable acceptance decision made by an appropriately authorized decision-maker for a defined delivered-work result and declared acceptance purpose.

DWADS recognizes that successful delivery, technical verification, prerequisite satisfaction, and acceptance readiness do not independently establish acceptance.

A delivered result may be complete without being accepted.

A technical verification may pass without granting acceptance authority.

A readiness assessment may establish that acceptance can be considered without determining whether acceptance should be granted.

A recipient may express satisfaction without possessing authority to accept.

A manager may approve a workflow step without issuing an acceptance decision.

An AI system may recommend acceptance without possessing the authority to make that decision.

A decision-maker may possess general organizational authority while lacking authority for the specific object, purpose, transaction, or acceptance scope.

DWADS requires these distinctions to remain explicit.

An acceptance decision must be associated with an identifiable decision event, an identifiable object and version, a defined purpose, applicable readiness findings, a valid authority basis, sufficient decision evidence, and a determinable decision outcome.

Where the governing framework requires informed acceptance, the decision-maker must have access to the material information necessary to understand the result, its limitations, unresolved conditions, and the consequences of the proposed acceptance.

Where the framework requires independence, competence, additional approval, or a particular decision procedure, those requirements must be satisfied.

An acceptance decision must not be inferred solely from silence, payment, possession, automated workflow completion, or operational use unless the applicable governing framework expressly establishes the relevant legal or procedural effect and its conditions are demonstrated.

DWADS distinguishes acceptance from certification, technical conformity, contractual performance, liability allocation, ownership transfer, and operational authorization.

Its principal governance sequence is:

Delivered Work Identity → Acceptance Purpose → Intent Alignment → Prerequisite Readiness → Decision Eligibility → Decision Authority → Informed Decision Evidence → Decision Evaluation → Decision Issuance → Decision Communication → Historical Preservation

Its principal output is the Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Profile.

2. Architectural Position

DWADS operates as the fifth Parent Standard within DWAGA™.

The preceding Parent Standards establish the necessary governance context:

DWHS — Delegated Work Handover Standard

Establishes the identity, version, representation, and handover boundaries of delivered work.

DWAPS — Delegated Work Acceptance Purpose Standard

Establishes the purpose for which acceptance is being considered and the intended reliance associated with that purpose.

DWIAS — Delegated Work Intent Alignment Standard

Evaluates correspondence between delegated intent and delivered results.

DWPRS — Delegated Work Acceptance Prerequisites Standard

Establishes prerequisite satisfaction and acceptance readiness.

DWADS — Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Standard

Governs the authority, eligibility, evidence, execution, and preservation of the acceptance decision itself.

Downstream Parent Standards govern conditions, applicability, challenges, and revisions without replacing DWADS decision authority.

3. Core Question

Who is legitimately authorized to accept a specific delegated-work result for a declared purpose, under which demonstrated readiness and decision conditions may that authority be exercised, what decision was actually made, and how can its scope, evidence, limitations, validity, and historical continuity be preserved without confusing successful delivery, readiness, informal approval, or operational use with legitimate acceptance?

4. Foundational Principle

Acceptance is an identifiable and authority-bounded decision, not an automatic consequence of completed work, successful verification, or demonstrated readiness.

The following distinctions apply:

Work Delivered ≠ Work Accepted.

Technical Verification Passed ≠ Acceptance Granted.

Prerequisites Satisfied ≠ Acceptance Granted.

Acceptance Ready ≠ Acceptance Approved.

Recipient Satisfaction ≠ Authorized Acceptance.

Decision Recommended ≠ Decision Authorized.

Authority Claimed ≠ Authority Verified.

Workflow Approved ≠ Acceptance Decision Issued.

Conditional Acceptance ≠ Unconditional Acceptance.

Acceptance Granted ≠ Authorized for Reality.

1. What Is DWADS?

DWADS — Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Standard is the fifth Parent Standard within DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

DWADS governs how decisions concerning the acceptance of delegated-work results are identified, evaluated, authorized, issued, communicated, and historically preserved.

Its central purpose is to establish a clear governance boundary between a result being ready for acceptance and an actual acceptance decision.

A delegated-work result may be complete, technically verified, supported by sufficient evidence, and classified as ready for acceptance without having been accepted.

Acceptance requires an identifiable decision or another acceptance mechanism expressly recognized by the applicable governing framework.

DWADS establishes the methodological conditions through which that decision can be evaluated.

It addresses who may decide, what may be accepted, under which conditions the decision is eligible, what evidence must be available, which outcome was actually issued, and how the decision remains bounded by its authority, purpose, scope, and time.

DWADS governs the legitimacy and traceability of acceptance decisions—not the technical performance of the work itself.

2. Why DWADS Exists

Delegated work frequently involves several distinct stages:

Work Execution

Work Delivery

Technical Verification

Prerequisite Satisfaction

Acceptance Readiness

Acceptance Decision

Operational Authorization

These stages may be connected, but they are not interchangeable.

A successful technical test may be treated as final approval.

An administrative workflow status may be interpreted as an acceptance decision.

An AI-generated recommendation may be executed as though it possessed decision authority.

A recipient may express satisfaction without having the authority to accept the result.

DWADS exists to prevent these governance boundaries from collapsing.

It requires acceptance to be supported by identifiable authority, applicable criteria, relevant evidence, an explicit or otherwise legitimately established decision mechanism, and a reconstructable decision record.

3. The Governance Problem

The central governance problem addressed by DWADS is:

How can an organization establish that a delegated-work result was legitimately accepted rather than merely delivered, verified, reviewed, recommended, or administratively approved?

A favorable result alone cannot answer this question.

The organization must be able to establish:

  • Which result was considered for acceptance.
  • Which version of the result was involved.
  • For what purpose acceptance was considered.
  • Whether the applicable prerequisites permitted the decision.
  • Who possessed the authority to decide.
  • Whether that authority was valid and sufficient.
  • Which information and evidence supported the decision.
  • What decision was actually issued.
  • Which conditions and limitations applied.
  • When the decision became effective.
  • Whether the decision was later changed, revoked, or superseded.

DWADS establishes the methodological structure for answering these questions.

4. Core Question

Who is legitimately authorized to accept a specific delegated-work result for a declared purpose, under which demonstrated readiness and decision conditions may that authority be exercised, what decision was actually made, and how can its scope, evidence, limitations, validity, and historical continuity be preserved without confusing successful delivery, readiness, informal approval, or operational use with legitimate acceptance?

1. Canonical Definition

It governs the question of who may legitimately make a particular acceptance decision concerning a defined delegated-work result.

89. Canonical Closing Statement

The identity of the person or system making a decision does not establish the legitimacy of the authority exercised.

A professional may possess technical competence without acceptance authority.

A manager may hold organizational responsibility without authority for a particular deliverable.

An automated system may record a decision without permission to make it.

An AI agent may generate a recommendation without authority to approve the result.

A delegation may exist while its scope, validity, or intermediate authority remains defective.

DAIM makes these distinctions explicit.

As the first Core Module of DWADS — Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Standard, DAIM establishes the identity and authority foundation upon which decision eligibility, outcomes, scope, and continuity can be evaluated.

It ensures that an acceptance decision can be traced to an identifiable actor and a demonstrable authority source whose permissions apply to the relevant decision, object, purpose, and time.

The purpose of DAIM is not to decide whether the work deserves acceptance. Its purpose is to establish whether the actor claiming the power to decide actually possesses that power.

1. Canonical Definition

DEEM — Decision Eligibility & Evidence Module is the second Core Module within DWADS — Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Standard, operating under DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

DODM — Decision Outcome & Determination Module is the third Core Module within DWADS — Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Standard, operating under DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

DODM establishes the methodological requirements for identifying, classifying, verifying, recording, and preserving the actual outcome of a delegated-work acceptance decision.

It governs the distinction between a decision that could legitimately be made and the decision that was actually made.

DODM recognizes that verified decision authority and demonstrated decision eligibility do not independently establish an affirmative acceptance outcome.

An authorized decision-maker may refuse acceptance.

An eligible decision may be deferred.

A decision may be conditionally favorable rather than unconditional.

A decision-maker may abstain where the governing procedure permits abstention.

A workflow may display an approved status without an identifiable acceptance decision.

An AI system may recommend acceptance without the recommendation being adopted by an authorized decision-maker.

A decision record may be incomplete, contradictory, or insufficient to establish what actually occurred.

DODM requires these distinctions to remain explicit.

It evaluates the identifiable decision event, the applicable decision procedure, the expression of the decision, the supporting evidence, and the relationship between the actual decision and its recorded outcome.

DODM distinguishes the substantive acceptance outcome from the methodological governance determination.

An authorized and correctly recorded refusal may satisfy the applicable governance requirements.

An affirmative acceptance issued without required authority or eligibility may constitute a governance non-conformity.

A favorable administrative status must not override contradictory decision evidence.

DODM does not independently establish acceptance authority, prerequisite readiness, or decision eligibility.

72. Governance Determination

DODM applies:

PASS — Applicable decision-outcome governance requirements are satisfied.

CONDITIONAL — The governance assessment is supportable only under explicit, permissible conditions or limitations.

FAIL — A material outcome-governance requirement is demonstrably violated or contradicted.

INDETERMINATE — Evidence is insufficient for a defensible governance determination.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed governance requirement does not apply.

A governance PASS does not mean that acceptance was granted.

A properly governed refusal, deferral, or abstention may receive a governance PASS.

An affirmative acceptance issued without required authority or eligibility may receive a governance FAIL.

76. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum DODM implementation establishes eight governance layers.

Layer 1 — Decision Context Intake

Receive decision identity, delivered-object identity, object version, acceptance purpose, authority references, and eligibility references.

Evaluate contradictory records, collective decisions, sequential decisions, communication, and acknowledgment.

Issue a substantive outcome assessment and separate governance determination with confidence and limitations.

Layer 8 — Historical Continuity & Interoperability

Preserve original outcome records, decision events, communication references, changes, and historical determinations.

Conformance depends on demonstrated governance behavior and supporting evidence.

1. Canonical Definition

DSBM — Decision Scope & Boundary Module is the fourth Core Module within DWADS — Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Standard, operating under DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

DSBM establishes the methodological requirements for identifying, defining, verifying, limiting, and historically preserving the scope and boundaries of delegated-work acceptance decisions.

It governs what an acceptance decision actually covers and what remains outside that decision.

DSBM recognizes that an identifiable acceptance outcome does not automatically establish unrestricted acceptance of every component, version, purpose, or possible use of a delivered result.

An authorized decision-maker may accept only part of a deliverable.

A decision may concern one technical configuration while another configuration remains outside its scope.

A result may be accepted for a defined informational purpose without being accepted for operational deployment.

A decision may be conditional upon obligations that remain outstanding.

An acceptance record may exclude specific components, unresolved deviations, or future modifications.

An administrative system may display a general ACCEPTED status even though the underlying decision applies only to a limited object or purpose.

A valid acceptance decision may coexist with separate certification, licensing, ownership, contractual, or operational authorization requirements.

DSBM requires these distinctions to remain explicit.

It establishes the binding between the actual decision outcome and the delivered object, object version, acceptance purpose, intended reliance, included components, excluded components, conditions, limitations, and applicable restrictions.

DSBM also evaluates whether the declared decision scope is consistent with the decision-maker's verified authority and the established decision eligibility.

It prevents an acceptance outcome from being extended beyond its demonstrated boundaries through inference, administrative simplification, automated propagation, or unsupported interpretation.

DSBM does not independently create rights, certify technical conformity, determine contractual liability, or authorize operational execution.

Its principal governance sequence is:

Decision Outcome → Delivered Object Binding → Version Binding → Purpose & Reliance Binding → Component Coverage → Conditions & Exclusions → Authority & Eligibility Boundary Verification → Scope Determination → Historical Preservation

122. Canonical Closing Statement

A decision's current status is not a substitute for the historical truth of how that decision was made and changed.

An acceptance decision may be valid when issued and later expire.

An authorized decision may be superseded without becoming historically nonexistent.

A new object version may require reassessment without rewriting the earlier accepted version.

A later authority change may affect future decisions without automatically invalidating earlier decisions.

New evidence may change the current assessment without having been available to the original decision-maker.

A correction may repair an error without concealing the original record.

A revocation may terminate operative effect without erasing the historical acceptance event.

DCCM preserves these distinctions.

As the fifth Core Module of DWADS — Delegated Work Acceptance Decision Standard, DCCM establishes the continuity boundary through which acceptance decisions remain identifiable across changes, corrections, amendments, revocations, supersessions, and reassessments.

It ensures that the original decision, subsequent events, applicable authority, supporting evidence, current validity, and historical states can be reconstructed without conflation.

The purpose of DCCM is not to preserve every decision as permanently valid. Its purpose is to preserve the integrity of what was decided, what subsequently changed, and which decision state legitimately applies at each relevant point in time.

A Decision May Change Its Effect. It Must Never Lose Its History.
