Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Layer

Official Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture for Human-Performed Work, Organizational Delegation, Industrial Production, Engineering, Construction, Professional Services, Software Deliverables, AI-Generated Work, Autonomous Agents, Multi-Agent Systems, Digital Services, and Future Delegated Work Systems

DWAGA™ serves as the official OOF® Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture used for Delegated Work Handover governance, Acceptance Purpose governance, Intent Alignment governance, Acceptance Prerequisites governance, Acceptance Decision governance, Acceptance Conditions governance, Acceptance Applicability governance, and Acceptance Challenge & Revision governance across human, organizational, automated, AI-assisted, autonomous, and hybrid delegated-work arrangements.

DWAGA™ does not prescribe how delegated work must be performed, replace technical verification, perform acceptance decisions on behalf of legitimate authorities, certify delivered work, determine legal or contractual acceptance independently of applicable requirements, grant operational authorization, replace organizational accountability, or substitute for specialized technical, safety, security, regulatory, professional, or contractual obligations. DWAGA™ defines the governance architecture required to ensure that delegated work is identifiable at handover, assessed against an explicit acceptance purpose, evaluated for alignment with delegated intent, subject to established prerequisites, decided upon by legitimate authority, governed through enforceable conditions, bounded by verified applicability, and open to legitimate challenge and authorized revision with preserved historical accountability.

DWAGA™ consists of 8 Core Parent Standards and 40 Core Modules forming the primary Delegated Work Acceptance Governance backbone within the OOF® ecosystem.

Additional compatible standards, bridge standards, and specialized Delegated Work Acceptance Governance standards may extend the architecture when new governance spaces are identified. However, the standards listed below form the official core governance structure of DWAGA™.

Canonical Definition

DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture is the OOF® reference governance architecture for governing the transfer of completed or purportedly completed delegated work into a legitimate acceptance relationship, from identifiable handover through acceptance purpose, intent alignment, prerequisites, acceptance decision, acceptance conditions, applicability, and challenge or revision, while preserving the distinction between work production, delivery, receipt, acceptance, reliance, and authorization for operational reality.

DWAGA™ establishes the methodological conditions under which an identifiable work result may be presented for acceptance, examined against the purpose and intent of the delegation, evaluated for prerequisite satisfaction, subjected to an authorized acceptance decision, restricted through applicable conditions, relied upon within defined boundaries, and subsequently challenged or revised through a legitimate and historically accountable process.

The architecture governs the acceptance relationship.

It does not presume that completion establishes conformity.

It does not presume that delivery establishes receipt.

It does not presume that receipt establishes acceptance.

It does not presume that acceptance establishes unrestricted applicability.

It does not presume that acceptance establishes authorization for operational use.

Delegated Work Produced ≠ Delegated Work Accepted.

Core Question

How can delegated work become legitimately accepted, conditionally accepted, refused, deferred, or otherwise determined when its identity, intended purpose, delegated intent, prerequisites, evidence, authority, conditions, applicability, and subsequent challenge or revision must remain independently verifiable?

Governance Flow

Delegated Work Handover → Acceptance Purpose → Intent Alignment → Acceptance Prerequisites → Acceptance Decision → Acceptance Conditions → Acceptance Applicability → Acceptance Challenge & Revision

The governance flow establishes the primary logical relationships among the eight Parent Standards.

It does not require every delegated-work arrangement to follow a single chronological workflow.

Some governance requirements operate before physical delivery.

Some conditions arise during acceptance.

Some applicability assessments must be repeated after transfer, modification, or environmental change.

A challenge may arise before downstream reliance or long after the original acceptance decision.

A revision may require renewed assessment of previously established acceptance foundations.

Governance Flow ≠ Mandatory Linear Execution Sequence.

Acceptance Decision ≠ End of Acceptance Governance.

Core Parent Standards

Governed Space: Delegated Work Handover Governance

Core Question: How can delegated work, its delivered object, handover evidence, recipient, and transfer boundary remain identifiable and verifiable before acceptance is considered?

DWHS establishes the governance foundation for identifying what has been delivered, by whom, to whom, under which handover conditions, and with which evidence.

Its five Core Modules are:

DWIDM — Delivered Work Identification Module

HSDM — Handover Scope Definition Module

DOVIM — Delivered Object Verification & Identification Module

RIBM — Receipt Identification & Boundary Module

HCEM — Handover Continuity & Evidence Module

Primary Governance Boundary: Work production, delivery, receipt, and acceptance must remain distinct.

Handover Completed ≠ Acceptance Granted.

2. Delegated Work Acceptance Purpose Standard (DWAPS)

Governed Space: Delegated Work Acceptance Purpose Governance

Core Question: For what identifiable purpose is delegated work being considered for acceptance, and how can the intended acceptance purpose, reliance context, boundaries, and continuity remain governable?

DWAPS establishes the acceptance-purpose foundation against which the relevance and legitimacy of subsequent acceptance assessments are determined.

Its five Core Modules are:

APIDM — Acceptance Purpose Identification Module

APBM — Acceptance Purpose Boundary Module

PAVM — Purpose Applicability Verification Module

Work Delivered ≠ Acceptance Purpose Established.

Core Question: To which objects, versions, purposes, recipients, environments, time periods, and reliance contexts does an acceptance decision legitimately apply, and how can those boundaries remain verifiable when circumstances change?

Compatible OOF® Architectures

DWAGA™ interoperates with multiple OOF® architectures governing complementary spaces surrounding delegated work, acceptance authority, evidence, operational reality, identity, integrity, and lifecycle continuity.

GOA™ — Governance Architecture provides the constitutional governance layer defining how governance itself is structured, bounded, delegated, validated, and maintained, while DWAGA™ governs the specific acceptance relationship associated with delegated work.

ORA™ — Operational Reality Architecture governs what is actually occurring in Operational Reality, while DWAGA™ governs the conditions under which delegated work may be accepted and relied upon. An acceptance record does not independently establish the actual operational state of the delivered object.

AGA™ — Accountability Governance Architecture governs responsibility, accountability, attribution, authority, and related accountability structures, while DWAGA™ governs their application to delegated-work handover, acceptance decisions, conditions, applicability, and revision.

AIG® — AI Governance Architecture governs AI behavioral integrity and behavioral governance, while DWAGA™ governs the acceptance of work produced, assisted, reviewed, or delivered through AI systems without independently governing the AI system's complete behavioral domain.

ASGA™ — Autonomous Systems Governance Architecture governs autonomous-system identity, authority, execution, constraints, coordination, intervention, escalation, and operational governability, while DWAGA™ governs acceptance relationships involving outputs and deliverables produced or transferred through autonomous systems.

ADIT® — Continuous Audit & Evidence Governance Architecture governs evidential observation, preservation, correlation, verification, examination, and auditability, while DWAGA™ defines the delegated-work acceptance requirements and determinations to which relevant evidence applies.

VALIDOS™ — Validation Governance Architecture governs whether a validation basis sufficiently supports a Validation Determination, while DWAGA™ governs whether the validated information is applicable and sufficient for a legitimate delegated-work acceptance relationship and decision.

INTEGROS® — Integrity Governance Architecture governs integrity across objects, states, evidence, and processes, while DWAGA™ governs integrity requirements specific to delegated-work handover, acceptance, conditions, applicability, and challenge or revision.

OBIDENITY® — Origin-Bound Identity Governance Architecture governs Origin, Identity, Ownership, Authority, Permission, Responsibility, Provenance, Transfer, Continuity, and Auditability, while DWAGA™ governs the identity and authority relationships applicable to delivered work, acceptance recipients, decision-makers, reviewers, and revision authorities.

SIMULOS® — Simulation Governance Architecture governs simulation purpose, structure, execution, outputs, and lifecycle, while DWAGA™ governs acceptance relationships involving simulated evidence or simulation-produced work without treating simulated success as proof of actual operational suitability.

SLGA™ — Software Lifecycle Governance Architecture governs software requirements, creation, dependencies, configuration, quality, security, release, deployment, change management, retirement, and lifecycle control, while DWAGA™ governs acceptance relationships concerning software deliverables and other delegated software-related work.

DWAGA™ serves as the common Delegated Work Acceptance Governance architecture supporting the broader OOF® ecosystem by ensuring that acceptance is treated as a distinct, authority-bounded, evidence-supported, context-specific, and historically accountable governance relationship rather than an automatic consequence of work completion, delivery, testing, or operational availability.

DWAGA™ governs the complete Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Lifecycle from identifiable handover through acceptance purpose, intent alignment, prerequisites, acceptance decision, conditions, applicability, and legitimate challenge or revision.

The architecture progresses through:

Delegated Work Handover → Acceptance Purpose → Intent Alignment → Acceptance Prerequisites → Acceptance Decision → Acceptance Conditions → Acceptance Applicability → Acceptance Challenge & Revision

Together, these standards govern:

What work was delivered.

Which delivered object and version are involved.

Who transferred and received the work.

Why acceptance is being considered.

For which purpose reliance is intended.

Whether the delivered result aligns with the delegated intent.

Which prerequisites must be satisfied.

Which evidence supports acceptance readiness.

DWAGA™ remains distinct from the activities through which work is performed, tested, delivered, operated, maintained, or independently certified.

Human Work / Organizational Work / Automated Work / AI-Generated Work / Autonomous Work / Hybrid Work → Delegated Work Result → Handover → Governed Acceptance Relationship under DWAGA™

Work Performed ≠ Work Delivered.

Work Delivered ≠ Work Received.

Work Received ≠ Work Accepted.

Work Accepted ≠ Authorized for Reality.

DWAGA™ governs delegated-work acceptance — not the execution of the delegated work itself.

Core Governed Delegated Work Acceptance Spaces

DWAGA™ establishes eight distinct governable acceptance spaces.

Delegated Work Handover

→ what work has been delivered, which object and version are involved, what evidence accompanies the handover, who transfers the work, who receives it, and where the delivery-to-receipt boundary exists.

Acceptance Purpose

→ why acceptance is required, which intended reliance is contemplated, what acceptance is meant to establish, and which purpose restrictions govern the acceptance relationship.

Intent Alignment

→ whether the delivered result corresponds to the legitimate delegated intent, including instructions, outcomes, constraints, interpretations, deviations, and authorized changes.

Acceptance Prerequisites

→ which requirements must be satisfied before an acceptance decision, how their satisfaction is evidenced, how dependencies are evaluated, and how exceptions or waivers are governed.

Acceptance Decision

→ who possesses authority to decide, which evidence supports the decision, what outcome is established, which scope applies, and how the decision remains identifiable and controlled.

Acceptance Conditions

→ which conditions accompany acceptance, what dependencies those conditions establish, how satisfaction is verified, when waiver or enforcement is legitimate, and how conditions remain governed through change.

Acceptance Applicability

→ which objects, versions, purposes, recipients, environments, activities, and time periods fall within the legitimate scope of acceptance, and when transfer or changed circumstances require reassessment.

Acceptance Challenge & Revision

→ who may challenge an acceptance decision, on which grounds, through which review authority, using which evidence, and how findings, authorized revisions, affected reliance, and historical decision states remain governed.

Each space answers a different methodological question.

Each can fail independently.

Their separation is foundational to DWAGA™.

A valid handover does not establish a valid acceptance purpose.

A valid purpose does not establish intent alignment.

Intent alignment does not establish prerequisite satisfaction.

Prerequisite satisfaction does not independently establish decision authority.

A legitimate decision does not eliminate acceptance conditions.

Acceptance conditions do not establish universal applicability.

Applicable acceptance does not eliminate the right to legitimate challenge.

A substantiated challenge does not independently authorize revision.

Architectural Origin

DWAGA™ evolved from the foundational methodological problem that delegated work is frequently treated as accepted merely because it has been completed, delivered, tested, acknowledged, or made available for use.

Traditional delegated-work arrangements may proceed:

Assignment → Execution → Delivery → Inspection → Approval → Use

while the distinctions among delivery, receipt, purpose, delegated intent, prerequisite satisfaction, acceptance authority, conditional acceptance, applicability, and subsequent challenge remain insufficiently governed.

This becomes increasingly inadequate where:

the creator differs from the recipient

the recipient differs from the acceptance decision-maker

the acceptance decision-maker differs from the operational authority

a subcontractor performs work for an organization that is not the final user

a technical test passes but does not cover the intended use

a delivered object differs from the tested object

a component is accepted but later integrated into a different assembly

a software artifact is accepted but deployed under a different configuration

an AI system generates work without possessing authority to accept it

an autonomous agent completes a task beyond its permitted scope

a recipient acknowledges delivery without agreeing to acceptance

acceptance is granted before required prerequisites are established

acceptance is conditional but its restrictions are not enforced

a decision applies to one version but is relied upon for another

the original acceptance authority is no longer valid

an acceptance decision is transferred to a new recipient or context without reassessment

new evidence contradicts the original acceptance basis

a review identifies a defect but lacks revision authority

a revised decision overwrites the original decision history

or:

the existence of an acceptance record is incorrectly treated as proof that the work is safe, suitable, or authorized for operational reality

DWAGA™ therefore establishes acceptance as an identifiable, evidence-supported, authority-bounded, purpose-specific, and historically accountable governance relationship.

Its foundational governance sequence is:

Handover → Purpose → Intent → Prerequisites → Decision → Conditions → Applicability → Challenge & Revision

The architecture ensures that the acceptance relationship remains governable even when actors, evidence, objects, versions, conditions, authority, and intended reliance change.

Architectural Structure

Architecture: DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture

Category: Governance & Enforcement

Architecture Model: Acceptance-Lifecycle-Based · Modular · Methodology-Neutral · Technology-Neutral · Authority-Bounded · Evidence-Based

Primary Governance Domain: Delegated Work Acceptance Governance

The architecture deliberately separates governable spaces that can independently fail.

Delegated work may:

  • be delivered without a stable object identity;
  • be received without an identifiable handover boundary;
  • be evaluated without an explicit acceptance purpose;
  • satisfy a technical specification while failing delegated intent;
  • lack a required prerequisite despite successful testing;
  • be accepted by an actor without the required authority;
  • receive an acceptance outcome unsupported by evidence;
  • be conditionally accepted while conditions remain unresolved;
  • be relied upon beyond the accepted object or version;
  • be transferred into an incompatible environment;
  • be challenged without a legitimate review process;
  • be reviewed using evidence that was unavailable at the relevant historical time;
  • be revised without the necessary authorization;
  • be withdrawn without identifying affected reliance;
  • or lose the historical relationship between original and revised decisions.

DWAGA™ treats these not as one generic work acceptance problem, but as distinct governance failures requiring distinct methodological controls.

Complete Acceptance Lifecycle Principle

DWAGA™ does not treat delivery, receipt, or the initial acceptance decision as completion of acceptance governance.

The complete governance relationship may proceed:

Delegated Work → Handover → Acceptance Purpose → Intent Alignment → Prerequisite Verification → Acceptance Decision → Condition Governance → Applicability Verification → Challenge or Revision

After the original acceptance decision:

Conditions may remain outstanding.

Applicability may change.

The accepted object may be modified.

The intended reliance may change.

The recipient may change.

The operational environment may change.

New evidence may emerge.

A prerequisite may be discovered to have been invalid.

A challenge may be submitted.

A review may establish a material defect.

A revision may be authorized.

A decision may be suspended or withdrawn.

Downstream reliance may require reassessment.

The acceptance relationship may therefore proceed:

Existing Acceptance → Changed Circumstance → Impact Assessment → Applicable Governance Re-Entry → Reverification → Authorized Decision Action → Updated Conditions or Applicability → Continued Governed Reliance

Acceptance Issued ≠ Acceptance Governance Completed.

Conditions Satisfied Once ≠ Conditions Permanently Satisfied.

Applicability Verified Once ≠ Applicability Permanently Valid.

Decision Revised ≠ Original Decision Erased.

Recursive Acceptance Governance Principle

DWAGA™ does not assume that acceptance governance progresses through its eight Parent Standards only once.

A changed acceptance purpose may require renewed intent-alignment assessment.

A newly discovered prerequisite defect may require reconsideration of an existing acceptance decision.

A revised acceptance condition may affect applicability.

An object modification may invalidate the evidence supporting earlier acceptance.

A change of recipient may require renewed reliance and authority assessment.

A substantiated challenge may require renewed verification of the acceptance foundations.

A withdrawal may affect downstream decisions that relied upon the original acceptance.

DWAGA™ therefore supports targeted recursive governance.

The canonical re-entry rule is:

Re-enter the earliest materially affected Delegated Work Acceptance Governance space.

The canonical reassessment rule is:

Reassess what materially changed. Preserve what remains valid.

Re-entry does not require repeating every acceptance assessment by default.

Only materially affected governance spaces and their dependent relationships require renewed evaluation.

Changed Acceptance Context ≠ Complete Acceptance Restart by default.

Acceptance Reassessment ≠ New Work Identity by default.

Decision Revision ≠ New Delivered Object by default.

Re-entry must preserve the original acceptance history.

Delegated Assignment ≠ Delivered Object.

Delivered Object ≠ Delivered Object Version.

Work Completed ≠ Work Handed Over.

Handover Evidence ≠ Receipt Evidence.

Receipt Evidence ≠ Acceptance Evidence.

Each material delivered object and transfer relationship must remain identifiable within its applicable governance context.

Acceptance Purpose & Intent Principle

DWAGA™ distinguishes the purpose for which work is accepted from the intent under which the work was delegated.

The delegated intent concerns what the performing actor was instructed or authorized to accomplish.

The acceptance purpose concerns why the receiving or deciding authority is considering the delivered result for acceptance and intended reliance.

These dimensions may overlap, but they are not interchangeable.

A deliverable may satisfy the literal wording of an instruction while failing the intended acceptance purpose.

A technically correct deliverable may be unsuitable for the recipient's actual intended reliance.

A changed acceptance purpose may require renewed assessment even where the delivered object remains unchanged.

Therefore:

Delegated Intent ≠ Acceptance Purpose.

Instruction Followed ≠ Intended Outcome Achieved.

Purpose Defined ≠ Purpose Applicability Verified.

Technical Correctness ≠ Acceptance Suitability.

DWAGA™ requires the relevant purpose and intent relationships to remain separately identifiable.

Required Dependencies

A missing prerequisite must not be silently treated as satisfied.

Test Passed ≠ Prerequisites Satisfied.

Prerequisite Identified ≠ Prerequisite Verified.

Acceptance Decision & Authority Principle

DWAGA™ distinguishes evidence supporting an acceptance decision from authority to issue that decision.

An actor may possess technical competence without acceptance authority.

An inspector may verify an object without possessing authority to accept the work on behalf of the recipient.

An automated system may calculate a result without possessing authority to issue a binding acceptance decision.

An AI agent may recommend acceptance without authority to approve it.

A manager may possess organizational responsibility without possessing unrestricted acceptance authority.

Therefore:

Technical Competence ≠ Acceptance Authority.

Evidence Evaluation ≠ Decision Authorization.

Recommendation ≠ Acceptance Decision.

Decision-Maker Identity ≠ Decision-Maker Authority.

Authority Established ≠ Unlimited Decision Scope.

DWAGA™ requires each material acceptance decision to remain attributable to a legitimate authority operating within an identifiable scope.

Not applicable is not evidence that a requirement was satisfied.

Challenge & Revision Principle

DWAGA™ recognizes that an acceptance decision may require legitimate challenge, review, correction, suspension, withdrawal, or replacement.

However, challenge and revision must remain governed by evidence, authority, scope, procedure, and historical integrity.

A challenge must be identifiable.

The challenger must satisfy applicable standing requirements.

The challenge must be assessed for admissibility.

The reviewer must possess the required authority and competence.

The review must distinguish allegations from verified findings.

Material contradictions must remain preserved.

A substantiated challenge must not automatically authorize revision.

Revision authority must remain distinct from review authority.

Any revision must identify its scope and effective time.

Original decision records must remain historically reconstructable.

Challenge Submitted ≠ Challenge Admissible.

Challenge Admissible ≠ Challenge Substantiated.

Review Finding ≠ Revision Authorization.

Revision Authorized ≠ Revision Executed.

Decision Withdrawn ≠ Decision Historically Erased.

Acceptance Reliance & Operational Boundary Principle

DWAGA™ distinguishes acceptance from subsequent reliance and operational authorization.

A delivered object may be accepted for a limited purpose.

A recipient may rely upon acceptance only within its applicable scope.

Operational use may require additional authorization.

Architecture-Wide Governance Integrity Rules

DWAGA™ applies its architecture-wide normative integrity framework R1–R24 across the eight Parent Standards and forty Core Modules.

The architecture-wide integrity framework preserves the following twenty-four non-bypassable boundary objectives. These statements summarize the architectural control objectives and do not replace the detailed normative rule definitions in the canonical architecture baseline.

R1 — Governed Work Identity: Material acceptance claims must remain bound to identifiable delegated work.

R2 — Object Version Integrity: Acceptance evidence and decisions must remain attributable to the relevant delivered-object version.

R3 — Handover Boundary: Work production, delivery, receipt, and acceptance must remain distinguishable.

R4 — Acceptance Purpose Identity: Acceptance must reference an identifiable purpose where required.

R5 — Intended Reliance Boundary: Acceptance must not silently authorize reliance beyond the established purpose.

R6 — Delegated Intent Integrity: The delegated intent and its authorized changes must remain identifiable.

R7 — Intent Alignment Evidence: Technical conformity must not substitute automatically for delegated-intent alignment.

R8 — Prerequisite Identity: Material acceptance prerequisites must remain explicit.

R9 — Prerequisite Satisfaction: Required prerequisites must not be presumed satisfied without applicable evidence.

R10 — Exception & Waiver Authority: Exceptions and waivers must not bypass applicable authorization requirements.

R11 — Decision Authority: Acceptance decisions must be attributable to legitimate authority.

R12 — Decision Evidence: Material acceptance outcomes must remain supported by applicable evidence.

R13 — Decision Outcome Integrity: Acceptance outcomes must remain explicit and must not be silently conflated.

R14 — Decision Scope: Acceptance authority and outcomes must remain bounded to their applicable scope.

R15 — Condition Identity: Material acceptance conditions must remain identifiable.

R16 — Condition Satisfaction & Enforcement: Conditional acceptance must not be represented as unrestricted acceptance.

R17 — Applicability Identity: The object, purpose, recipient, context, and time to which acceptance applies must remain identifiable where material.

R18 — Applicability Transfer: Acceptance must not automatically transfer to materially different contexts without the required assessment.

R19 — Challenge Identity: Legitimate challenges must remain identifiable and traceable.

R20 — Challenge Admissibility & Review Authority: A challenge must not bypass applicable standing, admissibility, competence, independence, or review-authority requirements.

R21 — Review Evidence Integrity: Allegations, evidence, contradictions, findings, and challenge determinations must remain distinguishable.

R22 — Revision Authorization: Review findings must not automatically authorize decision correction, revision, suspension, withdrawal, or replacement.

R23 — Historical Continuity: Original and revised decisions, evidence, authority, effective times, and affected reliance must remain reconstructable.

R24 — Operational Authorization Boundary: Delegated-work acceptance must not independently be represented as authorization for operational reality.

These objectives apply across the complete architecture.

A conforming implementation must apply the actual normative requirements and their applicable dependencies, not merely reproduce these rule statements in documentation.

Eight-Layer Institutional Implementation Framework

DWAGA™ supports an eight-layer institutional implementation model.

Layer 1 — Governed Work & Handover Identity

Identify acceptance conditions, verify satisfaction, establish applicability boundaries, and govern changes in context or reliance.

Preserve original and revised acceptance states, evidence availability, authority history, effective times, conditions, applicability, challenge records, and downstream reliance relationships.

These layers describe a governance implementation model, not a mandatory software architecture or fixed organizational structure.

Canonical Principle

DWAGA™ does not prescribe delegated-work execution methods, replace engineering or professional judgment, perform technical verification, issue acceptance decisions independently of legitimate authority, certify work, grant operational permission, replace contractual or regulatory requirements, govern general accountability, perform general evidence governance, or substitute for specialized domain-specific decision systems. DWAGA™ governs the methodological conditions under which identifiable delegated work may be handed over, considered for a defined acceptance purpose, assessed for alignment with delegated intent, evaluated against applicable prerequisites, subjected to a legitimate acceptance decision, governed through acceptance conditions, relied upon within verified applicability boundaries, and challenged or revised through authorized, evidence-supported, and historically accountable processes.

Delegated work may be performed by humans, organizations, automated systems, AI systems, autonomous agents, or hybrid arrangements. It may contain multiple deliverables, versions, recipients, purposes, technical contexts, acceptance conditions, and downstream reliance relationships. It may be modified after acceptance, transferred to another context, challenged through new evidence, or subjected to authorized correction, suspension, withdrawal, or replacement. DWAGA™ provides the governance architecture for that complete acceptance relationship while preserving the distinction between work completion, technical verification, legitimate acceptance, applicable reliance, and operational authorization.

DWAGA™ governs the transition from Delegated Work to Legitimate Acceptance — and preserves the authority, evidence, boundaries, and accountability of that acceptance throughout its lifecycle.
