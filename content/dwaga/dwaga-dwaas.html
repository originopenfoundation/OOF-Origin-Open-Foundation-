1. Canonical Definition

DWAAS — Delegated Work Acceptance Applicability Standard is the seventh Parent Standard within DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

DWAAS establishes the methodological governance requirements for identifying, defining, evaluating, verifying, restricting, and historically preserving the applicability of delegated-work acceptance decisions across objects, versions, purposes, recipients, reliance contexts, organizational boundaries, operational environments, and time.

DWAAS governs the distinction between an acceptance decision that exists and an acceptance decision that legitimately applies to a particular use or reliance context.

An acceptance decision may be validly issued.

Its decision authority may be legitimate.

Its prerequisites may have been satisfied.

Its conditions may have been fulfilled or properly governed.

Its supporting evidence may be authentic.

However, none of these findings automatically establishes that the acceptance decision applies to every object, version, recipient, purpose, environment, jurisdiction, or future use.

An acceptance may apply only to a specific delivered object.

It may apply only to a particular version.

It may apply only to a defined acceptance purpose.

It may be restricted to an identified recipient.

It may permit reliance for one activity while excluding another.

It may depend on a specific technical configuration.

It may remain applicable only while defined environmental conditions exist.

It may expire.

It may be suspended.

Its applicability may be reduced following new evidence.

Its scope may be expanded only through an authorized process.

A previously applicable acceptance decision may cease to apply after material changes.

An acceptance decision may remain historically valid even when it is no longer applicable to a current context.

DWAAS requires these distinctions to remain explicit.

It establishes a governance framework through which acceptance applicability remains identifiable, authority-bounded, evidence-supported, context-specific, temporally accurate, and historically reconstructable.

DWAAS does not independently issue acceptance decisions, establish technical conformity, grant operational authorization, transfer ownership rights, or establish legal liability.

Its principal governance sequence is:

Acceptance Decision → Applicability Identity → Applicable Object & Version → Purpose & Recipient Binding → Contextual Boundary → Authority & Restrictions → Evidence & Verification → Applicability Determination → Historical Continuity

Its principal output is the Delegated Work Acceptance Applicability Profile.

2. Core Question

To which delivered object, version, purpose, recipient, activity, operational context, organizational or jurisdictional boundary, and time period does a delegated-work acceptance decision legitimately apply, what authority and evidence establish that applicability, which restrictions limit reliance, and how can changes or transfers of context remain independently verifiable without expanding acceptance beyond its authorized scope?

3. Foundational Principle

Acceptance applies only within the object, purpose, context, authority, and temporal boundaries for which its applicability can be legitimately established and independently verified.

An acceptance decision must not be treated as universally applicable merely because it was validly issued.

Applicability must be established separately from decision existence.

Governs legitimate acceptance-decision authority, eligibility, outcomes, and decision integrity.

DWAAS does not duplicate the authority to issue an acceptance decision.

It governs whether that decision applies to a specific context.

103. Confidence Classification

DWAAS applies:

Confirmed — Sufficient applicable evidence supports the assessment.

Reconciled — The assessment is supported through documented reconciliation of material conflicts.

Estimated — The assessment depends materially on assumptions or indirect evidence.

Conflicted — Material evidence supports incompatible conclusions.

Unknown — Available evidence is insufficient.

Confidence classification does not replace applicability determination, verification status, or governance determination.

104. Governance Determination

DWAAS applies:

PASS — Applicable acceptance-applicability governance requirements are satisfied.

CONDITIONAL — Governance is supportable only under explicit, permissible limitations.

FAIL — A material applicability-governance requirement is demonstrably violated or contradicted.

INDETERMINATE — Evidence is insufficient for a defensible governance determination.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed governance requirement does not apply.

A governance PASS does not require an APPLICABLE outcome.

A properly established NOT APPLICABLE determination may receive PASS.

A properly documented expiration may receive PASS.

An unauthorized applicability expansion may receive FAIL even when the original acceptance decision was legitimate.

1. What Is DWAAS?

DWAAS — Delegated Work Acceptance Applicability Standard is the seventh Parent Standard within DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

DWAAS establishes the methodological governance framework for determining whether an existing delegated-work acceptance decision legitimately applies to a particular object, version, purpose, recipient, activity, operational environment, organizational boundary, jurisdiction, and time.

DWAAS addresses a fundamental distinction:

An acceptance decision may exist without being applicable to the context in which someone intends to rely on it.

A delivered result may have been accepted for one purpose.

That acceptance does not automatically extend to every possible use.

A software build may have been accepted in one configuration.

That does not automatically establish applicability to another build or configuration.

An engineering component may have been accepted for a defined operating environment.

That does not automatically establish applicability under different environmental conditions.

An AI-generated output may have been accepted for internal analysis.

That does not automatically establish applicability for an external operational decision.

DWAAS ensures that acceptance applicability is established through explicit boundaries, legitimate authority, relevant evidence, and appropriate verification.

Its purpose is not to repeat the acceptance decision.

Its purpose is to determine the legitimate scope of reliance on that decision.

2. Why DWAAS Exists

Acceptance decisions are frequently represented as simple outcomes:

Accepted.

Approved.

Completed.

Verified.

However, these outcomes may conceal important limitations.

An acceptance may concern only a specific object.

It may apply only to one version.

It may be valid only for a defined purpose.

It may permit reliance by one recipient but not another.

It may depend on a particular technical configuration.

It may be restricted to a defined operating environment.

It may be subject to conditions.

It may have a limited validity period.

It may no longer apply after material changes.

Without applicability governance, an acceptance decision can be extended beyond its original legitimate scope.

DWAAS addresses this governance problem.

It establishes the methodological distinction between:

Acceptance Decision Existence

Acceptance Decision Validity

Acceptance Applicability

Reliance Eligibility

Operational Authorization

These dimensions must not be silently merged.

3. The Core Governance Problem

The central problem addressed by DWAAS is unauthorized generalization of acceptance.

An acceptance decision may be legitimate in its original context but unsupported in another.

This may occur through:

  • Applying acceptance to the wrong delivered object.
  • Applying acceptance to a different object version.
  • Expanding acceptance beyond its original purpose.
  • Allowing an unauthorized recipient to rely on acceptance.
  • Transferring acceptance across organizations without authority.
  • Applying acceptance under incompatible operating conditions.
  • Ignoring technical configuration changes.
  • Overlooking acceptance conditions or reliance restrictions.
  • Treating expired applicability as current.
  • Assuming that successful verification establishes unrestricted operational authorization.

DWAAS requires each material applicability claim to be assessed against its actual governance boundaries.

1. Canonical Definition

ACVM — Applicability Compatibility & Verification Module is the third Core Module within DWAAS — Delegated Work Acceptance Applicability Standard, operating under DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

ACVM establishes the methodological governance requirements for evaluating whether a proposed reliance context is compatible with the authorized applicability boundaries of a delegated-work acceptance decision and whether the resulting applicability claim can be supported by sufficient, relevant, attributable, and independently verifiable evidence.

ACVM governs the distinction between an acceptance decision whose applicability boundaries have been identified and a particular reliance context that demonstrably satisfies those boundaries.

An acceptance decision may be legitimate.

Its applicability relationship may be identified.

Its object and version boundaries may be defined.

Its acceptance purpose may be established.

Its intended recipients may be specified.

Its operational conditions may be documented.

Its technical configuration may be bounded.

Its validity period may be known.

However, none of these findings automatically establishes that a proposed reliance context satisfies the applicable boundaries.

The proposed object must be evaluated.

The proposed version must be compared.

The intended purpose must be assessed.

The recipient and actor must fall within the relevant eligibility boundaries.

The organizational and jurisdictional context must be considered.

The operational activity must be compatible.

The environmental conditions must remain within the applicable limits.

The technical configuration must be evaluated.

The intended reliance must respect applicable restrictions.

The relevant time must fall within the authorized applicability period.

Any material differences must be identified.

Any claimed equivalence must be supported.

The supporting evidence must be evaluated for provenance, authenticity, integrity, applicability, sufficiency, and temporal relevance.

The verification process must preserve authority boundaries, material contradictions, and unresolved uncertainty.

ACVM prevents compatibility from being presumed merely because two objects are similar, two versions share a name, two environments appear comparable, or an acceptance decision was previously verified.

It establishes an evidence-based compatibility and verification framework through which a specific applicability claim can be evaluated without silently expanding the original acceptance scope.

ACVM does not independently issue acceptance decisions, redefine authorized applicability boundaries, approve cross-context transfers, waive acceptance conditions, or grant operational authorization.

Its principal governance sequence is:

Identified Applicability Relationship → Authorized Scope & Boundaries → Proposed Reliance Context → Context Comparison → Material Difference Assessment → Applicable Restrictions & Dependencies → Evidence Assessment → Verification → Applicability Determination → Historical Preservation

Its principal output is the Applicability Compatibility & Verification Profile.

2. Canonical Definition Extension

ACVM recognizes that compatibility is a relationship between an established acceptance-applicability boundary and a specific proposed reliance context.

Compatibility is not an intrinsic property of a delivered object.

A component may be compatible with one accepted configuration but incompatible with another.

A software build may be accepted within one runtime environment but require reassessment under a different dependency configuration.

An AI model may have been evaluated under a defined tool-access configuration but not under expanded permissions.

A manufacturing process may have produced an accepted result under one technical specification without establishing applicability to a materially different specification.

An acceptance decision may remain historically valid while its applicability to a new context remains unverified.

ACVM therefore requires compatibility claims to remain:

Context-Specific

Boundary-Referenced

Authority-Aware

Evidence-Supported

Verification-Aware

Restriction-Aware

Time-Referenced

Historically Reconstructable

Compatibility must not be confused with technical equivalence alone.

A technically equivalent context may still fall outside the authorized recipient, purpose, jurisdictional, or temporal scope.

Likewise, an authorized recipient may propose a context that is technically incompatible.

ACVM evaluates these dimensions separately before producing a consolidated applicability finding.

ATRM governs the distinction between an acceptance decision that is applicable within an established context and a proposal to rely on that decision within another context or under modified applicability conditions.

An acceptance decision may be valid.

Required verification must be completed.

The transfer decision must be attributable.

Its effective time must be established.

Its limitations must remain explicit.

2. Canonical Definition Extension

ATRM recognizes that acceptance applicability is bounded by authority as well as evidence.

A new context may be technically compatible with an existing acceptance decision without being authorized for reliance.

A recipient may possess a legitimate acceptance record without being entitled to rely upon it.

An organization may have authority to accept delegated work internally without possessing authority to extend that acceptance to external organizations.

An engineering component may remain technically unchanged while its proposed operating environment falls outside the original acceptance scope.

An AI model may retain the same model identifier while receiving expanded tool permissions that materially change its operating context.

A software system may be technically compatible with a new deployment environment while its acceptance decision remains limited to the original deployment.

ATRM therefore separates:

Technical Compatibility

Applicability Eligibility

Transfer Authority

Transfer Authorization

Applicable Restrictions

Operational Authorization

These dimensions must not be silently merged.

A successful compatibility assessment does not independently authorize a transfer.

An authorized transfer does not independently establish technical compatibility.

An applicability expansion does not automatically modify the underlying acceptance decision.

A restriction waiver does not automatically establish condition satisfaction.

A reactivation does not automatically erase the history of suspension.

ATRM preserves the authority, evidence, and temporal boundaries governing each change.

5. Foundational Principle

Acceptance applicability may cross a boundary only when the governing authority permits the crossing and the required evidence supports the resulting scope.

The following distinctions apply:

Existing Applicability ≠ Transfer Authorization.

Context Compatibility ≠ Transfer Authority.

Recipient Identity ≠ Recipient Transfer Eligibility.

Organizational Access ≠ Organizational Reliance Rights.

Technical Equivalence ≠ Authorized Scope Expansion.

Transfer Requested ≠ Transfer Approved.

Transfer Approved ≠ Operational Authorization.

Restriction Waived ≠ Acceptance Condition Satisfied.

Suspension Lifted ≠ Historical Suspension Erased.

Acceptance Decision Valid ≠ Every Transfer Permitted.

119. Governance Determination

ATRM applies:

PASS — Applicable transfer and restriction-governance requirements are satisfied.

CONDITIONAL — Governance is supportable only under explicit, permissible limitations.

FAIL — A material transfer or restriction-governance requirement is demonstrably violated or contradicted.

INDETERMINATE — Evidence is insufficient for a defensible governance determination.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed requirement does not apply.

A governance PASS does not necessarily mean a transfer was authorized.

A properly documented REFUSED transfer may receive PASS.

An unauthorized transfer represented as approved may receive FAIL even when the original acceptance decision is valid.

137. Confidence Classification

ACCM applies:

Confirmed — Sufficient applicable evidence supports the assessment.

Reconciled — The assessment is supported through documented reconciliation of material conflicts.

Estimated — The assessment depends materially on assumptions or indirect evidence.

Conflicted — Material evidence supports incompatible conclusions.

Unknown — Available evidence is insufficient.

Confidence classification does not replace continuity verification, continuity determination, applicability determination, or governance determination.

138. Governance Determination

ACCM applies:

PASS — Applicable change and continuity-governance requirements are satisfied.

CONDITIONAL — Governance is supportable only under explicit, permissible limitations.

FAIL — A material change or continuity-governance requirement is demonstrably violated or contradicted.

INDETERMINATE — Evidence is insufficient for a defensible governance determination.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed requirement does not apply.

A governance PASS does not necessarily mean applicability remained continuous.

A correctly documented INTERRUPTED or EXPIRED state may receive PASS.

An unsupported CONTINUOUS determination may receive FAIL even when the underlying acceptance decision is valid.

154. Non-Bypassable Governance Rules

The following rules apply throughout ACCM:

R1. An unchanged acceptance decision must not automatically establish unchanged applicability.

R2. Material applicability changes must not be silently absorbed into existing determinations.

R3. A new object version must not automatically inherit applicability from the previous version.

R4. Authority changes must not automatically establish authority continuity.

R5. Scope changes must not become effective without the applicable authority basis.

R6. New evidence must not be represented as historically available before its actual availability.

R7. Reassessment must not silently overwrite previous determinations.

R8. Suspension, expiration, and reactivation must remain distinct historical states.

R9. Restriction changes must not erase prior restrictions.

R10. Unknown continuity must not automatically be treated as uninterrupted continuity.

R11. Historical applicability records must remain reconstructable across material changes.

R12. Historical continuity verification must not independently grant operational authorization.

These rules implement the ACCM change and continuity boundary and do not replace the architecture-wide normative rules of DWAGA™.

156. Canonical Closing Statement

Acceptance applicability is not a permanent property of a document or delivered object. It is a governed relationship whose legitimacy must remain traceable across change and time.

The original scope must remain identifiable.

Every material change must be recorded.

The change source must remain traceable.

The applicable authority must be preserved.

The affected context must be evaluated.

Material differences must remain visible.

Conditions and restrictions must remain enforceable within the applicable governance framework.

Required reassessments must be completed.

Evidence updates must preserve provenance.

Contradictory evidence must not disappear.

Suspensions must remain historically visible.

Expirations must not erase past applicability.

Reactivations must preserve their preceding states.

Later determinations must not silently rewrite earlier findings.

Historical authority and scope must remain reconstructable.

ACCM preserves these distinctions.

As the fifth Core Module of DWAAS — Delegated Work Acceptance Applicability Standard, ACCM establishes the historical continuity boundary through which every material change in acceptance applicability remains identifiable, attributable, evidence-supported, time-referenced, and independently reviewable.

It ensures that acceptance applicability can evolve without losing the integrity of the governance record.

The purpose of ACCM is not to prevent legitimate change. Its purpose is to ensure that every change preserves the evidence, authority, scope, and historical truth necessary to determine what acceptance legitimately applied at any given time.

Applicability May Change—Its History Must Never Disappear.
