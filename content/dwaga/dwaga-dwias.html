1. Canonical Definition

DWIAS — Delegated Work Intent Alignment Standard is the third Parent Standard within DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

DWIAS establishes the methodological requirements for identifying, interpreting, comparing, verifying, and historically preserving the alignment between the intent under which work was delegated and the delivered-work result proposed for acceptance.

It governs the relationship between the originating delegated intent, the work that was actually produced and delivered, the declared acceptance purpose, the recipient's interpretation, and the evidence supporting any claim that the delivered result corresponds to the intended objective.

DWIAS recognizes that a delegated-work result may be technically complete, correctly delivered, and suitable for a newly proposed purpose while remaining materially different from what was originally delegated.

It also recognizes that a result may differ from the original instructions in ways that are legitimate, authorized, or immaterial.

Accordingly, DWIAS does not require literal correspondence between every instruction and every implementation detail.

It requires a defensible determination of whether the delivered result preserves the applicable delegated intent, whether material deviations have been identified, and whether any changes to that intent are supported by appropriate evidence and authority references.

DWIAS distinguishes:

Delegated Intent

Delivered Work

Declared Acceptance Purpose

Recipient Interpretation

Intent Alignment

Acceptance Decision

These are related but independent governance objects.

The principal governance sequence is:

Delegated Intent → Intent Interpretation → Delivered Work Reference → Acceptance Purpose Reference → Alignment Criteria → Deviation Assessment → Evidence Verification → Governance Determination → Historical Continuity

The principal output is the Delegated Work Intent Alignment Profile.

2. Architectural Position

DWIAS operates after:

DWHS — Delegated Work Handover Standard

and

DWAPS — Delegated Work Acceptance Purpose Standard.

DWHS establishes what was delivered.

DWAPS establishes the purpose for which the delivered result is proposed to be accepted or relied upon.

DWIAS evaluates whether that result and proposed purpose remain aligned with the intent under which the work was delegated.

DWIAS does not independently establish whether all acceptance prerequisites have been satisfied or whether acceptance should be granted.

Those questions are governed by subsequent DWAGA™ Parent Standards.

3. Core Question

Does the delivered-work result proposed for acceptance correspond to the identifiable and applicable intent of the original delegation, including its objectives, scope, constraints, permitted interpretations, and authorized changes, and can that alignment be demonstrated through evidence without confusing technical completion, recipient satisfaction, or acceptance purpose with delegated intent?

4. Foundational Principle

Delegated Work Must Be Evaluated Against the Intent It Was Authorized to Fulfill, Not Merely Against the Result It Happened to Produce.

The following distinctions apply:

Task Completed ≠ Delegated Intent Satisfied.

Delivered Result ≠ Intended Result.

Literal Instruction Compliance ≠ Substantive Intent Alignment.

Recipient Satisfaction ≠ Delegated Intent Alignment.

Technical Validity ≠ Intent Alignment.

Purpose Compatibility ≠ Intent Equivalence.

Intent Interpretation ≠ Intent Modification.

Intent Deviation ≠ Automatically Unauthorized Deviation.

Intent Alignment Verified ≠ Work Accepted.

Acceptance for a New Purpose ≠ Retrospective Satisfaction of the Original Delegation.

5. Governance Objective

DWIAS aims to establish a consistent methodological basis for determining whether delegated work corresponds to the intent governing its production.

Its objectives include:

  1. Preserve identifiable delegated intent.
  2. Distinguish intent from task instructions and output descriptions.
  3. Establish evidence-supported interpretation.
  4. Bind intent to the relevant delivered work.
  5. Bind intent to the applicable acceptance purpose.
  6. Identify material deviations and substitutions.
  7. Distinguish legitimate adaptation from unauthorized intent change.
  8. Preserve conflicts and uncertainty.
  9. Support bounded alignment determinations.
  10. Maintain historical intent continuity.

10. Intent Versus Instructions

Instructions describe actions, methods, or requirements.

Intent describes the objective and substantive meaning governing the delegated work.

An instruction may be fulfilled literally while the underlying objective remains unsatisfied.

Conversely, an alternative method may satisfy the delegated intent when that alternative is permitted by the applicable delegation.

DWIAS requires both distinctions to remain assessable.

11. Intent Versus Output Specification

An output specification describes the expected characteristics of a result.

Delegated intent may include broader objectives or restrictions not fully expressed in the output specification.

DWIAS must not assume that specification conformity alone establishes complete intent alignment.

39. Objective Alignment

40. Outcome Alignment

41. Scope Alignment

Scope alignment evaluates whether the delivered result remains within the applicable delegated-work scope.

67. Confidence Classification

DWIAS applies:

Confirmed — Sufficient applicable evidence supports the alignment determination.

Reconciled — The determination is supported through documented, justified reconciliation.

Estimated — The determination depends materially on assumptions or indirect evidence.

Conflicted — Material evidence supports incompatible alignment conclusions.

Unknown — Available evidence is insufficient.

Confidence classification does not replace the governance determination.

68. Governance Determination

DWIAS applies:

PASS — Applicable delegated-intent identification, interpretation, alignment, and evidence requirements are satisfied.

CONDITIONAL — Alignment is supportable only under explicit limitations or unresolved conditions.

FAIL — A material intent-alignment governance requirement is demonstrably violated or contradicted.

INDETERMINATE — Evidence is insufficient for a defensible determination.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed requirement does not apply.

A verified material deviation may justify FAIL.

An unresolved original intent may justify INDETERMINATE.

A documented authorized adaptation may remain compatible with PASS when applicable criteria are satisfied.

DWIAS PASS establishes only the bounded intent-alignment determination. It does not establish technical validation, acceptance, or operational authorization.

74. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum DWIAS implementation establishes eight governance layers.

Layer 1 — Intent Identity

Register the delegated intent, its source, objective, scope, and relevant constraints.

Layer 2 — Interpretation

Preserve explicit declarations, contextual interpretations, assumptions, and uncertainty.

Layer 3 — Work & Purpose Binding

Bind the delegated intent to the delivered-work result, handover event, and proposed acceptance purpose.

Layer 4 — Alignment Criteria

1. What Is DWIAS?

DWIAS — Delegated Work Intent Alignment Standard is the third Parent Standard within DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

DWIAS establishes a structured methodology for determining whether a delegated-work result corresponds to the original intent governing its production.

It evaluates the relationship between the objective that was delegated, the instructions and constraints associated with that objective, the work actually delivered, and the purpose for which the recipient proposes to accept or rely upon the result.

DWIAS addresses a fundamental governance distinction:

Producing a successful result does not necessarily mean producing the result that was requested or authorized.

A result may be technically correct, complete, useful, or commercially valuable while failing to satisfy the original delegated objective.

Conversely, a result may differ from the original implementation instructions while remaining aligned with the delegated intent when the variation is permitted and adequately supported.

DWIAS provides the methodological foundation for evaluating these differences without automatically treating every deviation as a failure.

2. Why DWIAS Exists

Delegated work increasingly involves complex chains of human decisions, software processes, AI agents, automated systems, and external service providers.

Within these chains, the meaning of the original delegation can become distorted.

A task may be marked complete because a workflow has finished.

An AI agent may optimize a measurable target rather than the actual objective.

A contractor may deliver an alternative solution that appears technically superior but violates an important project constraint.

A recipient may accept a result for a new purpose and incorrectly assume that the original delegation has therefore been satisfied.

Traditional completion records do not necessarily resolve these questions.

DWIAS exists to establish a separate, evidence-supported assessment of whether the delivered result remains faithful to the applicable delegated intent.

3. Core Question

Does the delivered-work result correspond to the identifiable and applicable intent of the original delegation, including its objectives, expected outcomes, scope, constraints, permitted interpretations, and authorized changes?

DWIAS requires this question to be evaluated independently of technical completion, recipient satisfaction, or the acceptance decision.

4. Foundational Principle

Delegated Work Must Be Evaluated Against the Intent It Was Authorized to Fulfill, Not Merely Against the Result It Happened to Produce.

Key distinctions include:

Task Completed ≠ Delegated Intent Satisfied.

Technical Correctness ≠ Intent Alignment.

Recipient Satisfaction ≠ Original Intent Fulfilled.

Literal Instruction Compliance ≠ Substantive Objective Alignment.

Permitted Adaptation ≠ Unauthorized Substitution.

Acceptance for a New Purpose ≠ Satisfaction of the Original Delegation.

5. What DWIAS Governs

DWIAS governs the methodological relationships necessary to assess delegated-intent alignment.

Its principal governance areas include:

  • Identification of the original delegated intent.
  • Identification of objectives and expected outcomes.
  • Preservation of scope, constraints, and assumptions.
  • Interpretation of explicit and implicit intent.
  • Relationships between intent, delivered work, and acceptance purpose.
  • Objective, outcome, scope, and constraint correspondence.
  • Identification of material deviations and substitutions.
  • Evaluation of permitted adaptations.
  • Reconciliation of conflicting interpretations.
  • Verification of supporting evidence.
  • Preservation of changes and historical determinations.

These elements form the Delegated Work Intent Alignment Profile.

6. What DWIAS Does Not Govern

DWIAS does not independently:

  • Establish the technical correctness of delivered work.
  • Certify professional competence or product safety.
  • Determine whether all acceptance prerequisites are satisfied.
  • Grant acceptance of a delivered result.
  • Establish operational or deployment authorization.
  • Determine ownership or licensing rights.
  • Issue legal judgments or determine liability.
  • Replace the responsibilities of other DWAGA™ Parent Standards.

An intent-alignment PASS is a bounded methodological finding, not unrestricted approval of the delivered work.

8. How DWIAS Works

DWIAS follows a structured governance sequence:

Identify Intent → Interpret Intent → Bind Delivered Work → Evaluate Correspondence → Assess Deviations → Verify Evidence → Determine Alignment → Preserve History

Each stage addresses a distinct question.

The original intent must be identifiable before meaningful interpretation.

The delivered result must be identifiable before correspondence can be assessed.

Material deviations must be evaluated before a defensible alignment determination is issued.

Historical records must remain available when the intent or delivered work changes.

This sequence prevents a completion status or acceptance-purpose declaration from replacing an actual intent-alignment assessment.

9. Delegated Intent Versus Task Instructions

Delegated intent and task instructions are related but not identical.

Instructions may specify the steps or methods through which an objective should be achieved.

Intent establishes the substantive objective and applicable meaning of the delegation.

A worker or AI agent may follow instructions literally while failing to achieve the intended result.

An alternative method may satisfy the intent when the delegation permits that flexibility.

DWIAS therefore evaluates substantive correspondence rather than assuming that literal compliance always establishes alignment.

10. Objective & Outcome Alignment

DWIAS distinguishes the objective of delegated work from the outcome actually delivered.

The objective describes what the delegation sought to achieve.

The delivered outcome describes what was produced or accomplished.

Alignment requires evidence supporting the relationship between the two.

A completed workflow does not independently demonstrate that the intended outcome was achieved.

11. Scope, Constraints & Assumptions

Delegated intent may include limitations beyond the primary objective.

These may concern technical requirements, safety-related constraints, resources, operating conditions, permitted methods, or organizational responsibilities.

DWIAS evaluates whether material constraints remain respected.

It also preserves assumptions that affect interpretation.

A result that achieves the primary objective while violating an applicable material constraint may fail the alignment assessment.

12. Intent Interpretation & Ambiguity

Delegated intent is not always perfectly expressed.

Instructions may be incomplete, contradictory, or open to several reasonable interpretations.

DWIAS requires material interpretations to remain distinguishable from the original source declaration.

Where uncertainty cannot be resolved, the assessment must preserve that uncertainty rather than inventing a definitive original intent.

This is particularly important when AI systems generate interpretations or reformulate goals.

13. Intent Alignment & Permitted Adaptation

DWIAS does not require every delivered result to reproduce the originally expected implementation method.

A permitted alternative may achieve the delegated objective more effectively.

However, an adaptation must remain consistent with the applicable intent and constraints.

Where a material change requires authorization, the relevant authority references must be identifiable.

A Better-Looking Result Is Not Automatically an Authorized Result.

14. Intent Deviation & Substitution

A deviation occurs when the delivered work differs from the applicable delegated intent.

A substitution occurs when a materially different objective replaces the original objective.

DWIAS distinguishes material deviations from insignificant implementation differences.

It also evaluates whether a deviation was permitted, appropriately documented, or remains unresolved.

An unauthorized substitution must not be presented as successful fulfillment of the original delegation merely because the new result is useful.

15. AI-Agent Goal Interpretation

AI agents may interpret tasks, infer objectives, select methods, and optimize measurable targets.

These capabilities introduce a distinct governance challenge.

An agent may successfully optimize a proxy metric while failing to satisfy the underlying objective.

It may also reinterpret a task without preserving the original instruction or identifying the change.

DWIAS requires material goal interpretations, substitutions, and resulting deviations to remain traceable.

Optimization Success ≠ Delegated Objective Satisfaction.

DIIDM does not independently decide which interpretation is correct, whether the delivered result satisfies the intent, whether a deviation is justified, or whether the work should be accepted.

65. Governance Determination

DIIDM applies:

PASS — Applicable intent-identification, source, binding, and evidence requirements are satisfied.

CONDITIONAL — The identification is supportable only under explicit limitations or unresolved conditions.

FAIL — A material intent-identification governance requirement is demonstrably violated or contradicted.

INDETERMINATE — Evidence is insufficient for a defensible identification determination.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed requirement does not apply.

A confirmed identifier collision may justify FAIL.

A missing original declaration may justify INDETERMINATE.

A supported identity with limited contextual uncertainty may justify CONDITIONAL where applicable criteria permit it.

DIIDM PASS establishes only the bounded intent-identification determination.

1. Canonical Definition

DIIM — Delegated Intent Interpretation Module is the second Core Module within DWIAS — Delegated Work Intent Alignment Standard, operating under DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

DIIM establishes the methodological requirements for interpreting, distinguishing, documenting, evaluating, verifying, and historically preserving the meaning attributed to an identifiable delegated intent.

It governs the relationship between an original intent declaration, its source, the applicable delegation context, the interpretation produced by a human or system, the assumptions and evidence supporting that interpretation, and the uncertainty or conflicts associated with the resulting meaning.

DIIM recognizes that delegated intent is not always fully expressed through literal instructions.

A delegation may contain incomplete objectives, implicit constraints, contextual dependencies, technical terminology, conditional requirements, or multiple reasonable interpretations.

A human recipient may understand an instruction differently from its originator.

An AI agent may reformulate the original objective into an operational task.

A multi-agent system may progressively alter the meaning of a delegation as instructions are transmitted between agents.

An organizational workflow may substitute a standardized purpose label for a more specific original objective.

DIIM requires these interpretation relationships to remain identifiable and evidence-supported.

It does not automatically treat every difference in wording as a substantive difference in meaning.

It also does not allow semantic similarity, plausible inference, or successful task execution to substitute for evidence of the applicable delegated intent.

DIIM distinguishes interpretation from modification.

It does not independently establish whether the delivered work satisfies the interpreted intent, whether a deviation is legitimate, whether an interpretation is legally authoritative, or whether the work should be accepted.

Its principal governance sequence is:

Identified Intent → Original Declaration → Contextual Evidence → Interpretation Candidate → Assumptions & Alternatives → Meaning Assessment → Conflict & Uncertainty Assessment → Verification → Historical Preservation

Its principal output is the Delegated Intent Interpretation Profile.

1. Canonical Definition

IACM — Intent Alignment & Correspondence Module is the third Core Module within DWIAS — Delegated Work Intent Alignment Standard, operating under DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

IACM establishes the methodological requirements for identifying, evaluating, verifying, and historically preserving the correspondence between an identifiable delegated intent and the delivered-work result associated with that intent.

It governs whether the delivered result corresponds to the applicable objectives, expected outcomes, scope, constraints, and permitted variations of the original delegation, using explicit alignment criteria and supporting evidence.

IACM recognizes that correspondence cannot be established solely through task completion, successful execution, technical validity, recipient satisfaction, or the existence of a delivered artifact.

A result may be technically functional but address the wrong objective.

A result may satisfy the principal objective while omitting a material secondary requirement.

A result may achieve an intended outcome through a different method that remains permissible under the delegation.

A result may satisfy the original objective but be proposed for acceptance under a materially different purpose.

A result may appear aligned while its supporting evidence applies to another object version, project context, or delegation.

IACM therefore requires correspondence to be evaluated against an identifiable and applicable intent baseline rather than inferred from the apparent usefulness of the result.

It distinguishes objective correspondence, outcome correspondence, scope correspondence, constraint correspondence, and method flexibility.

It also preserves partial alignment, unresolved gaps, conflicting evidence, and limitations.

IACM does not independently redefine the delegated intent, approve deviations, certify technical correctness, establish acceptance prerequisites, grant acceptance, or authorize operational execution.

Its principal governance sequence is:

Identified Intent → Applicable Interpretation → Delivered Work Identity → Alignment Criteria → Objective & Outcome Correspondence → Scope & Constraint Correspondence → Evidence Verification → Alignment Determination → Historical Preservation

Its principal output is the Intent Alignment & Correspondence Profile.

1. Canonical Definition

IDRM — Intent Deviation & Reconciliation Module is the fourth Core Module within DWIAS — Delegated Work Intent Alignment Standard, operating under DWAGA™ — Delegated Work Acceptance Governance Architecture.

IDRM establishes the methodological requirements for identifying, classifying, evaluating, reconciling, verifying, and historically preserving material deviations between the applicable delegated intent and the delivered-work result.

It governs differences involving objectives, expected outcomes, scope, constraints, methods, interpretations, substitutions, and changes in the meaning or direction of delegated work.

IDRM recognizes that a difference between an original delegation and a delivered result does not automatically constitute a governance failure.

Some differences represent permitted implementation flexibility.

Others reflect legitimate adaptations supported by applicable authority and evidence.

Some deviations remain unresolved because their materiality, origin, or authorization cannot be established.

Other deviations demonstrate that the delivered result no longer corresponds to the objective under which the work was delegated.

IDRM requires these states to remain distinguishable.

A deviation must not be automatically classified as authorized because the delivered result is useful.

A reconciliation must not be treated as valid merely because the recipient agrees with the outcome.

A later acceptance purpose must not retrospectively transform an unsupported deviation into fulfillment of the original delegation.

An AI-generated interpretation or optimization result must not silently replace the delegated objective.

IDRM distinguishes the identification of a deviation from its evaluation, the proposal of reconciliation from its verification, and the resolution of a deviation from authorization to accept or use the resulting work.

It does not independently redefine delegated intent, grant modification authority, certify technical correctness, establish acceptance prerequisites, issue acceptance decisions, or determine legal liability.

84. Governance Determination

ICCM applies:

PASS — Applicable intent-change identity, classification, authority-reference, temporal, evidence, and continuity requirements are satisfied within the assessed scope.

CONDITIONAL — The change-and-continuity assessment is supportable only under explicit limitations or unresolved conditions.

FAIL — A material change-governance requirement is demonstrably violated or contradicted.

INDETERMINATE — Evidence is insufficient for a defensible determination.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed requirement does not apply.

A demonstrated silent overwrite of an original intent state may justify FAIL.

A change with an unresolved effective time may justify INDETERMINATE or CONDITIONAL according to the applicable criteria.

A documented, appropriately authorized prospective modification with preserved historical continuity may support PASS.

ICCM PASS establishes only the bounded intent-change-and-continuity governance determination.

It does not independently establish delivered-work alignment, acceptance, or operational authorization.

Preserve original and revised intent states, change events, evidence, reassessments, and historical determinations in reconstructable form.

103. Canonical Closing Statement

Delegated intent may evolve, but its history must not be rewritten to make a later result appear consistent with an earlier objective.

A clarification must remain distinguishable from a modification.

A proposal must remain distinguishable from an approval.

An approval must remain distinguishable from its effective time.

A new intent state must remain distinguishable from the original delegation.

A later acceptance purpose must not automatically replace the original objective.

A reassessment must preserve the determination and evidence state that existed before the reassessment.

ICCM establishes the methodological structure necessary to govern these distinctions.

As the fifth Core Module of DWIAS — Delegated Work Intent Alignment Standard, ICCM completes the intent-governance lifecycle by preserving the relationship between original objectives, subsequent changes, delivered results, reassessments, and historical determinations.

It ensures that legitimate change remains possible without sacrificing the integrity of earlier governance states.

ICCM ensures that every material change to delegated intent can be traced to its source, evaluated against applicable authority and evidence, applied within a defensible temporal boundary, and reconstructed without erasing the original delegation.
