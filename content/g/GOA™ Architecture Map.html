GOA™ Architecture Map

Governance Architecture

Official Governance Reference Map for Organizations, Governments,
Institutions, AI Systems, Autonomous Systems, Human-AI Environments,
and Multi-Organization Governance Ecosystems.

GOA™ serves as the official governance reference map used to identify,
classify, navigate, validate, and understand governance spaces
across organizations, governments, institutions, AI systems, autonomous
systems, Human-AI environments, and future governance ecosystems.

The purpose of GOA™ is not to govern organizations.

The purpose of GOA™ is to identify, define, and map
the governable governance spaces of complex systems.

GOA™ serves as the official governance reference map for:
  • Organizations
  • Governments
  • Institutions
  • Enterprises
  • AI Systems
  • Autonomous Systems
  • Human-AI Environments
  • Multi-Agent Ecosystems
  • Multi-Organization Governance
  • Future Governance Ecosystems

Core Architectural Principle

Every governance space answers one primary question.

No governance space duplicates the purpose of another governance space.

Every governance space begins where the previous governance space ends.

Together these governance spaces form the official governance
reference map of GOA™.

Official Governance Flow

Authority → Boundary → Scope → Delegation → Accountability →
Validation → Integrity → Transparency → Continuity → Interoperability

1. Governance Authority Standard (GAS)

Governed Space: Governance Authority

Core Question
Who has the legitimate authority to govern?

Canonical Definition
GAS defines the structural conditions under which governance authority
becomes legitimate, attributable, verifiable, and governable.

Governance Boundary
Begins when governance authority is required.
Ends when legitimate governance authority has been established.

2. Governance Boundary Standard (GBS)

Governed Space: Governance Boundaries

Core Question
Where are the governance boundaries?

Canonical Definition
GBS defines the structural conditions under which governance boundaries
remain identifiable, enforceable, coordinated, and governable.

Governance Boundary
Begins when governance authority requires defined limits.
Ends when governance boundaries have been established.

3. Governance Scope Standard (GSS)

Governed Space: Governance Scope

Core Question
What falls within governance?

Canonical Definition
GSS defines the structural conditions under which governance scope
remains clearly defined, measurable, governable, and operationally valid.

Governance Boundary
Begins when governance boundaries have been established.
Ends when governance scope has been clearly defined.

4. Governance Delegation Standard (GDS)

Governed Space: Governance Delegation

Core Question
How may governance authority be delegated?

Canonical Definition
GDS defines the structural conditions under which governance authority
may be delegated while preserving legitimacy, accountability,
traceability, and governance integrity.

Governance Boundary
Begins when governance authority is transferred.
Ends when delegated governance authority has been validated.

5. Governance Accountability Standard (GAS-A)

Governed Space: Governance Accountability

Core Question
Who remains accountable for governance outcomes?

Canonical Definition
GAS-A defines the structural conditions under which governance accountability
remains attributable, continuous, demonstrable, and governable.

Governance Boundary
Begins when governance responsibilities are accepted.
Ends when governance accountability can be continuously demonstrated.

6. Governance Validation Standard (GVS)

Governed Space: Governance Validation

Core Question
How is governance validated?

Canonical Definition
GVS defines the structural conditions under which governance remains
verifiable, measurable, evidence-based, and operationally valid.

Governance Boundary
Begins when governance activities require verification.
Ends when governance validity has been demonstrated.

7. Governance Integrity Standard (GIS)

Governed Space: Governance Integrity

Core Question
How is governance protected from manipulation?

Canonical Definition
GIS defines the structural conditions under which governance remains
protected, trustworthy, consistent, and resistant to manipulation
or corruption.

Governance Boundary
Begins when governance integrity becomes vulnerable.
Ends when governance integrity has been preserved.

8. Governance Transparency Standard (GTS)

Governed Space: Governance Transparency

Core Question
How visible and understandable must governance be?

Canonical Definition
GTS defines the structural conditions under which governance remains
visible, explainable, documented, traceable, and understandable.

Governance Boundary
Begins when governance information must be communicated.
Ends when governance can be independently understood and reviewed.

9. Governance Continuity Standard (GCS)

Governed Space: Governance Continuity

Core Question
How does governance remain functional over time?

Canonical Definition
GCS defines the structural conditions under which governance remains
continuous, resilient, recoverable, transferable, and operationally
sustainable throughout change.

Governance Boundary
Begins when governance must survive organizational or operational change.
Ends when governance continuity has been preserved.

10. Governance Interoperability Standard (GOIS)

Governed Space: Governance Interoperability

Core Question
How do multiple governance systems operate together?

Canonical Definition
GOIS defines the structural conditions under which multiple governance
systems remain compatible, coordinated, aligned, translatable,
interoperable, and operationally trustworthy across complex ecosystems.

Governance Boundary
Begins when two or more governance systems interact.
Ends when governance interoperability has been successfully established.

Together these governance spaces govern how governance is established,

AI systems, autonomous systems, Human-AI environments,
and future governance ecosystems.
