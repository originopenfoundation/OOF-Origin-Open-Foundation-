Canonical Definition System

Canonical Definition

Behavioral Evidence Availability Module (BEAvM) defines the structural
conditions under which AI behavioral evidence remains accessible, retrievable,
usable, governance-ready, and available to authorized governance participants
throughout the complete behavioral lifecycle.

BEAM governs behavioral evidence availability.

The module establishes the governance conditions required to ensure that
trustworthy evidence is available whenever legitimate governance, regulatory,
compliance, accountability, operational, or investigative activities require
it.

Evidence may be preserved.

Evidence must also remain available.

BEAM governs that availability.

Module Operational Role

BEAM defines the behavioral-evidence-availability layer of BVES.

Its role is to ensure continuous availability of governance-valid behavioral
evidence.

Module Operational Space

BEAM governs:

  • behavioral evidence availability
  • evidence accessibility
  • governance evidence access
  • evidence retrieval
  • evidence readiness
  • authorized evidence access
  • governance information availability
  • evidence availability governance


The module applies wherever AI behavioral evidence must remain available to
authorized governance participants.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • accessible behavioral evidence,
  • retrievable governance records,
  • governance-ready evidence,
  • authorized evidence availability,
  • operational evidence access,
  • trustworthy governance information.


Its function is to ensure that preserved evidence can be accessed when
governance decisions, audits, investigations, or regulatory reviews require
it.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Behavioral Evidence Availability Object

The organization must define which behavioral evidence must remain
continuously available.

2. Define Availability Conditions

The system must define conditions for:

  • authorized access,
  • retrieval capability,
  • governance readiness,
  • operational availability,
  • evidence accessibility,
  • availability validation.


3. Define Availability Failure Detection Logic

The system must identify:

  • unavailable evidence,
  • inaccessible records,
  • retrieval failures,
  • delayed evidence access,
  • governance access failures,
  • governance-invalid evidence availability.


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

Governance response may include:

  • availability review,
  • evidence recovery,
  • access validation,
  • governance intervention,
  • compliance review,
  • operational restoration.


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • evidence access,
  • retrieval activities,
  • governance reviews,
  • authorization records,
  • availability validations,
  • resulting governance outcomes.


An AI behavioral environment must not remain evidence-valid if materially
significant behavioral evidence cannot be accessed, retrieved, reviewed,
validated, preserved, or governed by authorized governance participants.

Use Case 1 — Financial Regulatory Audit

Scenario

A financial regulator requests immediate access to behavioral evidence
supporting AI-driven investment decisions.

Application

BEAM ensures that authorized auditors can retrieve complete governance
evidence without delay.

Result

The organization strengthens regulatory compliance, improves governance
transparency, and increases confidence in AI-supported financial operations.

Use Case 2 — National Critical Infrastructure

Scenario

Following an operational incident, investigators require immediate access to
AI behavioral evidence generated across multiple autonomous systems.

Application

BEAM ensures that authorized investigators can retrieve governance-valid
evidence quickly and consistently.

Result

The organization accelerates investigations, strengthens operational
resilience, supports accountability, and improves trust in AI governance.

Canonical Closing Statement

Evidence has governance value only when it can be used. Availability ensures
that trustworthy evidence remains within reach whenever organizations need to
understand the past, govern the present, or make responsible decisions about
the future.
