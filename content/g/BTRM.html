Canonical Definition System

Canonical Definition

Behavioral Trust Recovery Module (BTRM) defines the structural conditions
under which lost or degraded trust in AI behavior can be systematically
restored through governance validation, behavioral correction, accountable
operation, transparent evidence, and sustained demonstration of trustworthy
performance.

BTRM governs behavioral trust recovery.

The module establishes the governance conditions required to ensure that trust
recovery follows objective governance evidence rather than assumptions,
declarations, or temporary improvements.

Trust may be damaged.

Recovery must be earned.

BTRM governs the path from lost confidence to renewed trust.

Module Operational Role

BTRM defines the behavioral-trust-recovery layer of BTS.

Its role is to restore justified trust after behavioral failures, governance
violations, or operational degradation.

Module Operational Space

BTRM governs:

  • trust recovery
  • confidence restoration
  • governance rehabilitation
  • behavioral trust rebuilding
  • post-incident trust
  • trust revalidation
  • governance recovery
  • recovery governance


The module applies wherever organizations must restore confidence in AI
behavior following significant governance events.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • structured trust recovery,
  • governance-based rehabilitation,
  • evidence-supported confidence,
  • accountable behavioral improvement,
  • transparent recovery processes,
  • sustainable trust restoration.


Its function is to ensure that recovered trust is supported by demonstrated
behavioral improvement rather than unsupported reassurance.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Trust Recovery Object

The organization must define which trust failures require formal recovery
governance.

2. Define Trust Recovery Conditions

The system must define governance conditions including:

  • behavioral correction,
  • governance validation,
  • accountability restoration,
  • evidence verification,
  • transparency,
  • sustained behavioral improvement.


3. Define Recovery Validation Logic

The system must identify:

  • successful recovery,
  • incomplete recovery,
  • recurring failures,
  • unresolved governance issues,
  • insufficient evidence,
  • trust-invalid recovery conditions.


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

Governance response may include:

  • recovery assessment,
  • governance review,
  • additional validation,
  • operational supervision,
  • staged restoration,
  • corrective governance actions.


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • recovery activities,
  • governance reviews,
  • behavioral improvements,
  • validation evidence,
  • restoration decisions,
  • resulting trust status.


An AI behavioral environment must not be considered trust-restored unless
recovery can be independently demonstrated, reviewed, validated, preserved,
and governed.

Use Case 1 — AI Healthcare Assistant

Scenario

An AI clinical assistant loses organizational trust after producing a series
of incorrect recommendations.

Application

BTRM governs the structured recovery process through behavioral correction,
governance validation, clinical review, and demonstrated operational
improvement.

Result

The healthcare organization restores confidence using objective governance
evidence rather than assumptions.

Use Case 2 — Autonomous Financial Trading System

Scenario

A financial AI temporarily loses stakeholder confidence after governance
failures affecting trading decisions.

Application

BTRM governs structured trust restoration through validated behavioral
improvements, transparent governance oversight, and continuous operational
verification.

Result

The financial institution rebuilds regulatory confidence, strengthens
governance credibility, and restores sustainable organizational trust.

Canonical Closing Statement

Trust cannot be restored by promises alone. Lasting confidence returns only
when improved behavior is consistently demonstrated, independently verified,
and repeatedly confirmed through responsible governance.
