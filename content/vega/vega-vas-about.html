What Is This Standard?

The Value Attribution Standard (VAS) is the VEGA™ Parent Standard for governing how materially relevant contributions to validated Value are identified, evidenced, differentiated, and attributed.

It provides a structured methodology for tracing Value backward to the human, AI, agent, model, tool, data, infrastructure, methodology, and operational contributions that materially helped produce, preserve, or validate it.

VAS does not assume that participation equals contribution or that every contributor contributed equally.

Its purpose is to make the formation of Value traceable enough to support evidence-based attribution without creating artificial precision.

Why Does This Standard Exist?

Modern intelligence systems rarely create Value through one identifiable contributor.

A business outcome may involve:

human expertise,

AI reasoning,

multiple agents,

models,

tools,

data,

software,

infrastructure,

validation,

and human correction.

Traditional attribution can become unreliable when it relies on job titles, system ownership, activity volume, compute consumption, or the visibility of the final output.

VAS instead begins with the governed Value and asks:

What materially contributed to producing this Value, and what evidence supports that conclusion?

This creates a methodological bridge between Value creation and the contributors that materially enabled it.

What This Standard Is Not

VAS is not a legal ownership framework.

It is not an intellectual-property allocation system.

It is not a compensation methodology.

It does not determine salaries, royalties, bonuses, commissions, or revenue shares.

It is not a tax-attribution or transfer-pricing methodology.

It does not determine Accountability, Responsibility, Harm, or Liability.

And it does not grant authority merely because a human, AI system, agent, model, or other mechanism contributed to Value.

VAS governs:

Methodological Value Attribution.

How It Works

VAS follows the governance flow:

Value Source Identification → Contribution Attribution → Human–AI Attribution → Multi-Agent Attribution → Attribution Evidence

The process begins with a sufficiently defined and validated Value object.

It then identifies the sources and contributors connected to that Value.

Their participation is tested for material contribution.

Where necessary, VAS distinguishes:

direct and indirect contribution,

enabling and operational contribution,

human and AI contribution,

individual and joint contribution,

positive and negative contribution,

production and validation contribution,

and observed evidence from counterfactual estimates.

Contribution may then be represented through Contribution Events, Chains, or Networks.

Evidence, provenance, uncertainty, confidence, overlap, and attribution conflicts remain attached to the resulting attribution state.

VAS therefore does not require every contributor to receive an artificial percentage.

Where the evidence supports precision, attribution may become more precise.

Where it does not, uncertainty remains visible.

Principal Governance Output

The principal output of VAS is:

Governed Value Attribution State

This state can identify:

what Value was attributed,

which contributors materially contributed,

what type of contribution occurred,

how contributions interacted,

where contribution was joint or overlapping,

what evidence supports the attribution,

how confident the attribution is,

and where uncertainty or conflict remains.

The output creates a traceable relationship:

Validated Value ↔ Value Source ↔ Contribution ↔ Contributor ↔ Contribution Event ↔ Evidence ↔ Attribution Conclusion

Use Case 1 — Human and AI Both Claim the Value

A professional uses an advanced AI system to produce an important business analysis.

The AI performs most of the research, synthesis, and initial reasoning.

The human defines the problem, selects the relevant evidence, identifies a critical error, changes the final recommendation, and validates the result before operational use.

The final analysis produces substantial validated Value.

Simply labeling the result “AI-generated” would ignore material human contribution.

Labeling it “human-created” would ignore substantial AI contribution.

VAS reconstructs the contribution structure.

It identifies:

the human problem-definition contribution,

the AI analytical contribution,

the human correction,

the validation contribution,

and the interaction through which the final Value emerged.

The result may therefore be represented as a governed Human–AI contribution rather than forcing the Value into a single- source attribution.

Human–AI Value can be jointly formed without being equally formed.

Use Case 2 — Ten Agents Participate, but Not All Create Value

An organization deploys a multi-agent system containing specialized agents for:

planning,

research,

retrieval,

analysis,

critique,

coordination,

validation,

and execution.

Ten agents participate in the workflow.

Execution logs show that several agents repeatedly produce information that does not materially affect the final outcome.

One research agent discovers the decisive evidence.

A reasoning agent transforms that evidence into the central conclusion.

A validation agent detects a material error.

A coordination mechanism routes the correction back into the workflow.

The final result produces validated Value.

VAS does not attribute Value equally across all ten agents merely because all participated.

It identifies which contributions materially affected the Value formation process and preserves the supporting evidence.

This can reveal both genuine contribution and unnecessary complexity.

More Agents ≠ More Value Contribution.

Canonical Closing Statement

Value may be visible long before its origin is understood.

In modern intelligence systems, the final outcome may emerge from humans, models, agents, tools, data, infrastructure, validation, correction, and coordination acting together.

Governed attribution cannot therefore begin with who owns the system, who consumed the most resources, who produced the most output, or who appeared last in the workflow.

It must begin with the Value.

Then it must follow the evidence backward.

Participation ≠ Value Contribution.

Productivity ≠ Value Attribution.

Resource Consumption ≠ Value Contribution.

Joint Contribution ≠ Equal Contribution by default.

Attribution Precision must not exceed Evidence Precision.

The purpose of VAS is not to assign credit by assumption.

It is to make material contribution visible enough to be identified, evidenced, differentiated, challenged, and governed.

VAS makes the path from Value to contribution traceable.
