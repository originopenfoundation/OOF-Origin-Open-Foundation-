What Is This Standard?

The Intelligence Sustainability Standard (ISS) is the VEGA™ Parent Standard for governing whether an intelligence operating model can remain viable, proportionate, supportable, and sufficiently resilient across its intended operational lifecycle.

An intelligent system may work today, create Value today, and even be economically attractive today while depending on operating conditions that cannot remain viable over time.

ISS therefore extends the governance question beyond current performance.

It asks:

Can this intelligence operating model remain viable and proportionate over its intended lifecycle?

Why Does This Standard Exist?

Intelligence systems increasingly depend on combinations of:

compute,

energy,

specialized hardware,

infrastructure,

data,

external providers,

software ecosystems,

human expertise,

and continuing economic support.

These conditions are not static.

Workloads grow.

Energy requirements change.

Hardware ages.

Infrastructure reaches capacity.

Provider economics change.

Dependencies become concentrated.

Technologies become obsolete.

And systems that were efficient at limited scale may become difficult or disproportionately expensive to sustain at operational scale.

ISS exists to make those long-term conditions governable.

Its purpose is not merely to ask whether intelligence can continue operating.

It asks whether the operating model required to preserve its justified Value can remain viable.

What This Standard Is Not

ISS is not a general environmental sustainability standard.

It does not govern the complete production, distribution, or societal allocation of energy.

It does not govern the broader allocation of finite resources across society.

It does not replace corporate sustainability frameworks.

It does not determine Harm or Liability.

It does not determine whether an organization must continue, scale, migrate, or retire an intelligence system.

And it does not assume that lower consumption automatically means greater sustainability.

ISS governs a narrower question:

Can the resources and supporting conditions required by this intelligence operating model remain viable over its intended lifecycle?

How It Works

ISS evaluates the intelligence operating model through the governance flow:

Sustainability Threshold → Execution Energy Viability → Infrastructure Sustainability → Economic Continuity → Dependency Sustainability

The assessment begins by defining:

the intelligence capability that must be preserved,

the operating model required to support it,

the applicable sustainability boundary,

and the intended lifecycle horizon.

ISS then examines whether the required resource, energy, infrastructure, economic, technical, and dependency conditions can remain viable as the system continues operating and changes in scale.

It also examines structural exposures such as:

capacity constraints,

hardware lifecycle,

dependency concentration,

limited substitutability,

provider lock-in,

cost deterioration,

technology obsolescence,

and Sustainability Drift.

The result is not a permanent declaration that a system is “sustainable.”

It is an evidence-based governance state that can change when the operating model changes.

Canonical Phrases

Current Viability ≠ Long-Term Sustainability.

Successful Pilot ≠ Sustainable Scale.

Available Infrastructure ≠ Sustainable Infrastructure.

Current Economic Proportionality ≠ Economic Continuity.

Current Availability ≠ Long-Term Availability.

Operational Continuity ≠ Sustainability.

Low Energy Consumption ≠ High Value Efficiency.

Dependency ≠ Unsustainability by default.

Alternative Availability ≠ Operational Substitutability.

Projection ≠ Evidence of Future Sustainability.

Strategic Value ≠ Sustainability.

Sustainability is not the ability to keep intelligence running at any cost.

A system can remain operational while becoming progressively less sustainable.

The operating model must remain viable, not merely the intelligence capability.

Scaling intelligence also scales the conditions required to sustain it.

Sustainability begins where point-in-time performance ends.

Intelligence is sustainable only while the conditions required to preserve its justified Value remain viable.

Potential Business Applications

ISS can support organizations evaluating whether AI and other intelligence systems can remain operationally and economically viable beyond initial deployment.

Potential applications include:

AI Infrastructure Sustainability Assessment Assess whether compute, hardware, energy, networking, storage, and supporting infrastructure can sustain expected intelligence demand.

AI Scaling Sustainability Assessment Evaluate whether a successful pilot or limited deployment remains viable as workloads, users, agents, or operational coverage increase.

AI Economic Continuity Assessment Examine whether recurring execution, infrastructure, provider, maintenance, and support costs remain viable over the intended lifecycle.

AI Dependency & Lock-In Assessment Identify critical dependencies on models, providers, APIs, cloud infrastructure, specialized hardware, data, software, or expertise.

AI Migration Readiness Assessment Evaluate whether critical intelligence capabilities can realistically transition to alternative providers, models, hardware, or infrastructure.

AI Energy Viability Assessment Examine whether the energy required by intelligence execution remains proportionate to the validated Value being produced.

AI Infrastructure Lifecycle Assessment Evaluate hardware replacement, capacity growth, obsolescence, maintenance, and future infrastructure requirements.

Long-Term AI Operating Model Assessment Evaluate the combined sustainability of the complete intelligence operating model rather than examining individual cost or

infrastructure components in isolation.

Use Case 1 — Successful AI Pilot Faces Enterprise Scale

An organization deploys an AI system to a limited group of users.

The pilot performs well.

Its costs are manageable.

Infrastructure capacity is sufficient.

The system creates validated operational Value.

The organization now considers deployment across the enterprise.

ISS does not assume that pilot success establishes long-term sustainability.

The larger deployment may materially change:

execution volume,

compute requirements,

energy consumption,

infrastructure capacity,

support requirements,

provider costs,

and dependency exposure.

ISS evaluates whether the operating model remains viable at the expected scale.

The system may therefore be:

successful at pilot scale

while

unsustainable at enterprise scale.

Successful Pilot ≠ Sustainable Scale.

Use Case 2 — High-Value AI System Depends on One Provider

An organization operates an intelligence system producing substantial validated Value.

Its current economics are acceptable.

Its infrastructure is stable.

However, a critical capability depends on:

one external model provider,

one specialized API,

and infrastructure that cannot easily be migrated.

ISS evaluates:

dependency concentration,

substitutability,

migration feasibility,

switching burden,

continuity exposure,

and the consequences of provider or technology change.

The system may remain economically successful and operationally reliable while still carrying a material long-term sustainability constraint.

Current Availability ≠ Long-Term Availability.

Alternative Availability ≠ Operational Substitutability.

Closing

Intelligence sustainability is not determined by whether a system can continue running today.

The relevant question is whether the operating model required to preserve its Value can continue to make sense tomorrow.

Resources may tighten.

Energy requirements may grow.

Infrastructure may reach its limits.

Economics may deteriorate.

Dependencies may become difficult to replace.

And scale may transform a viable system into an unsustainable one.

ISS makes those conditions visible before operational continuity is mistaken for long-term viability.

Current Viability ≠ Long-Term Sustainability.

Sustainability begins where point-in-time performance ends.
