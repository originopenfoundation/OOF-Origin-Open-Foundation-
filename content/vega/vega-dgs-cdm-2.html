1. Canonical Definition

The Continuation Decision Module (CDM) governs whether an existing intelligence operating model remains sufficiently justified to continue operating under its current or explicitly modified conditions.

CDM evaluates the Governed Decision Evidence State to determine whether continued operation remains supported by validated Value, sufficient productivity, proportionate resource consumption, computational efficiency, economic justification, legitimate Strategic Value, sustainability, Value Attribution, and applicable governed constraints.

CDM distinguishes continued operation from justified continuation.

The fact that an intelligence system is already deployed, technically functional, funded, integrated, or operationally familiar does not by itself justify continued use.

CDM may establish continuation as:

unconditional,

conditional,

temporarily justified,

reassessment-dependent,

or unsupported.

It does not authorize continued operation.

Its principal output is:

Governed Continuation Decision State

Its foundational distinction is:

Current Operation ≠ Justified Continuation.

2. Operational Role

The operational role of CDM is:

to determine whether the governed evidence continues to justify maintaining an intelligence operating model in operation, under what conditions that justification remains valid, and when continuation must be reassessed, modified, suspended, or passed to another lifecycle decision pathway.

CDM is therefore not a historical assessment of why a system was originally deployed.

It asks whether the system remains justified now and across the relevant continuation horizon.

3. Position in the DGS Internal Flow

The DGS internal governance flow is:

Decision Evidence Consolidation → Continuation Decision → Scaling Decision → Migration Decision → Retirement Decision

CDM governs:

Continuation Decision

Its principal upstream input is:

Governed Decision Evidence State

Its principal output is:

Governed Continuation Decision State

That state may support subsequent:

Scaling Decision Governance

where expansion is being considered.

Continuation may also expose conditions requiring optimization, reconfiguration, migration, suspension, reassessment, or eventual retirement.

4. Central Operational Question

Does the governed evidence continue to justify operation of the intelligence operating model, under what conditions, for what period, and with what requirements for monitoring or reassessment?

5. Continuation Decision Object

Every CDM assessment should identify the intelligence operating model whose continuation is being evaluated.

The object may include:

a model deployment,

agent system,

automation capability,

decision-support system,

workflow,

robotic intelligence configuration,

hybrid Human–AI operating model,

or another bounded intelligence capability.

6. Continuation Boundary

CDM should define exactly what is proposed to continue.

Continuation of:

a capability,

a model,

an execution configuration,

an infrastructure configuration,

or an entire operating model

may represent different decisions.

The continuation boundary should therefore remain explicit.

7. Continuation Horizon

Every continuation assessment should define the period over which continuation is being evaluated.

Possible horizons may include:

next execution period,

next operational cycle,

temporary extension,

defined review period,

medium-term continuation,

or intended lifecycle continuation.

Continuation Today ≠ Continuation Indefinitely.

8. Continuation Context

Continuation should be evaluated under the conditions in which the system is expected to operate.

Relevant context may include:

purpose,

workload,

scale,

configuration,

environment,

cost conditions,

resource conditions,

dependency conditions,

and applicable constraints.

9. Continuation Baseline

CDM should identify the baseline against which continuation is evaluated.

The baseline may represent:

current operation,

previous configuration,

minimum acceptable state,

alternative mechanism,

or no continuation.

The baseline should be explicit where it materially affects the decision.

10. Continuation Evidence

CDM consumes the Governed Decision Evidence State established by DECM.

It should not silently substitute raw metrics for governed evidence.

Relevant evidence may concern:

Value,

execution,

productivity,

resources,

Computational Optimization,

economics,

Strategic Value,

sustainability,

attribution,

and applicable governed constraints.

11. Value Continuation Condition

Continuation should remain connected to legitimate governed Value.

A system that continues producing outputs but no longer produces sufficient Value may no longer justify continuation.

Continued Output ≠ Continued Value.

12. Value Persistence

CDM should evaluate whether previously validated Value remains present.

Value may:

persist,

increase,

decline,

change form,

become conditional,

or disappear.

Historical Value does not automatically establish current Value.

13. Value Deterioration

Material deterioration in Value should trigger continuation reassessment.

Deterioration may occur because:

the operational need changed,

outputs became less useful,

another mechanism became more effective,

or the Value threshold changed.

14. Value Transformation

An intelligence operating model may cease producing its original Value while creating another legitimate form of Value.

CDM should distinguish Value transformation from Value disappearance.

Any new Value claim should be appropriately governed rather than assumed.

15. Productivity Continuation Condition

Continuation should consider whether the system continues producing sufficient validated productive output.

High operational activity does not establish sufficient productivity.

Continued Activity ≠ Continued Productivity.

16. Productivity Deterioration

CDM should identify material decline in productive performance.

Possible causes may include:

changed workload,

model degradation,

workflow complexity,

increasing correction,

or changing operational conditions.

17. Resource Continuation Condition

Continued operation should remain proportionate to the resources it consumes.

Resource evidence may concern:

compute,

energy,

hardware,

infrastructure,

human effort,

time,

or another constrained resource.

18. Resource Escalation

A system may continue producing the same Value while consuming increasing resources.

CDM should identify whether resource escalation remains justified.

Stable Value + Rising Consumption requires renewed justification.

19. Computational Continuation Condition

The current execution configuration should remain sufficiently computationally proportionate to the required outcome.

CDM may consume COS evidence concerning:

Problem-Space Efficiency,

Cognitive Efficiency,

Orchestration Efficiency,

Model Allocation Efficiency,

Compute Allocation Efficiency,

and Hardware Execution Efficiency.

20. Over-Intelligence Condition

An operating model may use more intelligence than the task now requires.

Changes in:

task structure,

automation,

algorithms,

models,

or operational knowledge

may make a simpler mechanism sufficient.

CDM should preserve this condition for reconfiguration or optimization.

Previously Justified Intelligence ≠ Permanently Justified Intelligence.

21. Economic Continuation Condition

Continuation should remain economically justified where economic Value is relevant.

CDM may consider:

Effective Task Cost,

Failure Spend,

Correction Cost,

Intelligence ROI,

and Value-to-Cost evidence.

22. Cost Deterioration

Cost deterioration may occur even where the system remains technically successful.

Examples include:

increased inference cost,

higher infrastructure cost,

higher human-review cost,

increased correction,

or increasing maintenance burden.

Material deterioration should affect continuation evaluation.

23. Strategic Value Continuation Condition

An intelligence operating model may remain justified despite weak immediate return where legitimate Strategic Value persists.

Strategic Value should remain evidenced.

Strategic Value ≠ Permanent Exemption from Performance Evidence.

24. Strategic Value Maturation

As continuation extends and investment accumulates, Strategic Value claims should mature through stronger evidence.

Strategic justification should not remain indefinitely dependent on the same early assumptions.

25. Strategic Milestone Continuation

Where continuation depends on Strategic Milestones, CDM should identify whether those milestones are:

achieved,

progressing,

delayed,

failed,

or unresolved.

Persistent milestone failure may weaken continuation justification.

26. Sustainability Continuation Condition

Continuation should remain compatible with the governed Intelligence Sustainability state.

A system may be viable now while becoming unsustainable across its intended lifecycle.

Current Viability ≠ Long-Term Sustainability.

27. Sustainability Deterioration

Material deterioration in:

energy viability,

infrastructure sustainability,

economic continuity,

or dependency sustainability

should trigger continuation reassessment.

28. Dependency Continuation Condition

Continuation may depend on:

providers,

models,

hardware,

infrastructure,

data,

tools,

specialized expertise,

or other critical dependencies.

CDM should consume the relevant governed dependency state rather than recreate it.

29. Dependency Concentration

A system may produce strong Value while becoming increasingly dependent on a single critical dependency.

This does not automatically require discontinuation.

But it may justify:

conditions,

mitigation,

migration analysis,

or increased monitoring.

30. Attribution Continuation Condition

Value Attribution may reveal whether the operating model's current contribution structure remains justified.

For example, a complex AI configuration may appear valuable while attribution shows that most material Value now comes from a simpler human or technical component.

Such evidence may affect continuation.

31. Human Contribution Change

If human intervention becomes increasingly necessary to preserve Value, the operating model may have changed materially.

CDM should preserve evidence of:

increasing correction,

manual recovery,

human validation,

or human substitution.

32. AI Contribution Change

AI contribution may increase, decrease, or change in function over time.

Continuation should reflect the current Value-producing structure rather than historical assumptions about AI contribution.

33. Multi-Agent Contribution Change

In multi-agent systems, agents may accumulate while only some remain materially relevant.

CDM should consider whether system complexity remains justified by actual contribution.

More Agents ≠ More Continuation Value.

34. Applicable Constraint Continuation

Continuation is feasible only within applicable governed constraints.

Strong Value, productivity, or economics cannot by themselves justify continuation where a competent non-compensable constraint is violated.

35. Constraint Persistence

CDM should determine whether applicable constraints remain satisfied throughout the continuation horizon.

A system should not receive unconditional continuation where compliance with a material constraint is temporary or uncertain.

36. New Constraint

A new legal, institutional, technical, operational, or governance constraint may materially change continuation feasibility.

CDM should support reassessment when a competent new constraint becomes applicable.

37. Continuation Evidence Sufficiency

Evidence supporting continuation should be sufficient for the scope, duration, and materiality of the continuation being considered.

A short reversible extension may require a different evidence threshold from long-term mission-critical continuation.

38. Continuation Evidence Freshness

Evidence should remain sufficiently current.

Historical success alone should not justify continuation where:

configuration,

cost,

Value,

dependencies,

or operating conditions

have materially changed.

39. Continuation Confidence

Possible confidence states may include:

High Confidence

Moderate Confidence

Conditional

Evidence-Limited

Low Confidence

Conflicted

Unresolved

Confidence should correspond to the underlying evidence.

40. Continuation Uncertainty

Material uncertainty should remain explicit.

Uncertainty may concern:

future Value,

cost,

resource demand,

Strategic Value,

dependency,

sustainability,

or another continuation condition.

Uncertainty does not automatically prevent continuation.

It may require conditional continuation.

41. Continuation Conflict

Evidence may support continuation in one dimension and challenge it in another.

For example:

Value may remain high,

cost may deteriorate,

Strategic Value may remain strong,

and sustainability may be uncertain.

CDM should preserve the conflict.

42. Continuation Trade-Off

Where continuation requires accepting a material trade-off, that trade-off should be explicit.

A trade-off may involve:

Value versus cost,

capability versus dependency,

productivity versus resource consumption,

or short-term Value versus long-term sustainability.

43. Non-Compensable Continuation Condition

Some continuation conditions cannot be offset by strength elsewhere.

A non-compensable constraint remains binding regardless of:

ROI,

productivity,

Strategic Value,

or technical performance.

44. Continuation Threshold

CDM may define thresholds indicating whether continuation remains sufficiently justified.

Thresholds may be:

quantitative,

categorical,

conditional,

or composite.

No universal threshold is required.

45. Threshold Breach

A material threshold breach should trigger an appropriate response.

Possible responses include:

reassessment,

optimization,

reconfiguration,

conditional continuation,

migration analysis,

suspension,

or retirement analysis.

46. Continuation State

Possible continuation states may include:

Continue

Continue Conditionally

Continue Temporarily

Continue with Optimization

Continue with Reconfiguration

Continue Pending Migration Assessment

Reassess

Suspend Pending Evidence

Continuation Not Supported

Evidence-Limited

Conflicted

Unresolved

These states are methodological decision states.

They are not authorizations.

47. Unconditional Continuation

Unconditional Continuation may be supported where the evidence sufficiently establishes that:

Value remains justified,

the operating model remains proportionate,

material constraints remain satisfied,

and no material continuation condition requires intervention.

“Unconditional” applies within the defined decision horizon.

It does not mean permanent approval.

48. Conditional Continuation

Conditional Continuation applies where continued operation remains justified only if one or more explicit conditions are satisfied.

Conditions should be:

material,

measurable where practicable,

time-bounded where appropriate,

and linked to reassessment.

49. Continuation Condition Object

Each material continuation condition should identify:

the required state,

the current state,

the evidence source,

the threshold,

the monitoring mechanism,

the review point,

and the consequence of failure.

50. Optimization Condition

Continuation may remain justified subject to optimization.

Examples may include:

reducing unnecessary reasoning,

improving orchestration,

changing model allocation,

reducing compute,

or improving hardware utilization.

Optimization should not become an indefinite substitute for resolving persistent failure.

51. Reconfiguration Condition

Continuation may require a material configuration change.

Potential changes include:

model,

agent structure,

tools,

routing,

validation,

human involvement,

compute,

or infrastructure.

The current capability may remain valuable even where its current configuration does not.

52. Evidence Acquisition Condition

Continuation may be temporarily justified while missing evidence is acquired.

The missing evidence and acquisition deadline should remain explicit.

Temporary Continuation ≠ Evidence Sufficiency.

53. Dependency Mitigation Condition

Continuation may require reduction or mitigation of a material dependency.

Possible responses include:

alternative providers,

redundancy,

substitution capability,

migration preparation,

or reduced concentration.

54. Cost Reduction Condition

Where economic deterioration threatens continuation justification, continued operation may depend on measurable cost improvement.

The condition should remain linked to Value preservation.

Reducing cost while destroying Value does not satisfy the underlying objective.

55. Sustainability Improvement Condition

Continuation may require improvement in:

energy viability,

infrastructure sustainability,

economic continuity,

or dependency sustainability.

The required state should be measurable where practicable.

56. Validation Condition

Where Value or performance evidence remains insufficiently validated, continuation may depend on additional validation.

CDM does not replace VALIDOS™.

It preserves validation requirements as continuation conditions.

57. Human Oversight Condition

Where another competent governance architecture requires human oversight, CDM may preserve that requirement as a continuation condition.

CDM does not independently create Human Override or oversight authority.

58. Continuation Monitoring

Material continuation conditions should be monitored.

Monitoring should focus on the states capable of changing continuation justification.

Not every available metric requires continuous monitoring.

59. Monitoring Proportionality

Monitoring intensity should correspond to:

decision materiality,

system volatility,

condition criticality,

reversibility,

and rate of change.

60. Continuation Trigger

A Continuation Trigger is an event or state requiring review.

Triggers may include:

Value deterioration,

cost increase,

resource escalation,

dependency failure,

threshold breach,

configuration change,

constraint change,

or material new evidence.

61. Event-Driven Continuation Review

CDM should support reassessment when material events occur.

A system should not wait for a scheduled review where evidence already indicates that continuation conditions materially changed.

62. Periodic Continuation Review

Where appropriate, continuation should also be reviewed periodically.

Review frequency should reflect the operating environment and decision materiality.

63. Continuation State Expiration

A Governed Continuation Decision State may have an explicit expiration point.

Expiration requires:

renewal,

reassessment,

or transition to another lifecycle state.

Continuation is a governed state, not a permanent inheritance.

64. Continuation Drift

Continuation Drift occurs where a system remains operational while the evidence supporting its original continuation gradually changes.

This may include:

Value drift,

cost drift,

resource drift,

dependency drift,

or contribution drift.

65. Silent Continuation

Silent Continuation occurs where operation continues because no explicit decision to stop has been made.

CDM rejects this as a sufficient governance basis.

No Decision to Stop ≠ Decision to Continue.

66. Inertia Risk

Operational familiarity may create resistance to reassessment.

A system may continue because:

teams know it,

processes depend on it,

contracts exist,

or replacement appears inconvenient.

These factors may be relevant.

They do not establish Value justification.

67. Sunk-Cost Bias

Past investment may influence organizational preferences.

CDM should preserve:

Historical Investment ≠ Future Value Justification.

The relevant question is whether future continuation remains justified.

68. Legacy Bias

An established system should not receive preferential continuation merely because it has operated for a long period.

System Age ≠ Continuation Justification.

69. Success Bias

Past success may be relevant evidence.

It is not sufficient where present conditions materially differ.

Historical Success ≠ Current Continuation Justification by default.

70. Capability Bias

A highly capable system may still be disproportionate to the operational requirement.

Capability ≠ Continuation Justification.

71. Availability Bias

A system may continue because it is immediately available.

Availability can affect transition feasibility.

But:

Availability ≠ Value Justification.

72. Contractual Lock-In

Existing contracts may affect the economic or operational feasibility of change.

CDM may preserve this evidence.

It does not convert contractual lock-in into Value.

73. Technical Lock-In

Technical integration may make replacement difficult.

This may support temporary continuation while migration becomes feasible.

It should not automatically establish long-term continuation justification.

74. Operational Dependency

Operations may become materially dependent on an intelligence capability.

Dependency may increase the cost of discontinuation.

It may also increase the importance of sustainability and migration planning.

Dependency ≠ Justification by itself.

75. Continuation Alternatives

CDM should consider whether continuation is being evaluated against realistic alternatives.

Alternatives may include:

current configuration,

optimized configuration,

reconfigured system,

simpler intelligence mechanism,

deterministic mechanism,

human process,

migration,

or discontinuation.

76. No-Intelligence Alternative

Where appropriate, CDM should preserve the possibility that the intelligence mechanism is no longer required.

The VEGA™ allocation principle remains relevant:

Use the least complex mechanism capable of reliably producing the required validated outcome.

77. Simpler-Mechanism Alternative

A simpler mechanism may become capable of producing the required validated outcome.

Continuation of a more complex intelligence configuration should then require renewed justification.

78. Human Alternative

Where appropriate, a human or Human–AI process may be a relevant alternative.

CDM does not assume automation is inherently preferable.

The comparison should follow governed Value and resource evidence.

79. Alternative Configuration

Continuation may be justified at the capability level while the current configuration is not.

CDM should therefore distinguish:

Continue Capability

from

Continue Current Configuration.

80. Continuation Reversibility

CDM should assess how easily continued operation can later be changed.

Possible states may include:

Highly Reversible

Reversible

Conditionally Reversible

Difficult to Reverse

Effectively Irreversible

Lower reversibility may justify stronger continuation evidence.

81. Continuation Commitment

Continuation may create additional:

capital commitment,

technical integration,

data dependency,

operational dependency,

or organizational lock-in.

These effects should be visible where material.

82. Continuation Optionality

A continuation decision may preserve or reduce future options.

Temporary or conditional continuation may preserve optionality while additional evidence develops.

83. Continuation-to-Scaling Boundary

Continuation does not automatically justify scaling.

A system may remain justified at its current scope without being justified at larger scale.

Justified Continuation ≠ Justified Scaling.

Scaling is governed separately.

84. Continuation-to-Migration Boundary

Continuation and migration are not opposites.

A system may continue temporarily while migration is prepared.

CDM may therefore establish:

Continue Pending Migration Assessment

where appropriate.

85. Continuation-to-Retirement Boundary

Continuation Not Supported does not automatically establish immediate retirement.

Other lifecycle states may include:

suspension,

reconfiguration,

migration,

or further evidence acquisition.

Retirement is governed separately.

86. Continuation-to-Optimization Relationship

Optimization may preserve continuation where the underlying Value remains legitimate but execution has become disproportionate.

CDM identifies the continuation condition.

COS governs the applicable Computational Optimization mechanisms.

87. Continuation-to-Sustainability Relationship

ISS establishes whether the intelligence operating model can remain viable across its intended lifecycle.

CDM consumes that evidence to determine whether continuation remains justified.

CDM does not recreate sustainability governance.

88. Continuation-to-Attribution Relationship

VAS may reveal which contributors actually produce the Value supporting continuation.

This can expose configurations where:

complexity persists,

but material Value comes from only a limited subset of the system.

CDM may use this evidence to support reconfiguration or optimization conditions.

89. Continuation-to-Decision Authority Boundary

CDM produces a governed continuation state.

It does not authorize continued operation.

The competent Decision Authority may be:

human,

organizational,

institutional,

regulatory,

or otherwise legitimately established.

Governed Continuation State ≠ Continuation Authorization.

90. Automated Continuation Assessment

CDM may be implemented through automated or semi-automated assessment.

Automation may:

monitor evidence,

detect thresholds,

identify drift,

or propose continuation states.

Automated Continuation Assessment ≠ Automated Authority.

91. Continuation Recommendation

The recommendation should preserve:

evidence,

conditions,

confidence,

uncertainty,

and authority boundary.

93. Minimum Implementation Framework

A conforming implementation of CDM must provide exactly the following six capabilities:

1. Continuation Object, Boundary & Horizon Governance

Capability to identify the intelligence operating model, continuation scope, operating context, decision horizon, and relevant baseline.

2. Continuation Justification Governance

Capability to evaluate whether Value, productivity, resources, Computational Optimization, economics, Strategic Value, sustainability, attribution, and applicable governed constraints continue to support operation.

3. Continuation Condition & Threshold Governance

Capability to establish Continuation Thresholds, Non-Compensable Conditions, Conditional Continuation requirements, optimization conditions, reconfiguration conditions, evidence conditions, and other measurable continuation requirements.

4. Continuation Monitoring & Reassessment Governance

Capability to monitor material continuation conditions, detect threshold breaches and drift, establish triggers, support periodic and event-driven review, and define Continuation State Expiration.

5. Continuation Integrity & Alternative Governance

Capability to control sunk-cost, legacy, success, capability, availability, dependency, and lock-in biases while preserving realistic continuation alternatives, reversibility, and optionality.

6. DGS Scaling Decision Handoff Governance

Capability to produce a Governed Continuation Decision State sufficiently explicit for subsequent Scaling Decision Governance while preserving conditions, uncertainty, and Decision Authority boundaries.

Governed Continuation Decision State

99. Use Case 1 — The AI System Still Works, but Its Cost Has Doubled

An organization operates an AI system that continues producing the same validated operational Value it produced one year earlier.

Technical performance remains stable.

However:

inference cost has doubled,

human correction requirements have increased,

and a smaller model can now produce a comparable validated outcome.

CDM does not treat continued technical operation as sufficient continuation justification.

It evaluates:

Value persistence,

cost deterioration,

resource proportionality,

the simpler-mechanism alternative,

and the feasibility of reconfiguration.

The resulting state may be:

Continue with Reconfiguration

rather than unconditional continuation or immediate retirement.

Current Operation ≠ Justified Continuation of the Current Configuration.

100. Use Case 2 — The Project Has No Immediate ROI but Is Building Strategic Capability

An organization is developing an intelligence capability with weak immediate financial return.

The system has not yet reached commercial-scale economics.

However, governed Strategic Value evidence shows:

material capability development,

validated technical progress,

Strategic Milestones being achieved,

and a credible pathway toward future operational Value.

CDM does not reject continuation solely because immediate ROI remains weak.

It evaluates whether the Strategic Value remains sufficiently evidenced and whether continued investment remains proportionate.

The resulting state may be:

Continue Conditionally

with defined Strategic Milestones, cost limits, and a future reassessment point.

Strategic Value can justify continuation.

But:

Strategic Value must become more evidenced as commitment becomes more committed.

101. Canonical Closing Statement

Continuation should never become the default state of intelligence simply because stopping requires an explicit decision.

A system can remain technically functional after its Value has weakened.

It can remain valuable after its configuration has become inefficient.

It can remain strategically important while immediate economics remain weak.

And it can remain successful while a simpler, cheaper, or more sustainable operating model becomes available.

Continuation therefore requires renewed justification.

Current Operation ≠ Justified Continuation.

Historical Success ≠ Current Continuation Justification.

Historical Investment ≠ Future Value Justification.

Capability ≠ Continuation Justification.

Dependency ≠ Justification by itself.

Justified Continuation ≠ Justified Scaling.

Governed Continuation State ≠ Continuation Authorization.

The purpose of CDM is not to keep intelligence running and not to stop it.

It is to determine whether the evidence still supports keeping it alive under the conditions in which it must now operate.

CDM turns continuation from organizational inertia into a governed requirement for intelligence to keep earning its place in operation.
