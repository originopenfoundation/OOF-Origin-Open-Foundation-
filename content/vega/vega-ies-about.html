About Intelligence Economy Standard (IES)

Architecture:

VEGA™ — Value Execution Governance Architecture

Standard:

Intelligence Economy Standard (IES)

Governed Space:

Intelligence Execution Economics

What Is This Standard?

The Intelligence Economy Standard (IES) is the VEGA™ Parent Standard for governing the economics of intelligence execution.

It establishes how the complete economic burden required to produce a validated intelligence outcome can be identified, structured, measured, compared, and related to the validated value produced.

IES moves economic analysis beyond individual model prices, token costs, compute costs, or infrastructure charges.

Its governing principle is:

The true cost of intelligence is the governed total cost of producing a validated outcome.

Why Does This Standard Exist?

The visible price of intelligence can differ substantially from its real operational cost.

A model invocation may appear inexpensive while the complete execution requires additional:

routing,

agents,

tools,

compute,

retries,

correction,

validation,

infrastructure,

or human intervention.

A cheaper model can therefore produce a more expensive validated outcome if it fails more frequently or requires greater correction.

IES exists to make these economic effects governable.

It shifts the economic question from:

“How much did the AI call cost?”

to:

“What did it cost to produce the validated outcome?”

What This Standard Is Not

IES is not a general accounting standard.

It does not govern:

corporate finance,

taxation,

market pricing,

general investment analysis,

legal Liability,

Harm determination,

or final lifecycle authority.

It also does not define Value itself.

VGS governs Value.

IES governs the economic relationship between the cost of intelligence execution and the validated Value produced.

How It Works

IES follows the economic path of intelligence from execution to validated outcome.

Its internal governance flow is:

Effective Task Cost → Failure Spend → Correction Cost → Intelligence ROI → Value-to-Cost

The standard first establishes the real cost of performing the task.

It then identifies economic consumption associated with failed execution and the cost required to correct or complete insufficient outcomes.

Once Total Governed Cost is established, that cost can be related to the validated Value produced.

Effective Task Cost

IES distinguishes the visible price of execution from the effective cost of completing the task.

Effective Task Cost may include:

model execution,

compute,

orchestration,

tools,

supporting infrastructure,

validation,

retry,

correction,

and economically relevant human intervention.

Therefore:

Model Cost ≠ Intelligence Cost.

Failure Spend

Failed execution consumes resources even when it produces no usable outcome.

IES therefore treats failure as an economic state.

Repeated model calls, unsuccessful agent paths, failed tool operations, and other unsuccessful execution can contribute materially to the true cost of intelligence.

Failed execution is still economic consumption.

Correction Cost

An initially inexpensive output may become expensive when substantial additional work is required to make it usable.

Correction may involve:

additional model execution,

new tool calls,

validation,

human review,

or complete re-execution.

IES keeps those costs attached to the outcome they were required to produce.

Intelligence ROI

IES supports governed evaluation of the relationship between economic investment in intelligence and the validated Value attributable to that intelligence.

ROI should use a sufficiently complete cost boundary.

It should not be improved artificially by excluding:

failure,

retry,

correction,

validation,

or material supporting execution.

Immediate negative ROI also does not automatically mean that an intelligence activity has no legitimate strategic value.

Strategic Value is governed separately by SVGS.

Value-to-Cost

IES ultimately relates:

Validated Value ↔ Total Governed Cost

A high-cost intelligence system may be economically justified where it produces sufficiently greater validated Value.

A low-cost system may be economically weak where its outcomes create little Value or require substantial correction.

Therefore:

Low Cost ≠ Good Economics by default.

Principal Governance Output

The principal output of IES is:

Governed Intelligence Economy State

This state establishes whether the economic relationship between intelligence execution and validated Value is:

economically insufficient,

proportionate,

excessive,

an optimization candidate,

Value-insufficient,

constraint-limited,

evidence-limited,

or unresolved.

The output provides economic evidence for subsequent VEGA™ governance without independently making the final lifecycle decision.

Potential Business Applications

IES can support practical intelligence-economics activities such as:

AI Cost Assessment,

Cost per Validated Outcome Analysis,

AI ROI Assessment,

AI Operating Cost Optimization,

Model Economics Comparison,

Agentic System Cost Assessment,

Failure Spend Analysis,

Correction Cost Analysis,

Human-AI Cost Analysis,

AI Workflow Economics Assessment,

Compute Economics Assessment,

AI Deployment Economics,

and AI Cost Sustainability analysis.

It can also provide a methodological foundation for organizations seeking to understand why apparently inexpensive AI systems sometimes become expensive when evaluated across the complete operational execution path.

Use Case 1 — The Cheaper Model Produces the More Expensive Outcome

A company replaces an advanced model with a cheaper model because the price per invocation is substantially lower.

The initial usage bill decreases.

However, the new model fails more frequently, requires additional retries, produces more outputs requiring correction, and increases human review.

IES evaluates the complete economic path rather than the individual invocation price.

The analysis may show that the cheaper model has a higher cost per validated completion.

Cheap Attempt ≠ Cheap Validated Completion.

Use Case 2 — Multi-Agent System Creates Value but Its True Cost Is Unknown

An organization deploys a multi-agent system that produces valuable research outputs.

The organization measures the cost of the primary model but does not systematically account for:

agent coordination,

secondary model calls,

retrieval,

tools,

validation,

failed paths,

or human correction.

IES establishes the Economic Boundary and reconstructs the Total Governed Cost required to produce each validated outcome.

That cost can then be compared with the validated Value established through VEGA™.

The organization can evaluate the system using outcome economics rather than incomplete usage pricing.

Visible Cost ≠ Total Governed Cost.

Closing

Intelligence should not be called inexpensive because one model call is inexpensive.

Its economic meaning appears only when the complete path to a validated outcome is visible.

Execution, failure, retry, correction, validation, infrastructure, and relevant human intervention can all contribute to that path.

IES makes that path governable.

The true cost of intelligence is the governed total cost of producing a validated outcome.

Measure the economics of the outcome — not merely the price of the attempt.
