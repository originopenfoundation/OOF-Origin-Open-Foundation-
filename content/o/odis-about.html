About Operational Decision Integrity Standard - (ODIS)

Canonical Definition

Operational Decision Integrity Standard (ODIS) defines the structural conditions under which
runtime operational decisions, adaptive decision evolution, distributed decision
coordination, and consequence-bearing decision states remain materially stable, traceable,
governable, and operationally aligned. Operational validity is not determined only by
execution. It also depends on whether runtime decisions themselves remain valid.

What This Standard Is

ODIS is a parent standard defining the governance architecture for operational decision
integrity.

It governs:
  • runtime decision validity
  • decision traceability
  • adaptive decision stability
  • consequence-bearing decision governance
  • authority-aligned decision conditions
  • distributed decision coordination


What This Standard Is Not

ODIS is not a decision engine, optimization tool, ranking model, workflow automation system,
or AI output evaluator. It does not govern decision technology alone. It governs whether
operational decisions remain valid, traceable, and governable during runtime.

Why This Standard Exists

Future AI systems will not only execute tasks. They will increasingly make runtime
decisions. A system may perceive correctly, prioritize correctly, and still make an invalid
decision. This creates risks such as decision drift, authority-invalid decisions,
consequence misalignment, escalation corruption, and distributed decision fragmentation.
ODIS exists because runtime decisions become a governance condition in autonomous
operational systems.

Core Insight

A system is not operationally valid merely because it produces an output. It becomes valid
only when the decision behind that output remains governable.

Operational Architecture Space

ODIS defines the operational architecture space for decision integrity, runtime decision
governance, decision traceability, adaptive decision stability, consequence-bearing
decisions, and authority-aligned decision validity.

Use Case 1 — Autonomous AI Decision System

Scenario
An AI system makes runtime decisions across delegated workflows and autonomous execution
environments.

Application
ODIS preserves decision traceability, authority alignment, and consequence-bearing decision
validity.

Result
The organization gains stronger decision governance and reduced hidden decision drift.

Use Case 2 — Robotics Runtime Decision Layer

Scenario
A robotics system selects operational actions during changing runtime conditions.

Application
ODIS governs whether runtime decisions remain aligned with context, authority, perception,
and operational consequence.

Result
The environment gains stronger operational safety and reduced invalid autonomous decision
behavior.

Architectural Position

Within the OOF system, ODIS belongs under:
  • Governance & Enforcement
  • Operational Decision Integrity Architecture


It extends the cognition governance stack beyond execution, context, memory, intent,
attention, and perception into runtime decision validity.

Closing Definition

Operational decisions are not valid merely because systems act. Operational decisions become
valid only when decision formation, authority alignment, consequence logic, and runtime
traceability remain materially governable.
