Canonical Definition

Responsibility Continuity Module (RCTM) defines the structural
conditions under which responsibility associated with a governed
identity remains continuous, attributable, transferable, and
continuously governable throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

RCTM governs responsibility continuity.

The module establishes the governance conditions required to ensure
that responsibility remains continuously preserved despite
organizational, operational, technological, or lifecycle changes.

Participants may change.

Responsibility must remain continuous.

RCTM governs that continuity.

Module Operational Role

RCTM defines the responsibility continuity layer of RIS.

Its role is to preserve uninterrupted governance responsibility
throughout every stage of the governed identity lifecycle.

Module Operational Space

RCTM governs:

  • responsibility continuity,
  • accountable continuity,
  • governance continuity,
  • responsibility preservation,
  • lifecycle responsibility,
  • continuous accountability,
  • responsibility succession,
  • governance responsibility continuity.


Module Function

The module applies wherever responsibility must remain continuously
preserved despite changes affecting a governed identity.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Responsibility Continuity Object

Identify the governed identity and the responsibilities requiring
continuous governance.

2. Define Continuity Conditions

Establish governance conditions governing responsibility preservation,
succession, transfer, replacement, and lifecycle continuity.

3. Define Continuity Failure Logic

Identify conditions where responsibility becomes interrupted,
abandoned, fragmented, inconsistent, or governance-invalid.

4. Define Governance Response

Define governance actions for responsibility succession, reassignment,
restoration, governance intervention, or continuity review.

5. Preserve Continuity Records

Preserve responsibility history, succession records, governance
decisions, supporting evidence, and accountability documentation.

Use Case 1 — Enterprise Identity Governance

Scenario

The governance responsibility for a strategic enterprise identity is
transferred to a successor after an organizational restructuring.

Application

RCTM preserves uninterrupted governance responsibility throughout
the transition.

Result

Governance continuity remains intact without creating
responsibility gaps.

Use Case 2 — Long-Term AI Identity Governance

Scenario

A governed AI identity operates for many years while administrators,
ownership structures, and operational teams change.

Application

RCTM preserves continuous responsibility across every
governance transition.

Result

Responsibility remains attributable, continuous, and continuously
governable throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

Canonical Closing Statement

Trustworthy governance is measured not only by who is responsible
today, but by whether responsibility is never lost tomorrow.
Responsibility Continuity preserves uninterrupted accountability
throughout the complete identity lifecycle.
