Governed Space

Identity Transfer

Core Question

How can a governed identity be transferred while preserving
governance integrity?

Canonical Definition

Transfer Integrity Standard (TIS) defines the structural conditions
under which a governed identity, together with its associated
governance relationships, may be transferred while preserving
legitimacy, continuity, traceability, accountability, and continuous
governability throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

TIS governs identity transfer.

The standard establishes the governance conditions required to ensure
that every legitimate transfer preserves the integrity of the governed
identity before, during, and after the transfer process.

Identity may be transferred.

Governance must never be broken.

TIS governs that transition.

Governance Flow Position

Origin → Identity → Ownership → Authority → Permission →
Responsibility → Provenance → Transfer → Continuity → Auditability

Standard Abstract

Governed identities may change owners, authorities, organizations,
jurisdictions, platforms, technologies, or generations.

These transitions must preserve governance integrity.

Without governed transfer:

  • governance continuity may be interrupted,
  • ownership disputes may arise,
  • authority may become unclear,
  • provenance may become fragmented,
  • identity trust may deteriorate.


TIS establishes the governance conditions required for legitimate,
traceable, and continuously governable identity transfer.

Operational Role

Transfer Integrity Standard provides the governance layer responsible
for governing how governed identities and their governance
relationships are transferred without compromising identity integrity,
provenance, accountability, or governance continuity.

Its role is to ensure that every transfer remains legitimate,
verifiable, traceable, and governance-approved.

Operational Space

TIS governs:

  • ownership transfer,
  • authority transfer,
  • identity transfer,
  • asset transfer,
  • transfer validation,
  • governance transition,
  • transfer traceability,
  • identity migration.


This governed space ensures that identity transfer remains
continuously compatible with trustworthy identity governance.

Why This Standard Exists

Governed identities cannot remain permanently static.

Ownership changes.

Authority changes.

Organizations change.

Platforms change.

Governance must preserve identity integrity across every
legitimate transition.

TIS exists because Identity Transfer represents the eighth governable
space of OBIDENITY® — Origin-Bound Identity Governance Architecture.

Foundational Principle

Trustworthy identity requires trustworthy transfer. Every governed
identity must preserve its governance integrity whenever legitimate
transfer occurs throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

Canonical Closing Statement

Identity may change hands, platforms, or jurisdictions, but governance
integrity must remain uninterrupted. Transfer Integrity preserves
trust by ensuring that every legitimate identity transfer remains
continuous, verifiable, and continuously governable throughout the
complete identity lifecycle.
