Canonical Definition

Provenance Verification Module (PVAM) defines the structural
conditions under which the provenance of a governed identity is
verified, authenticated, validated, evidence-supported, and
continuously governable throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

PVAM governs provenance verification.

The module establishes the governance conditions required to ensure
that the complete provenance of a governed identity can be
independently verified using trusted governance records, lifecycle
history, and supporting evidence.

Provenance creates trust.

Verification confirms that trust.

PVAM governs that verification.

Module Operational Role

PVAM defines the provenance verification layer of PRIS.

Its role is to independently verify the authenticity, continuity, and
integrity of the provenance associated with a governed identity.

Module Operational Space

PVAM governs:

  • provenance verification,
  • provenance authentication,
  • governance verification,
  • identity history verification,
  • evidence validation,
  • lifecycle verification,
  • provenance integrity,
  • trusted provenance assurance.


Module Function

The module applies wherever organizations must independently verify
the provenance of a governed identity before governance decisions
are made.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Provenance Verification Object

Identify the governed identity and the provenance requiring
independent verification.

2. Define Verification Conditions

Establish governance conditions governing provenance validation,
evidence requirements, historical consistency, authenticity, and
governance approval.

3. Define Verification Failure Logic

Identify conditions where provenance becomes unverifiable, incomplete,
inconsistent, disputed, unsupported by evidence,
or governance-invalid.

4. Define Governance Response

Define governance actions for verification, investigation, evidence
review, correction, rejection, or governance escalation.

5. Preserve Verification Records Preserve verification results,
governance decisions, supporting evidence, provenance history, review
documentation, and audit records.

Use Case 1 — Enterprise Identity Governance

Scenario

An organization must verify the complete provenance of a strategic
enterprise identity before approving a governance transfer.

Application

PVAM independently verifies the identity's lifecycle history and
supporting governance evidence.

Result

Governance decisions are based on authenticated and
verifiable provenance.

Use Case 2 — Cross-Platform AI Identity

Scenario

A governed AI identity operates across multiple platforms and
jurisdictions before being integrated into a new
governance environment.

Application

PVAM verifies the complete provenance across all participating
governance systems.

Result

The identity enters the new governance environment with independently
verified provenance and preserved governance trust.

Canonical Closing Statement

Provenance becomes trustworthy only when it can be independently
verified. Provenance Verification preserves governance confidence by
confirming that the complete history of a governed identity remains
authentic, evidence-supported, and continuously governable throughout
the complete identity lifecycle.
