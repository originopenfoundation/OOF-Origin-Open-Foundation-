Canonical Definition

Replacement Continuity Module (RECM) defines the structural conditions
under which a governed identity may be legitimately replaced while
preserving governance continuity, provenance, accountability,
traceability, and continuous governability throughout the complete
identity lifecycle.

RECM governs replacement continuity.

The module establishes the governance conditions required to ensure
that every legitimate replacement preserves the governance integrity
of the governed identity without creating discontinuity or loss of
governance trust.

Replacement may change implementation.

Governance identity must remain continuous.

RECM governs that continuity.

Module Operational Role

RECM defines the replacement continuity layer of CIS.

Its role is to preserve governance continuity whenever a governed
identity or its operational representation is legitimately replaced.

Module Operational Space

RECM governs:

  • identity replacement,
  • governance continuity,
  • replacement validation,
  • operational replacement,
  • lifecycle replacement,
  • governance preservation,
  • replacement traceability,
  • identity continuity.


Module Function

The module applies wherever governed identities require legitimate
replacement because of operational, organizational, technological, or
lifecycle changes.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Replacement Object

Identify the governed identity and the component, representation, or
implementation requiring replacement.

2. Define Replacement Conditions

Establish governance conditions governing replacement authorization,
continuity preservation, provenance retention, verification, and
governance approval.

3. Define Replacement Failure Logic

Identify conditions where replacement becomes unauthorized,
incomplete, inconsistent, unverifiable, misleading,
or governance-invalid.

4. Define Governance Response

Define governance actions for approval, correction, rollback,
investigation, revalidation, or governance escalation.

5. Preserve Replacement Records

Preserve replacement decisions, governance approvals, provenance
records, supporting evidence, lifecycle history, and
continuity documentation.

Use Case 1 — Enterprise Identity Infrastructure

Scenario

An organization replaces its identity management infrastructure while
preserving the same governed enterprise identities.

Application

RECM governs the replacement and validates that governance continuity
remains intact.

Result

The identities continue without interruption despite the
infrastructure replacement.

Use Case 2 — AI Identity Modernization

Scenario

A governed AI identity is migrated to a new AI model while retaining
the same governance identity and operational responsibilities.

Application

RECM verifies that the replacement preserves governance relationships,
provenance, and continuity.

Result

The governed identity remains trustworthy, attributable, and
continuously governable throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

Canonical Closing Statement

Replacement should renew capability, not erase identity. Replacement
Continuity preserves governance trust by ensuring that every
legitimate replacement carries forward the identity, history, and
governance relationships that define continuity throughout the
complete identity lifecycle.
