Governed Space

Identity Continuity

Core Question

How can a governed identity remain continuous throughout its
complete lifecycle?

Canonical Definition

Continuity Integrity Standard (CIS) defines the structural conditions
under which a governed identity preserves its continuity, legitimacy,
traceability, provenance, governance relationships, and continuous
governability throughout the complete identity lifecycle despite
legitimate lifecycle changes.

CIS governs identity continuity.

The standard establishes the governance conditions required to ensure
that a governed identity remains continuously recognizable,
attributable, and governance-valid across succession, inheritance,
replacement, merging, and other legitimate lifecycle transitions.

Identity may evolve.

Identity may transfer.

Identity must remain continuous.

CIS governs that continuity.

Governance Flow Position

Origin → Identity → Ownership → Authority → Permission →
Responsibility → Provenance → Transfer → Continuity → Auditability

Standard Abstract

Governed identities are expected to exist beyond individual events,
technologies, organizations, and operational environments.

Long-term governance requires continuity.

Without governed continuity:

  • identity relationships become fragmented,
  • governance history becomes disconnected,
  • ownership succession becomes uncertain,
  • long-term trust deteriorates,
  • lifecycle governance cannot be preserved.


CIS establishes the governance conditions required for continuous,
trustworthy, and governance-preserving identity continuity.

Operational Role

Continuity Integrity Standard provides the governance layer
responsible for governing how governed identities preserve their
integrity and governance relationships across legitimate
lifecycle transitions.

Its role is to ensure that every governed identity remains
continuously attributable, traceable, and governance-valid
despite change.

Operational Space

CIS governs:

  • identity succession,
  • inheritance,
  • merge continuity,
  • clone continuity,
  • replacement continuity,
  • lifecycle continuity,
  • governance continuity,
  • long-term identity preservation.


This governed space ensures that identity continuity remains
compatible with trustworthy identity governance throughout the
complete identity lifecycle.

Why This Standard Exists

Governed identities are designed to outlive individual owners,
systems, platforms, and operational environments.

Governance must therefore preserve continuity beyond individual
lifecycle events.

CIS exists because Identity Continuity represents the ninth governable
space of OBIDENITY® — Origin-Bound Identity Governance Architecture.
