Canonical Definition

Consequence-Bearing Perception Integrity Module (CBPIM) defines the structural
conditions under which operational perception affecting real operational outcomes,
consequence-bearing sensory interpretation, runtime operational reality recognition,
distributed perception awareness, and adaptive perception continuity remain materially
stable, traceable, and operationally governable across runtime environments.

A system satisfies CBPIM only if:
  • consequence-bearing operational perception remains materially governable
  • runtime operational reality recognition preserves operational alignment
  • consequence-bearing sensory interpretation remains operationally visible
  • distributed perception awareness remains coherent
  • consequence-bearing perception distortion does not silently destabilize
  • operational continuity


A system that preserves runtime execution while operational perception affecting real
operational outcomes materially

destabilizes does not satisfy CBPIM.

Module Operational Role

CBPIM defines the consequence-bearing perception governance layer of OPIS by preserving
governance-valid operational reality recognition across runtime systems affecting real
operational outcomes.

Module Operational Space

CBPIM governs the consequence-bearing perception space of OPIS by preserving runtime
operational-reality visibility, consequence-bearing sensory continuity, distributed
perception coordination, adaptive perception governance, and operational perception
stability across runtime systems.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:
  • consequence-bearing operational perception
  • runtime operational reality recognition
  • consequence-bearing sensory interpretation
  • distributed perception awareness
  • adaptive perception governance
  • operational-reality continuity


Its function is to ensure that operational perception affecting real operational outcomes
remains materially governable strongly enough to preserve valid runtime operational reality
alignment across execution environments.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Consequence-Bearing Perception Object

The organization must define which operational perception structures affecting real
operational outcomes require governance preservation.

This may include:
  • consequence-bearing runtime sensing
  • operational-reality interpretation
  • escalation-sensitive environmental perception
  • distributed perception awareness
  • adaptive sensory prioritization
  • orchestration-level perception continuity
  • operational-critical perception pathways


2. Define Consequence-Bearing Perception Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which operational perception affecting real
operational outcomes remains materially stable and operationally aligned.

This includes:
  • runtime operational-reality continuity
  • sensory interpretation visibility
  • distributed perception coherence
  • adaptive perception stability
  • governance-valid operational perception


3. Define Consequence-Bearing Perception Instability Detection Logic

The system must define how materially unstable consequence-bearing operational perception or
operational-reality distortion is identified.

This may include:
  • operational hallucination
  • environmental misinterpretation
  • runtime perception instability
  • distributed perception fragmentation
  • consequence-bearing perception invisibility


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for materially unstable consequence-bearing
perception conditions.

Governance response may include:
  • perception recalibration
  • operational reality verification
  • distributed synchronization restoration
  • adaptive perception restriction
  • operational review activation
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of consequence-bearing perception
continuity and operational- reality distortion states. A system must not remain
perception-valid if operational perception affecting real operational outcomes materially
destabilizes while systems continue assuming governance-valid runtime operational reality
alignment remains preserved.
