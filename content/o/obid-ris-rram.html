Canonical Definition

Responsibility Role Assignment Module (RRAM) defines the structural
conditions under which responsibility roles associated with a governed
identity are established, assigned, recorded, and continuously
governable throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

RRAM governs responsibility role assignment.

The module establishes the governance conditions required to ensure
that every governance responsibility is assigned to clearly
identifiable and legitimately authorized participants.

Responsibility must never be assumed.

Responsibility must be explicitly assigned.

RRAM governs that assignment.

Module Operational Role

RRAM defines the responsibility role assignment layer of RIS.

Its role is to establish legitimate governance responsibilities before
actions are performed on behalf of a governed identity.

Module Operational Space

RRAM governs:

  • responsibility role assignment,
  • governance responsibility,
  • accountable role assignment,
  • responsibility attribution,
  • governance role definition,
  • operational responsibility,
  • responsibility registration,
  • identity responsibility assignment.


Module Function

The module applies wherever organizations must establish and govern
responsibility roles associated with a governed identity.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Responsibility Object

Identify the governed identity and the responsibility
requiring assignment.

2. Define Responsibility Assignment Conditions

Establish governance conditions governing how responsibility roles are
assigned, approved, documented, and maintained.

3. Define Assignment Failure Logic

Identify conditions where responsibility becomes ambiguous,
duplicated, missing, unauthorized, or governance-invalid.

4. Define Governance Response

Define governance actions for assignment, correction, reassignment,
escalation, or governance review.

5. Preserve Responsibility Records

Preserve responsibility assignments, governance decisions, supporting
evidence, assignment history, and accountability records.

Use Case 1 — Enterprise Identity Governance

Scenario

An organization appoints an identity governance manager responsible
for a strategic digital identity.

Application

RRAM assigns the governance responsibility before operational
management begins.

Result

Responsibility is clearly attributable, documented, and
continuously governable.

Use Case 2 — Autonomous AI Identity

Scenario

An enterprise deploys a governed AI identity managed by multiple
operational teams.

Application

RRAM assigns defined governance responsibilities to each
authorized participant.

Result

Every governance responsibility remains clearly attributable
throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

Canonical Closing Statement

Governance begins when responsibility becomes explicit. Responsibility
Role Assignment ensures that every governed identity is connected to
clearly defined and continuously governable responsibility from the
very beginning of its lifecycle.
