Governed Space

Identity Auditability

Core Question

Can every governance aspect of a governed identity be
independently verified?

Canonical Definition

Auditability Integrity Standard (AUDIS) defines the structural
conditions under which every governance aspect of a governed identity
remains independently reviewable, verifiable, evidence-supported,
traceable, and continuously governable throughout the complete
identity lifecycle.

AUDIS governs identity auditability.

The standard establishes the governance conditions required to ensure
that every governance decision, relationship, responsibility,
transfer, continuity event, and supporting evidence associated with a
governed identity can be independently examined and verified.

Governance creates trust.

Auditability proves that trust.

AUDIS governs that proof.

Governance Flow Position

Origin → Identity → Ownership → Authority → Permission →
Responsibility → Provenance → Transfer → Continuity → Auditability

Standard Abstract

Trustworthy governance requires independent verification.

Every governed identity should be capable of demonstrating how it was
created, governed, modified, transferred, and preserved throughout
its lifecycle.

Without governed auditability:

  • governance cannot be independently verified,
  • evidence loses operational value,
  • accountability weakens,
  • compliance becomes uncertain,
  • long-term governance trust declines.


AUDIS establishes the governance conditions required for transparent,
evidence-based, and continuously verifiable identity governance.

Operational Role

Auditability Integrity Standard provides the governance layer
responsible for governing how the complete governance lifecycle of a
governed identity remains independently reviewable and
continuously verifiable.

Its role is to ensure that every governance element remains supported
by authentic evidence, complete traceability, and transparent
governance records.

Why This Standard Exists

Governance without verification depends on trust alone.

Trustworthy governance requires evidence.

Auditability transforms governance claims into independently
verifiable governance reality.

AUDIS exists because Identity Auditability represents the tenth
