Governed Space

Identity Authority

Core Question

Who has legitimate authority over a governed identity?

Canonical Definition

Authority Integrity Standard (AIS) defines the structural conditions
under which authority over a governed identity remains legitimate,
attributable, verifiable, limited, delegated, and continuously
governable throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

AIS governs identity authority.

The standard establishes the governance conditions required to ensure
that authority over a governed identity remains legitimate, validated,
traceable, appropriately delegated, and continuously governable.

Ownership establishes who owns the identity.

Authority establishes who may govern it.

AIS governs that authority.

Governance Flow Position

Origin → Identity → Ownership → Authority → Permission →
Responsibility → Provenance → Transfer → Continuity → Auditability

Standard Abstract

Ownership alone does not determine who may act.

Organizations delegate authority.

Individuals authorize representatives.

Systems assign operational control.

Without governed authority:

  • governance legitimacy becomes unclear,
  • authority may exceed intended limits,
  • delegated control becomes difficult to verify,
  • accountability weakens,
  • governance trust deteriorates.


AIS establishes the governance conditions required for legitimate,
controlled, and continuously governable authority.

Operational Role

Authority Integrity Standard provides the governance layer responsible
for governing how authority over a governed identity is established,
delegated, validated, limited, and exercised.

Its role is to ensure that every authority relationship remains
legitimate, attributable, verifiable, and governance-approved.

Operational Space

AIS governs:

  • authority assignment,
  • authority delegation,
  • authority validation,
  • authority limitations,
  • authority escalation,
  • governance authority,
  • identity control,
  • legitimate authority.


This governed space ensures that authority remains continuously
compatible with trustworthy identity governance.

Why This Standard Exists

Every governed identity requires legitimate authority before
governance actions may be performed.

Without governed authority, permissions cannot be legitimately
granted, responsibilities cannot be properly assigned, and governance
integrity cannot be preserved.

AIS exists because Identity Authority represents the fourth governable
space of OBIDENITY® — Origin-Bound Identity Governance Architecture.

Foundational Principle

Trustworthy identity requires trustworthy authority. Authority may be
delegated, but its legitimacy, boundaries, and accountability must
remain continuously governable.

Canonical Closing Statement

Ownership establishes who possesses a governed identity. Authority
determines who may legitimately act on its behalf. Authority Integrity
preserves that distinction by ensuring every exercise of authority
remains legitimate, traceable, and continuously governable throughout
the complete identity lifecycle.
