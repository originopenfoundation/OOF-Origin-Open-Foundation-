Canonical Definition

Decision Traceability Module (DTM) defines the structural conditions under which runtime
operational decisions, adaptive decision evolution, distributed decision coordination,
and consequence-bearing decision states remain materially reconstructable, reviewable,
attributable, and operationally governable across runtime environments.

A system satisfies DTM only if:
  • operational decisions remain materially traceable
  • runtime decision pathways remain reconstructable
  • adaptive decision evolution remains reviewable
  • distributed decision coordination preserves attribution continuity
  • hidden decision states do not destabilize operational legitimacy


A system that preserves runtime execution while operational decisions materially lose
traceability does not satisfy DTM.

Module Operational Role

DTM defines the decision traceability layer of ODIS by preserving reconstructable
runtime decision continuity across operational environments.

Module Operational Space

DTM governs the decision traceability space of ODIS by preserving operational decision
reconstruction, runtime attribution continuity, adaptive decision reviewability,
distributed coordination traceability, and consequence-bearing decision visibility
across runtime systems.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:
  • operational decision traceability
  • runtime decision reconstruction
  • adaptive decision reviewability
  • distributed attribution continuity
  • consequence-bearing decision visibility
  • governance-valid decision accountability


Its function is to ensure that runtime operational decisions remain materially
reconstructable strongly enough to preserve operational legitimacy across execution
environments.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Decision Traceability Object

The organization must define which operational decision structures require traceability
governance.

This may include:
  • autonomous runtime decisions
  • orchestration-level decision routing
  • adaptive decision evolution
  • distributed operational coordination
  • escalation-sensitive runtime choices
  • consequence-bearing operational actions
  • delegated decision pathways


2. Define Decision Traceability Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which operational decisions remain materially
reconstructable and operationally aligned.

This includes:
  • runtime decision visibility
  • attribution continuity
  • adaptive decision reviewability
  • distributed coordination traceability
  • governance-valid reconstruction capability


3. Define Decision Visibility Failure Detection Logic

The system must define how materially weakened decision traceability or hidden
decision-state instability is identified.

This may include:
  • unreconstructable runtime decisions
  • attribution fragmentation
  • adaptive decision opacity
  • distributed coordination invisibility
  • consequence-bearing hidden decision pathways


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for materially weakened decision traceability
conditions.

Governance response may include:
  • reconstruction enforcement
  • attribution synchronization
  • traceability escalation review
  • adaptive decision restriction
  • operational review activation
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of operational decision continuity and
hidden-decision states. A system must not remain decision-valid if runtime operational
decisions materially lose traceability while systems continue assuming governance-valid
operational legitimacy remains preserved.

Use Case 1 — Distributed AI Decision

Infrastructure

Scenario
A distributed AI environment continuously generates adaptive runtime decisions across
orchestration systems and autonomous operational infrastructures.

Application
DTM preserves governance-valid decision reconstruction through runtime attribution
governance and adaptive decision reviewability continuity.

Result
The organization gains stronger operational decision accountability and reduced hidden
runtime decision opacity across distributed AI systems.

Use Case 2 — Autonomous Operational Control

Environment

Scenario
An autonomous operational infrastructure continuously coordinates realtime decisions across
distributed runtime systems and adaptive execution environments.

Application
DTM preserves governance-valid runtime decision visibility through distributed coordination
traceability and operational reconstruction governance.

Result
The environment gains stronger runtime accountability and reduced hidden operational
decision instability across autonomous operational systems.

Canonical Closing Statement

If runtime operational decisions cannot remain materially reconstructable across
execution environments, systems may preserve operational continuity while decision
accountability progressively loses governance-valid visibility.
