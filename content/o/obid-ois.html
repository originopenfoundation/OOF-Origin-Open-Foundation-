Origin Integrity

Core Question

Where did the governed identity originate?

Canonical Definition

Origin Integrity Standard (OIS) defines the structural conditions
under which the origin of a governed identity remains authentic,
attributable, verifiable, traceable, and continuously governable
throughout its complete lifecycle.

OIS governs origin integrity.

The standard establishes the governance conditions required to ensure
that every governed identity begins from a legitimate, verifiable, and
attributable origin before identity, ownership, authority, permission,
responsibility, provenance, transfer, continuity, or auditability can
be established.

Every governed identity begins with origin.

Governance preserves trust in that origin.

OIS governs that origin.

Governance Flow Position

Origin → Identity → Ownership → Authority → Permission →
Responsibility → Provenance → Transfer → Continuity → Auditability

Standard Abstract

Every governed identity begins somewhere.

Without a trustworthy origin:

  • identity cannot be reliably established,
  • ownership cannot be confidently assigned,
  • authority cannot be legitimately delegated,
  • provenance cannot be continuously reconstructed,
  • long-term trust becomes difficult to preserve.


OIS establishes the governance conditions required for trustworthy,
attributable, and continuously governable identity origin.

Operational Role

Origin Integrity Standard provides the governance layer responsible
for establishing the legitimate beginning of every governed identity.

Its role is to ensure that identity origin remains authentic,
verifiable, attributable, and continuously governable throughout the
complete identity lifecycle.
