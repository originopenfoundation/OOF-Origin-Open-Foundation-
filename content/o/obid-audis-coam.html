Canonical Definition

Compliance Audit Module (COAM) defines the structural conditions under
which a governed identity is independently assessed for compliance
with established governance requirements while preserving
transparency, traceability, evidence integrity, and continuous
governability throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

COAM governs compliance auditing.

The module establishes the governance conditions required to ensure
that every governed identity can be independently evaluated against
applicable governance rules, standards, policies, and
compliance requirements.

Governance defines requirements.

Audit confirms compliance.

COAM governs that confirmation.

Module Operational Role

COAM defines the compliance audit layer of AUDIS.

Its role is to verify that the governance of a governed identity
continuously satisfies established governance requirements.

Module Operational Space

COAM governs:

  • compliance auditing,
  • governance compliance,
  • standards compliance,
  • policy verification,
  • governance assessment,
  • compliance evidence,
  • compliance traceability,
  • governance conformity.


Module Function

The module applies wherever organizations must independently evaluate
whether a governed identity complies with applicable
governance requirements.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Compliance Audit Object

Identify the governed identity and the governance requirements
requiring compliance verification.

2. Define Compliance Conditions

Establish governance conditions governing applicable standards,
policies, evidence requirements, verification criteria, and
governance approval.

3. Define Compliance Failure Logic

Identify conditions where governance becomes non-compliant,
incomplete, inconsistent, unverifiable, or governance- invalid.

4. Define Governance Response

Define governance actions for corrective measures, revalidation,
investigation, governance improvement, suspension, or escalation.

5. Preserve Compliance Audit Records

Preserve compliance reports, governance decisions, supporting
evidence, audit findings, corrective actions, and
verification history.

Use Case 1 — Enterprise Identity Governance

Scenario

An organization performs a periodic governance compliance audit of its
strategic enterprise identities.

Application

COAM evaluates compliance with established governance standards and
internal identity governance policies.

Result

Compliance status becomes independently verifiable and supported by
governance evidence.

Use Case 2 — AI Identity Governance

Scenario

A governed AI identity is evaluated before deployment into a regulated
operational environment.

Application

COAM verifies that the identity satisfies all required governance and
compliance conditions before approval.

Result

The governed identity enters operation with independently verified
governance compliance.

Canonical Closing Statement

Compliance is meaningful only when it can be independently
demonstrated. Compliance Audit preserves governance confidence by
ensuring that every governed identity continuously satisfies its
governance obligations through transparent and evidence-based
verification throughout the complete identity lifecycle.
