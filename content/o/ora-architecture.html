ORA™

Operational Reality Architecture

Official Operational Reality Architecture for Autonomous Systems,
AI Systems, Organizations, Critical Infrastructure, and Complex
Operational Environments

ORA™ serves as the official OOF® operational reality architecture
used for operational reality governance, operational state
identification, operational diagnostics, operational architecture
navigation, and operational problem placement across human,
organizational, AI, autonomous, industrial, and cyber-physical
environments.

Operational Reality Architecture™ defines the governance architecture
required to understand, govern, validate, coordinate, and preserve
operational reality throughout the complete operational lifecycle.

ORA™ consists of 12 Core Parent Standards forming the primary
governance backbone of operational reality.

Additional compatible standards, bridge standards, and specialized
operational standards may extend the architecture when new
operational spaces are identified. However, the standards listed
below form the official core operational structure of ORA™.

Canonical Definition

Operational Reality Architecture™ is the governance architecture
that defines the structural conditions under which operational
reality remains valid, observable, governable, accountable,
coordinated, auditable, recoverable, and operationally trustworthy
across complex systems.

Core Question

How can operational reality remain continuously valid, governable,
and auditable throughout execution?

Governance Flow

Reality → Boundary → Responsibility → Risk → Validity →
Constraint → Escalation → Authority → Decision → Dependency →
Evidence → Recovery

Core Parent Standards

1. Operational Reality Standard (ORS)

Governed Space: Operational Reality

Core Question: What is actually happening in
operational reality?

2. Operational Boundary Synchronization Standard (OBS)

Governed Space: Operational Boundaries

Core Question: Where are the operational boundaries
and are they synchronized?

3. Operational Responsibility Continuity Standard (ORCS)

Governed Space: Operational Responsibility

Core Question: Who remains operationally
responsible throughout execution?

4. Operational Risk & Trust Integrity Standard (ORTIS)

Governed Space: Operational Risk & Trust

Core Question: What operational risks exist and can the system
still be trusted?

5. Operational Validity Continuity Standard (OVCS)

Governed Space: Operational Validity

Core Question: Does the operational state remain
valid over time?

6. Operational Constraint Integrity Standard (OCNS)

Governed Space: Operational Constraints

Core Question: Which operational constraints must
never be violated?

7. Operational Escalation Integrity Standard (OESIS)

Governed Space: Operational Escalation

Core Question: When and how should operational
escalation occur?

8. Operational Authority Integrity Standard (OAIS)

Governed Space: Operational Authority

Core Question: Who has legitimate authority to act?

9. Operational Decision Integrity Standard (ODIS)

Governed Space: Operational Decision Integrity

Core Question: How are operational decisions made,
justified, and preserved?

10. Operational Dependency & Coordination Standard (ODCS)

Governed Space: Operational Dependencies & Coordination

Core Question: How are operational dependencies
coordinated across the system?

11. Operational Evidence & Auditability Standard (OEAS)

Governed Space: Operational Evidence & Auditability

Core Question: Can operational actions be
reconstructed, verified, and proven?

12. System Recovery & Continuity Standard (SRCS)

Governed Space: System Recovery & Operational Continuity

Core Question: How can operational continuity be restored
after disruption?

Compatible OOF Architectures

ORA™ interoperates with multiple OOF® architectures that govern
complementary operational spaces.

RIS™ — Runtime Integrity Standard provides the foundational
runtime integrity layer used across OOF architectures.

CLIA® — Cognitive Governance Intelligence Architecture governs
cognition, reasoning, interpretation, and cognitive integrity during
operational execution.

MGIA™ — Memory Governance Intelligence Architecture governs
memory continuity, traceability, and memory integrity supporting
operational environments.

AGA™ — Accountability Governance Architecture governs
accountability, responsibility allocation, delegation, and ownership
relationships.

GOA™ — Governance Architecture provides the constitutional
governance layer defining how governance itself is structured.

AIG™ and ASGA™ extend ORA™ into AI-governed and autonomous
operational environments.

Architectural Position

ORA™ governs the complete operational lifecycle.

The architecture progresses through:

Operational Reality → Operational Boundaries → Operational
Responsibility → Operational Risk → Operational Validity →
Operational Constraints → Operational Escalation → Operational
Authority → Operational Decision → Operational Dependencies →
Operational Evidence → Operational Recovery

Together these standards govern how operational reality remains
valid before, during, and after execution.

Canonical Principle

ORA™ does not govern what a system is intended to achieve. ORA™
governs whether operational reality remains valid, observable,
coordinated, accountable, auditable, and recoverable while the system
executes.
