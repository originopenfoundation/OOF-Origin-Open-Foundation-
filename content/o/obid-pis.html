Governed Space

Identity Permissions

Core Question

Who may legitimately access, use, modify, or interact with a
governed identity?

Canonical Definition

Permission Integrity Standard (PIS) defines the structural conditions
under which permissions associated with a governed identity remain
legitimate, attributable, verifiable, enforceable, revocable, and
continuously governable throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

PIS governs identity permissions.

The standard establishes the governance conditions required to ensure
that every permission granted over a governed identity remains
explicitly authorized, appropriately limited, continuously verifiable,
and governance-approved.

Authority determines who may govern.

Permissions determine what may be done.

PIS governs those permissions.

Governance Flow Position

Origin → Identity → Ownership → Authority → Permission →
Responsibility → Provenance → Transfer → Continuity → Auditability

Standard Abstract

A legitimate authority does not automatically possess
unlimited rights.

Every governance action requires appropriate permission.

Permissions define operational access.

Permissions define operational use.

Permissions define operational modification.

Without governed permissions:

  • unauthorized access may occur,
  • excessive privileges may emerge,
  • governance boundaries become unclear,
  • operational misuse becomes difficult to prevent,
  • governance trust deteriorates.


PIS establishes the governance conditions required for legitimate,
controlled, and continuously governable permissions.

Operational Role

Permission Integrity Standard provides the governance layer
responsible for governing how permissions over a governed identity are
granted, validated, exercised, revoked, and continuously governed.

Its role is to ensure that every permission remains legitimate,
traceable, enforceable, and governance-approved.

Operational Space

PIS governs:

  • permission assignment,
  • access permissions,
  • usage permissions,
  • modification permissions,
  • permission revocation,
  • permission governance,
  • authorization rights,
  • controlled identity access.


This governed space ensures that permissions remain continuously
compatible with trustworthy identity governance.

Why This Standard Exists

Authority identifies who may govern.

Permissions determine what that authority may actually do.

Without governed permissions, legitimate authority may still produce
unauthorized actions.

PIS exists because Identity Permissions represent the fifth governable
space of OBIDENITY® — Origin-Bound Identity Governance Architecture.

Foundational Principle

Trustworthy authority requires trustworthy permissions. Every
permission must remain explicitly authorized, appropriately limited,
continuously verifiable, and continuously governable throughout the
complete identity lifecycle.

Canonical Closing Statement

Authority establishes who may govern. Permission determines what may
be done. Permission Integrity preserves governance trust by ensuring
that every action performed on a governed identity remains
legitimately authorized throughout the complete identity lifecycle.
