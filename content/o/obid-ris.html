Governed Space

Identity Responsibility

Core Question

Who remains responsible for a governed identity and its
governed actions?

Canonical Definition

Responsibility Integrity Standard (RIS) defines the structural
conditions under which responsibility associated with a governed
identity remains legitimate, attributable, verifiable, continuous, and
continuously governable throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

RIS governs identity responsibility.

The standard establishes the governance conditions required to ensure
that every governed identity has clearly attributable, continuously
traceable, and governance-valid responsibility throughout its
complete lifecycle.

Authority determines who may act.

Responsibility determines who remains accountable for those actions.

RIS governs that responsibility.

Governance Flow Position

Origin → Identity → Ownership → Authority → Permission →
Responsibility → Provenance → Transfer → Continuity → Auditability

Standard Abstract

Every governed action produces responsibility.

Every governance decision creates accountable participants.

Without governed responsibility:

  • accountability becomes fragmented,
  • responsibility may become ambiguous,
  • governance obligations may be avoided,
  • operational trust declines,
  • governance reconstruction becomes difficult.


RIS establishes the governance conditions required for legitimate,
attributable, and continuously governable responsibility.

Operational Role

Responsibility Integrity Standard provides the governance layer
responsible for governing how responsibility is assigned, shared,
transferred, preserved, and continuously governed throughout the
complete identity lifecycle.

Its role is to ensure that every governance responsibility remains
legitimate, attributable, traceable, and governance- approved.

Operational Space

RIS governs:

  • responsibility assignment,
  • shared responsibility,
  • responsibility escalation,
  • responsibility continuity,
  • consequence attribution,
  • governance responsibility,
  • accountable participation,
  • identity responsibility.


This governed space ensures that responsibility remains continuously
compatible with trustworthy identity governance.

Why This Standard Exists

A governed identity cannot exist without clearly
defined responsibility.

Ownership identifies who possesses the identity.

Authority determines who may govern it.

Permissions determine what may be done.

Responsibility determines who remains answerable for
governance outcomes.

RIS exists because Identity Responsibility represents the sixth
governable space of OBIDENITY® — Origin-Bound Identity
Governance Architecture.

Foundational Principle

Trustworthy identity requires trustworthy responsibility. Every
governed identity must remain connected to clearly attributable
responsibility throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

Canonical Closing Statement

Authority enables action. Responsibility preserves accountability.
Responsibility Integrity ensures that every governed identity remains
connected to those responsible for its governance, decisions, and
consequences throughout the complete identity lifecycle.
