About Authority Integrity Standard (AIS)

Governing Identity Authority

Authority Integrity Standard (AIS) defines the governance conditions
under which authority over a governed identity remains legitimate,
attributable, verifiable, delegated, and continuously governable
throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

A governed identity may have an owner.

It may also have one or more authorized authorities acting on
its behalf.

AIS governs this identity governance space.

What Is Identity Authority?

Identity authority is the governed relationship that determines who
may legitimately control, administer, represent, manage, or exercise
governance rights over a governed identity while preserving governance
integrity, accountability, and trust.

It enables organizations to establish legitimate authority, delegate
governance rights, validate authority relationships, preserve
authority boundaries, and ensure that every governance action remains
authorized throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

Canonical Definition

Authority Integrity Standard (AIS) defines the structural conditions
under which authority over a governed identity remains legitimate,
attributable, verifiable, delegated, and continuously governable
throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

Why AIS Exists

Ownership alone does not determine who may legitimately act.

Organizations delegate authority.

Individuals authorize representatives.

Digital identities may be governed by designated administrators.

Without governed authority, governance actions become ambiguous,
permissions lose legitimacy, accountability weakens, and
trust deteriorates.

AIS exists because Identity Authority represents the fourth governable
space of OBIDENITY® — Origin-Bound Identity Governance Architecture.

What AIS Governs

AIS governs the structural conditions under which identity
authority remains:

  • legitimate,
  • attributable,
  • verifiable,
  • delegated,
  • bounded,
  • governable.


Rather than governing organizational hierarchies or legal
authorization frameworks, AIS governs the governance conditions
required for trustworthy identity authority.

Architectural Position

Authority Integrity Standard serves as the fourth governance space of
OBIDENITY® — Origin-Bound Identity Governance Architecture.

It establishes the governance foundation that determines who may
legitimately exercise governance over a governed identity before
permissions are granted, responsibilities are assigned, provenance is
established, transfers occur, continuity is preserved, and
auditability is achieved.

Without governed authority, trustworthy identity governance cannot
be sustained.

Canonical Closing Statement

Ownership answers who possesses a governed identity. Authority answers
who may legitimately govern it. Authority Integrity preserves this
distinction by ensuring that every exercise of authority remains
legitimate throughout the complete identity lifecycle.
