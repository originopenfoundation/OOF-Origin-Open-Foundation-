Canonical Definition

Ownership Conflict Resolution Module (OCRM) defines the structural
conditions under which ownership conflicts involving a governed
identity are identified, evaluated, resolved, documented, and
continuously governable throughout the complete identity lifecycle.

OCRM governs ownership conflict resolution.

The module establishes the governance conditions required to ensure
that competing ownership claims are resolved through legitimate,
transparent, evidence-based, and governance-valid processes.

Ownership conflicts may occur.

Governance resolves them.

OCRM governs that resolution.

Module Operational Role

OCRM defines the ownership conflict resolution layer of OWIS.

Its role is to preserve legitimate ownership by governing the
resolution of conflicting ownership claims.

Module Operational Space

OCRM governs:

  • ownership conflict resolution,
  • ownership dispute management,
  • competing ownership claims,
  • ownership investigation,
  • governance-based ownership resolution,
  • ownership reconciliation,
  • conflict documentation,
  • legitimate ownership restoration.


Module Function

The module applies wherever competing ownership claims or ownership
disputes require governance-based resolution.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Ownership Conflict Object

Identify the governed identity affected by an ownership conflict.

2. Define Conflict Resolution Conditions

Establish governance conditions governing ownership investigation,
evidence evaluation, decision-making, and conflict resolution.

3. Define Conflict Failure Logic

Identify conditions where ownership conflicts remain unresolved,
unsupported, inconsistent, biased, or governance- invalid.

4. Define Governance Response

Define governance actions for investigation, mediation, ownership
determination, reassignment, escalation, or further review.

5. Preserve Conflict Resolution Records

Preserve ownership claims, governance decisions, supporting evidence,
investigation records, and conflict history.

Use Case 1 — Competing AI Ownership Claims

Scenario

Two organizations claim ownership of the same governed AI identity.

Application

OCRM evaluates the competing ownership claims using
governance-approved evidence and resolution procedures.

Result

The legitimate ownership relationship is established through a
transparent and governance-valid process.

Use Case 2 — Enterprise Digital Identity Dispute

Scenario

An internal ownership dispute arises following
organizational restructuring.

Application

OCRM governs the investigation and resolution of the ownership
conflict before governance rights are reassigned.

Result

Ownership integrity is restored while preserving governance trust
and accountability.

Canonical Closing Statement

Ownership is strongest when it remains legitimate under challenge.
Ownership Conflict Resolution protects governance integrity by
transforming competing claims into one evidence-based,
governance-valid ownership relationship.
