About the Capability Readiness Governance

Standard (CRGS)

Canonical Definition

Capability Readiness Governance Standard (CRGS) defines the
structural conditions under which actors, systems, organizations,
contractors, agents, tools, resources, qualifications, competencies,
permissions, information, and operational environments remain
capable of performing assigned responsibilities in a valid,
traceable, governable, accountable, and operationally
reliable manner.

CRGS governs capability readiness.

The standard establishes the foundational conditions required to
determine whether a responsible actor is actually capable of
performing the responsibility assigned to them.

What This Standard Is

CRGS is a parent standard of the Accountability
Governance Architecture (AGA™).

It governs:

  • capability readiness
  • qualification readiness
  • competency readiness
  • certification readiness
  • execution readiness
  • tool readiness
  • resource readiness
  • operational readiness
  • Human-AI capability readiness
  • agent capability readiness


CRGS establishes the governance layer responsible for determining
whether execution capability actually exists before
operational activities begin.

What This Standard Is Not

CRGS is not:

  • a responsibility standard
  • an authority standard
  • a delegation standard
  • a validation standard
  • an escalation standard
  • a control standard
  • an oversight standard


CRGS does not determine who should perform a task.

CRGS determines whether the assigned actor is capable of
performing the task.

Why This Standard Exists

Many operational failures occur because governance assumes
capability instead of verifying capability.

Organizations often verify:

  • authority
  • responsibility
  • approvals
  • permissions


while failing to verify:

  • qualifications
  • competencies
  • certifications
  • tools
  • resources
  • operational conditions
  • information availability
  • execution readiness


As a result:

  • accidents occur
  • quality failures occur
  • compliance failures occur
  • contractor failures occur
  • certification failures occur
  • AI failures occur


The challenge is not assigning responsibility.

The challenge is ensuring capability exists.

CRGS exists because capability readiness has become a
distinct governance space.

Core Insight

Responsibility creates obligation. Capability enables execution.

Operational Architecture Space

CRGS defines the operational architecture space for:

  • capability readiness governance
  • qualification governance
  • competency governance
  • certification readiness
  • execution readiness
  • tool readiness
  • resource readiness
  • operational readiness
  • Human-AI capability governance
  • agent capability governance
  • execution capability validation


This space exists because governance must verify capability
before expecting execution.

CRGS governs whether capability remains sufficient.

General Use Case 1 — Contractor & Workforce Environment

Scenario

A company assigns a contractor to perform a critical operational
task requiring specific qualifications, certifications, tools,
documentation, and equipment.

Application

CRGS evaluates whether the assigned contractor possesses the actual
capability required for execution.

The evaluation may include:

  • qualifications
  • certifications
  • competencies
  • equipment availability
  • tool readiness
  • documentation access
  • operational resources


Result

Governance gains visibility into whether the assigned actor is
genuinely capable of performing the task.

General Use Case 2 — Human-AI Operational Environment

Scenario

An orchestration platform assigns responsibilities to AI agents
responsible for executing operational tasks.

Application

CRGS evaluates whether the AI agent possesses the required:

  • permissions
  • tools
  • models
  • system access
  • operational resources
  • execution capabilities


Result

Governance gains visibility into whether the AI agent is actually
capable of performing the assigned responsibility.

Architectural Position

Within AGA™, CRGS serves as the seventh foundational
governance layer.

The architecture progresses:

Relationship → Authority → Authority Delegation → Authority
Validation → Authority Escalation → Responsibility → Capability →
Control → Oversight → Evidence → Accountability → Consequence

CRGS governs whether a responsible actor is capable of execution
before governance evaluates operational control, oversight,
accountability, or consequences.

Strategic Importance

CRGS addresses one of the most common governance failures
in operational reality:

The assumption that assigned responsibility automatically
implies execution capability.

This assumption frequently proves false.

An actor may possess:

  • authority
  • responsibility
  • permissions
  • approvals


while lacking:

  • competency
  • qualification
  • resources
  • equipment
  • operational readiness


CRGS exists to expose this gap before operational failure occurs.

Closing Definition

Capability readiness is not the existence of responsibility.
Capability readiness is the governed condition under which a
responsible actor possesses the qualifications, competencies,
certifications, tools, resources, permissions, information, and
operational conditions required for successful execution.

Governance cannot require execution from actors that are not capable
of execution. Before responsibility can be fulfilled, capability
must first be demonstrated.
