Canonical Definition System

CGRM — Crisis Governance & Responsibility Standard
defines the minimum operational conditions required to ensure that
authority, responsibility, and decision-making remain clear, traceable,
and enforceable during crisis conditions.

A crisis is any operational state where standard governance is
insufficient to ensure system stability, safety, or integrity.

A system is considered governed only if responsibility is explicitly
defined and cannot be bypassed.

A. Standard Abstract

CGRM establishes a governance layer ensuring that responsibility,
authority, and decision-making remain operational under stress,
uncertainty, or failure conditions.

The objective is to ensure that:
  • responsibility remains anchored
  • authority remains defined
  • decisions remain traceable
  • governance remains enforceable
This standard does not replace governance. It ensures governance remains
operational when it is most needed.

B. Core Conditions (Non-Negotiable)

All five conditions MUST be present simultaneously. Partial
implementation is not valid.

All conditions MUST operate continuously and in real time during crisis
conditions.

1. Authority & Responsibility
The system MUST define:
  • Decision Authority (who decides)
  • Execution Authority (who executes)
  • Responsibility Holder (who is accountable)
Responsibility MUST be:
  • identity-bound
  • non-anonymous
 2. Decision Control
The system MUST define:
  • decision levels
  • authority scope
  • decision limits
 3. Escalation & Override
The system MUST define:
  • escalation triggers
  • authority transition rules
  • fallback authority
For AI-enabled systems:
  • human override MUST exist
  • override MUST be immediately executable
 4. Traceability & Audit
All decisions MUST be:
  • recorded
  • time-stamped
  • attributable
 5. Integrity & Enforcement
The system MUST ensure:
  • governance cannot be bypassed
  • violations are detectable
  • system integrity is observable

C. AI Requirements

If AI systems are used:
  • human accountability MUST be defined
  • AI cannot be a Responsibility Holder
  • decisions MUST be traceable
  • override MUST be enforceable

D. Prohibited Condition

No system may operate in crisis mode without defined responsibility,
traceable decisions, and enforceable governance.

E. Compatibility Statement

A system may declare:

CGRM Compatible

only if all core conditions are actively satisfied.

Compatibility is:
  • operational
  • dynamic
  • revocable

F. Structural Principle

Governance must not collapse under pressure.
It must become stricter.

Canonical Closing Statement

Crisis does not remove responsibility.
It makes it non-negotiable.
