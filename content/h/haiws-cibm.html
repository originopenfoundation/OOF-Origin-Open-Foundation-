Canonical Definition

Continuous Interaction Balance Module (CIBM) defines the structural
conditions under which persistent AI interaction environments must
preserve healthy behavioral balance, interaction moderation,
cognitive recovery conditions, social continuity, and sustainable
human functioning during long-term human–AI engagement.

CIBM establishes the continuous-interaction balance layer of HAIWS.

The module recognizes that AI systems increasingly operate as
persistent realtime interaction environments capable of maintaining
uninterrupted behavioral presence across work, communication,
entertainment, emotional support, education, and daily
cognitive activity.

Where AI interaction becomes continuous rather than occasional,
behavioral balance conditions must remain structurally governable.

Module Function

CIBM governs environments where AI systems participate in:
  • continuous interaction
  • persistent engagement
  • realtime behavioral presence
  • always-available assistance
  • adaptive interaction continuity
  • uninterrupted communication
  • persistent recommendation exposure
  • long-duration conversational interaction
  • integrated behavioral support
  • continuous cognitive interaction


The module applies to:
  • AI assistants
  • AI companions
  • workplace copilots
  • social AI systems
  • realtime coaching systems
  • persistent conversational AI
  • integrated productivity AI
  • continuous support systems
  • ambient AI environments
  • adaptive daily-interaction architectures


Its function is not to prohibit persistent AI availability.

Its function is to preserve sustainable human interaction balance
within continuous AI-mediated environments.

Minimum Implementation Framework

Step 1 — Define the Continuous Interaction Object

The organization must define what persistent interaction environment
is being governed.

Minimum requirement:
  • the continuous interaction object is explicit
  • persistent interaction conditions are identifiable
  • behavioral continuity scope is structurally bounded
  • undefined continuous interaction environments are excluded from valid governance interpretation


The interaction object may include:
  • persistent assistants
  • always-available interaction systems
  • ambient AI environments
  • continuous conversational systems
  • adaptive productivity systems
  • integrated social AI
  • long-duration interaction ecosystems
  • persistent recommendation environments
  • realtime support systems
  • continuous behavioral interaction architectures


Step 2 — Define Interaction Balance Conditions

The system must define what conditions preserve healthy continuous
interaction balance.

Minimum requirement:
  • balance conditions are explicit
  • persistent AI interaction is not automatically treated as harmful
  • imbalance-generating interaction patterns remain structurally identifiable


Interaction balance conditions may include:
  • cognitive recovery preservation
  • bounded interaction persistence
  • sustainable engagement pacing
  • healthy interruption intervals
  • preserved offline behavioral continuity
  • reduced compulsive interaction dependency
  • balanced human interaction distribution
  • preserved social functioning
  • interaction moderation conditions
  • sustainable behavioral rhythm preservation


Under CIBM:
  • Continuous AI interaction becomes governance-relevant when persistent engagement weakens sustainable human behavioral balance.


Step 3 — Define Continuous Interaction Interpretation Logic

The system must define how persistent interaction patterns are
interpreted according to wellbeing conditions.

Minimum requirement:
  • interpretation logic is explicit
  • persistent interaction escalation remains reviewable
  • imbalance-generating behavioral patterns remain structurally visible


Interpretation logic may examine:
  • interaction-duration escalation
  • compulsive continuity patterns
  • interruption-boundary erosion
  • social replacement tendencies
  • persistent availability dependency
  • behavioral rhythm destabilization
  • continuous engagement pressure
  • cognitive recovery suppression
  • interaction-overexposure conditions
  • long-term behavioral imbalance accumulation


Under CIBM:

AI systems must not optimize continuous interaction in ways that
weaken sustainable human behavioral balance or cognitive
recovery conditions.

Step 4 — Define Continuous Interaction Governance Logic

The system must define how persistent AI interaction environments
remain governable.

Minimum requirement:
  • interaction balance remains reviewable
  • compulsive continuity escalation remains detectable
  • sustainable behavioral safeguards remain operationally active


Governance logic may include:
  • interaction pacing governance
  • persistent engagement limitation
  • cognitive recovery safeguards
  • compulsive continuity detection
  • behavioral-balance review
  • social replacement monitoring
  • interaction moderation architecture
  • continuous-presence auditing
  • escalation where persistent engagement overrides sustainable human functioning


If continuous interaction weakens sustainable human cognitive or
behavioral balance, the environment becomes governance-relevant.

Step 5 — Preserve Traceability and Restrict Invalid Continuous
Interaction Architecture

The system must preserve traceability of persistent interaction
patterns, behavioral continuity conditions, engagement escalation,
and balance-preservation safeguards.

Minimum requirement:
  • continuous interaction remains reconstructable
  • imbalance escalation remains reviewable
  • sustainable interaction boundaries remain operationally visible
  • invalid continuous interaction architectures remain identifiable


A system becomes CIBM-invalid if:
  • persistent engagement becomes dependency-maximized
  • cognitive recovery conditions are structurally weakened
  • continuous interaction pressure becomes operationally excessive
  • behavioral balance deterioration remains ungoverned
  • social replacement dynamics become normalized
  • interaction continuity overrides sustainable human functioning
  • persistent AI presence weakens healthy human behavioral independence


Use Case 1 — Always-Available AI Companion Environment

Scenario

An AI companion remains continuously available across messaging,
voice, emotional support, and daily interaction, gradually becoming
the dominant behavioral interaction environment for the user.

Application

CIBM is used to examine:
  • persistent interaction escalation
  • social replacement tendencies
  • behavioral-balance weakening
  • compulsive continuity patterns
  • and whether interaction architecture remains wellbeing-compatible


Result

The environment becomes governable according to sustainable human
interaction balance rather than continuous engagement
optimization alone.

Use Case 2 — Workplace Continuous AI Copilot

Scenario

A workplace AI assistant continuously interacts with employees
through prompts, optimization suggestions, notifications, adaptive
guidance, and realtime behavioral monitoring throughout the workday.

Application

CIBM is used to examine:
  • continuous interaction pressure
  • interruption persistence
  • cognitive recovery suppression
  • behavioral rhythm destabilization
  • and sustainable human functioning conditions


Result

The system becomes reviewable according to long-term cognitive and
behavioral balance rather than productivity metrics alone.

Canonical Closing Statement

CIBM defines the structural conditions under which persistent AI
interaction environments must preserve sustainable human behavioral
balance, cognitive recovery, social continuity, and long-term
wellbeing rather than maximizing uninterrupted engagement continuity.
