Canonical Definition System

Canonical Definition

Evidence Accountability Standard (EAS) defines the structural
conditions under which evidence remains identifiable, attributable,
traceable, governable, accountable, reconstructable, reviewable,
verifiable, and operationally valid throughout the
evidence lifecycle.

EAS governs evidence.

The standard establishes the foundational conditions required to
determine where evidence originated, what evidence supports a
conclusion, whether evidence remains valid, how evidence can be
reconstructed, and who remains accountable for evidence integrity.

Governance produces conclusions.

Evidence supports conclusions.

EAS governs evidentiary validity.

A. Standard Abstract

Every governance environment relies on evidence.

Organizations rely on evidence.

Audits rely on evidence.

Investigations rely on evidence.

Compliance systems rely on evidence.

Certification systems rely on evidence.

AI governance systems rely on evidence.

Human-AI environments rely on evidence.

Without evidence governance:

  • conclusions become unreliable
  • investigations become weakened
  • accountability deteriorates
  • audit quality declines
  • governance transparency decreases
  • operational reality becomes difficult to verify


EAS exists to govern these conditions.

B. Core Principle

Governance conclusions remain trustworthy only when supporting
evidence remains identifiable, traceable, reconstructable,
accountable, verifiable, and operationally valid throughout
the evidence lifecycle.

D. Why This Standard Exists

Many governance failures occur because evidence itself is
not governed.

Examples include:

  • unverifiable evidence
  • missing evidence
  • manipulated evidence
  • fragmented evidence chains
  • undocumented evidence sources
  • weak audit evidence
  • AI-generated evidence ambiguity
  • governance decisions unsupported by evidence


The challenge is not reaching conclusions.

The challenge is proving conclusions.

EAS exists because evidence governance has become a
distinct governance space.

E. Evidence Accountability Integrity Logic

Evidence integrity exists only when the following remain
materially preservable:

1. Evidence Origin Integrity

Evidence remains attributable to a legitimate source.

2. Evidence Scope Integrity

Evidence remains connected to clearly defined
evidentiary boundaries.

3. Evidence Validity Integrity

Evidence remains legitimate, verifiable, and operationally valid.

4. Evidence Traceability Integrity

Evidence remains reconstructable throughout the evidence lifecycle.

5. Evidence Accountability Integrity

Evidence remains connected to accountable governance structures.

G. Difference Between Oversight and Evidence

Oversight evaluates governance.

Evidence supports governance conclusions.

Oversight asks:

Was governance monitored?

Evidence asks:

What supports the conclusion?

GOS governs oversight.

EAS governs evidence.

I. Evidence Failure Rule

Evidence failure occurs when evidence cannot be reliably identified,
reconstructed, verified, attributed, reviewed, governed, or
connected to accountable governance structures.

Examples include:

  • missing evidence
  • manipulated evidence
  • undocumented evidence origins
  • evidence-chain failures
  • unverifiable evidence
  • governance-blind evidence handling
  • AI-generated evidence uncertainty
  • unsupported governance conclusions


EAS exists to expose and govern these conditions.

J. Validity Logic

An evidence-governance environment is valid under EAS when:

  • evidence sources remain identifiable
  • evidence boundaries remain visible
  • evidence validity remains demonstrable
  • evidence history remains reconstructable
  • evidence accountability remains preservable
  • evidence remains reviewable
  • evidence governance remains operationally valid


An evidence-governance environment becomes invalid when:

  • evidence cannot be identified
  • evidence origins disappear
  • evidence validity becomes unverifiable
  • evidence history cannot be reconstructed
  • evidence accountability is lost
  • governance conclusions cannot be supported
