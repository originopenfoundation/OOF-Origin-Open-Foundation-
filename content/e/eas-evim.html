Canonical Definition

Evidence Validity Integrity Module (EVIM) defines the structural
conditions under which evidence remains authentic, legitimate,
verifiable, reliable, reviewable, governable, accountable,
reconstructable, and operationally valid throughout the
evidence lifecycle.

EVIM governs evidence validity.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine whether evidence remains trustworthy, whether evidence
accurately represents operational reality, whether evidence has been
compromised, and whether evidence remains suitable for
governance decision-making.

Module Operational Role

EVIM defines the evidence-validity governance layer of EAS.

Its role is to ensure that evidence remains trustworthy and
governance-valid throughout its lifecycle.

Module Operational Space

EVIM governs:

  • evidence validity
  • evidence authenticity
  • evidence reliability
  • evidence legitimacy
  • evidence verification
  • evidence trustworthiness
  • evidence integrity
  • evidentiary-governance validity


The module applies wherever governance requires confirmation that
evidence remains valid.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • authentic evidence
  • reliable evidence
  • verifiable evidence
  • reconstructable evidence history
  • governance-valid evidence
  • operationally trustworthy evidence


Its function is to ensure that governance can determine whether
evidence remains suitable for supporting conclusions, decisions,
findings, and governance actions.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Evidence Validity Object

The organization must define which evidence environments
require validity governance.

This may include:

  • investigations
  • audits
  • compliance systems
  • certification environments
  • legal proceedings
  • operational records
  • AI-generated evidence systems
  • Human-AI governance environments


2. Define Evidence Validity Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which evidence
remains valid.

This includes:

  • authenticity requirements
  • reliability requirements
  • verification requirements
  • accountability requirements
  • governance requirements
  • evidence-valid conditions


3. Define Validity Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how evidence-validity failures
are identified.

This may include:

  • manipulated evidence
  • altered evidence
  • unverifiable evidence
  • corrupted evidence
  • unreliable evidence
  • governance-invalid evidence conditions


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for
evidence-validity failures.

Governance response may include:

  • evidence review
  • authenticity verification
  • governance intervention
  • evidence reassessment
  • corrective actions
  • evidentiary validation
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • evidence creation
  • evidence modifications
  • validation activities
  • governance reviews
  • intervention procedures
  • resulting evidence states


An evidence-governance environment must not remain validity-valid if
materially significant evidence cannot be reconstructed, reviewed,
verified, validated, or governed.

Use Case 1 — Audit & Investigation Environment

Scenario

An investigation relies upon documents, emails, production records,
photographs, system logs, and witness statements to
support governance conclusions.

Application

EVIM evaluates whether the evidence remains authentic, reliable,
verifiable, and suitable for supporting conclusions.

Result

Governance gains visibility into the validity and trustworthiness
of evidence.

Use Case 2 — AI Evidence Environment

Scenario

An AI system generates analytical outputs, recommendations, reports,
and event records used in governance decision- making.

Application

EVIM evaluates whether AI-generated evidence remains authentic,
reliable, explainable, and suitable for governance use.

Result

Governance gains visibility into the validity of evidence across
intelligent operational ecosystems.

Canonical Closing Statement

Evidence Validity Integrity Module (EVIM) defines the structural
conditions under which evidence remains authentic, legitimate,
verifiable, reliable, reviewable, governable, accountable,
reconstructable, and operationally valid throughout the
evidence lifecycle.

Evidence may exist, but evidence that cannot be trusted cannot
support trustworthy conclusions. Evidence validity integrity
therefore becomes a foundational condition of trustworthy
evidence governance.
