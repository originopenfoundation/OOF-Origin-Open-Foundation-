1. Canonical Definition

RSVM — Resource Sustainability Validation Module is the Core Module within RCSS that governs how Resource Sustainability claims, assessments, classifications, and supporting evidence are systematically examined, tested, reconciled, bounded, validated, rejected, qualified, monitored for validity, and reassessed to determine whether a Resource System's continued operation is supportable under defined Resource availability, consumption, replenishment, depletion, dependency, environmental, economic, geographic, temporal, and intergenerational conditions.

RSVM establishes the methodological conditions under which a Resource Sustainability conclusion may be treated as supported by sufficient, relevant, traceable, and appropriately bounded evidence.

It integrates the governed findings of Resource continuity assessment, Resource consumption and replenishment sustainability, and Resource Sustainability impact and tradeoff evaluation.

It distinguishes the existence of a Sustainability claim from the validity of that claim.

It also distinguishes methodological validation from operational certification, regulatory approval, environmental compliance, and guarantees of future Resource availability.

Validation remains conditional upon the assessment boundary, evidence quality, relevant Resource conditions, assessment horizon, and the absence of unresolved material contradictions.

Sustainability Claimed ≠ Sustainability Validated.

Evidence Available ≠ Evidence Sufficient.

Methodological Validation ≠ Regulatory Approval or Certification.

Resource Sustainability Validated Within Defined Conditions ≠ Permanent Resource Sustainability.

3. Operational Question

How should Resource Sustainability conclusions be validated so that continued Resource operation is recognized as supportable only when Resource continuity, consumption, replenishment, depletion, dependencies, environmental constraints, economic viability, geographic and temporal burdens, and material tradeoffs are supported by sufficient and consistent evidence within clearly defined assessment boundaries and validity conditions?

87. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum operational RSVM implementation should contain ten governed layers.

Layer 1 — Sustainability Claim & Validation Identity

Establish the Resource System, validation object, claim identity, validation purpose, and assessment context.

Layer 2 — Validation Boundary & Horizon

Define the operational, Resource, geographic, temporal, environmental, economic, and evidence boundaries.

Layer 3 — Validation Criteria

Establish applicable Resource Sustainability criteria and their methodological basis.

Layer 4 — Resource Continuity Validation

Review the Resource Continuity Assessment Profile and its relevant evidence.

Layer 5 — Consumption & Replenishment Validation

Review the Resource Consumption & Replenishment Sustainability Profile, including depletion and replenishment conditions.

Layer 6 — Impact & Tradeoff Validation

Review the Resource Sustainability Impact & Tradeoff Profile, including cross-Resource, geographic, temporal, and non- substitutable constraints.

Layer 7 — Evidence Sufficiency & Consistency

Evaluate evidence provenance, completeness, relevance, uncertainty, and material conflicts.

Layer 8 — Validation Tests & Classification

Execute applicable validation tests and establish the evidence-supported Sustainability validation state.

Layer 9 — Validity Conditions & Revalidation

Establish conditional validity, monitoring requirements, and revalidation triggers.

Layer 10 — Governed Output & Handoff

Produce the Resource Sustainability Validation Profile and preserve the downstream handoff to Resource Continuity & Sustainability Monitoring Governance.

95. Use Case 1 — AI Data Center Sustainability Claim

An AI data center publicly claims that its expanded operations are sustainable because its computing infrastructure has become more energy-efficient.

RSVM establishes:

Resource System: AI data center.

Sustainability Claim: Expanded operations are sustainable.

Validation Object: Resource Sustainability of the expanded computing operation.

Validation Boundary: Electricity, water, cooling, hardware, supporting infrastructure, and relevant Resource dependencies.

Assessment Horizon: Defined operating and expansion period.

Resource Continuity Evidence: Resource Continuity Assessment Profile.

Consumption & Replenishment Evidence: Resource Consumption & Replenishment Sustainability Profile.

Impact & Tradeoff Evidence: Resource Sustainability Impact & Tradeoff Profile.

Validation Criteria: Relevant Resource availability, consumption, replenishment, dependency, environmental constraint, and tradeoff conditions.

Evidence Review: Electricity demand, water use, infrastructure Resource requirements, demand growth, and relevant geographic conditions.

Potential Conflict: Energy efficiency per computational task improves while absolute electricity consumption increases.

Output: Resource Sustainability Validation Profile.

The validation cannot rely solely on energy efficiency.

Computational Efficiency Improved ≠ AI Infrastructure Sustainability Validated.

The conclusion must reflect the entire relevant Resource evidence boundary.

A high water recovery rate may support Resource efficiency while remaining insufficient to establish sustainable local water use.

Water Recovery Validated ≠ Water Sustainability Validated.

99. Cross-Architecture Mechanism Principle

An operational mechanism may produce inputs for multiple governance architectures, but it belongs only to the architecture whose governed domain includes that mechanism itself. Other architectures govern only its domain-

specific relevance or consequences.

RSVM may use Resource availability, consumption, replenishment, depletion, recovery, substitution, resilience, scarcity, infrastructure, environmental, and operational evidence.

It governs whether that evidence is sufficient and consistent for a bounded Resource Sustainability conclusion.

It does not redefine the independent mechanisms that produced the evidence.

100. Canonical Boundary

RSVM governs how Resource Sustainability claims, assessments, classifications, and supporting evidence are systematically examined, tested, reconciled, bounded, validated, rejected, qualified, monitored for validity, and reassessed to determine whether a Resource System's continued operation is supportable under defined Resource availability, consumption, replenishment, depletion, dependency, environmental, economic, geographic, temporal, and intergenerational conditions.

It does not independently create Resource availability, authorize Resource operations, certify environmental compliance, establish regulatory approval, determine Harm or Liability, or guarantee permanent Resource Sustainability.

Its principal governed output is the Resource Sustainability Validation Profile .

101. Canonical Closing Statement

Resource Sustainability cannot be established through declarations, isolated efficiency indicators, recovery percentages, theoretical replenishment, operational resilience, or selective evidence. A Sustainability conclusion becomes methodologically supportable only when its governing Resource conditions are explicitly defined, its relevant evidence is sufficient and consistent, its material tradeoffs are examined, and its limitations remain visible across the applicable assessment boundary and time horizon.

RSVM establishes the validation layer of RCSS — ensuring that Resource Sustainability is never treated as a permanent label, a marketing assertion, or a consequence of operational success, but as a bounded, evidence- dependent conclusion that must remain open to qualification, rejection, monitoring, and revalidation whenever the Resource conditions supporting it change.
