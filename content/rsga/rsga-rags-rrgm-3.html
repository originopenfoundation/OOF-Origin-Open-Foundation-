1. Canonical Definition

RRGM — Resource Rights Governance Module governs how a claimed, established, delegated, transferred, conditional, temporary, shared, exclusive, suspended, expired, or otherwise relevant right or entitlement relating to access to a Resource or Resource capacity is identified, traced to its legitimate basis, bounded, evidenced, validated, maintained, modified, transferred where permitted, suspended, terminated, and reassessed within Resource Access Governance.

RRGM establishes whether a claimed Resource Access right exists as a sufficiently governed right, who or what holds it, what Resource it concerns, what scope it carries, where its authority originates, and under which conditions it remains valid.

Claimed Resource Right ≠ Established Resource Right.

2. Operational Role

RAEM determines:

Is the actor eligible for Resource Access?

RACM determines:

How is legitimate Resource Access operationally controlled?

RRGM determines:

What Resource Access right or entitlement exists, who holds it, where it comes from, what it permits, and whether it remains valid?

RRGM prevents statements such as:

“We have always used this Resource.”

“We own the facility.”

“We need the Resource.”

“The Resource is critical to us.”

from being treated automatically as evidence of an established Resource right.

3. Core Question

What legitimate right or entitlement, if any, does the governed actor hold in relation to access to the defined Resource or Resource capacity, from what authority or governing basis does that right originate, what is its scope, duration, transferability, exclusivity, conditions, and evidence, and does it remain valid under the current governed conditions?

4. Position in RAGS Governance

Resource Access Governance may require several distinct determinations:

Eligibility → Rights / Entitlement → Authorization → Access Control → Restrictions / Conditions → Active Access

These relationships are not universally linear.

Some Resource contexts may require an established right before authorization.

Other contexts may permit authorization without a persistent underlying Resource right.

RRGM therefore governs Resource rights as an independent Access dimension rather than assuming that every authorization is itself a permanent right.

5. Governed Space

RRGM governs:

Resource Rights Governance

This space concerns the existence, legitimacy, origin, holder, scope, conditions, continuity, transferability, modification, suspension, termination, evidence, and reassessment of Resource-related Access rights and entitlements.

6. Governed Object

The primary Governed Object is:

A Resource Right or Resource Access Entitlement held by a sufficiently identified actor in relation to a defined Resource or Resource capacity.

Canonical relationship:

**Right Holder

Resource Rights Basis Authority Origin Rights Scope Conditions Evidence → Resource Right State**

7. Scope

RRGM governs:

Resource Right identification; rights claims; right-holder identification; rights basis; authority origin; rights legitimacy; rights scope; Resource-specific rights; Resource Source-specific rights; quantity and capacity rights; rate-bound rights; temporal rights; spatial rights;

purpose-bound rights; conditional rights; shared rights; exclusive rights; temporary rights; persistent rights; delegated rights; transferred rights; transferability; rights modification; rights restriction; rights suspension; rights termination; rights expiration; rights renewal; rights conflict; rights evidence; rights uncertainty; rights reassessment; rights traceability.

RRGM does not independently:

determine Resource Access eligibility; create external legal rights; issue Resource Access authorization; implement technical Access Controls; determine Resource Priority; allocate Resource capacity; govern actual Resource Consumption; determine Scarcity response; establish long-term Sustainability.

8. Resource Right Principle

A Resource right must have a sufficiently legitimate governing basis.

A Resource right does not arise merely because an actor:

wants the Resource; needs the Resource; depends upon the Resource; has historically consumed the Resource; can technically access the Resource; creates substantial Value from the Resource.

9. Rights Claim

A Rights Claim is an assertion that an actor possesses a Resource-related right or entitlement.

A claim is evidence requiring governance.

It is not the right itself.

Rights Claim ≠ Established Resource Right.

10. Right Holder

Every established Resource right should identify a sufficiently defined Right Holder.

The holder may be:

person; organization; public institution; facility where governance permits; system where appropriately established; other legally or operationally recognized governed actor.

11. Right-Holder Identity

Rights should not be attached to ambiguous identity where identity materially affects ownership, entitlement, transferability, authority, or enforcement.

12. Resource Identity

A Resource right must refer to a sufficiently defined Resource or Resource capacity.

The Resource should remain traceable to RCHS characterization.

13. Rights Basis

The Rights Basis explains why the right exists.

Possible bases may include:

law; regulation; contract; license; permit; ownership; public mandate; service entitlement; delegation; transfer; institutional rule; other legitimate governance basis.

RRGM does not determine the external legal validity of a legal instrument beyond the evidence available to the governed methodology.

14. Authority Origin

A Resource right should remain traceable to the authority capable of creating, recognizing, assigning, or transferring that right where material.

Canonical relationship:

Authority: Origin → Rights Basis → Right Holder → Resource Right

15. Authority Boundary

RRGM identifies and governs the Resource relevance of authority evidence.

It does not manufacture authority where no legitimate authority exists.

Methodological Recognition ≠ Creation of External Legal Authority.

16. Rights Legitimacy

A Resource right is sufficiently established where its:

holder; Resource; basis; authority; scope; conditions; validity; evidence

are sufficient for the intended governance purpose.

17. Rights Scope

A Resource right may be bounded by:

Resource; Resource Source; quantity; capacity; rate; purpose; function; location; time; operating state; transferability; other governed conditions.

18. Scope Principle

A right should not be interpreted beyond the scope established by its legitimate basis.

Limited Resource Right ≠ Unlimited Resource Entitlement.

19. Resource-Specific Right

A right relating to one Resource does not automatically create a right to another.

Right to Resource A ≠ Right to Resource B.

20. Source-Specific Right

A Resource right may apply only to a particular Source.

Right to Source A ≠ Right to Every Source of the Resource.

21. Quantity-Bound Right

A right may establish entitlement to access a defined Resource quantity.

The existence of the right does not guarantee that the quantity is currently physically available.

22. Capacity-Bound Right

A Resource right may concern a defined capacity.

Examples include:

electrical capacity; compute capacity; network capacity; storage capacity; cooling capacity.

23. Rate-Bound Right

Some Resource rights may be limited by:

flow rate; power draw; delivery rate; throughput; other Resource-specific rate.

24. Temporal Right

A Resource right may exist only during a defined period.

Examples include:

fixed contractual period; seasonal period; temporary operating period; emergency period; scheduled Access period.

25. Spatial Right

A Resource right may be valid only at a defined:

facility; delivery point; network node; region; jurisdiction; infrastructure area.

26. Purpose-Bound Right

A Resource right may be valid only for a defined purpose.

A right granted for:

essential service operation

does not automatically establish a right for:

unrelated commercial use.

27. Conditional Right

A Resource right may exist only while specified conditions remain satisfied.

28. Persistent Right

A persistent Resource right continues until:

termination; revocation where permitted; transfer; supersession; material governing condition; other legitimate closure mechanism.

Persistent does not mean unlimited or immutable.

29. Temporary Right

A temporary Resource right must preserve its:

effective time; expiration; scope; conditions; rights basis.

30. Shared Right

Multiple actors may hold legitimate rights relating to the same Resource.

The existence of shared rights does not prove sufficient capacity to satisfy every holder simultaneously.

Multiple Valid Resource Rights ≠ Sufficient Resource Capacity.

31. Exclusive Right

Where a Resource right is exclusive, the basis for exclusivity should be explicit.

Current sole use does not prove exclusive entitlement.

Sole Current Use ≠ Exclusive Resource Right.

32. Rights and Ownership

Ownership may create Resource rights in some contexts.

Ownership should not automatically be interpreted as unlimited authority over every Resource relationship associated with the owned asset.

Ownership ≠ Unlimited Resource Right.

33. Rights and Possession

Possession or physical control of a Resource does not by itself establish a legitimate Resource right.

Possession ≠ Resource Entitlement.

34. Rights and Historical Use

Long-standing Resource use may be relevant evidence depending upon the governing context.

It does not universally establish a current right.

Historical Use ≠ Current Resource Right.

35. Rights and Existing Consumption

Current consumption demonstrates operational Resource use.

It does not independently establish entitlement.

Consumption ≠ Resource Right.

36. Rights and Resource Demand

A valid Demand Profile establishes Resource requirement.

It does not create a Resource right.

Resource Demand ≠ Resource Right.

37. Rights and Dependency

A Resource Dependency establishes reliance.

It does not create a Resource right.

Resource Dependency ≠ Resource Right.

38. Rights and Criticality

Criticality establishes the significance of a Resource Dependency.

It does not create entitlement.

Resource Criticality ≠ Resource Right.

39. Rights and Value

A participant may create substantial Value through a Resource.

That does not independently create entitlement to the Resource.

Resource Value ≠ Resource Right.

40. Rights and Availability

A valid Resource right may exist while the Resource is unavailable.

Likewise, a Resource may be abundant while an actor possesses no right to it.

Resource Right ≠ Resource Availability.

41. Rights and Eligibility

Eligibility determines whether an actor qualifies for Access consideration.

Rights determine whether a legitimate entitlement exists.

These are separate dimensions.

Eligibility ≠ Resource Right.

42. Rights and Authorization

A Resource right may provide a basis for Access authorization.

The right and the authorization remain distinct.

Resource Right ≠ Active Access Authorization.

Some Resource contexts may require a separate authorization step before the right can be exercised.

43. Rights and Access Control

RACM may enforce Access derived from an established right and authorization.

The technical control does not create the right.

Access Control ≠ Resource Right.

44. Rights and Priority

Resource rights may be relevant evidence in Priority Governance.

They do not automatically establish highest Priority.

Resource Right ≠ Highest Resource Priority.

45. Rights and Allocation

A valid Resource right does not guarantee current Allocation where Resource capacity is constrained and the governing system legitimately permits competing claims or limitations.

Resource Right ≠ Current Resource Allocation.

46. Rights and Scarcity

Scarcity does not automatically erase existing Resource rights.

It may create circumstances in which rights, restrictions, Priority, Allocation, and Scarcity Governance interact.

47. Rights Modification

A Resource right may be modified where its governing basis permits.

Modification may affect:

quantity; capacity; rate; purpose; location; duration; conditions; transferability; exclusivity.

48. Modification Authority

A Resource right should not be modified by an actor lacking competent authority to modify it.

49. Rights Restriction

A right may remain valid while its exercise becomes restricted under legitimate governance conditions.

Restriction should not automatically be represented as termination.

50. Rights Suspension

A Resource right may be temporarily suspended where the governing basis permits.

Suspension preserves the distinction between:

temporary inability to exercise the right

and:

termination of the right.

51. Rights Termination

Termination ends the relevant Resource right.

The termination should remain traceable to:

authority; basis; effective time; affected right; evidence.

52. Rights Expiration

A time-limited Resource right expires when its governed validity period ends unless legitimately renewed.

Expired Right ≠ Current Resource Entitlement.

53. Rights Renewal

A Resource right may be renewed where its governing basis permits.

Historical validity does not guarantee renewal.

54. Rights Delegation

A Resource right or authority associated with its exercise may be delegable where permitted.

Delegation should preserve:

original holder; delegate; scope; duration; conditions; Resource; delegation authority.

55. Delegation Boundary

A delegate cannot legitimately exercise a broader Resource right than was delegated.

Delegated Right ≠ Expanded Right.

56. Rights Transfer

Where permitted, a Resource right may be transferred from one holder to another.

Transfer should preserve:

transferor; transferee; Resource; rights basis; scope; effective time; transfer authority; evidence.

57. Transferability

Not every Resource right is transferable.

Transferability must be established rather than assumed.

Resource Right ≠ Transfer Authority.

58. Partial Transfer

Where permitted, only part of a Resource right may be transferred.

The retained and transferred scopes must remain distinguishable.

59. Transfer Finality

A completed transfer should establish whether the original holder:

retains part of the right; retains no right; retains conditional rights; retains delegated authority.

60. Delegation vs Transfer

Delegation and transfer are different mechanisms.

Delegation allows another actor to exercise defined rights or authority while the originating relationship remains relevant.

Transfer changes the holder of the relevant right where permitted.

Delegation ≠ Transfer.

61. Rights Succession

Where Resource rights may continue through organizational, institutional, ownership, or other succession, the continuity basis should be evidenced.

Continuity should not be assumed solely because operational use continues.

62. Rights Conflict

Rights Conflict exists where two or more materially incompatible Resource rights or claims concern the same Resource relationship.

63. Overlapping Rights

Multiple rights may overlap without necessarily conflicting.

Conflict exists when their simultaneous interpretation or exercise becomes materially incompatible.

64. Competing Rights

Two valid Resource rights may compete for insufficient Resource capacity.

RRGM establishes the rights.

It does not resolve downstream Resource Priority or Allocation.

65. Rights Hierarchy

Where a governing system establishes hierarchy among rights, RRGM may preserve that hierarchy as evidence.

RRGM does not invent hierarchy merely to resolve competition.

66. Rights Conflict Resolution

Canonical logic:

Conflicting Rights / Claims → Holder Identity Check → Resource Identity Check

→ Rights Basis Check → Authority Origin Check → Scope Check → Temporal Check → Spatial Check → Validity Check → Competent Resolution → Rights Redetermination

67. Rights Uncertainty

Uncertainty may concern:

holder identity; Resource identity; rights basis; authority; scope; validity; transferability; delegation; duration; termination; conflicting claims.

68. Uncertainty Propagation

Material uncertainty must remain visible in the Resource Right State.

Uncertain Right ≠ Established Right.

69. Rights Evidence

Evidence may include:

law; regulatory record; contract; license; permit; ownership record; service agreement; delegation record; transfer record; institutional mandate; rights register; other competent evidence.

70. Evidence Provenance

Material rights evidence should preserve where relevant:

source; issuer; holder; Resource; scope; effective date; expiration;

version; authority reference.

71. Evidence Validity

The existence of a document does not prove that the right remains valid.

Rights Document Exists ≠ Resource Right Currently Valid.

72. Evidence Freshness

RRGM should distinguish current rights evidence from superseded, expired, revoked, or historical evidence.

73. Evidence Sufficiency

Rights evidence is sufficient when it adequately establishes the Resource Right State required for the intended governance purpose.

74. Rights Evidence Gap

A material Evidence Gap exists where missing evidence could alter:

right existence; holder; scope; authority; duration; transferability; current validity.

75. Rights State

RRGM represents the governed status of the relevant Resource right through a Resource Right State.

76. Minimum Resource Right States

At minimum:

Right Established

Right Conditionally Established

Right Restricted

Right Suspended

Right Transferred

Right Expired

Right Terminated

Right Evidence-Limited

Right Conflicted

Right Unknown

Right Unresolved

77. Right Established

The Resource right is sufficiently supported and currently valid within its governed scope.

78. Right Conditionally Established

The right exists only while explicit conditions remain satisfied.

79. Right Restricted

The right remains valid but its exercise or scope is currently restricted.

80. Right Suspended

The right exists structurally or historically but its exercise is temporarily suspended.

81. Right Transferred

The relevant right has been legitimately transferred to another holder where permitted.

82. Right Expired

The validity period has ended.

83. Right Terminated

The right no longer exists under the relevant governance basis.

84. Right Evidence-Limited

Available evidence does not support a stronger rights determination.

85. Right Conflicted

Credible evidence supports materially incompatible Resource Right States.

86. Right Unknown

Available evidence does not establish whether the relevant Resource right exists.

87. Right Unresolved

Relevant evidence exists but final determination cannot yet be completed.

88. Rights Lifecycle

A Resource right may move through:

Claimed → Evaluated → Established → Active / Conditional / Restricted → Modified / Delegated / Transferred where permitted → Suspended → Renewed / Expired / Terminated

The exact lifecycle depends upon the governing basis.

89. Rights Continuity

A Resource right should remain continuous only while its legitimate basis remains valid.

Operational continuity alone does not establish rights continuity.

90. Rights Reassessment

Resource rights should be reassessed where material changes could alter their existence, holder, scope, or validity.

91. Reassessment Triggers

Triggers may include:

holder change; Resource change; Resource Source change; contract change; license change; ownership change; authority change; delegation; transfer; expiration; restriction; suspension; termination; new rights claim; new conflicting evidence; new governing rule.

92. Targeted Reassessment

Where only one rights dimension changes, RRGM should reassess the affected dimension and its consequences rather than unnecessarily reconstructing unrelated evidence.

93. Rights Versioning

Material changes to a Resource right should create a new governed version where traceability requires it.

94. Historical Rights

Historical rights should remain traceable without being represented as current entitlement.

Former Right ≠ Current Right.

95. Rights Traceability

A Resource Right State should remain traceable through:

Resource Right State → Rights Scope → Rights Basis → Authority Origin → Right Holder → Resource → Evidence.

96. Rights Explainability

A governed Resource right should be able to explain materially:

who holds the right; which Resource it concerns; why the right exists; who or what established it; what scope it carries; which conditions apply; whether it can be delegated or transferred; when it expires or terminates; what evidence supports it.

97. Rights Auditability

Where required, the Resource right should remain reconstructable across:

claim; establishment; modification; restriction; delegation; transfer; suspension; renewal; expiration; termination.

98. Rights Minimality

RRGM should not infer broader rights than the evidence establishes.

Where evidence supports a limited right, the governed representation should remain limited.

99. Rights Expansion

Expansion of a Resource right requires a legitimate basis capable of expanding it.

Increased Resource Need ≠ Rights Expansion.

100. Rights Reduction

Reduction in rights scope should preserve:

authority; basis; effective time; new scope; evidence.

101. Rights Integrity

A Resource right should not be manipulated merely to achieve a preferred downstream Priority or Allocation outcome.

Desired Allocation ≠ Resource Right Evidence.

102. Automated Rights Evaluation

Systems may assist with:

rights evidence retrieval; validity checks; scope comparison; expiration detection; conflict detection; rights-state classification.

Automation does not create the underlying right.

103. Automated Rights Boundary

Machine Recognition of a Right ≠ Creation of the Right.

115. Failure Conditions

RRGM should not be considered sufficiently completed where:

a Rights Claim is treated as an established right; Right Holder identity is materially ambiguous; Resource identity is unclear; Rights Basis is missing; Authority Origin is materially unsupported; Rights Scope is assumed rather than evidenced; a limited right is represented as unlimited; historical use is treated automatically as current entitlement; existing consumption is treated as a Resource right; Resource Demand is treated as a Resource right; Resource Dependency is treated as a Resource right; Criticality is treated as a Resource right; Value is treated as a Resource right; ownership is treated automatically as unlimited Resource entitlement; delegation is confused with transfer; transferability is assumed; expired rights are represented as current; terminated rights remain represented as valid; material rights conflict is hidden; material uncertainty is suppressed; technical Access Control is treated as evidence that a right exists; Resource rights are manipulated to obtain a preferred Priority or Allocation outcome.

116. Use Case 1 — AI Data Center Compute Capacity Rights

An organization operates workloads within a shared AI compute facility.

The organization claims a right to:

500 GPU units of compute capacity

based upon a long-term infrastructure agreement.

RRGM examines:

Right Holder — Organization

Resource — Defined GPU Compute Capacity

Rights Basis — Infrastructure Agreement

Authority: Origin — Competent Facility / Contractual Authority

Capacity Scope — 500 GPU units

Purpose — Defined workload classes

Temporal Boundary — Contract period

Transferability — Restricted

Evidence — Current agreement and rights records

RRGM determines:

Resource Right State — Right Established.

This does not mean that 500 GPUs are currently available.

It does not itself activate technical Access.

It does not establish that the organization receives higher Resource Priority than another legitimate rights holder during a severe Resource constraint.

Those remain separate governance questions.

117. Use Case 2 — Regional Water Rights

A regional agricultural operator claims entitlement to withdraw water from a defined Resource Source.

RRGM evaluates:

Right Holder; Resource Source; Rights Basis; permitted withdrawal quantity; seasonal conditions; location; effective period; transferability; restrictions; evidence.

The evidence establishes that the operator holds a valid seasonal right subject to a defined maximum withdrawal rate.

RRGM therefore determines:

Right Conditionally Established

during the relevant season and conditions.

The operator's historical use above that limit does not expand the right.

During severe water constraint, the existence of the right does not by itself determine the final Allocation.

RRGM establishes the Resource right.

RPAS and other competent Resource governance determine downstream Priority and Allocation.

119. Cross-Architecture Boundary

RRGM may consume:

RCHS — Resource identity and characterization.

RACS — Resource Availability and capacity context.

RDGS — Resource Demand context.

RDCS — Resource Dependency and Criticality context.

RAEM — Resource Access Eligibility evidence.

RACM — Access Control evidence where relevant to actual implementation.

GOA™ — Authority, Delegation, Accountability, and governance-boundary evidence.

OBIDENITY® — Origin, Identity, Ownership, Authority, Permission, Provenance, Transfer, and Continuity evidence where relevant.

ORA™ — Operational Reality evidence concerning actual Resource use.

LIGA™ — Liability evidence where rights conflict or misuse becomes liability-relevant.

RRGM does not absorb these mechanisms.

120. Cross-Architecture Mechanism Principle

An operational mechanism may produce inputs for multiple governance architectures, but it belongs only to the architecture whose governed domain includes that mechanism itself. Other architectures govern only its domain- specific relevance or consequences.

Ownership may provide evidence for a Resource right.

Identity establishes who the Right Holder is.

Authority: establishes whether the Rights Basis is legitimate.

Transfer mechanisms may establish rights continuity.

Operational evidence may establish whether the Resource is actually being used.

RRGM governs only their relevance to the Resource Right State within Resource Access Governance.

121. Canonical Boundary

RRGM governs how Resource-related Access rights and entitlements are identified, traced to a legitimate Rights Basis and Authority Origin, attached to a sufficiently identified Right Holder and Resource, bounded by scope and conditions, evidenced, validated, modified, delegated, transferred where permitted, restricted, suspended, expired, terminated, and reassessed.

RRGM does not create external legal rights, determine Resource Access eligibility, issue Resource Access authorization, implement technical Access Controls, establish Resource Priority, allocate Resource capacity, govern Resource Consumption, determine Scarcity response, or establish long-term Resource Sustainability.

Its principal governed output is the Resource Rights Profile .

122. Canonical Closing Statement

A Resource right does not exist merely because a Resource has been used, is needed, is valuable, or is critical to the actor that depends upon it.

RRGM establishes the rights layer explicitly — determining who holds what Resource entitlement, from which legitimate basis, within what scope and conditions, and with what continuity and evidence — so that Resource Access is governed by established rights rather than assumed entitlement.
