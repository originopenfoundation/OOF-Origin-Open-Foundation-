1. Canonical Definition

RRRGM — Recovered Resource Recognition & Governance Module is the Core Module within RRCS that governs how outputs produced through Resource Recovery processes are identified, traced, assessed, tested, validated, classified, recognized, restricted, monitored, and reassessed against defined Resource-quality, identity, provenance, and intended-use requirements.

RRRGM establishes the conditions under which a candidate recovered output may be recognized as a validated recovered Resource.

It distinguishes Recovery process completion from verified Resource capability and ensures that recovered Resource status is supported by evidence rather than assumed from the operation of a Recovery process.

Recovery Process Completed ≠ Recovered Resource Validated.

2. Operational Role

RRRGM governs the transition from Recovery process output to recognized recovered Resource.

Its role is to determine what Resource was actually produced, whether its identity and origin are traceable, which characteristics have been established, and whether the output satisfies the applicable validation requirements.

RRRGM:

Identifies candidate recovered outputs. Preserves Recovery process provenance. Establishes recovered Resource identity. Evaluates recovered quantity and quality. Verifies applicable validation criteria. Records testing and inspection evidence. Distinguishes validation for different intended uses. Identifies restrictions and unresolved conditions. Establishes recovered Resource recognition states. Supports reassessment when evidence or Resource condition changes.

RRRGM does not authorize subsequent Resource Access, Allocation, substitution, or reintegration.

3. Operational Question

Under what evidenced conditions can an output of a Resource Recovery process be recognized as a recovered Resource with established identity, provenance, quantity, quality, and defined use-specific suitability, without confusing process completion, technical recovery, validation, availability, or authorization for subsequent use?

RSGA™ Governance Flow

Resource Characterization → Availability & Capacity → Demand → Dependency & Criticality → Access → Prioritization & Allocation → Consumption & Utilization → Scarcity & Constraint → Recovery & Circularity → Substitution & Flexibility → Interdependency & Resilience → Continuity & Sustainability

RRCS Governance Flow

RRRGM occupies the fourth governance position.

It receives candidate recovered outputs and process evidence from RURPM.

Its validated outputs provide the Resource-quality and identity basis required for subsequent secondary Resource governance.

5. Governed Space

Recovered Resource Recognition & Validation Governance

This governed space concerns the establishment of reliable recovered Resource identity, condition, quantity, quality, provenance, and validation state.

It includes the evidence required to determine whether recovered outputs satisfy defined Resource requirements.

Validation is always associated with an explicit assessment boundary.

A recovered Resource may satisfy one application while failing another.

6. Governed Object

The primary Governed Object is:

A Candidate Recovered Resource Output associated with an identifiable Recovery process, provenance record, measurable Resource characteristics, and applicable validation criteria.

Canonical relationship:

Recovery Process Output → Recovered Output Identification → Provenance Verification → Resource Characterization → Validation Criteria → Testing & Evidence → Validation Decision → Recovered Resource Recognition State

7. Scope

RRRGM governs:

Candidate recovered output identification. Recovered Resource identity. Recovery process provenance. Resource quantity verification. Resource quality characterization. Composition and purity assessment. Functional performance assessment. Contamination and degradation assessment. Validation criteria. Intended-use-specific validation. Testing and inspection evidence. Evidence sufficiency and conflict. Recovered Resource recognition states. Conditional validation. Resource restrictions. Revalidation and reassessment. Validation traceability and versioning.

RRRGM does not govern the actual Recovery process, Resource Access authorization, Resource Allocation, substitution decisions, operational reintegration, Circularity performance, or long-term Sustainability.

14. Recovered Resource Quality

Recovered Resource quality concerns the characteristics that determine whether an output satisfies defined Resource requirements.

Relevant characteristics may include:

Material composition. Purity. Contamination. Mechanical properties. Structural integrity. Chemical properties. Water quality. Energy characteristics. Functional performance. Reliability. Other Resource-specific parameters.

Quality must be evaluated against an explicit validation basis.

15. Quality Validation Boundary

A Resource cannot be declared universally suitable merely because one characteristic has been measured.

One Passing Quality Parameter ≠ Complete Resource Validation.

16. Intended-Use Requirements

Recovered Resource validation may depend on the intended future use.

For example:

Recovered water may satisfy industrial cooling requirements but not drinking-water requirements.

Recovered metal may satisfy one manufacturing specification but not another.

Recovered hardware may support low-demand computation but not high-performance operation.

Resource Validation Is Use-Specific Where Requirements Differ.

54. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum operational RRRGM implementation should contain eight governed layers.

Layer 1 — Candidate Output Identity

Establish the recovered output identity and Recovery process reference.

Layer 2 — Provenance Verification

Verify the traceable relationship between the original Resource, residual Resource, Recovery process, and candidate output.

Layer 3 — Resource Characterization

Establish recovered quantity, composition, condition, quality, and relevant functional characteristics.

Layer 4 — Validation Basis

Define the applicable intended-use requirements, technical criteria, measurement methods, and competent validation references.

Layer 5 — Testing and Evidence

Conduct or obtain the applicable testing, inspection, sampling, and measurement evidence.

Layer 6 — Validation Decision

Determine the Resource validation state, including conditional, failed, Evidence-Limited, Conflicted, or Unknown outcomes.

Layer 7 — Recognition and Restrictions

Establish the recovered Resource recognition state, applicable restrictions, validity conditions, and reassessment triggers.

Layer 8 — Governed Output

Produce the Recovered Resource Validation Profile .

62. Use Case 1 — AI Data Center Accelerator Recovery

An AI data center retires accelerator servers.

The hardware undergoes a Recovery process.

RURPM records the disassembly, component separation, refurbishment, and candidate recovered outputs.

RRRGM evaluates the recovered accelerator cards.

Candidate Resource: Refurbished Accelerator Card

Recovery Process: Hardware Refurbishment

Validation Requirements:

Component identity. Provenance. Electrical integrity. Functional performance. Thermal behavior. Memory integrity. Reliability evidence.

Intended-use requirements.

A card may pass functional testing but fail the performance requirements of its original AI workload.

It may nevertheless satisfy a lower-demand application.

RRRGM must preserve the distinction between those validation scopes.

Validated for Lower-Demand Compute ≠ Validated for Original High-Performance AI Compute.

The module produces a Recovered Resource Validation Profile specifying the applicable conditions.

It does not authorize deployment or Resource Allocation.

63. Use Case 2 — Recovered Industrial Water Validation

An industrial facility operates a wastewater Recovery process.

RURPM records the treatment stages, Resource inputs, recovered water output, and process completion.

RRRGM evaluates the recovered water.

Candidate Resource: Treated Industrial Water

Recovery Process: Wastewater Treatment

Validation Requirements:

Water identity and Source. Recovered volume. Chemical composition. Contaminant concentrations. Physical water-quality parameters. Applicable intended-use requirements. Laboratory evidence. Sampling validity.

The recovered water may satisfy industrial cooling requirements but fail requirements for a more demanding application.

RRRGM records the use-specific validation outcome.

Validated for Industrial Cooling ≠ Validated for Every Water Use.

The module does not authorize water reintegration into the industrial system.

That decision belongs to subsequent governance.

65. Cross-Architecture Mechanism Principle

An operational mechanism may produce inputs for multiple governance architectures, but it belongs only to the architecture whose governed domain includes that mechanism itself. Other architectures govern only its domain- specific relevance or consequences.

Recovered Resource testing may produce evidence relevant to Resource Availability, substitution, operational safety, Value, or Sustainability.

RRRGM governs the Resource Recovery validation state itself.

Other architectures govern their respective downstream decisions and consequences.

66. Canonical Boundary

RRRGM governs how outputs produced through Resource Recovery processes are identified, traced, assessed, tested, validated, classified, recognized, restricted, monitored, and reassessed against defined Resource-quality, identity, provenance, and intended-use requirements.

It does not execute Recovery, independently create technical certification authority, determine Resource Access or Allocation, authorize substitution, govern operational reintegration, establish Circularity achievement, or determine long- term Sustainability.

Its principal governed output is the Recovered Resource Validation Profile .

67. Canonical Closing Statement

A Resource is not validated merely because a Recovery process produced it, because its quantity was measured, or because one technical characteristic passed inspection. Recovered Resource capability must be established through traceable identity, applicable quality requirements, appropriate evidence, and explicit validation boundaries.

RRRGM establishes the governance transition from Recovery output to recognized Resource capability — ensuring that recovered Resources are validated for what they can actually support, without confusing recognition with availability, authorization, reintegration, or Sustainability.
