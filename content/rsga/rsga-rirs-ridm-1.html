1. Canonical Definition

RIDM — Resource Interdependency Mapping Module is the Core Module within RIRS that governs how Resources, Resource capacities, operational functions, supporting infrastructure, and their direct, indirect,

shared, conditional, and reciprocal dependency relationships are identified, represented, evidenced, bounded, versioned, and maintained as a traceable Resource Interdependency Network.

RIDM establishes the methodological foundation for understanding how a Resource System is structurally connected before disturbance propagation, vulnerability, resilience capability, or recovery is evaluated.

It distinguishes the existence of a Resource relationship from its operational significance, an observed dependency from an assumed dependency, and the presence of multiple Resource pathways from their actual independence.

RIDM does not determine that a dependency is critical merely because it exists.

It does not establish resilience merely because a network has been mapped.

Resource Relationship Identified ≠ Operational Dependency Validated.

Dependency Mapped ≠ Dependency Criticality Established.

Multiple Resource Pathways ≠ Independent Resource Pathways.

Resource Interdependency Network Established ≠ Resource Resilience Demonstrated.

2. Operational Role

RIDM governs the identification and representation of Resource interdependency structures.

Its operational role is to establish an evidence-based network of Resources and operational functions that can support subsequent disturbance propagation, vulnerability, resilience, and recovery governance.

RIDM governs:

Resource System identity. Resource System mapping boundaries. Constituent Resource identification. Operational function references. Resource capacity references. Supporting infrastructure references. Direct dependency identification. Indirect dependency identification. Reciprocal dependency identification. Conditional dependency identification. Shared dependency identification. Dependency direction. Dependency conditions. Dependency pathways. Resource dependency concentration. Potential common dependency points. Network completeness. Evidence provenance. Unknown dependencies. Conflicting dependency evidence. Network versioning. Dependency change recognition. Interdependency mapping outputs. Downstream governance handoffs.

RIDM does not independently establish disturbance consequences, vulnerability severity, resilience sufficiency, operational authorization, or Sustainability.

3. Core Question

How should the Resources, operational functions, capacities, supporting infrastructure, and dependency relationships of a Resource System be identified, validated, represented, and maintained so that direct, indirect, shared, conditional, and reciprocal dependencies remain traceable without confusing network connectivity with

operational criticality, pathway diversity with independence, or dependency mapping with demonstrated resilience?

5. Governed Space

Resource Interdependency Identification & Mapping Governance

This governed space concerns the identification, validation, representation, and maintenance of dependency relationships among Resources, Resource capacities, operational functions, and supporting infrastructure.

It includes:

Resource network boundaries. Resource and function identities. Dependency relationships. Dependency direction. Conditional dependencies. Shared dependencies. Reciprocal dependencies. Indirect dependency pathways. Network evidence. Unknown relationships. Mapping limitations. Network changes.

It does not independently govern Resource characterization, dependency criticality classification, disturbance propagation, resilience capability, operational response, or recovery.

18. Relationship Identity Principle

Resource Connection ≠ Operational Dependency.

Two Resources may be connected without one being required for the other's operational function.

RIDM must preserve the basis on which a connection is classified as a dependency.

19. Direct Dependencies

A direct dependency exists where one node relies immediately on another node for a defined operational function.

Example:

Cooling Equipment → Electricity Supply

The dependency must remain associated with its operating conditions.

20. Indirect Dependencies

An indirect dependency exists where a node relies on another Resource through one or more intermediate nodes.

Example:

Computational Processing → Cooling Equipment → Water Supply

RIDM must preserve the intermediate pathway.

No Direct Dependency ≠ No Indirect Dependency.

21. Reciprocal Dependencies

A reciprocal dependency exists where two nodes depend on each other under relevant operational conditions.

Example:

An operational process may depend on cooling, while cooling control depends on computing infrastructure supporting the same process.

Reciprocal relationships must not be represented as one-way dependencies.

22. Reciprocal Dependency Principle

Reciprocal Dependency ≠ Equal Dependency Strength.

The two directions may differ in their required capacity, operating conditions, or significance.

23. Shared Dependencies

A shared dependency exists where multiple nodes rely on the same supporting Resource or infrastructure.

Example:

Computing Infrastructure → Electricity Distribution

Cooling Infrastructure → Electricity Distribution

RIDM must identify the common supporting node.

24. Shared Dependency Principle

Multiple Dependent Functions ≠ Multiple Independent Supporting Resources.

Shared dependencies may become relevant to subsequent common-cause failure assessment.

RIDM identifies the structure but does not determine the disturbance consequences.

25. Conditional Dependencies

A conditional dependency exists only under specified operational states or requirements.

Examples include:

Backup Generator → Fuel Supply During Backup Operation

Alternative Cooling → Additional Electricity Under High Temperature

Water Treatment → Chemical Input Under Specific Water Quality Conditions

Conditional dependencies must preserve their activation conditions.

26. Conditional Dependency Principle

Dependency Absent in Normal Operation ≠ Dependency Absent During Disturbance.

27. Temporal Dependencies

A temporal dependency concerns a Resource relationship that varies over time.

Examples include:

Time-Restricted Electricity Availability

Seasonal Water Supply

Scheduled Transport Capacity

Periodic Storage Replenishment

Time-Limited Backup Capacity

RIDM must preserve relevant temporal conditions.

28. Temporal Dependency Principle

Resource Dependency Valid at Time A ≠ Resource Dependency Valid at Time B.

29. Spatial Dependencies

A spatial dependency concerns a Resource relationship influenced by location.

Examples include:

Remote Electricity Supply

External Water Treatment

Regional Transport Infrastructure

Geographically Distributed Computing

Remote Material Supply

RIDM must preserve material location references.

30. Spatial Dependency Principle

Geographically Separate Resources ≠ Operationally Independent Resources.

31. Dependency Direction

Every directed dependency must preserve which node depends on which supporting node.

Direction must not be inferred from visual layout alone.

Canonical form:

Dependent Node → Supporting Node

The direction convention must remain consistent throughout the network.

32. Dependency Direction Integrity

Dependency Direction Reversed ≠ Equivalent Network Representation.

An incorrect direction may materially distort subsequent disturbance propagation analysis.

40. Potential Common Dependency Points

63. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum operational RIDM implementation should contain nine governed layers.

Layer 1 — Resource System Identity

Establish the Resource System, operational functions, mapping boundary, and relevant operating conditions.

Layer 2 — Node Identification

Identify constituent Resources, operational functions, supporting infrastructure, and relevant capacity references.

Layer 3 — Dependency Relationship Identification

Identify direct, indirect, reciprocal, shared, and conditional dependencies.

Layer 4 — Dependency Direction & Conditions

Preserve dependency direction, required operating conditions, temporal validity, and spatial relevance.

Layer 5 — Dependency Pathway Mapping

Represent direct and indirect pathways, alternative pathways, shared segments, and dependency cycles.

Layer 6 — Shared Dependency & Concentration Recognition

Identify common supporting nodes, converging dependencies, and potential common dependency points.

Layer 7 — Evidence & Validation

Preserve evidence provenance, inferred relationships, unknown dependencies, conflicts, and validation states.

Layer 8 — Network Versioning & Change Recognition

Maintain the network across material operational and Resource configuration changes.

Layer 9 — Governed Output & Handoff

Produce the Resource Interdependency Network Profile and preserve downstream disturbance, vulnerability, and resilience governance references.

71. Use Case 1 — AI Data Center Resource Interdependency Mapping

An AI data center operates computing infrastructure, cooling systems, backup electricity capacity, and water-dependent equipment.

RIDM establishes:

Resource System: AI data center.

Operational Functions: Computational processing and thermal control.

Resource Nodes: Electricity, water, computing capacity, cooling capacity, backup energy.

Infrastructure Nodes: Electrical distribution, cooling infrastructure, water distribution, computing equipment.

Direct Dependencies: Computing infrastructure depends on electricity. Cooling equipment depends on electricity.

Indirect Dependencies: Computational processing depends on water through cooling infrastructure where water-based cooling is used.

Shared Dependencies: Computing and cooling may share the same electrical distribution infrastructure.

Alternative Pathways: Backup electricity and alternative cooling configurations.

Shared Pathway Segments: Alternative arrangements may retain common electrical infrastructure.

Unknown Dependencies: Any supporting Resource relationships not yet validated.

Output: Resource Interdependency Network Profile.

RIDM identifies the Resource structure.

It does not conclude that the facility is resilient merely because backup systems exist.

Backup Systems Mapped ≠ Backup Resilience Demonstrated.

72. Use Case 2 — Industrial Water and Electricity Dependency Mapping

An industrial facility uses water for production and electricity for pumping, treatment, and processing.

RIDM establishes:

Resource System: Industrial production facility.

Operational Functions: Water supply, treatment, and manufacturing.

Resource Nodes: Water, electricity, production materials, treatment inputs.

Infrastructure Nodes: Pumps, treatment equipment, electricity distribution, production equipment.

Direct Dependencies: Pumps depend on electricity. Water treatment depends on electricity and treatment inputs.

Indirect Dependencies: Production water availability depends on electricity through pumping and treatment.

Shared Dependencies: Primary and alternative water sources may depend on the same pumping or electrical infrastructure.

Alternative Pathways: Secondary water supply and storage.

Conditional Dependencies: Additional treatment inputs may be required under changed water quality conditions.

Output: Resource Interdependency Network Profile.

The facility may have multiple water sources while retaining a single supporting electricity dependency.

Multiple Water Sources ≠ Independent Water Supply Pathways.

75. Cross-Architecture Mechanism Principle

An operational mechanism may produce inputs for multiple governance architectures, but it belongs only to the architecture whose governed domain includes that mechanism itself. Other architectures govern only its domain- specific relevance or consequences.

RIDM may use Resource characterization, operational configuration, infrastructure, capacity, and dependency evidence.

It governs the representation and traceability of those inputs as Resource interdependency relationships.

It does not redefine the independent mechanisms that produced them.

76. Canonical Boundary

RIDM governs how Resources, Resource capacities, operational functions, supporting infrastructure, and their direct, indirect, shared, conditional, and reciprocal dependency relationships are identified, represented, evidenced, bounded, versioned, and maintained as a traceable Resource Interdependency Network.

It does not independently determine Resource Access, Allocation, criticality, disturbance consequences, resilience capability, recovery success, Harm, Liability, or Sustainability.

Its principal governed output is the Resource Interdependency Network Profile .

77. Canonical Closing Statement

A Resource System cannot be understood through a list of Resources alone. Its operational reality is shaped by the dependency relationships connecting those Resources, the infrastructure supporting them, the conditions under which those relationships apply, and the shared pathways that may remain invisible when each Resource is assessed independently.

RIDM establishes the governance foundation for making those relationships explicit, traceable, and verifiable — ensuring that network connectivity is not confused with criticality, alternative pathways are not mistaken for independence, and an incomplete dependency map is never presented as proof of Resource System resilience.
