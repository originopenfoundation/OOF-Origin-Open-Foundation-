1. Canonical Definition

RURPM — Resource Recovery Process Module is the Core Module within RRCS that governs how eligible and appropriately authorized Resource Recovery processes are initiated, bounded, executed, measured, evidenced, controlled, completed, interrupted, and reassessed, including the traceability of Resource inputs, supporting Resource consumption, process transformations, recovered outputs, residuals, and losses.

RURPM establishes the governance continuity between an assessed Recovery pathway and the actual operational process used to recover Resource capability.

It ensures that Recovery activity is distinguishable from Recovery achievement and that every material Resource transformation remains traceable through an explicit process boundary.

Recovery Process Execution ≠ Validated Resource Recovery.

2. Operational Role

RURPM governs the operational execution stage of Resource Recovery.

ERRM establishes what Resource remains after original use.

RPMM establishes whether Recovery is feasible and under which conditions.

RURPM governs what actually happens when an eligible and authorized Recovery process is executed.

Its operational responsibilities include:

Establishing the Recovery process boundary. Verifying applicable authorization and execution conditions. Identifying Recovery inputs. Recording supporting Resource consumption. Monitoring Recovery process execution. Measuring intermediate transformations. Identifying outputs, residuals, and losses. Preserving process deviations and incidents. Establishing actual Recovery process performance. Producing an evidenced Recovery process record.

RURPM does not independently determine that recovered output satisfies the requirements of a validated secondary Resource.

3. Operational Question

How should an eligible and authorized Resource Recovery process be executed, measured, controlled, and evidenced so that its actual inputs, transformations, outputs, supporting Resource consumption, losses, deviations, and completion state remain traceable without confusing process activity with validated Recovery achievement?

4. Position in Architecture Governance Flow

RSGA™ Governance Flow

Resource Characterization → Availability & Capacity → Demand → Dependency & Criticality → Access → Prioritization & Allocation → Consumption & Utilization → Scarcity & Constraint → Recovery & Circularity → Substitution & Flexibility → Interdependency & Resilience → Continuity & Sustainability

RRCS Governance Flow

1. End-of-Use Resource Recognition — ERRM 2. Resource Recovery Potential — RPMM 3. Resource Recovery Process — RURPM 4. Recovered Resource Validation 5. Secondary Resource Reintegration

RURPM occupies the third position.

It converts an appropriately established Recovery pathway into an evidenced operational Recovery process.

Its outputs become inputs for subsequent recovered Resource validation.

5. Governed Space

Resource Recovery Process Governance

This space concerns the operational execution and evidence of Recovery processes involving residual, used, depleted, transformed, discarded, or otherwise recoverable Resources.

It includes physical, chemical, thermal, mechanical, biological, functional, and other applicable Recovery mechanisms.

RURPM governs the process as a Resource Recovery operation, not the engineering design of every underlying technology.

6. Governed Object

The primary Governed Object is:

An identifiable Resource Recovery Process Instance operating under defined Resource inputs, Recovery pathway, process boundary, operational conditions, execution authority, and evidence requirements.

Canonical relationship:

Recognized Resource → Recovery Potential → Recovery Eligibility & Authorization References → Process Boundary → Recovery Inputs → Process Execution → Resource Transformation → Process Outputs & Losses → Process Completion → Recovery Process Record

Each process instance must remain distinguishable from other Recovery operations.

7. Scope

RURPM governs:

Recovery process identification. Recovery process eligibility and authorization references. Process initiation conditions. Process boundaries. Input Resource identity and quantity. Supporting Resource inputs. Recovery technology and pathway references. Process operating conditions. Process transformations. Intermediate Resource states. Process monitoring. Resource consumption. Process output measurement. Process residuals and losses. Recovery yield accounting. Process deviations and interruptions. Process completion and failure. Evidence provenance. Process reassessment. Process versioning and auditability.

RURPM does not independently govern recovered Resource quality certification, secondary Resource eligibility, authorized reintegration, Circularity achievement, or long-term Sustainability.

8. Foundational Governance Principles

8.1 Recovery Execution Principle

Recovery Process Started ≠ Recovery Process Completed.

A process may be initiated without reaching its intended operational endpoint.

8.2 Recovery Achievement Principle

Recovery Process Completed ≠ Usable Resource Recovered.

A completed operation may produce insufficient, contaminated, degraded, or otherwise unusable output.

8.3 Input–Output Principle

Recovery Input Quantity ≠ Recovered Output Quantity.

Material or energy may be lost, transformed, dispersed, consumed, or diverted into other outputs.

8.4 Resource Burden Principle

Recovery Output ≠ Net Resource Benefit.

Recovery may require significant electricity, water, chemicals, materials, or infrastructure capacity.

8.5 Validation Boundary Principle

Measured Recovery Output ≠ Validated Secondary Resource.

Recovered Resource validation remains a separate governance stage.

Undefined Process Boundary ≠ Reliable Recovery Accounting.

18. Resource Transformation

Recovery may change the physical, chemical, functional, or energetic state of a Resource.

RURPM must preserve the relationship between the input Resource and its resulting outputs.

Resource Transformation ≠ Resource Recovery Achievement by definition.

A transformation may produce a usable output, an unusable residual, or a combination.

19. Intermediate Resource States

Partially separated materials. Partially treated water. Disassembled components. Intermediate chemical fractions. Partially refurbished equipment.

An intermediate state must not automatically be classified as a completed recovered Resource.

21. Candidate Recovered Output

A Candidate Recovered Output is an output produced through the Recovery process that may satisfy a subsequent recovered Resource validation procedure.

Its existence does not establish that it meets the requirements of a particular future use.

Candidate Recovered Output ≠ Validated Recovered Resource.

22. Residual Outputs

A Recovery process may generate additional residual Resources.

These may require separate identification and subsequent Recovery assessment.

Recovery Process Residual ≠ Irrecoverable Waste by definition.

Where appropriate, the residual may re-enter ERRM and RPMM under a new, traceable Resource-use or process context.

24. Resource Accounting

RURPM should establish a Resource accounting relationship appropriate to the governed Resource.

For conserved material quantities, a simplified accounting structure may be:

Material Inputs = Material Outputs + Material Accumulation + Accounted Losses + Measurement Difference

This relationship must be adapted to the actual physical process and system boundary.

Mass, energy, water volume, and functional capacity are not interchangeable accounting quantities.

25. Recovery Yield

Where input and output quantities are physically compatible and the eligibility boundary is defined:

Recovery Yield = Candidate Recovered Output Quantity / Eligible Recovery Input Quantity

This is a process-output indicator, not proof of validated recovered quality.

The denominator, measurement basis, exclusions, and time boundary must be explicit.

High Recovery Yield ≠ High Recovery Quality.

26. Recovery Efficiency

Recovery efficiency may consider process performance relative to supporting Resource consumption and other operational requirements.

However, a single universal efficiency formula must not be imposed across materially different Resources.

Recovery Yield ≠ Recovery Efficiency.

Recovery Efficiency ≠ Sustainability.

39. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum operational RURPM implementation should contain eight governed layers.

Layer 1 — Recovery Process Identity

Establish the process identity, Recovery pathway, input Resource, operating context, and responsible operational references.

Layer 2 — Eligibility and Authorization Verification

Confirm the applicable eligibility and execution-authority references without creating or transferring authority.

Layer 3 — Process Boundary and Inputs

Define the process boundary, accepted Resource inputs, supporting Resource requirements, and measurement basis.

Layer 4 — Process Execution

Record the actual execution of Recovery stages, operating conditions, and Resource transformations.

Layer 5 — Resource Accounting

Measure Resource inputs, supporting Resource consumption, outputs, residuals, and losses using appropriate physical units.

Layer 6 — Process Control

Monitor deviations, interruptions, failures, corrective actions, and completion conditions.

Layer 7 — Evidence Evaluation

Evaluate process evidence, provenance, sufficiency, uncertainty, and conflict.

Layer 8 — Governed Output

Produce the Resource Recovery Process Profile , including the candidate recovered outputs requiring subsequent validation.

Current Recovery Process Profile → New Process / Measurement / Output / Loss / Deviation Evidence → Materiality Assessment → Process Boundary Review → Resource Accounting Review → Completion-State Review → Evidence Reassessment → Profile Update → Version Update

Reassessment must preserve the historical record rather than silently overwrite it.

47. Use Case 1 — AI Data Center Hardware Recovery

An AI data center sends retired accelerator servers to an appropriately authorized Recovery facility.

ERRM has established the residual hardware identities.

RPMM has established feasible Recovery pathways.

RURPM governs the actual Recovery operation.

Recovery Process

Hardware intake → Inspection → Disassembly → Component Separation → Material Sorting → Candidate Recovered Outputs

Governed Inputs

Retired accelerator servers, supporting electricity, equipment capacity, and relevant consumables.

Governed Outputs

Separated accelerator components, memory modules, circuit boards, metal fractions, and residual material.

Governance Requirements

RURPM records actual input quantities, component separation, processing energy, damaged components, material losses, and process completion.

It distinguishes components collected during disassembly from components that have been validated for reuse.

A successfully disassembled server is not automatically a successfully recovered usable Resource.

The resulting outputs are transferred to subsequent validation governance.

48. Use Case 2 — Industrial Wastewater Recovery

An industrial facility operates a water Recovery system.

ERRM identifies the wastewater stream.

RPMM establishes that treatment is operationally feasible.

RURPM governs the treatment process.

Recovery Process

Wastewater Intake → Pretreatment → Filtration → Membrane Treatment → Output Collection → Residual Stream Management

Governed Inputs

Wastewater volume, electricity, treatment chemicals, membrane capacity, and other relevant inputs.

Governed Outputs

Candidate recovered water, concentrated residual streams, process losses, and other material outputs.

Governance Requirements

RURPM records:

Actual wastewater input volume. Treatment stages completed. Electricity and chemical consumption. Candidate recovered water volume. Residual discharge. Measurement uncertainty. Process interruptions. Completion evidence.

The process may be completed while recovered water quality remains unvalidated for its intended subsequent use.

Water Treatment Completed ≠ Water Reuse Authorized.

50. Cross-Architecture Mechanism Principle

An operational mechanism may produce inputs for multiple governance architectures, but it belongs only to the architecture whose governed domain includes that mechanism itself. Other architectures govern only its domain- specific relevance or consequences.

A Recovery process may produce evidence relevant to Resource Availability, Sustainability, Value, Harm, and Liability.

RURPM governs the Resource Recovery process itself, while other architectures govern the consequences within their respective domains.

51. Canonical Boundary

RURPM governs how eligible and appropriately authorized Resource Recovery processes are initiated, bounded, executed, measured, evidenced, controlled, completed, interrupted, and reassessed, including the traceability of Resource inputs, supporting Resource consumption, process transformations, recovered outputs, residuals, and losses.

It does not independently establish Recovery eligibility or authorization, certify recovered Resource quality, create secondary Resource status, authorize reintegration, or determine Circularity and long-term Sustainability.

Its principal governed output is the Resource Recovery Process Profile .

52. Canonical Closing Statement

Recovery cannot be established merely by operating a process, processing a quantity of material, or reporting an output. What entered the process, what was consumed, what was transformed, what was lost, and what actually emerged must remain distinguishable and evidenced.

RURPM establishes the governance continuity between Recovery potential and actual Recovery operations — ensuring that process execution is traceable, Resource accounting is explicit, and recovered output is not treated as validated Resource capability before the required evidence exists.
