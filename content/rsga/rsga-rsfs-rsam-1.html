1. Canonical Definition

RSAM — Resource Substitution Assessment Module is the Core Module within RSFS that governs how an original Resource requirement, its substitution need, relevant operational context, existing Resource dependency, candidate alternative Resources, and preliminary substitution conditions are identified, bounded, evidenced, classified, and prepared for subsequent compatibility and feasibility assessment.

RSAM establishes the governance basis for determining what Resource requirement is being considered for substitution, why substitution is being considered, which alternatives may warrant further assessment, and what evidence is required before those alternatives can be evaluated as potential substitutes.

The module does not determine final compatibility, operational feasibility, substitution readiness, authorization, or effectiveness.

Its foundational distinctions are:

Substitution Need ≠ Substitution Feasibility.

Alternative Resource Identified ≠ Alternative Resource Validated.

Candidate Alternative ≠ Approved Substitute.

Resource Requirement Defined ≠ Resource Replacement Justified.

2. Operational Role

RSAM governs the initial assessment stage of Resource Substitution & Flexibility Governance.

Its operational role is to establish a traceable relationship between an existing Resource requirement and the alternatives proposed to satisfy, supplement, reduce, or reorganize that requirement.

RSAM ensures that substitution does not begin with an unsupported assumption that another Resource is equivalent.

Instead, the assessment begins with the actual operational requirement.

The module establishes:

The identity of the original Resource requirement. The operational function supported by that requirement. The Resource currently used or expected to be used. The relevant Resource dependency. The reason substitution is being considered. The intended substitution objective. The boundaries of the proposed change. Candidate alternative Resources or configurations. Preliminary Resource characteristics. Preliminary availability and capacity references. Material known restrictions. Initial supporting Resource dependencies. Missing or conflicting evidence. The assessment state. The handoff to subsequent compatibility and feasibility governance.

RSAM does not convert candidate alternatives into validated substitutes.

3. Operational Question

What legitimate Resource requirement is being considered for substitution, why is substitution being considered, which alternative Resources or configurations warrant further assessment, and under what defined operational boundaries and evidence conditions can those alternatives enter the substitution governance process without prematurely establishing compatibility, feasibility, authorization, or expected benefit?

RSAM occupies the first governance position within RSFS.

It establishes the assessment basis required before candidate alternatives can undergo compatibility evaluation.

Its output is not a substitution decision.

It is a governed assessment of the original requirement, substitution need, candidate alternatives, and preliminary evidence.

5. Governed Space

Resource Substitution Need & Alternative Assessment Governance

This governed space concerns the initial recognition, definition, contextualization, identification, classification, and evidence-based assessment of Resource substitution needs and candidate alternatives.

It includes the boundary between identifying a possible substitution and determining whether that substitution is actually valid.

The governed space is not limited to Resource scarcity.

Substitution assessment may occur during normal operations, strategic planning, technological change, infrastructure modernization, Resource Recovery reintegration, or anticipated operational constraints.

6. Governed Object

The primary Governed Object is:

A Resource Substitution Assessment Case connecting an identifiable original Resource requirement with a defined substitution need, operational context, candidate alternatives, preliminary conditions, and an evidence- based assessment state.

A single assessment case may contain multiple candidate alternatives.

Each alternative must remain individually identifiable.

Canonical relationship:

Original Resource Requirement → Operational Context → Substitution Need → Substitution Objective → Assessment Boundary → Candidate Alternative Identification → Preliminary Resource Characterization → Preliminary Dependency & Constraint Identification → Evidence Sufficiency → Candidate Assessment State → Resource Substitution Assessment Profile → Compatibility Assessment Handoff

8. Foundational Governance Principles

8.1 Requirement-First Principle

Substitution assessment must begin with the Resource requirement, not with the attractiveness of an alternative.

An alternative cannot be meaningfully assessed without understanding the function it is expected to support.

8.2 Need–Feasibility Separation

Substitution Need ≠ Substitution Feasibility.

A legitimate reason to seek substitution does not establish that substitution is possible.

8.3 Candidate–Substitute Separation

Candidate Alternative ≠ Validated Substitute.

Identification establishes assessment eligibility, not substitution validity.

8.4 Function–Resource Separation

Resource Requirement ≠ Existing Resource Identity.

The operational requirement may remain legitimate even when the Resource currently satisfying it becomes unavailable or undesirable.

8.5 Evidence Boundary Principle

Preliminary Evidence ≠ Conclusive Substitution Evidence.

8.6 Scarcity Independence Principle

Resource Scarcity Is Not a Prerequisite for Substitution Assessment.

8.7 Authority Separation Principle

Assessment Recommendation ≠ Substitution Authorization.

Changed Requirement ≠ Same Substitution Assessment by default.

56. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum operational RSAM implementation should contain eight governed layers.

Layer 1 — Original Requirement Identity

Establish the Resource requirement, supported function, required capacity, quality, location, time, and applicable restrictions.

Layer 2 — Substitution Need

Identify why substitution is being considered and preserve the evidence supporting that need.

Layer 3 — Substitution Objective & Boundary

Define the intended substitution outcome and the operational boundary of the assessment.

Layer 4 — Candidate Alternative Identification

Identify candidate Resources, capacities, configurations, or pathways without prematurely validating them.

Layer 5 — Preliminary Resource Assessment

Preserve available candidate characteristics, capacity, availability, location, timing, restrictions, and dependencies.

Layer 6 — Evidence Evaluation

Evaluate evidence sufficiency, uncertainty, missing information, and material conflicts.

Layer 7 — Candidate Assessment Decision

Determine whether each candidate can proceed to compatibility assessment, requires additional evidence, or falls outside the defined assessment boundary.

Layer 8 — Governed Output

Produce the Resource Substitution Assessment Profile and the applicable compatibility assessment handoff.

64. Use Case 1 — AI Data Center Cooling Substitution Assessment

An AI data center seeks to reduce dependency on freshwater-intensive cooling.

The existing Resource requirement is not simply “freshwater.”

The actual operational requirement is to maintain adequate cooling capacity under defined equipment, temperature, reliability, and operating conditions.

RSAM establishes:

Original Requirement: Maintain required thermal conditions for computing infrastructure.

Existing Resource Context: Freshwater-dependent cooling configuration.

Substitution Need: Reduce freshwater dependency.

Substitution Objective: Identify alternative cooling Resource arrangements.

Candidate Alternatives:

Recovered industrial water. Alternative cooling configuration. Other technically relevant cooling Resource arrangements.

Assessment Boundary: Defined data center infrastructure, location, cooling load, time period, and operational conditions.

Preliminary Dependencies: Electricity, water treatment, infrastructure capacity, and supporting equipment.

RSAM identifies and characterizes the alternatives.

It does not determine whether recovered water satisfies the required cooling quality or whether a different cooling configuration can maintain the required thermal performance.

Those determinations belong to subsequent compatibility and feasibility governance.

Cooling Alternative Identified ≠ Cooling Requirement Satisfied.

65. Use Case 2 — Industrial Material Substitution Assessment

A manufacturer seeks an alternative to a primary material whose supply has become constrained.

RSAM establishes:

Original Requirement: Material with defined mechanical, chemical, dimensional, and operational properties.

Existing Resource Context: Primary material used in the production process.

Substitution Need: Reduce dependency on constrained primary material supply.

Substitution Objective: Identify alternative materials capable of supporting the same production requirement.

Candidate Alternatives:

Validated recovered material. Alternative primary material. Modified material configuration.

Assessment Boundary: Defined manufacturing process, product requirements, production volume, location, and time.

Preliminary Dependencies: Processing energy, material preparation, production equipment, and supply availability.

RSAM identifies the candidates and preserves the available evidence.

It does not declare the recovered material equivalent merely because it belongs to the same material family.

Material Alternative Identified ≠ Material Substitution Validated.

68. Cross-Architecture Mechanism Principle

An operational mechanism may produce inputs for multiple governance architectures, but it belongs only to the architecture whose governed domain includes that mechanism itself. Other architectures govern only its domain- specific relevance or consequences.

RSAM may use Resource characterization, availability, dependency, and Recovery evidence.

It governs only their relevance to the initial substitution assessment.

It does not redefine the mechanisms that produced those inputs.

69. Canonical Boundary

RSAM governs how an original Resource requirement, its substitution need, relevant operational context, existing Resource dependency, candidate alternative Resources, and preliminary substitution conditions are identified, bounded, evidenced, classified, and prepared for subsequent compatibility and feasibility assessment.

It does not determine final compatibility, operational feasibility, substitution readiness, authorization, transition execution, flexibility activation, or actual substitution effectiveness.

Its principal governed output is the Resource Substitution Assessment Profile .

70. Canonical Closing Statement

Resource substitution cannot begin with the assumption that an alternative is a replacement. It must begin with a clearly established Resource requirement, a legitimate reason for considering change, and traceable evidence identifying which alternatives deserve further evaluation.

RSAM establishes the governance foundation for Resource substitution — ensuring that alternatives enter the assessment process as candidates supported by defined requirements and evidence, not as presumed solutions whose compatibility, feasibility, or authority has yet to be established.
