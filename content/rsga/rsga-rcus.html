1. Canonical Definition

Resource Consumption & Utilization Standard (RCUS) is the Parent Standard within RSGA™ that governs how allocated Resources and Resource capacities are actually consumed, used, transformed, occupied, depleted, retained, released, or otherwise utilized in operational reality, including how Resource Consumption and Resource Utilization are measured, distinguished, attributed, evaluated, evidenced, and reassessed against the Allocation, purpose, time, Source, location, capacity, and other conditions under which Resource use was authorized or expected to occur.

RCUS establishes the governance layer between Resource Allocation and the downstream Resource states created by actual use.

Its foundational distinction is:

Allocated Resource ≠ Consumed Resource ≠ Utilized Resource.

A Resource may be allocated but never consumed.

A Resource may be consumed without being effectively utilized.

A Resource capacity may be occupied without being physically consumed.

A Resource may be used for a purpose different from the purpose for which it was allocated.

A Resource may retain residual or recoverable value after its primary use.

RCUS makes these distinctions explicit and governable.

2. Operational Role

RSGA™ cannot determine Resource sustainability solely from what was available, demanded, prioritized, or allocated.

It must also determine what actually happened to the Resource after Allocation.

RCUS governs that transition.

Its operational role is to establish:

what Resource was actually consumed; what Resource capacity was actually used; when use occurred; where use occurred; from which Resource Source use occurred; which participant, system, function, or process used it; for what purpose it was used; whether use corresponded to the governed Allocation; how much allocated capacity remained unused; how effectively consumed or occupied Resource supported the intended function; what residual Resource state remained after use; what evidence supports these determinations.

RCUS therefore converts Resource use from an assumed consequence of Allocation into an observable and auditable governance state.

3. Core Question

How should the actual consumption and utilization of allocated Resources and Resource capacities be measured, distinguished, attributed, evaluated, evidenced, and reassessed so that governance can determine what Resource was truly used, by whom, where, when, from which Source, for what purpose, with what degree of utilization, and with what resulting Resource state?

4. Position in RSGA™ Governance Flow

Canonical RSGA™ progression:

Resource Characterization → Availability & Capacity → Demand → Dependency & Criticality → Access → Prioritization & Allocation → Consumption & Utilization → Scarcity & Constraint → Recovery & Circularity → Substitution & Flexibility → Interdependency & Resilience → Continuity & Sustainability

RCUS governs:

Governed Resource Allocation → Actual Resource Use → Consumption & Utilization State

It provides operational evidence required by downstream Resource governance.

5. Governed Space

RCUS governs:

Resource Consumption & Utilization Governance

This governed space concerns the actual operational use of Resources and Resource capacities after they have entered a legitimate use context.

It includes both physically consumptive Resources and non-consumptive or capacity-based Resources whose governance significance arises from occupancy, throughput, degradation, transformation, availability reduction, or operational use.

6. Governed Object

The primary Governed Object is:

An actual Resource Consumption or Resource Utilization event, state, or pattern associated with a defined Resource, Resource capacity, participant, system, function, purpose, Source, location, and time context.

Canonical relationship:

**Resource Allocation

8. Resource Consumption

Resource Consumption represents the actual use, depletion, transformation, expenditure, or other materially relevant reduction or commitment of a Resource through operational activity.

The exact physical meaning of Consumption depends upon the Resource.

Examples may include:

electricity consumed; water withdrawn or consumed; fuel burned; material transformed; chemical feedstock used; battery energy discharged; compute capacity consumed over time; storage capacity occupied; network capacity used.

RSGA™ remains Resource-agnostic.

The applicable specialized Resource architecture or measurement system determines the domain-specific physical mechanism.

9. Consumption Boundary

Allocation ≠ Consumption.

Assigning Resource capacity does not prove that the Resource was actually consumed.

10. Resource Utilization

Resource Utilization represents the extent to which a Resource or Resource capacity is actually used in relation to the governed operational function, capacity, purpose, or Allocation context.

Utilization may concern:

physical Resource use; capacity use; time occupancy; throughput; productive use; functional use; operational activation; other Resource-specific use relationships.

11. Utilization Boundary

Consumption ≠ Utilization.

Consumption describes Resource use or expenditure.

Utilization describes how Resource or Resource capacity is actually used relative to the relevant operational context.

The two may correlate but are not interchangeable.

12. Allocation–Consumption–Utilization Separation

RCUS requires explicit separation of:

Resource Allocation

Resource Consumption

Resource Utilization

These states represent different governance facts.

A system may receive a large Allocation while consuming little.

A system may consume substantial Resource while using it poorly.

A capacity Resource may be highly utilized without being physically depleted.

13. Allocation Does Not Prove Use

Historical Allocation should not be interpreted automatically as historical Resource Consumption.

Allocated Resource ≠ Used Resource.

This distinction is necessary for:

Demand validation; future Allocation; Scarcity analysis; capacity planning; efficiency assessment; continuity governance.

14. Consumption Does Not Prove Useful Utilization

Resource expenditure alone does not demonstrate that the intended operational purpose was achieved.

Resource Consumed ≠ Resource Productively Utilized.

15. Utilization Does Not Prove Value

A Resource may be fully utilized while producing little or no validated Value.

High Utilization ≠ High Value.

VEGA™ governs Value-related evidence where applicable.

16. Utilization Does Not Prove Sustainability

A Resource may be highly utilized while its use remains unsustainable.

High Utilization ≠ Sustainable Utilization.

17. Resource-Use Event

RCUS should identify materially relevant Resource-use events where event-level governance is appropriate.

A Resource-use event may contain:

Resource; Resource Source; actor or system;

function; purpose; quantity or capacity; start time; end time; location; Allocation reference; measurement evidence.

18. Resource-Use State

Where event-level measurement is inappropriate, RCUS may govern Resource use as a state over a defined interval.

19. Consumption Measurement

Resource Consumption should be measured using the applicable native Resource unit where sufficiently measurable.

Examples may include:

kWh; MWh; litres; m³; kg; tonnes; CPU-hours; GPU-hours; storage-byte-time; bandwidth-time; other domain-appropriate units.

20. Native Measurement Principle

Native Resource Measurement ≠ Normalized Resource Significance.

RCUS preserves native Resource measurement before any cross-resource normalization.

21. Measurement Boundary

A measurement should specify what exactly is being measured.

Examples:

withdrawal; gross Consumption; net Consumption; capacity occupancy; throughput; depletion; loss; transformation; release.

22. Measurement Context

A Consumption value without sufficient context may be governance-incomplete.

Relevant context may include:

Resource; Source; location; time; purpose; system; measurement boundary; measurement method.

23. Gross Consumption

Gross Consumption may represent the total Resource entering the relevant governed process or boundary.

24. Net Consumption

Net Consumption may represent Resource use after defined returns, releases, exports, or other Resource-specific adjustments.

25. Gross–Net Boundary

Gross Consumption ≠ Net Consumption.

The applicable measurement boundary must remain explicit.

26. Withdrawal vs Consumption

For some Resources, withdrawal and Consumption are distinct.

A Resource may be withdrawn and later returned, discharged, released, or recovered.

Resource Withdrawal ≠ Resource Consumption by definition.

27. Capacity Occupancy

Some Resources are governed primarily through capacity rather than physical depletion.

Examples may include:

compute capacity; storage capacity; network capacity; transport capacity; facility capacity.

RCUS should govern the actual occupancy or use of such capacity.

28. Occupancy Boundary

Capacity Occupancy ≠ Physical Resource Depletion.

29. Throughput

Resource throughput may be relevant where the governance significance depends on the amount of Resource passing through a system rather than remaining within it.

30. Resource Transformation

Consumption may transform a Resource rather than eliminate it.

The resulting material or Resource state should remain identifiable where relevant.

31. Transformation Boundary

Transformation ≠ Disappearance.

A Resource may move into another physical, chemical, digital, energetic, or operational state.

32. Resource Depletion

Where use materially reduces available Resource stock, RCUS should preserve the depleted quantity or state where measurable.

33. Renewable Resource Consumption

Consumption of a renewable or regenerative Resource remains Consumption.

Renewable Resource ≠ Non-Consumptive Resource.

34. Regeneration Boundary

The fact that a Resource may regenerate does not erase the Consumption event.

Regeneration belongs to the broader Resource lifecycle and sustainability context.

35. Direct Resource Use

Direct Resource use occurs where a Resource is consumed or utilized directly by the governed actor, system, function, or process.

36. Indirect Resource Use

Indirect Resource use may be attributed where another process or infrastructure consumes Resource materially because of the governed activity.

37. Indirect Attribution Boundary

Associated Resource Use ≠ Automatically Attributable Resource Use.

The attribution basis must be explicit.

38. Shared Resource Use

Where multiple participants or functions share the same Resource infrastructure or Consumption event, RCUS should establish an appropriate attribution basis where attribution is required.

39. Shared-Use Boundary

Shared Infrastructure ≠ Equal Resource Attribution by definition.

40. Concurrent Resource Use

Multiple systems may consume or utilize shared Resource capacity concurrently.

RCUS should preserve concurrency where it materially affects measurement or attribution.

41. Participant Attribution

Resource use should be attributed to the relevant participant where participant-level governance is required and evidence permits.

42. System Attribution

Resource use may be attributed to a system, subsystem, service, model, machine, facility, process, or other operational entity.

43. Functional Attribution

Resource use may be attributed to the function it supports.

Examples include:

production; cooling; inference; training; storage; transport; safety; maintenance.

44. Purpose Attribution

Where Allocation was purpose-specific, actual Resource use should be compared against that governed purpose.

45. Purpose Boundary

Authorized Resource Access ≠ Authorization for Every Purpose.

46. Source Attribution

Where materially relevant, RCUS should identify the Resource Source actually used.

47. Source Boundary

Allocation from Resource Type ≠ Consumption from Every Source of that Resource Type.

48. Location Attribution

Resource Consumption and Utilization may depend materially upon location.

The actual Resource-use location should be preserved where relevant.

49. Temporal Attribution

RCUS should preserve when Resource use occurred.

Relevant dimensions may include:

timestamp; duration; period; frequency; peak interval; operating window; season.

50. Temporal Boundary

Same Quantity at Different Times ≠ Same Governance Significance by definition.

Resource use during a constrained peak period may differ materially from equivalent use during surplus capacity.

51. Allocation-to-Consumption Comparison

RCUS should compare actual Resource Consumption with the applicable Resource Allocation where such Allocation exists.

Canonical relationship:

Allocated Resource − Consumed Resource = Unconsumed Allocation

subject to the applicable Resource and measurement model.

52. Allocation-to-Utilization Comparison

RCUS should compare allocated capacity with actual Utilization where relevant.

53. Unused Allocation

Allocated Resource capacity that was not consumed or utilized should remain identifiable.

54. Unused Allocation Boundary

Unused Allocation ≠ Resource Consumption.

It should not inflate historical Consumption evidence.

55. Idle Allocated Capacity

Capacity may remain reserved or allocated but operationally idle.

RCUS should distinguish idle Allocation from active Utilization.

56. Idle Boundary

Allocated Capacity ≠ Utilized Capacity.

57. Underutilization

Underutilization exists where actual Resource Utilization remains materially below the relevant governed or expected utilization context.

58. Underutilization Boundary

Low Utilization ≠ Automatically Governance Failure.

Legitimate reasons may include:

reserve requirements; resilience; standby capacity; safety margins; Demand variability; continuity requirements.

59. Overutilization

Where the Resource context defines an expected or authorized utilization boundary, actual use above that boundary should remain identifiable.

60. Overutilization Boundary

High Utilization ≠ Automatically Efficient Utilization.

High utilization may increase:

degradation; failure risk; Scarcity exposure; continuity risk.

61. Resource-Use Intensity

RCUS may establish Resource-use intensity where a meaningful denominator exists.

Generic form:

Resource-Use Intensity = Resource Consumption / Defined Operational Unit

The denominator may represent:

output; transaction; workload; service unit; production unit; time; other governed operational measure.

62. Intensity Boundary

Lower Resource-Use Intensity ≠ Automatically Better Governance.

Reduced Resource use may correspond to reduced output, reduced quality, shifted Consumption, or other consequences.

63. Resource Utilization Ratio

Where appropriate:

Resource Utilization Ratio = Utilized Resource Capacity / Available or Allocated Resource Capacity

The relevant denominator must be explicit.

64. Utilization Ratio Boundary

Utilization Percentage Without Denominator Context ≠ Sufficient Governance Evidence.

65. Resource Efficiency Evidence

RCUS may produce Consumption and Utilization evidence relevant to Resource efficiency.

It does not independently determine the full governance meaning of efficiency.

66. Efficiency Boundary

Resource Efficiency Evidence ≠ Resource Sustainability Determination.

67. Standby Resource Use

Resources consumed while maintaining readiness, redundancy, safety, or continuity should remain distinguishable from primary productive use where materially relevant.

68. Standby Boundary

Standby Consumption ≠ Automatically Waste.

Standby use may support legitimate resilience or continuity requirements.

69. Background Resource Use

Resource Consumption not directly associated with a primary workload may include:

cooling; monitoring; control systems; lighting; idle infrastructure; support services.

Where materially relevant, such Consumption should remain visible.

70. Background Boundary

Indirect Operational Consumption ≠ Zero-Value Consumption by definition.

71. Resource Loss

Resource Loss represents Resource that is consumed, dissipated, leaked, degraded, discarded, or otherwise removed from useful availability without corresponding intended utilization.

72. Loss Boundary

Resource Loss ≠ Resource Utilization.

73. Avoidable and Unavoidable Loss

Where evidence permits, Resource Loss may be differentiated into:

avoidable loss; unavoidable process loss; uncertain loss.

74. Loss Attribution

Resource Loss should be attributed where the causal or operational basis is sufficiently supported.

75. Residual Resource

After primary Consumption or Utilization, part of the Resource may remain in a residual state.

Examples may include:

waste heat; wastewater; material residue; unused chemical feedstock; battery charge; recoverable material; released capacity.

76. Residual Boundary

Primary Use Completion ≠ Resource End-of-Life.

77. Recoverability Signal

RCUS may identify that a residual Resource appears potentially recoverable.

It does not govern the Recovery process itself.

78. Recovery Boundary

Potentially Recoverable Resource ≠ Recovered Resource.

RRCS governs Recovery & Circularity.

79. Released Capacity

Capacity-based Resources may become available again after use.

Examples include:

GPU capacity; storage; bandwidth; transport capacity; facility capacity.

RCUS should identify release where materially relevant to the Resource state.

80. Release Boundary

Released Capacity ≠ Newly Created Capacity.

It represents capacity returning from an occupied state.

85. State Separation

Consumption State ≠ Utilization State.

The states should not be collapsed into a single generic Resource-use status.

86. Resource-Use Boundary

The Resource-use boundary determines which Resource flows, capacities, systems, and activities are included in the Consumption or Utilization measurement.

87. Boundary Consistency

Comparative Consumption or Utilization evidence should use sufficiently consistent boundaries.

88. Boundary Change

Where the Resource-use boundary changes, the change should remain visible.

89. Measurement Method

The method used to determine Resource Consumption or Utilization should be identifiable where materially relevant.

Possible methods include:

direct metering; system telemetry; transaction records; inventory change; engineering calculation; validated estimation; model-based attribution; other domain-specific methods.

90. Direct Measurement

Direct measurement should be distinguished from estimation.

91. Estimated Consumption

Where direct measurement is unavailable, Consumption may be estimated using a defined methodology.

92. Estimation Boundary

Estimated Consumption ≠ Directly Measured Consumption.

93. Estimated Utilization

Utilization may similarly be inferred from telemetry, activity records, scheduling data, or other evidence.

94. Evidence Confidence

RCUS should preserve material uncertainty or confidence associated with measured or estimated Resource use.

95. Measurement Uncertainty

Measurement uncertainty should not be silently represented as exact Resource use.

96. Measurement Gap

Where material Resource use cannot be measured or sufficiently estimated, the gap should remain visible.

97. Evidence Sufficiency

RCUS should determine whether available evidence is sufficient to support the relevant Consumption or Utilization state.

98. Evidence Provenance

Material Resource-use evidence should preserve where relevant:

evidence source; measurement source; time; Resource; Resource Source; location; system; participant; method; version.

99. Consumption Attribution Conflict

Conflicting evidence concerning who or what consumed a Resource should remain explicit.

100. Measurement Conflict

Where multiple measurement systems produce materially inconsistent results, the conflict should not be silently averaged away.

101. Unknown Resource Use

Where actual Resource use cannot be established, RCUS should support an Unknown state rather than assuming zero Consumption.

No Measurement ≠ No Consumption.

102. Consumption Baseline

Historical Consumption may establish a baseline for comparison where the boundary and context remain sufficiently comparable.

103. Baseline Boundary

Historical Consumption ≠ Future Resource Entitlement.

Past use may inform governance without creating automatic rights to future Allocation.

104. Utilization Baseline

Historical Utilization may similarly support operational comparison.

105. Baseline Drift

Changes in:

Resource boundary; technology; workload; location; Source;

measurement method; operating conditions

may reduce baseline comparability.

106. Consumption Variance

RCUS may determine variance between expected or allocated Resource Consumption and actual Consumption.

107. Utilization Variance

RCUS may determine variance between expected or allocated Resource utilization and actual Utilization.

108. Variance Boundary

Variance ≠ Governance Failure by definition.

The cause and significance of the variance must be evaluated.

109. Persistent Underutilization

Repeated material underutilization may provide evidence relevant to future Demand validation or Allocation.

RCUS provides the evidence.

It does not independently reduce future Allocation.

110. Persistent Overuse

Repeated Resource use beyond governed expectations or limits may require:

Allocation reassessment; Access review; Scarcity analysis; capacity review; other governance action.

RCUS records the operational evidence.

111. Consumption Pattern

RCUS may govern Resource Consumption as a pattern across time rather than only isolated events.

Patterns may include:

steady use; cyclical use; peak use; bursty use; seasonal use; persistent growth; declining use.

112. Utilization Pattern

Utilization patterns may reveal:

persistent idle capacity; saturation; cyclical occupancy; load imbalance; temporal concentration; other operational conditions.

113. Peak Consumption

Peak Resource Consumption may have governance significance distinct from average Consumption.

114. Peak Boundary

Average Consumption ≠ Peak Consumption.

115. Average Utilization

Average Utilization may conceal short periods of saturation or idleness.

116. Average Boundary

Average Utilization ≠ Utilization Distribution.

117. Temporal Granularity

Measurement granularity should be appropriate to the Resource and governance question.

Annual totals may be insufficient for Resources constrained hourly, daily, or seasonally.

118. Spatial Granularity

Aggregate Consumption may conceal local Resource stress or infrastructure constraints.

Where relevant, Consumption should preserve adequate spatial resolution.

119. Source Granularity

Aggregating multiple Sources may conceal materially different Resource characteristics.

Where Source matters, RCUS should preserve Source-level evidence.

120. Purpose Granularity

Aggregating all Resource use under one participant may conceal materially different operational purposes.

Where purpose matters, RCUS should preserve purpose-level evidence.

121. Consumption Compliance

Where Allocation contains Resource-use conditions, RCUS should determine whether actual use remained consistent with those conditions.

122. Allocation Compliance Boundary

Consumption Within Quantity Limit ≠ Full Allocation Compliance.

Use may still violate:

Source; location; purpose; time; rate; other conditions.

123. Allocation Overrun

Actual Resource use exceeding an applicable Allocation boundary should remain identifiable.

124. Allocation Underrun

Actual Resource use materially below Allocation may also remain identifiable where relevant.

125. Overrun Boundary

Resource Overrun ≠ Automatic Access Revocation.

The consequence belongs to the competent governance mechanism.

126. Unauthorized Use Signal

RCUS may identify Resource use inconsistent with the applicable Allocation or Access context.

It does not independently determine the final authorization or enforcement consequence.

127. Use Outside Allocated Purpose

Purpose-divergent Resource use should remain visible where purpose restrictions apply.

128. Use Outside Allocated Time

Resource use outside the governed Allocation period should remain visible.

129. Use Outside Allocated Location

Resource use outside the governed location should remain visible where location restrictions apply.

130. Use Outside Allocated Source

Resource use from a non-authorized or non-allocated Source should remain visible where Source restrictions apply.

131. Consumption Accountability

Resource-use evidence should support attribution sufficient for relevant governance accountability without RCUS itself determining liability.

132. Accountability Boundary

Consumption Attribution ≠ Liability Determination.

LIGA™ governs Liability where applicable.

133. Environmental Consequence Boundary

Resource Consumption may produce environmental consequences.

RCUS records Resource-use evidence but does not absorb the governance of all resulting harms or environmental effects.

134. Harm Boundary

Resource Consumption ≠ Harm by definition.

HAGA™ governs Harm where applicable.

135. Economic Cost Boundary

Resource Consumption may create economic cost.

Consumption ≠ Cost.

Economic consequences belong to the relevant governance context.

136. Value Boundary

Resource use may support Value creation.

Consumption ≠ Value.

VEGA™ governs validated Value relationships where applicable.

137. Consumption Optimization Boundary

RCUS may provide Resource-use evidence to optimization mechanisms.

It does not independently authorize operational changes merely because lower Consumption appears possible.

Optimization ≠ Permission.

138. Resource-Use Reassessment

Consumption and Utilization states should be reassessed when materially relevant new evidence becomes available.

139. Reassessment Triggers

Triggers may include:

new meter data; corrected telemetry; revised attribution; measurement-method change; Allocation change; Source change; boundary change; operational anomaly; material evidence conflict.

140. Reassessment Boundary

New Evidence ≠ Automatic Historical Deletion.

Previous determinations should remain traceable where versioning is required.

RCUS may consume:

RCHS — Resource identity, type, state, Source, and context.

RACS — Availability and capacity evidence.

RDGS — validated Demand and Demand-pattern evidence.

RDCS — Dependency and Criticality evidence.

RSFS — substitution and flexibility evidence.

RCSS — continuity and sustainability constraints.

GOA™ — governance authority evidence where Resource-use decisions require authority.

OBIDENITY® — identity, ownership, authority, permission, and provenance evidence.

PGA™ — governed prediction evidence for expected Resource use.

SIMULOS® — simulated Resource-use scenarios.

VEGA™ — Value and efficiency evidence where Resource use contributes to intelligence execution or other Value- governed contexts.

HAGA™ — Harm governance where Resource Consumption produces or contributes to Harm.

LIGA™ — Liability governance where Resource-use evidence becomes relevant to liability determination.

RCUS does not absorb these architectures or their governed mechanisms.

150. Cross-Architecture Mechanism Principle

An operational mechanism may produce inputs for multiple governance architectures, but it belongs only to the architecture whose governed domain includes that mechanism itself. Other architectures govern only its domain- specific relevance or consequences.

RCUS governs actual Resource Consumption and Utilization.

It may provide evidence to:

Scarcity governance; Recovery governance; Resilience governance; Sustainability governance; Value governance; Harm governance; Liability governance.

Those downstream consequences remain governed by their competent architectures.

151. Canonical Boundary

RCUS governs the measurement, distinction, attribution, evaluation, evidence, state representation, and reassessment of actual Resource Consumption and Resource Utilization after Resource Allocation, including quantity, capacity, Source, location, time, purpose, participant, system, function, unused Allocation, underutilization, overutilization, Resource Loss, residual Resource state, and released capacity.

It does not determine Resource identity, physical Availability, Demand, Dependency, Criticality, Access authorization, Resource Priority, Resource Allocation, Scarcity Governance as a whole, Resource Recovery mechanisms, substitution, system resilience, long-term Sustainability, Harm, Liability, Cost, or Value.

Its principal governed outputs are the Resource Consumption Profile and the Resource Utilization Profile .

152. Canonical Closing Statement

Resource governance cannot end at Allocation, because an allocated Resource may never be used, a consumed Resource may never be effectively utilized, and a highly utilized Resource may still fail to produce legitimate Value or sustainable outcomes.

RCUS establishes the operational evidence layer between Resource Allocation and downstream Resource governance — determining what was actually consumed, what was actually utilized, where, when, by whom, from which Source, for what purpose, and what Resource state remained after use.

Architecture: RSGA™ — Resource Sustainability Governance Architecture

Category: Governance & Enforcement

Subcategory: Resource Governance

Canonical Definition

Resource Consumption & Utilization Standard (RCUS) is the Parent Standard within RSGA™ that governs how allocated Resources and Resource capacities are actually consumed, used, transformed, occupied, depleted, retained, released, or otherwise utilized in operational reality, including how Resource Consumption and Resource Utilization are measured, distinguished, attributed, evaluated, evidenced, and reassessed against the Allocation, purpose, time, Source, location, capacity, and other conditions under which Resource use was authorized or expected to occur.

Governed Space:

Resource Consumption & Utilization Governance

Scope

RCUS governs the transition from Resource Allocation to actual Resource use , including Resource Consumption, Resource Utilization, capacity occupancy, Resource-use measurement and attribution, Allocation-to-use comparison, unused Allocation, underutilization, overutilization, Resource Loss, residual Resource state, released capacity, evidence, uncertainty, and reassessment.

It establishes what actually happened to an allocated Resource rather than assuming that Allocation itself proves Resource use.

Allocated Resource ≠ Consumed Resource ≠ Utilized Resource. Resource Consumption does not prove productive Utilization, and high Utilization does not by itself establish Value or Sustainability.
