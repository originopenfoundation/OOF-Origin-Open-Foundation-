1. Canonical Definition

RCRM — Resource Constraint Resolution Module

governs how Resource Constraints and Resource

Scarcity conditions are determined to be

stabilizing, relaxing, partially resolved, resolved,

recurring, persistent, unresolved, or reopened

based on evidenced changes in Resource

sufficiency, Constraint mechanisms, applicable

capacity, legitimate Resource requirements,

Source, location, time, quality, rate,

accessibility, delivery, regeneration,

dependencies, and other materially relevant

Resource conditions.

RCRM determines whether the Resource

Constraint has actually changed or ended .

It prevents temporary improvement, additional supply,

reduced Demand, successful intervention, or

disappearance of an observed symptom from being

automatically treated as genuine Constraint Resolution.

Its foundational distinctions are:

Constraint Stabilization ≠ Constraint Resolution.

Constraint Relaxation ≠ Constraint Resolution.

Temporary Improvement ≠ Constraint Resolution.

Additional Resource Supply ≠ Constraint

Resolution by itself.

Response Completion ≠ Constraint Resolution.

Constraint Resolution ≠ Permanent Resource

Sufficiency.

whether the condition remains recurring or

3. Operational Question

Has the Resource Constraint or Scarcity

condition materially stabilized, relaxed, partially

resolved, fully resolved, persisted, recurred, or

reopened, relative to the applicable legitimate

Resource requirement and Resource conditions,

and what evidence is required before the

constrained state can legitimately be closed?

8. Resource Constraint Resolution

Resource Constraint Resolution exists where sufficient

evidence establishes that the Resource conditions

responsible for the governed Constraint no longer

materially constrain the applicable Resource

requirement within the relevant context.

9. Resolution Principle

Improvement ≠ Resolution.

Resolution requires evidence that the materially relevant

Constraint condition has ceased or no longer produces

the governed insufficiency.

10. Constraint-State Comparison

RCRM compares:

prior governed Constraint condition

with:

current governed Resource condition.

The comparison should preserve materially relevant

changes in:

capacity;

requirement;

Source;

location;

time;

rate;

quality;

accessibility;

delivery;

infrastructure;

regeneration;

dependencies;

Constraint mechanisms.

11. Comparison Principle

Current Resource State Without Prior Constraint

Context ≠ Resolution Evidence by itself.

Resolution is a transition determination.

12. Resolution Baseline

Every material Resolution determination should identify

the prior Constraint state against which improvement or

Resolution is evaluated.

13. Baseline Principle

Unknown Prior Constraint State ≠ Reliable

Resolution Baseline.

14. Stabilization

Constraint Stabilization exists where the constrained

Resource condition is no longer materially worsening

but remains active.

15. Stabilization Principle

Constraint Stabilization ≠ Constraint Resolution.

16. Scarcity Stabilization

Scarcity Stabilization exists where the Scarcity

condition remains active but its state or severity has

ceased materially worsening.

17. Stabilization Boundary

A stable shortage remains a shortage.

Stable Scarcity ≠ Resource Sufficiency.

18. Constraint Relaxation

Constraint Relaxation exists where the Resource

limitation materially decreases while the Constraint

remains active.

19. Relaxation Principle

Constraint Relaxation ≠ Constraint Resolution.

20. Scarcity Relaxation

Scarcity Relaxation exists where Resource sufficiency

improves but remains below the level required to

establish Resolution.

21. Relaxation Boundary

Improved Resource Sufficiency ≠ Sufficient

Resource.

22. Partial Resolution

Partial Resolution exists where one or more material

Constraint mechanisms resolve while other relevant

Constraint mechanisms remain active.

23. Partial-Resolution Principle

Resolution of One Constraint ≠ Resolution of All

Constraints.

24. Multi-Constraint Resolution

Where multiple Constraint Types contribute to Scarcity,

RCRM should evaluate the resolution state of each

material Constraint mechanism.

25. Multi-Constraint Principle

One Resolved Constraint Type ≠ Resolved

Scarcity by default.

26. Binding-Constraint Transition

Resolving one Binding Constraint may expose another

Constraint that was previously non-binding.

Example:

Grid Capacity Constraint resolved

→ Cooling Capacity becomes Binding Constraint

27. Binding-Constraint Principle

Removal of the Current Binding Constraint ≠

Guaranteed Resource Sufficiency.

28. Complete Constraint Resolution

Complete Constraint Resolution exists where all

materially relevant Constraint mechanisms within the

governed Resolution boundary have ceased to

constrain the applicable Resource requirement.

29. Resolution Boundary Principle

Resolution should always be evaluated within a defined:

Resource;

Source;

location;

time horizon;

requirement;

capacity basis;

operational context.

30. Context Principle

Constraint Resolved in One Context ≠ Constraint

Resolved Everywhere.

31. Resource Sufficiency Restoration

Where Scarcity existed, Scarcity Resolution requires

revalidation that applicable Resource conditions have

become sufficient relative to the legitimate Resource

requirement.

32. Sufficiency Principle

Constraint Improvement ≠ Restored Resource

Sufficiency.

33. Scarcity Resolution

Scarcity Resolution exists where sufficient evidence

establishes that the applicable Resource conditions

again satisfy the legitimate Resource requirement within

the governed context.

34. Scarcity-Resolution Principle

Scarcity Resolution ≠ Resource Abundance.

A Resource need only become sufficiently available for

the applicable requirement.

35. Requirement Revalidation

RCRM should confirm that the Resource requirement

used to establish Resolution remains valid.

36. Requirement-Change Boundary

Scarcity may appear to resolve because the Resource

requirement changed.

That change must remain explicit.

Reduced Requirement ≠ Increased Resource

Availability.

37. Requirement-Reduction Resolution

Where legitimate Resource requirements are reduced,

the constrained condition may genuinely resolve

relative to the new requirement.

The cause of Resolution should remain identifiable.

38. Resolution-Cause Principle

Resolution Through Reduced Requirement ≠

Resolution Through Increased Capacity.

39. Capacity Restoration

Constraint Resolution may result from restored

Resource capacity.

Examples include:

additional usable capacity;

restored infrastructure;

increased delivery rate;

recovered Source;

restored quality;

restored accessibility.

40. Capacity Principle

Additional Capacity ≠ Constraint Resolution by

itself.

The resulting Resource sufficiency must be evaluated.

41. Additional Supply

Additional Resource supply may contribute to

Resolution.

However:

Additional Resource Supply ≠ Scarcity

Resolution by itself.

42. Supply Boundary

New supply may remain:

inaccessible;

unusable;

wrongly located;

temporally unavailable;

insufficient in rate;

inadequate in quality.

43. Source Restoration

A Source Constraint may resolve when the relevant

Resource Source becomes sufficiently available again.

44. Source Boundary

Source Restoration ≠ Resource-System

Resolution automatically.

Other constraints may remain active.

45. Location Restoration

A Location Constraint may resolve where Resource

sufficiency is restored at the location where it is

required.

46. Location Principle

Resource Availability Elsewhere ≠ Local

Constraint Resolution.

47. Rate Restoration

A Rate Constraint may resolve where Resource delivery,

processing, transmission, or withdrawal rate becomes

sufficient for the applicable requirement.

48. Rate Principle

Sufficient Total Quantity ≠ Rate-Constraint

Resolution.

49. Temporal Resolution

A Temporal Constraint may resolve when the Resource

becomes sufficiently available within the required time

window.

50. Temporal Principle

Resource Available After the Required Window ≠

Temporal Constraint Resolution for That

Requirement.

51. Quality Restoration

A Quality Constraint may resolve when sufficient

Resource again meets the required quality conditions.

52. Quality Principle

Quantity Restoration ≠ Quality-Constraint

Resolution.

53. Accessibility Restoration

An Accessibility Constraint may resolve when

applicable Resource capacity becomes accessible to the

relevant participant, function, system, or location.

54. Accessibility Boundary

Available Resource ≠ Accessibility-Constraint

Resolution.

55. Delivery Restoration

A Delivery Constraint may resolve where the Resource

can again be delivered with the required quantity, rate,

timing, reliability, and applicable conditions.

56. Delivery Principle

Upstream Resource Availability ≠ Delivery-

Constraint Resolution.

57. Infrastructure Restoration

Infrastructure repair or expansion may contribute to

Constraint Resolution.

However:

Infrastructure Restoration ≠ Resource

Sufficiency by definition.

58. Regenerative Recovery

A Regenerative Constraint may relax or resolve where

effective regeneration again supports the applicable

Resource condition.

59. Regeneration Principle

Improved Regeneration ≠ Regenerative-

Constraint Resolution by itself.

The relationship between regeneration, withdrawal,

Resource stock, and legitimate requirement remains

relevant.

60. Dependency Restoration

A Dependency-Induced Constraint may resolve where

the dependent Resource-enabling mechanism becomes

sufficiently restored.

61. Dependency Principle

Upstream Dependency Restoration ≠

Downstream Resource Resolution automatically.

Downstream Resource conditions must be revalidated.

62. Cross-Resource Resolution

Where Constraint propagation occurred across

Resource systems, Resolution should preserve whether

each affected Resource has actually recovered.

63. Cross-Resource Principle

Resolution in Resource A ≠ Resolution in

Resource B automatically.

64. Response Outcome

RCRM may consume the outcome of RSRM-governed

Resource Scarcity Responses.

Examples include:

Allocation reviewed;

reserve released;

Recovery initiated;

substitution implemented;

infrastructure restored;

Demand adjusted;

Access conditions changed.

65. Response-Outcome Principle

Response Completion ≠ Constraint Resolution.

The Resource condition must be re-evaluated.

66. Successful Intervention

An intervention may operate exactly as intended without

fully resolving Scarcity.

Successful Intervention ≠ Complete Resolution.

67. Failed Intervention

Where intervention does not materially improve the

Resource condition, the Constraint remains governed as

active or unresolved.

68. Intervention Boundary

RCRM evaluates Resource-state consequences.

It does not determine whether the original intervention

was authorized or appropriate.

69. Constraint Persistence

Constraint Persistence exists where the materially

relevant Constraint remains active across the applicable

governance horizon.

70. Persistence Principle

Persistent Constraint ≠ Permanent Constraint.

71. Persistent Scarcity

Scarcity remains Persistent where Resource conditions

continue to be insufficient relative to the applicable

legitimate Resource requirement.

72. Structural Constraint

A Constraint may be structural where it results from

enduring characteristics of the Resource System.

RCRM may preserve an established structural

classification.

It does not infer structural status merely from duration.

73. Structural Principle

Long Duration ≠ Structural Constraint by itself.

74. Recurring Constraint

A Recurring Constraint re-emerges after a period in

which the Constraint was inactive or materially resolved.

75. Recurrence Principle

Temporary Resolution ≠ Elimination of

Recurrence Risk.

76. Recurring Scarcity

Recurring Scarcity exists where Resource insufficiency

repeatedly returns under materially related conditions.

77. Recurrence Pattern

RCRM should preserve where applicable:

recurrence frequency;

recurrence interval;

recurrence duration;

recurrence trigger;

Constraint Type;

Source;

location;

affected requirement;

severity pattern.

78. Recurrence Boundary

Repeated Scarcity Events ≠ Same Scarcity Event

by default.

Event identity and continuity should remain traceable.

79. Reopened Constraint

A previously resolved Constraint may be reopened

where new evidence establishes that the limiting

condition has returned or was not genuinely resolved.

80. Reopening Principle

Prior Resolution Determination ≠ Permanent

Immunity From Reassessment.

81. False Resolution

False Resolution exists where a Constraint was

classified as resolved but subsequent evidence shows

that the materially relevant Resource insufficiency had

not actually ended.

82. False-Resolution Principle

Absence of Observed Symptoms ≠ Evidence of

Constraint Resolution.

83. Apparent Resolution

A Resource Constraint may appear resolved because:

monitoring stopped;

Demand temporarily declined;

affected activity stopped;

measurement changed;

Resource requirements were omitted;

the Constraint shifted location;

the Constraint shifted to another Resource.

These conditions require governance scrutiny.

84. Displacement Principle

Constraint Displacement ≠ Constraint

Resolution.

85. Geographic Displacement

A Resource Constraint may be reduced in one location

by transferring Resource pressure elsewhere.

86. Geographic Boundary

Local Resolution ≠ System-Wide Resolution.

87. Temporal Displacement

A Resource Constraint may be deferred rather than

resolved.

88. Temporal-Displacement Principle

Constraint Delayed ≠ Constraint Resolved.

89. Cross-Resource Displacement

A Resource Constraint may be reduced by increasing

dependence on another constrained Resource.

Example:

Water-saving cooling strategy

→ higher electricity requirement

This may resolve one Resource Constraint while

creating or intensifying another.

90. Cross-Resource Boundary

Resolution of One Resource Constraint ≠ Net

Resource-System Improvement by definition.

RIRS and RCSS govern broader resilience and

Sustainability implications.

91. Recovery-Driven Resolution

Recovered Resource may contribute to Constraint

Resolution.

RRCS governs the Recovery mechanism.

RCRM evaluates whether the resulting Resource state

actually resolves the Constraint.

92. Recovery Principle

Recovered Resource ≠ Constraint Resolution by

itself.

93. Substitution-Driven Resolution

Resource substitution may reduce or eliminate a

Constraint affecting the original Resource requirement.

RSFS governs substitution.

RCRM evaluates the resulting Constraint state.

94. Substitution Principle

Substitution Implemented ≠ Constraint Resolved

by definition.

95. Allocation-Driven Resolution

Reallocation may restore Resource sufficiency for one

requirement.

RCRM should preserve whether this creates or transfers

Scarcity elsewhere.

96. Allocation Boundary

Constraint Resolution for Participant A ≠

Constraint Resolution for the Resource System.

97. Demand-Driven Resolution

A Constraint may resolve because legitimate Resource

Demand decreases.

RCRM should preserve that causal pathway.

98. Demand Principle

Reduced Demand ≠ Increased Resource

Capacity.

99. Consumption-Driven Resolution

Reduced Consumption may contribute to restored

Resource sufficiency.

RCUS governs Consumption & Utilization evidence.

RCRM evaluates the resulting Constraint state.

100. Consumption Principle

Reduced Consumption ≠ Constraint Resolution

by itself.

101. Resolution Threshold

A Resolution Threshold represents the governed

condition required before a Constraint or Scarcity state

may transition to Resolved.

102. Resolution Criteria

Resolution criteria may include:

restored Resource sufficiency;

resolved binding Constraint;

sufficient applicable capacity;

required Source availability;

required location availability;

required rate;

required quality;

required accessibility;

required delivery capability;

sufficient regeneration;

resolved Dependency limitation;

sufficient evidence persistence.

103. Threshold Principle

Crossing One Resolution Threshold ≠ Complete

Resolution by default.

All materially applicable criteria must be evaluated.

104. Resolution Persistence Requirement

Some Resource contexts may require the improved

state to persist for a defined period before Resolution is

established.

105. Persistence-Evidence Principle

Single Sufficient Observation ≠ Durable

Resolution by default.

106. Resource-Specific Resolution

Resolution criteria should reflect the characteristics of

the Resource.

A suitable persistence window for electrical capacity

may differ materially from one for groundwater

regeneration.

107. Resolution-Evidence Basis

stabilization;

109. Evidence Sufficiency

Resolution should not be declared without sufficient

evidence.

Insufficient Resolution Evidence ≠ Resolved

Constraint.

110. Resolution Evidence-Limited

Resolution Evidence-Limited applies where

improvement is observed but evidence remains

insufficient to determine whether Resolution has

occurred.

111. Resolution Unknown

Resolution Unknown applies where the current

Resolution state cannot be adequately determined.

112. Unknown-State Principle

Resolution Unknown ≠ Constraint Resolved.

113. Resolution Conflicted

Resolution Conflicted applies where material evidence

supports incompatible Resolution determinations.

114. Conflict Principle

Conflicting Resolution Evidence ≠ Permission to

Declare Resolution.

115. Resolution Confidence

RCRM may preserve confidence in the Resolution

determination.

116. Confidence Principle

High Resolution Confidence ≠ Permanent

Resource Sufficiency.

118. Post-Resolution Monitoring

A resolved Constraint may require continued monitoring

where recurrence, delayed effects, Resource

regeneration, dependency recovery, or residual

uncertainty remains material.

119. Monitoring Principle

Constraint Resolved ≠ Monitoring No Longer

Required by definition.

120. Monitoring Horizon

Post-resolution monitoring should reflect:

Resource dynamics;

recurrence risk;

Constraint mechanism;

previous severity;

regeneration period;

Dependency recovery;

evidence confidence.

121. Residual Constraint

A Residual Constraint remains where the original

Resource limitation has materially improved but some

constrained condition persists.

122. Residual-Constraint Principle

Material Improvement ≠ Absence of Residual

Constraint.

123. Residual Scarcity

A Resource may remain scarce even after substantial

Constraint reduction.

124. Residual-Scarcity Principle

Reduced Constraint Magnitude ≠ Scarcity

Resolution.

125. Closure

A Constraint case may close when:

Resolution is sufficiently evidenced;

required post-resolution conditions are satisfied;

remaining uncertainty is acceptable under the

applicable governance method;

required records are preserved;

no active reassessment trigger remains.

126. Closure Principle

Administrative Closure ≠ Resource Constraint

Resolution.

The Resource state must support closure.

127. Resolution Record

Resolution should preserve the transition from the

previous constrained state to the resolved or modified

state.

128. Historical Traceability

Historical Scarcity and Constraint states should remain

traceable after Resolution.

Resolved ≠ Erased.

129. Resolution Versioning

Material changes in Resolution determination should

create a traceable version update.

130. Reassessment

Resolution should be reassessed where materially

relevant evidence changes.

131. Reassessment Triggers

Triggers may include:

Resource capacity decline;

Source degradation;

location limitation;

rate reduction;

quality deterioration;

Dependency failure;

132. Reassessment Principle

Resolved Once ≠ Resolved Forever.

133. Prediction Boundary

Predicted Resolution may support planning.

However:

Predicted Resolution ≠ Actual Resolution.

PGA™ governs Prediction methodology where

applicable.

134. Simulation Boundary

Simulation may test whether an intervention could

resolve a Resource Constraint.

However:

Simulated Resolution ≠ Observed Resolution.

SIMULOS® governs simulation.

135. Operational Reality Boundary

Resolution should ultimately be grounded in applicable

operational Resource evidence where the governed

condition concerns operational reality.

ORA™ may provide relevant evidence relationships.

136. Scarcity-State Boundary

RSSM governs the Resource Scarcity State.

RCRM determines whether a previously constrained or

scarce state has materially transitioned toward or into

Resolution.

137. Constraint-Type Boundary

RCTM governs Constraint Type and mechanism

classification.

RCRM consumes that classification to determine

whether the relevant mechanism remains active.

138. Evaluation Boundary

RSEM governs Magnitude, Severity, Materiality, and

overall Scarcity Evaluation.

RCRM may consume changes in those evaluations as

Resolution evidence.

139. Response Boundary

RSRM governs the Resource Scarcity Response

Requirement.

RCRM evaluates whether resulting changes actually

altered the Resource Constraint.

Response Completed ≠ Resolution Proven.

140. Demand Boundary

RDGS governs Resource Demand.

RCRM may consume changed legitimate Resource

requirements without redefining Demand.

141. Access Boundary

RAGS governs Resource Access.

Access restoration may contribute to Constraint

Resolution.

RCRM does not create Access rights.

142. Allocation Boundary

RPAS governs Resource Priority and Allocation.

Allocation changes may contribute to Resolution.

RCRM does not determine Allocation.

143. Consumption Boundary

RCUS governs Resource Consumption & Utilization.

Changes in Consumption may contribute to Resolution.

RCRM does not redefine Consumption.

144. Recovery Boundary

RRCS governs Resource Recovery & Circularity.

Recovered Resource may contribute to Resolution.

RCRM determines only whether the Constraint state

changed.

145. Substitution Boundary

RSFS governs Resource Substitution & Flexibility.

Substitution may contribute to Resolution.

RCRM does not authorize substitution.

146. Resilience Boundary

RIRS governs Resource Interdependency & Resilience.

Constraint Resolution may provide resilience evidence

but does not determine resilience.

147. Sustainability Boundary

RCSS governs Resource Continuity & Sustainability.

Constraint Resolution ≠ Sustainability

Determination.

A currently resolved Constraint may remain structurally

likely to recur.

148. Value Boundary

Economic or operational Value does not determine

whether a Resource Constraint has resolved.

High Value ≠ Resolution Evidence.

VEGA™ governs Value relationships.

149. Harm Boundary

Reduced Harm does not by itself establish Resource

Constraint Resolution.

HAGA™ governs Harm.

150. Liability Boundary

Resolution of a Resource Constraint does not determine

Liability for the preceding constrained condition.

Constraint Resolution ≠ Liability Resolution.

LIGA™ governs Liability.

151. Automation Boundary

Software may evaluate Resolution criteria and produce

Resolution recommendations.

However:

Automated Resolution Classification ≠

Governance Authority.

152. Authority Boundary

Where a Resolution determination affects Access,

Allocation, reserves, operational restrictions, or other

governed decisions, the competent mechanism retains

authority.

Resolution Evidence ≠ Authority Transfer.

163. Use Case 1 — AI Data Center Constraint Resolution

An AI data center previously experienced:

Electrical Capacity Constraint

Cooling Capacity Constraint

Active Localized Compute Scarcity

A grid upgrade adds 8 MW of electrical capacity.

A superficial assessment could declare the problem

resolved.

RCRM reassesses the complete Resource condition.

Evidence shows:

electrical capacity is now sufficient;

the previous electrical Constraint is resolved;

cooling capacity remains below the level

required for full workload activation;

water-delivery rate remains constrained during

peak temperature;

usable compute capacity therefore remains

below the legitimate workload requirement.

RCRM determines:

Electrical Constraint — Resolved

Cooling Constraint — Active

Water Rate Constraint — Active

Compute Scarcity — Relaxing but Not Resolved

The grid intervention succeeded.

The Resource Scarcity did not fully resolve.

This prevents:

Resolution of One Constraint ≠ Resolution of All

Constraints.

164. Use Case 2 — Regional Water Scarcity Resolution

A region experiencing drought receives substantial

rainfall.

Reservoir levels increase and regional water quantity

the drought-related Scarcity resolved.

RCRM reassesses:

reservoir quantity;

groundwater regeneration;

treatment capacity;

delivery capacity;

municipal Resource sufficiency;

Source condition;

legitimate Resource requirements.

Evidence shows:

reservoir availability has materially improved;

groundwater remains below sustainable

recovery conditions;

one municipality continues to face delivery

limitations;

regional drinking-water requirements are

currently satisfied;

agricultural requirements remain partially

constrained.

RCRM therefore establishes:

Regional Quantity Constraint — Relaxing

Drinking-Water Scarcity — Resolved for the

current horizon

Groundwater Regenerative Constraint —

Persistent

Municipal Delivery Constraint — Active

Agricultural Scarcity — Partially Resolved

The Resource system is not forced into a false binary

classification of:

“drought” or “no drought.”

RCRM preserves which Resource constraints actually

ended, which remain active, and which require

continued monitoring.

166. Cross-Architecture Mechanism Principle

An operational mechanism may produce inputs

for multiple governance architectures, but it

belongs only to the architecture whose governed

domain includes that mechanism itself. Other

architectures govern only its domain-specific

relevance or consequences.

RCRM governs whether the Resource Constraint or

Scarcity condition has actually stabilized,

relaxed, persisted, recurred, reopened, or

resolved .

It does not govern the independent mechanisms that

may have produced that change.

167. Canonical Boundary

RCRM governs how Resource Constraints and

Resource Scarcity conditions are determined to

be stabilizing, relaxing, partially resolved,

resolved, recurring, persistent, unresolved, or

reopened based on evidenced changes in

Resource sufficiency, Constraint mechanisms,

applicable capacity, legitimate Resource

requirements, Source, location, time, quality,

rate, accessibility, delivery, regeneration,

dependencies, response outcomes, and other

materially relevant Resource conditions.

It does not independently determine Resource Demand,

grant Access, determine Priority or Allocation, measure

Consumption as an originating mechanism, perform

Recovery, authorize substitution, determine resilience or

long-term Sustainability, determine Value, Harm, or

Liability, or execute operational intervention.

Its principal governed output is the Resource

Constraint Resolution Profile .

168. Canonical Closing Statement

A Resource Constraint is not resolved because

conditions improved, because additional supply

arrived, because an intervention was completed,

or because the visible symptom disappeared.

Resolution exists only when the Resource

conditions that created the governed Constraint

no longer materially prevent the applicable

legitimate Resource requirement from being

satisfied.

RCRM closes the Scarcity governance lifecycle

by proving what actually ended, what merely

improved, what remains constrained, what

shifted elsewhere, and what may return —

ensuring that Resource Resolution is an

evidenced state transition rather than an

optimistic declaration.
