About RSGA™ — Resource Sustainability Governance Architecture

Governing Resource Sustainability Across Interdependent Resource Systems

RSGA™ is the common OOF® governance architecture for finite, regenerative, recoverable, flow-constrained, capacity-constrained, strategically concentrated and interdependent Resources and Resource Systems.

It governs Resource Sustainability conditions, not the specialized physical mechanisms of Resource production, extraction, distribution, consumption, recovery or regeneration. It does not independently create Governance Authority, determine Accountability, replace Operational Reality, or govern general Evidence, Validation, Integrity, Identity, Prediction or Value.

Canonical Purpose

RSGA™ identifies, separates and connects the governance spaces through which Resources are characterized; availability and capacity are established; demand and dependencies are assessed; access, priority and allocation are governed; consumption, scarcity, recovery, substitution, resilience, continuity and sustainability remain governable.

Resource Existence ≠ Resource Availability. Demand ≠ Entitlement. Dependency ≠ Criticality. Allocation ≠ Authority. Consumption ≠ Utilization. Resilience ≠ Continuity. Continuity ≠ Sustainability.

Architecture

RSGA™ contains 12 Core Parent Standards and 60 Core Modules. Its governance progression is methodological, not an irreversible physical lifecycle: changed Resource conditions may require reassessment while valid prior records remain preserved.
