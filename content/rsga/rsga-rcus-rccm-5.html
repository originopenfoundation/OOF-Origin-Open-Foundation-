1. Canonical Definition

RCCM — Resource Consumption Compliance Module governs how actual Resource Consumption and Resource Utilization are compared with the applicable governed Resource Allocation, Allocation limits, purpose, Resource Source, location, time, rate, capacity, participant, system, and other Resource-use conditions in order to determine whether actual Resource use remained within, diverged from, exceeded, underused, or could not be sufficiently evaluated against the conditions under which Resource use was allocated or otherwise governed.

RCCM establishes the governed comparison between:

what Resource use was allocated or conditioned

and

what Resource use actually occurred.

Its foundational distinctions are:

Resource Allocation ≠ Resource Consumption.

Resource Allocation ≠ Resource Utilization.

Resource-Use Variance ≠ Governance Violation by definition.

Compliance Determination ≠ Authority to Modify Resource Allocation.

2. Operational Role

RCMM establishes what Resource was actually consumed.

RUSM establishes how Resource capacity was actually utilized.

RCBM establishes the applicable Resource Consumption Boundary.

RUCM establishes who or what used the Resource, for what function and purpose, from which Source, where, when, and under which operational conditions.

RCCM brings these evidence layers together and compares operational reality with the applicable Resource Allocation and Resource-use conditions.

Its role is to determine whether actual Resource use was:

within governed conditions; below an applicable Allocation; at an applicable limit; above an applicable Allocation or limit; used at a different rate; used from a different Source; used at a different location; used during a different time interval; used for a different purpose; used by a different participant or system; underutilized relative to Allocation; otherwise materially divergent; or impossible to determine because evidence is insufficient or conflicted.

RCCM does not assume that every variance is improper.

It establishes the variance first.

Governance interpretation and intervention remain subject to the competent authority, exception, reallocation, emergency, Access, or other governance mechanism.

3. Operational Question

Did actual Resource Consumption and Resource Utilization remain within the applicable governed Resource Allocation, limits, purpose, Source, location, time, rate, capacity, participant, system, and other Resource-use conditions, and where divergence occurred, what was the nature, magnitude, evidence, materiality, legitimacy status, and required governance response?

7. Scope

RCCM governs:

Allocation-to-use comparison; Consumption-to-Allocation variance; Utilization-to-Allocation variance; quantity compliance; capacity compliance; rate compliance; Source compliance; location compliance; temporal compliance; purpose compliance; participant compliance; system compliance; functional compliance where applicable; minimum-use conditions where legitimately established; maximum-use conditions; conditional Resource-use requirements; unused Allocation; underutilized Allocation; Resource overrun; rate exceedance; capacity exceedance; purpose divergence; Source divergence; location divergence; temporal divergence; participant divergence; system divergence; multiple simultaneous variances; authorized exceptions; emergency context; reallocation context; compliance evidence; comparison compatibility; compliance uncertainty; compliance conflict; compliance state; variance materiality; escalation signals; reassessment; traceability; versioning.

RCCM does not independently:

create Resource Allocation; change Resource Priority; modify Allocation limits; authorize exceptions; grant Resource Access; revoke Resource Access; determine Scarcity; govern Resource Recovery; determine Resource Value; determine Harm; determine Liability; determine legal compliance; determine long-term Sustainability.

8. Allocation-to-Use Comparison

RCCM compares governed Resource-use conditions with actual Resource-use evidence.

The comparison may include:

quantity; capacity; rate; Source; location; time; purpose; participant; system; function; other applicable conditions.

9. Comparison Principle

Allocated Resource ≠ Consumed Resource ≠ Utilized Resource.

Allocation is the governed reference.

Consumption and Utilization represent operational reality.

RCCM compares them without collapsing them into a single state.

10. Applicable Allocation

Every compliance determination should identify the applicable Resource Allocation or other legitimate Resource-use reference.

The applicable reference should preserve:

Allocation ID; Allocation version; effective period; Resource; quantity or capacity; limits; conditions; purpose; Source; location; participant or system where applicable.

11. Allocation-Version Principle

Current Allocation ≠ Historical Allocation.

The Allocation version applicable when Resource use occurred must be preserved where material.

12. Allocation Validity

RCCM should determine whether the applicable Allocation was valid during the Resource-use interval.

13. Allocation Validity Boundary

Expired Allocation ≠ Valid Resource-Use Basis.

14. Quantity Comparison

Where Allocation establishes Resource quantity, RCCM should compare actual Consumption with the applicable quantity.

Generic relationship:

Quantity Variance = Actual Consumption − Allocated Quantity

where such comparison is valid for the governed Resource.

15. Quantity Compliance

Actual Resource Consumption may be:

below Allocation; equal to Allocation; within permitted tolerance; above Allocation; unknown relative to Allocation.

16. Quantity Boundary

Consumption Below Allocation ≠ Automatically Compliant Resource Use.

Other governed conditions may still diverge.

17. Resource Overrun

Resource Overrun exists where actual Resource Consumption exceeds an applicable maximum quantity.

18. Overrun Principle

Resource Overrun ≠ Automatic Access Revocation.

RCCM determines the variance.

Competent governance determines the response.

19. Cumulative Overrun

Repeated individually small overruns may produce a material cumulative variance.

Individually Immaterial Variances ≠ Collectively Immaterial Variance.

20. Capacity Comparison

Where Allocation concerns capacity rather than physical quantity, RCCM should compare actual utilized capacity with applicable Allocation conditions.

21. Capacity Compliance

Capacity use may be:

below Allocation; within Allocation; at Allocation limit; above Allocation; unknown.

22. Capacity Boundary

Capacity Occupancy Within Allocation ≠ Full Resource-Use Compliance by definition.

23. Rate Comparison

Resource-use conditions may establish maximum or minimum rates.

Examples include:

power draw; water flow; fuel flow; compute throughput; bandwidth; material throughput.

24. Rate Exceedance

A Resource may remain within its total quantity Allocation while exceeding an applicable instantaneous or interval rate.

25. Rate Principle

Consumption Within Total Quantity Limit ≠ Rate Compliance.

26. Peak-Rate Compliance

Peak Resource use should be evaluated separately where peak limits apply.

Average Resource Use Within Limit ≠ Peak Resource Use Within Limit.

27. Temporal Compliance

Resource Allocation may apply only during a defined:

start time; end time; time window; day; season; operational event; other temporal condition.

28. Temporal Divergence

Temporal Divergence exists where Resource use occurs materially outside the applicable temporal condition.

29. Temporal Principle

Authorized Quantity ≠ Authorization at Every Time.

30. Location Compliance

Resource Allocation may be location-specific.

RCCM should compare actual Resource-use location with the governed location condition.

31. Location Divergence

Location Divergence exists where Resource use occurs at a materially different location from the applicable Allocation condition.

32. Location Principle

Authorization to Use Resource at Location A ≠ Authorization to Use It at Location B.

33. Source Compliance

Allocation may specify or constrain Resource Source.

RCCM should compare actual Source with the applicable Source condition.

34. Source Divergence

Source Divergence exists where actual Resource use comes from a different Source than the governed condition.

35. Source Principle

Authorization for Resource Type X ≠ Authorization for Every Source of Resource Type X.

36. Source Mix Compliance

Where multiple Sources are permitted under defined conditions, RCCM may evaluate the actual Source mix against those conditions.

37. Purpose Compliance

Resource Allocation may be purpose-specific.

RCCM should compare actual-purpose evidence with the governed purpose.

38. Purpose Divergence

Purpose Divergence exists where actual Resource use materially differs from the governed purpose.

39. Purpose Principle

Authorized Resource Access ≠ Authorization for Every Purpose.

40. Purpose-Divergence Boundary

Purpose Divergence ≠ Automatic Misuse Determination.

Changed conditions, emergencies, authorized exceptions, or incomplete evidence may explain the divergence.

41. Participant Compliance

Allocation may apply to a defined participant.

RCCM should compare actual participant attribution with the applicable Allocation condition.

42. Participant Divergence

Participant Divergence exists where actual Resource use is attributed to a materially different participant.

43. Participant Principle

Allocation to Participant A ≠ Allocation to Every Participant Sharing the Same Infrastructure.

44. System Compliance

Allocation may apply to a defined:

system; machine; process; workload; facility; infrastructure component.

45. System Divergence

System Divergence exists where actual Resource use is attributed to a materially different system.

46. System Principle

Resource Available Within Shared Infrastructure ≠ Allocation to Every System Within That Infrastructure.

47. Functional Compliance

Where Allocation is function-specific, actual Resource use may be compared with the governed function.

48. Functional Divergence

Resource use supporting a materially different function should remain identifiable.

49. Multi-Dimensional Compliance

A Resource-use event may comply with some conditions while diverging from others.

For example:

quantity — compliant; rate — exceeded; Source — compliant; purpose — divergent; time — compliant.

50. Multi-Dimensional Principle

Compliance With One Allocation Dimension ≠ Compliance With All Allocation Dimensions.

51. Full Compliance

Full Compliance exists where sufficient evidence indicates that all materially applicable Resource-use conditions were satisfied.

52. Partial Compliance

Partial Compliance exists where some materially applicable conditions were satisfied while others materially diverged.

53. Compliance Evidence Boundary

Absence of Detected Variance ≠ Proof of Full Compliance when Evidence Is Incomplete.

54. Unused Allocation

Unused Allocation exists where Resource quantity or capacity was allocated but not consumed or utilized during the applicable interval.

55. Unused Allocation Principle

Unused Allocation ≠ Resource Consumption.

56. Unused Allocation Interpretation

Unused Allocation may result from:

Demand reduction; reserve requirements; contingency planning; operational delay; system failure; changed conditions; forecast uncertainty; other legitimate conditions.

Therefore:

Unused Allocation ≠ Governance Failure by definition.

57. Underutilized Allocation

An Allocation may be materially underutilized relative to the capacity made available.

58. Underutilization Principle

Low Allocation Utilization ≠ Automatic Resource Misallocation.

Further governance context is required.

59. Overutilized Allocation

Actual Resource Utilization may exceed the capacity expected or permitted under the applicable Allocation.

60. Overutilization Boundary

High Utilization ≠ Allocation Violation by itself.

A relevant Allocation condition must exist.

61. Minimum Resource-Use Condition

Some governed Allocations may contain legitimate minimum-use conditions.

RCCM may compare actual use with those conditions.

62. Minimum-Use Boundary

Low Resource Use ≠ Non-Compliance unless a Legitimate Minimum Condition Exists.

63. Conditional Allocation

An Allocation may be valid only when defined conditions are satisfied.

64. Conditional Compliance

RCCM should preserve whether those conditions were active during actual Resource use.

65. Conditional Principle

Allocation Approval ≠ Unconditional Resource-Use Authority.

66. Exception Context

A Resource-use variance may be covered by an authorized exception.

RCCM should preserve:

exception identity; exception authority; scope; conditions; effective period.

67. Exception Principle

Exception ≠ Governance Bypass.

68. Claimed Exception

A claimed exception without sufficient authority or evidence should not automatically convert variance into compliant Resource use.

Claimed Exception ≠ Authorized Exception.

69. Reallocation Context

RPAS may modify Resource Allocation through governed reallocation.

RCCM should use the Allocation state applicable at the time of actual Resource use.

70. Reallocation Principle

Later Reallocation ≠ Automatic Retroactive Authorization of Earlier Resource Use.

Unless competent governance explicitly establishes retroactive effect.

71. Emergency Context

Actual Resource use may legitimately diverge from ordinary conditions during emergencies.

RCCM should preserve:

emergency condition; applicable emergency authority; Resource-use variance; effective period; evidence.

72. Emergency Principle

Emergency ≠ Absence of Governance.

73. Operational Necessity

Operational necessity may explain Resource-use variance.

It does not automatically establish authority.

Operational Necessity ≠ Resource-Use Authorization by itself.

74. Compliance Variance

A Compliance Variance represents a material difference between applicable governed Resource-use conditions and actual Resource use.

75. Variance Types

RCCM may identify:

quantity variance; capacity variance; rate variance; Source variance; location variance; temporal variance; purpose variance; participant variance; system variance; functional variance; other applicable variance.

76. Variance Magnitude

Where quantitatively meaningful, RCCM should preserve the magnitude of variance.

77. Variance Direction

Variance may represent:

underuse; overuse; different use; shifted use; unverified use.

78. Variance Materiality

RCCM should determine whether a variance is materially relevant to governance where such determination is required.

Materiality may depend upon:

Resource quantity; Resource Criticality; Scarcity context; rate; duration; purpose; location; Source; recurrence; operational consequence; applicable Allocation condition.

79. Materiality Principle

Numerically Small Variance ≠ Automatically Immaterial Variance.

A small deviation may be critical for a highly constrained Resource, protected purpose, Source, location, or time period.

80. Persistent Variance

Repeated or persistent divergence may indicate structural mismatch among:

Demand; Allocation; actual Resource use; operational conditions.

81. Persistent-Variance Boundary

Persistent Variance ≠ Automatic Proof of Misconduct.

It may indicate that the Allocation or another governance mechanism requires reassessment.

84. Compliant

Sufficient evidence indicates that actual Resource use remained within all materially applicable governed Resource-use conditions.

85. Conditionally Compliant

Resource use satisfies applicable governance because a valid:

conditional basis; authorized exception; emergency authority; or other governed condition

applies.

86. Compliance Unknown

Evidence is insufficient to determine compliance.

Compliance Unknown ≠ Compliant.

87. Compliance Conflicted

Material evidence supports incompatible compliance determinations.

Conflicting evidence should remain visible until sufficiently resolved.

88. Compliance Unresolved

Relevant evidence exists, but a sufficiently supported final determination cannot yet be established.

89. Measurement Uncertainty

RCMM measurement uncertainty should propagate into the compliance determination where material.

Uncertain Consumption Measurement ≠ Certain Compliance Determination.

90. Utilization Uncertainty

RUSM uncertainty should propagate where Utilization conditions are relevant.

91. Boundary Uncertainty

RCBM boundary uncertainty should remain visible where it affects comparison.

92. Context Uncertainty

RUCM attribution or context uncertainty should remain visible where it affects compliance.

93. Comparison Compatibility

RCCM should determine whether Allocation evidence and actual-use evidence are sufficiently compatible for comparison.

Compatibility: may require alignment of:

Resource identity; unit; Consumption Boundary; Source; location; time interval; capacity basis; purpose; participant; system.

94. Comparison Principle

Numerically Comparable Values ≠ Governance-Compatible Values by default.

95. Unit Alignment

Where Allocation and actual Consumption use different units, a governed conversion may be required.

Converted Comparison ≠ Original Measurement Equality.

96. Boundary Alignment

Allocation and actual Resource-use boundaries should be sufficiently aligned.

Same Resource Quantity Under Different Boundaries ≠ Direct Compliance Comparison by default.

97. Temporal Alignment

Allocation period and actual Resource-use interval should be sufficiently aligned.

Monthly Allocation ≠ Hourly Resource-Use Limit unless explicitly defined.

98. Source Alignment

Source-specific Allocation should be compared with Source-specific actual use where evidence permits.

99. Purpose Alignment

Purpose-specific Allocation should be compared with actual-purpose evidence rather than assumed purpose.

100. Compliance Evidence

RCCM should preserve evidence supporting both sides of the comparison.

Reference evidence may include:

Allocation decision; Allocation limit; Access condition; authorized exception; reallocation record; emergency authority; other competent governance record.

Actual-use evidence may include:

RCMM Resource Consumption Measurement Profile; RUSM Resource Utilization State Profile; RCBM Resource Consumption Boundary Profile; RUCM Resource Use Context Profile; supporting operational evidence.

101. Evidence Provenance

Material evidence should preserve sufficient provenance for auditability.

102. Evidence Sufficiency

RCCM should determine whether available evidence is sufficient for the required compliance determination.

Missing Evidence ≠ Evidence of Compliance.

103. Evidence Freshness

Compliance evidence should correspond sufficiently to the relevant Resource-use interval and applicable Allocation version.

104. Compliance Conflict

Conflict may arise from:

different Allocation versions; conflicting Resource measurements; uncertain boundaries; conflicting attribution; exception ambiguity; temporal mismatch; Source mismatch; other evidence inconsistency.

105. Conflict Resolution

Resolution may require:

Allocation validation; measurement validation;

boundary alignment; context validation; exception validation; additional evidence; competent governance review.

106. Escalation Signal

RCCM may produce an escalation signal when a material variance requires action by another competent governance mechanism.

107. Escalation Boundary

Escalation Signal ≠ Final Governance Decision.

108. Reallocation Review Signal

Material or persistent variance may indicate that Resource Allocation should be reviewed.

Evidence Supporting Reallocation Review ≠ Authority to Reallocate.

RPAS retains Resource Allocation and reallocation governance.

109. Access Review Signal

Certain Resource-use variances may justify Access review.

Resource-Use Variance ≠ Automatic Access Revocation.

RAGS retains Access governance.

110. Demand Reassessment Signal

Persistent unused Allocation or overrun may indicate that Demand should be reassessed.

Actual Consumption ≠ Future Demand by definition.

RDGS retains Demand governance.

111. Scarcity Signal

Resource-use patterns may provide evidence relevant to Scarcity governance.

Resource Overrun ≠ Resource Scarcity by definition.

RSCS retains Scarcity and Constraint governance.

112. Efficiency Boundary

RCCM may provide evidence relevant to Resource efficiency.

However:

Resource-Use Compliance ≠ Resource Efficiency.

A compliant system may be inefficient.

An efficient system may still diverge from governed Allocation conditions.

113. Value Boundary

Resource-Use Compliance ≠ Validated Value.

VEGA™ governs Value relationships where applicable.

114. Sustainability Boundary

Resource-Use Compliance ≠ Resource Sustainability.

Compliance with current Resource-use conditions does not establish long-term Resource sustainability.

115. Harm Boundary

Resource-Use Non-Compliance ≠ Harm by definition.

HAGA™ governs Harm.

116. Liability Boundary

Resource-Use Non-Compliance ≠ Liability by definition.

LIGA™ governs Liability.

117. Legal Compliance Boundary

RCCM is a methodological Resource-governance mechanism.

RCCM Compliance ≠ Legal Compliance.

Legal or regulatory compliance remains subject to the competent legal or regulatory framework.

118. Automated Compliance Evaluation

Software may automatically compare actual Resource use with governed Resource-use conditions.

Automated Compliance Evaluation ≠ Enforcement Authority.

119. Automated Intervention

A technical system may be capable of:

throttling; suspending; redirecting; stopping; limiting Resource use.

The existence of technical capability does not establish governance authority.

Technical Intervention Capability ≠ Governance Authority.

120. Optimization Boundary

Optimization may recommend Resource-use changes.

Optimization ≠ Permission.

121. Predicted Compliance

Future Resource use may be predicted to exceed Allocation conditions.

Predicted Non-Compliance ≠ Actual Non-Compliance.

Prediction may support preventive governance but does not replace observed operational evidence.

122. Simulated Compliance

Simulation may test whether proposed Resource use would remain within governed conditions.

Simulated Compliance ≠ Operational Compliance.

123. Historical Compliance

Historical Resource-use compliance may support trend analysis.

Historical Compliance ≠ Future Resource Entitlement.

124. Compliance Trend

RCCM may preserve recurring patterns of:

underuse; overuse; rate exceedance; Source divergence; location divergence; purpose divergence; other material variance.

135. Use Case 1 — AI Compute Resource Allocation

An AI compute environment allocates a workload:

1,000 GPU-hours; a defined GPU cluster; an approved training purpose; a specific operating period; a maximum power condition.

RCMM establishes actual Resource Consumption.

RUSM determines actual GPU capacity utilization.

RCBM confirms the applicable compute and Resource Consumption Boundary.

RUCM establishes that:

850 GPU-hours supported the approved training workload; 70 GPU-hours were associated with a separate workload; the Resource Source and location remained unchanged.

Total Resource Consumption remained below 1,000 GPU-hours.

However, part of the Resource use diverged from the approved purpose.

RCCM therefore does not classify the Resource use as fully compliant merely because total Consumption remained below the quantity limit.

It records the Purpose Divergence , preserves the supporting evidence, and produces the appropriate governance signal without independently changing the Allocation or Access state.

136. Use Case 2 — Regional Water Allocation

A regional Resource-governance system allocates an industrial facility:

10,000 m³ of water; from Source A; within a defined monthly period; subject to a maximum daily withdrawal rate.

RCMM establishes actual water Consumption.

RUSM establishes relevant infrastructure utilization.

RCBM confirms the applicable facility, Source, and temporal boundary.

RUCM establishes actual Source, location, time, and industrial purpose.

The facility uses only 9,500 m³ during the month.

However, on several days it exceeds the governed daily withdrawal rate.

RCCM determines:

monthly quantity — within Allocation; Source — compliant; location — compliant; purpose — compliant; daily rate — exceeded.

The Resource use therefore cannot be represented as fully compliant merely because monthly Consumption remained below 10,000 m³.

RCCM preserves the rate variance and its evidence for the competent governance mechanism.

138. Cross-Architecture Mechanism Principle

An operational mechanism may produce inputs for multiple governance architectures, but it belongs only to the architecture whose governed domain includes that mechanism itself. Other architectures govern only its domain- specific relevance or consequences.

RCCM governs the comparison between actual Resource use and applicable governed Resource-use conditions.

Its outputs may support:

Resource reallocation; Access review; Demand reassessment; Scarcity analysis; efficiency evaluation; Value governance; Harm governance; Liability governance; Resilience governance; Sustainability governance.

Those downstream mechanisms remain governed by their competent architectures.

139. Canonical Boundary

RCCM governs how actual Resource Consumption and Resource Utilization are compared with applicable Resource Allocation, Allocation limits, quantity, capacity, rate, Source, location, time, purpose, participant, system, function, exception, emergency, and other Resource-use conditions in order to determine Resource Consumption Compliance, variance, materiality, evidence sufficiency, conflict, and required governance signals.

It does not create or modify Resource Allocation, grant or revoke Resource Access, authorize exceptions, determine Resource Scarcity, govern Recovery, determine Resource Value, determine Harm or Liability, establish legal compliance, or determine long-term Resource Sustainability.

Its principal governed output is the Resource Consumption Compliance Profile .

140. Canonical Closing Statement

Resource governance cannot determine compliance by asking only whether total Resource use remained below an Allocation. A Resource may remain within its quantity limit while diverging from its rate, Source, location, time, purpose, participant, system, or other governed conditions.

RCCM closes the Resource Consumption & Utilization governance chain by comparing governed Resource-use conditions with operational reality — identifying where actual use aligned, where it diverged, what evidence supports that determination, and which competent governance mechanism must act next without converting measurement, variance, automation, or technical capability into authority.
