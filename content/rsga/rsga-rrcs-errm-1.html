1. Canonical Definition

ERRM — End-of-Use Resource Recognition Module governs how Resources, Resource components, Resource capacities, and residual Resource streams are identified, distinguished, bounded, classified, evidenced, and recorded when they complete, exit, interrupt, or materially change their original operational use.

ERRM establishes the governed identity and condition of what remains after a Resource-use stage.

It determines whether a Resource has reached the end of its original function, what residual Resource exists, which characteristics remain relevant, and what evidence is required before the residual Resource can enter subsequent Recovery assessment.

ERRM does not determine whether Recovery is technically feasible, economically justified, authorized, successful, or sustainable.

Its foundational distinction is:

Functional End-of-Use ≠ Resource End-of-Life.

2. Operational Role

ERRM establishes the entry point into Resource Recovery & Circularity Governance.

Its role is to prevent Resources from disappearing from governance merely because their original operational use has ended.

A Resource may leave its original use while retaining:

Physical material. Functional capability. Recoverable components. Residual energy. Residual water. Reusable structural properties. Chemical or material potential. Other Resource-specific characteristics.

ERRM identifies and records these remaining Resource conditions without prematurely classifying them as recoverable.

It transforms:

Original Resource Identity + Resource-Use Evidence + End-of-Use Event + Remaining Resource Condition

into:

an evidenced End-of-Use Resource Recognition State.

3. Operational Question

What identifiable Resource, Resource component, capacity, or residual stream remains when an original Resource use ends or materially changes, what is its actual condition and boundary, and what evidence is sufficient to establish its identity and post-use state without assuming recoverability or future usability?

Recovery assessment must not invent Resource identity, condition, or quantity that was not established through sufficient evidence.

5. Governed Space

End-of-Use Resource Recognition Governance

This governed space concerns the recognition and evidencing of Resources after their original use has ended, been interrupted, changed, or become unsuitable for its previous operational requirements.

It includes residual Resources that remain physically present, operationally measurable, identifiable through process outputs, or otherwise supported by sufficient Resource evidence.

6. Governed Object

The primary Governed Object is:

An End-of-Use Resource Recognition State associated with an identifiable Resource, Resource component, Resource capacity, or residual Resource stream after an original or preceding Resource-use stage.

Canonical relationship:

Original Resource → Original Use → End-of-Use Event → Post-Use Resource Observation → Residual Resource Identification → Resource Boundary → Resource Condition → Recognition State

7. Scope

ERRM governs:

End-of-use event recognition. Original Resource identity and use references.

Post-use Resource identification. Residual Resource identification. Residual Resource quantity and condition. Residual Resource origin and Source. Residual Resource location and time. Residual Resource composition where applicable. Functional and material state. Residual Resource boundaries. Mixed Resource streams. Resource fragmentation and aggregation. Residual contamination evidence. Resource ownership or custody references where relevant. Evidence sufficiency, uncertainty, and conflict. Recognition-state monitoring and reassessment. Historical traceability and versioning.

ERRM does not govern the final assessment of recoverability, Recovery eligibility, Recovery authorization, Recovery process execution, Recovery yield, recovered Resource validation, secondary Resource classification, reintegration, or Circularity performance.

8. End-of-Use Recognition Principle

The completion of an original Resource function is a transition in Resource use.

It is not necessarily the disappearance or exhaustion of the Resource itself.

End of Original Use ≠ End of Resource Existence.

End of Functional Suitability ≠ End of All Usable Potential.

End-of-Use Recognition ≠ Recovery Determination.

9. End-of-Use Event

An End-of-Use Event is an identifiable event or condition through which a Resource ceases, interrupts, or materially changes its original operational function.

Examples include:

Scheduled retirement

Operational replacement

Functional degradation

Performance threshold failure

Physical damage

Process completion

Resource transformation

Resource discharge

Resource release

Resource disposal

Change of operational requirements

Transfer to another Resource-use context

The event should be recorded with sufficient context to distinguish what happened to the Resource.

10. End-of-Use Event Classification

ERRM should distinguish at minimum:

Planned End-of-Use

Unplanned End-of-Use

Temporary Use Interruption

Permanent Exit From Original Use

Partial Functional Exit

Full Functional Exit

Transformation Into Another Resource State

End-of-Use Unknown

These classifications must not automatically determine Recovery potential.

11. Temporary Interruption

A Resource may temporarily cease operation without reaching the end of its original use.

For example, an industrial machine may be taken offline for maintenance.

Temporary Non-Use ≠ End-of-Use by definition.

ERRM should preserve this distinction.

12. Functional End-of-Use

Functional End-of-Use exists where the Resource no longer satisfies the applicable requirements of its original function.

This may result from degradation, damage, changed operational requirements, or replacement.

The Resource may nevertheless retain other usable properties.

Functional End-of-Use ≠ Resource End-of-Life.

13. Partial Functional End-of-Use

A Resource may lose only part of its original functional capability.

For example, a battery may no longer satisfy high-power vehicle requirements while retaining lower-demand storage capability.

ERRM records the loss or change of the original function.

It does not establish that a second-life application is technically suitable.

14. Original Resource Identity

End-of-use recognition should preserve the identity of the original Resource where available.

Minimum references may include:

Resource ID

Resource Type

Resource Source

Original Function

Original Operational Context

Original Resource State

Prior Resource-Use Reference

This supports traceability between the original Resource and its post-use condition.

15. Resource Identity Continuity

A Resource should not lose its traceable origin merely because its operational classification changes.

Change of Resource State ≠ Loss of Resource Identity.

Where transformation prevents direct identity continuity, ERRM should preserve a traceable origin relationship.

16. Residual Resource

A Residual Resource is an identifiable Resource, Resource component, Resource capacity, or Resource stream remaining after a defined use, transformation, or operational process.

A Residual Resource may be physically present, measurable, or evidenced through an applicable Resource accounting method.

Residual status does not establish recoverability.

Residual Resource ≠ Recoverable Resource.

17. Residual Resource Types

ERRM may recognize:

Residual Material

Residual Water

Residual Energy

Residual Component

Residual Functional Capacity

Residual Chemical Resource

Residual Process Output

Residual Mixed Resource Stream

Other Resource-Specific Residual

Each classification must preserve the actual Resource characteristics.

18. Residual Material

Residual Material may include:

Manufacturing scrap. Used structural materials. Discarded components. Process by-products. Material remaining after dismantling. Material remaining after transformation.

ERRM establishes its identity, origin, quantity, condition, and relevant composition.

19. Residual Water

Residual Water may include:

Used process water. Cooling-system discharge. Treated or untreated wastewater. Condensate. Water remaining after a defined operational use.

ERRM records the actual water stream and relevant quality evidence.

It does not establish that the water is suitable for reuse.

20. Residual Energy

Residual Energy may include:

Waste heat. Residual thermal energy. Unused recoverable process energy. Other measurable post-use energy streams.

Recognition should distinguish the physical energy stream from its practical recoverability.

Residual Energy Existence ≠ Usable Energy Recovery.

21. Residual Components

A component may exit its original assembly or application while remaining physically identifiable.

ERRM should preserve:

component identity; origin; prior function; condition; damage; and relevant operational history.

The suitability of repair, refurbishment, or reuse belongs to subsequent Recovery assessment.

22. Residual Functional Capacity

Some Resources retain partial capability after the original operational requirement is no longer satisfied.

ERRM may record measurable remaining capability.

Examples include:

remaining battery capacity; reduced mechanical performance; remaining storage capability; or residual processing capacity.

The presence of capability does not establish suitability for another use.

23. Residual Resource Boundary

Every material Residual Resource should have an identifiable boundary.

The boundary establishes what belongs to the recognized Resource state.

It may include:

physical extent; process origin; Resource composition; quantity; location; time; condition; and identifiable components.

Without a defined boundary, Resource quantities may be duplicated, omitted, or incorrectly attributed.

24. Boundary Principle

Unbounded Residual Material ≠ Reliably Identified Residual Resource.

Where boundaries are uncertain, ERRM should preserve the uncertainty rather than invent precise Resource quantities.

25. Resource Fragmentation

An original Resource may separate into multiple identifiable residual Resources.

Example:

Original Industrial Equipment → Metal Components + Electronic Components + Fluid Residuals

Each resulting Resource may require a distinct identity and condition record.

26. Fragmentation Principle

One Original Resource ≠ One Residual Resource by definition.

ERRM should preserve parent-child Resource relationships.

27. Resource Aggregation

Multiple Resources may enter a shared residual stream.

Examples include:

mixed industrial scrap; combined wastewater; collected electronic components; mixed demolition material.

ERRM should preserve known origins and composition limits where material.

28. Aggregation Principle

One Residual Stream ≠ One Homogeneous Resource.

Mixed streams must not be assigned unsupported uniform characteristics.

29. Resource Transformation

A Resource may be transformed during use into a materially different Resource state.

For example:

Fuel → Heat + Emissions + Residual Material

Process Water → Wastewater Stream

Raw Material → Product + Scrap + Process Residuals

ERRM recognizes identifiable residual outputs.

It does not treat all outputs as equivalent or recoverable.

30. Resource Conservation Boundary

Where applicable, ERRM should preserve a consistent physical accounting boundary between Resource inputs, outputs, transformations, and residuals.

This does not imply that all inputs remain recoverable in their original form.

Physical Conservation ≠ Functional Recoverability.

31. Residual Quantity

Residual quantity should be recorded using an appropriate Resource-specific measurement.

Examples include:

kilograms; tonnes; cubic metres; litres; kilowatt-hours; component counts; or other valid units.

Measurement units must remain explicit.

32. Quantity Principle

Residual Quantity ≠ Residual Quality.

A large residual quantity may have limited usable potential.

A small residual quantity may contain a highly significant Resource component.

33. Residual Quality

Residual quality concerns the measurable properties relevant to the post-use Resource condition.

Examples include:

purity; contamination; structural integrity; chemical composition; functional performance; thermal characteristics; or other Resource-specific properties.

ERRM records available quality evidence without declaring future-use suitability.

34. Quality Boundary

Known Residual Quality ≠ Validated Recovery Output Quality.

The latter belongs to recovered Resource validation.

35. Residual Condition

ERRM should preserve relevant physical, functional, chemical, thermal, or operational conditions.

Possible states include:

Intact

Degraded

Damaged

Transformed

Contaminated

Mixed

Partially Functional

Condition Unknown

These states may coexist where they describe different dimensions.

36. Residual Contamination

Contamination may affect future Recovery potential.

ERRM should record observed or measured contamination where relevant.

However:

Contamination Identified ≠ Recovery Infeasible by definition.

Recovery feasibility belongs to subsequent assessment.

37. Resource Origin

Residual Resource origin should remain traceable where material.

Relevant origin information may include:

Source; production process; operational system; prior Resource use; geographic location; and transformation history.

38. Origin Principle

Residual Resource Identity Without Origin Evidence ≠ Complete Resource Traceability.

Where origin is unknown, the record should explicitly preserve that limitation.

39. Resource Location

Residual Resources may exist in different locations from their original use.

ERRM should preserve the actual location of the residual Resource where relevant.

Location may affect later Recovery assessment but does not itself establish recoverability.

40. Temporal Context

ERRM should preserve:

end-of-use time; recognition time; observation time; measurement time; and relevant storage duration.

Temporal differences may affect Resource condition.

41. Temporal Principle

Condition at End-of-Use ≠ Condition at Recovery Assessment by definition.

Resource condition may change during storage, transport, or delay.

42. Resource Custody

Where applicable, ERRM may preserve references to:

Resource owner; custodian; operator; transfer record; storage responsibility; or other relevant custody evidence.

ERRM does not determine ownership rights or liability.

43. Custody Boundary

Resource Custody Evidence ≠ Ownership Determination.

Ownership Determination ≠ Recovery Authorization.

44. Resource Availability Boundary

A recognized residual Resource may not be available for Recovery.

It may be inaccessible, reserved, restricted, dispersed, or located outside applicable operational reach.

Recognized Residual Resource ≠ Available Recovery Input.

RACS and RAGS govern their respective availability and Access conditions.

45. Resource Demand Boundary

The existence of Demand for a residual Resource does not establish its condition or recoverability.

Demand for Residual Resource ≠ Recovery Feasibility.

RDGS governs Resource Demand.

46. Scarcity Boundary

Scarcity may increase the significance of residual Resources.

However:

Scarcity ≠ Evidence of Recoverability.

RSCS governs Scarcity and Constraint states.

47. Value Boundary

A residual Resource may have economic, operational, or strategic significance.

ERRM does not determine that significance as validated Value.

Market Value ≠ Residual Resource Condition.

VEGA™ governs relevant Value relationships.

48. Recovery Assessment Boundary

ERRM provides evidence for subsequent Recovery assessment.

It does not determine:

technical recoverability; operational feasibility; Recovery eligibility; Recovery pathway; Recovery authorization; or expected Recovery yield.

These belong to subsequent RRCS governance mechanisms.

49. Recognition Evidence

ERRM should preserve the evidence supporting recognition.

Evidence may include:

operational records; Resource inventories; process measurements; inspection reports; sensor readings; laboratory results; maintenance records; component identification; or other relevant evidence.

50. Evidence Provenance

Evidence should preserve sufficient provenance to identify:

source; method; time; location; Resource reference; and version.

The evidence must remain distinguishable from the methodology used to evaluate it.

Evidence Content ≠ Methodology Instruction.

51. Evidence Sufficiency

ERRM should determine whether available evidence is sufficient to establish the proposed recognition state.

Recognition should not be treated as complete where materially relevant Resource identity or condition remains unsupported.

52. Evidence-Limited Recognition

Where evidence is insufficient, ERRM should preserve an Evidence-Limited state.

It may identify additional evidence required for recognition.

Missing Resource Evidence ≠ Evidence of Resource Absence.

53. Conflicted Recognition

Where material evidence supports incompatible Resource identities, quantities, or conditions, ERRM should preserve the conflict.

Conflicting Evidence ≠ Permission to Select a Convenient Resource State.

54. Recognition Uncertainty

ERRM should preserve material uncertainty concerning:

identity; quantity; composition; condition; origin; location; and temporal state.

Uncertainty should not be silently converted into precise Resource classifications.

58. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum operational ERRM implementation should contain seven governed layers.

Layer 1 — Original Resource

Establish the Resource identity, type, Source, original function, and prior operational state.

Layer 2 — End-of-Use Event

Identify whether the original use ended, was interrupted, partially ceased, or materially changed.

Layer 3 — Post-Use Observation

Determine what Resource, component, capacity, or residual stream remains.

Layer 4 — Resource Boundary

Establish the relevant residual Resource identity, quantity, composition, condition, location, and time.

Layer 5 — Recognition Classification

Classify the post-use Resource state without determining recoverability.

Layer 6 — Evidence Validation

Evaluate evidence sufficiency, provenance, uncertainty, and conflict.

Layer 7 — Governed Output

Produce the End-of-Use Resource Recognition Profile .

61. Minimum Reassessment Framework

At minimum:

Current Recognition Profile → New Resource / Condition / Quantity / Composition / Location / Time Evidence → Materiality Test → Recognition-State Reassessment → Residual Resource Boundary Review → Evidence Sufficiency Review → Profile Update → Version Update

Reassessment may be required where:

New residual components are discovered. A mixed Resource stream is separated. Previously unknown composition becomes measurable. Resource condition changes during storage. Quantity measurements are corrected. New contamination evidence appears. Resource origin or custody evidence changes. Earlier recognition is shown to be incomplete or incorrect.

66. Use Case 1 — AI Data Center Hardware End-of-Use

An AI data center retires a group of accelerator servers.

The servers no longer satisfy the operational requirements of their original high-performance AI workload.

ERRM establishes:

Original Resource: Accelerator Server Hardware

End-of-Use Event: Planned Retirement

Original Function: High-Performance AI Compute

Post-Use Condition: Mixed

Residual Resources Identified:

Accelerator cards. Memory modules. Power supply units.

Cooling components. Structural metal. Electronic circuit boards.

ERRM records component identities, quantities, physical conditions, and known operational histories.

Some components may retain functional capability.

Others may be damaged or degraded.

ERRM does not declare the hardware reusable, recyclable, or economically recoverable.

It establishes the evidence required for subsequent Recovery assessment.

Hardware Retirement ≠ Resource End-of-Life.

67. Use Case 2 — Industrial Process Water End-of-Use

An industrial facility uses water in a manufacturing process.

After the process, the water exits the original operational function as a wastewater stream.

ERRM establishes:

Original Resource: Industrial Process Water

End-of-Use Event: Process Discharge

Residual Resource: Used Process Water

Residual Quantity: Measured Discharge Volume

Residual Condition: Changed Chemical and Physical Quality

Residual Location: Industrial Wastewater Collection System

Residual Composition: Based on available measurement evidence

ERRM identifies the wastewater stream and records its quantity, condition, origin, and relevant quality characteristics.

It does not declare the water reusable.

It does not authorize treatment or reintegration.

It establishes the governed residual Resource record needed for subsequent Recovery assessment.

Used Water ≠ Recoverable Water by definition.

69. Cross-Architecture Mechanism Principle

An operational mechanism may produce inputs for multiple governance architectures, but it belongs only to the architecture whose governed domain includes that mechanism itself. Other architectures govern only its domain- specific relevance or consequences.

ERRM governs the recognition of the post-use Resource state.

It does not govern the technical Recovery mechanism or the downstream consequences of Resource Recovery.

70. Canonical Boundary

ERRM governs how Resources, Resource components, Resource capacities, and residual Resource streams are identified, distinguished, bounded, classified, evidenced, and recorded when they complete, exit, interrupt, or materially change their original operational use.

It does not determine technical recoverability, operational Recovery feasibility, Recovery eligibility, Recovery authorization, Recovery process execution, Recovery yield, recovered Resource validation, secondary Resource classification, reintegration, Circularity performance, or long-term Sustainability.

Its principal governed output is the End-of-Use Resource Recognition Profile .

71. Canonical Closing Statement

A Resource does not cease to matter when its original function ends. What remains must first be identified, bounded, measured, and evidenced before Recovery potential can be meaningfully assessed.

ERRM establishes that first governance boundary — preserving Resource identity and post-use reality without confusing the existence of a residual Resource with proof that it can be recovered, reused, or legitimately returned to operation.
