1. Canonical Definition

RRWM — Resource Rights & Access Withdrawal Module governs how an established Resource right, entitlement, authorization, or active Access State is legitimately restricted, suspended, withdrawn, revoked, terminated, or allowed to expire, including the authority, grounds, scope, timing, conditions, evidence, operational propagation, restoration possibilities, and finality associated with that change.

RRWM governs the decision that legitimate Resource Access or the underlying Resource right must cease or become narrower.

It does not treat the technical disabling of Access as sufficient evidence that legitimate authority to withdraw Access existed.

Technical Access Removal ≠ Legitimate Access Withdrawal.

2. Operational Role

Resource Access Governance must govern not only how Access begins, but also how it changes or ends.

RAEM establishes eligibility.

RRGM establishes relevant Resource rights or entitlements.

RACM translates governed Access into enforceable controls.

RRWM governs when and how an existing right, authorization, or Access State may legitimately be:

restricted; suspended; withdrawn; revoked; terminated; expired; or restored where applicable.

It establishes the governance decision.

RACM may subsequently enforce that decision through Resource Access Controls.

3. Operational Question

Under what legitimate authority, grounds, conditions, scope, and timing may an established Resource right, entitlement, authorization, or active Access State be restricted, suspended, withdrawn, revoked, terminated, or allowed to expire, what evidence supports that change, and what conditions govern restoration or finality?

4. Position in RAGS Governance

Canonical relationship:

Established Resource Right / Authorization / Access State → Withdrawal Trigger → Authority Validation → Withdrawal Basis → Scope & Proportionality → Withdrawal Decision → Effective State Change → Control Propagation → Evidence → Restoration or Finality

RRWM governs the legitimacy and state transition.

RACM governs operational enforcement where technical or physical controls are required.

5. Governed Space

RRWM governs:

Resource Rights & Access Withdrawal Governance

This space concerns the legitimate reduction or termination of previously established Resource Access positions.

6. Governed Object

The primary Governed Object is:

A governed change that reduces, suspends, withdraws, revokes, terminates, or closes an established Resource right, entitlement, authorization, or Access State.

Canonical relationship:

**Existing Access Position

Withdrawal Trigger Competent Authority Legitimate Basis Scope Evidence → Withdrawal State**

7. Scope

RRWM governs:

withdrawal triggers; withdrawal authority; withdrawal basis; rights restriction; Access restriction; rights suspension; Access suspension; authorization withdrawal; authorization revocation; rights termination; Access termination; expiration; partial withdrawal; full withdrawal; temporary withdrawal; permanent withdrawal; conditional withdrawal; emergency withdrawal; withdrawal scope; withdrawal proportionality; effective time; notice where applicable; withdrawal evidence; withdrawal conflict; withdrawal uncertainty; restoration conditions; reinstatement; withdrawal finality;

withdrawal traceability; reassessment.

RRWM does not independently:

establish initial Resource Access eligibility; create Resource rights; create Access Authority; determine Resource Priority; allocate Resource capacity; implement technical Access Controls; determine Resource Consumption; govern Scarcity response as a whole; establish long-term Sustainability.

8. Withdrawal Principle

A previously legitimate Resource Access position should not be treated as permanent unless its governing basis establishes permanence.

At the same time, Access should not be removed merely because a system is technically capable of removing it.

Withdrawal requires a legitimate governance basis.

9. Withdrawal Object

Withdrawal may apply to:

Resource right; Resource entitlement; Resource Access authorization; active Resource Access; part of an Access scope; specific Resource Source; defined quantity or capacity; defined purpose; defined location; defined time period.

10. Withdrawal Trigger

A Withdrawal Trigger is an event or condition capable of initiating reassessment of an existing Resource Access position.

A trigger does not itself prove that withdrawal is justified.

Withdrawal Trigger ≠ Withdrawal Decision.

11. Possible Withdrawal Triggers

Triggers may include:

eligibility loss; authorization expiration; rights expiration; contract termination; permit termination; material condition breach; technical non-compliance; safety condition;

authority change; Resource Source change; emergency condition; fraud or invalid evidence where established; transfer of rights; completion of authorized purpose; other legitimate governance condition.

12. Trigger Validation

RRWM should determine whether the claimed trigger:

actually occurred; applies to the relevant actor; applies to the relevant Resource; falls within the relevant governing basis; is sufficiently evidenced.

13. Withdrawal Authority

Withdrawal must originate from or be exercised under competent authority.

The authority capable of granting Access is not necessarily identical to the authority capable of terminating every associated right.

14. Authority Traceability

Where material:

Authority: Origin → Withdrawal Authority → Withdrawal Decision → Resulting Resource State

should remain traceable.

15. Authority Scope

A withdrawal authority may be limited by:

Resource; actor; rights type; authorization type; quantity; location; time; cause; jurisdiction; other governing conditions.

16. Authority Boundary

An actor should not withdraw broader Resource rights or Access than its legitimate authority permits.

Withdrawal Capability ≠ Withdrawal Authority.

17. Withdrawal Basis

The Withdrawal Basis explains why the existing Access position may legitimately be changed.

Possible bases include:

expiration; condition failure; rights termination; authorization revocation; contractual basis; regulatory basis; safety basis; emergency basis; transfer; purpose completion; other competent governance basis.

18. Basis Sufficiency

The existence of an undesirable operational outcome does not automatically create a legitimate Withdrawal Basis.

19. Rights Restriction

A Resource right may remain valid while its permitted exercise is narrowed.

Restriction should remain distinguishable from termination.

20. Access Restriction

Resource Access may continue under reduced:

quantity; capacity; rate; purpose; time; location; Resource Source; operational scope.

21. Restriction vs Withdrawal

Access Restriction ≠ Full Access Withdrawal.

A restricted Access State preserves some legitimate Access.

22. Suspension

Suspension temporarily prevents exercise of an existing Resource right or Access authorization.

The underlying relationship may remain capable of restoration.

23. Suspension Principle

Suspension should preserve:

authority; reason; scope; effective time; conditions for continuation; conditions for restoration; evidence.

24. Suspension vs Revocation

Suspension ≠ Revocation.

Suspension is potentially reversible without establishing a completely new Resource Access relationship.

Revocation terminates the relevant authorization unless legitimately re-established.

25. Authorization Withdrawal

An Access authorization may be withdrawn where its governing basis permits.

Withdrawal should identify the exact authorization affected.

26. Revocation

Revocation terminates a previously granted Resource Access authorization.

It should preserve:

revoking authority; revocation basis; Resource; actor; scope; effective time; evidence.

27. Rights Termination

Where the underlying Resource right itself terminates, RRWM should distinguish that event from mere Access suspension.

Right Terminated ≠ Access Temporarily Suspended.

28. Expiration

Some Resource rights or Access authorizations end automatically at a defined time or condition.

Expiration does not require the same decision mechanism as discretionary revocation where the governing basis already establishes the endpoint.

29. Expiration Verification

RRWM should still establish that the relevant expiration condition has occurred.

Scheduled Expiration ≠ Verified Expiration State.

30. Purpose Completion

Resource Access granted for a defined purpose may terminate when that purpose is completed.

Purpose completion should be sufficiently established where it triggers Access termination.

31. Eligibility Loss

Loss of eligibility may trigger Access reassessment.

It does not necessarily produce identical consequences in every Resource context.

The governing Access relationship determines the appropriate response.

32. Eligibility Loss Boundary

Eligibility Loss ≠ Automatic Rights Termination in every context.

A Resource right may survive temporary loss of an operational eligibility condition.

33. Partial Withdrawal

Withdrawal may affect only part of an Access position.

Examples:

10 MW → 5 MW

continuous Access → emergency-only Access

all Resource Sources → Source A only

multiple purposes → essential purpose only

34. Partial Withdrawal Integrity

The remaining valid Resource Access must remain explicitly distinguishable from the withdrawn portion.

35. Full Withdrawal

Full withdrawal removes the relevant Resource Access authorization or exercisable Resource right within the defined governed relationship.

36. Temporary Withdrawal

Temporary withdrawal has a defined or condition-dependent restoration pathway.

37. Permanent Withdrawal

Permanent withdrawal establishes finality for the relevant Resource Access relationship unless a new right or authorization is subsequently created.

38. Conditional Withdrawal

Access may remain withdrawn until specified conditions are satisfied.

The conditions should be explicit and assessable.

39. Emergency Withdrawal

Emergency conditions may justify accelerated or immediate Resource Access withdrawal where legitimate authority permits.

Emergency action does not eliminate governance requirements.

40. Emergency Boundary

Emergency Withdrawal ≠ Unlimited Withdrawal Authority.

The action must remain within the relevant emergency authority and scope.

41. Withdrawal Scope

Every withdrawal should identify what is being changed.

Scope may include:

entire Resource Access relationship; specific Resource; specific Resource Source; defined capacity; defined quantity; defined rate; purpose; location; time; function.

42. Scope Minimality

Where legitimate governance requires only partial restriction, broader withdrawal should not be assumed automatically.

43. Withdrawal Proportionality

Where multiple legitimate withdrawal responses are available, the chosen response should correspond to the governing basis, severity, context, and required Resource protection.

RRWM does not impose a universal proportionality formula.

44. Proportionality Boundary

Proportionality cannot override mandatory withdrawal where competent governance requires full termination.

45. Effective Time

Every material withdrawal decision should establish when the resulting state becomes effective.

46. Immediate Effect

Some withdrawal decisions may require immediate effect.

The reason and authority should remain explicit.

47. Deferred Effect

Withdrawal may become effective at a future time.

Until that time, the current Access State should remain correctly represented.

48. Notice

Where applicable, withdrawal may require notice before becoming effective.

RRWM may preserve:

notice requirement; notice time; recipient; evidence of notice.

It does not impose universal legal notice requirements.

49. Grace Period

Where legitimate governance permits, a withdrawal may include a defined transition or grace period.

A grace period should not be confused with continued permanent entitlement.

50. Dependency Context

Withdrawal may affect a Resource upon which critical functions depend.

RDCS Criticality evidence may therefore be relevant to withdrawal consequences.

Criticality does not itself prevent legitimate withdrawal.

51. Criticality Boundary

High Resource Criticality ≠ Permanent Access Immunity.

Where Access must be withdrawn, Criticality may instead trigger continuity, substitution, emergency, or resilience mechanisms elsewhere in RSGA™.

52. Demand Boundary

Continued Resource Demand does not invalidate an otherwise legitimate withdrawal.

Continued Demand ≠ Continued Access Authority.

53. Value Boundary

Economic or operational Value generated through Resource Access does not independently prevent withdrawal.

High Value ≠ Permanent Resource Access.

54. Historical Use Boundary

Long-term historical Access does not prevent legitimate withdrawal where the underlying right or authorization ends.

Historical Access ≠ Withdrawal Immunity.

55. Ownership Boundary

Ownership may affect withdrawal authority or rights.

It does not universally prevent Resource Access restrictions arising from legitimate external governance.

56. Scarcity Boundary

Resource Scarcity may trigger reassessment or restriction mechanisms.

RRWM governs the withdrawal decision where Access is actually restricted, suspended, or terminated.

RSCS governs the broader Scarcity & Constraint state and response.

57. Priority Boundary

A lower Resource Priority does not automatically terminate an underlying Access right.

Priority and withdrawal remain distinct.

Lower Priority ≠ Access Revocation.

58. Allocation Boundary

Receiving zero Allocation during a constrained period does not necessarily mean Access authorization or Resource rights have been revoked.

Zero Allocation ≠ Access Withdrawal.

59. Consumption Boundary

Stopping Resource Consumption does not establish that Access has been withdrawn.

No Consumption ≠ Access Revoked.

60. Technical Disconnection

Physical or technical disconnection may enforce withdrawal.

It does not prove that withdrawal was legitimate.

Technical Disconnection ≠ Withdrawal Authority.

61. Control Propagation

Once a legitimate withdrawal decision becomes effective, relevant Resource Access Controls should be updated accordingly.

Canonical relationship:

Withdrawal Decision → Updated Governed Access State → RACM Control Update → Operational Enforcement

62. Propagation Timing

The required time for enforcement should reflect:

withdrawal urgency; Resource context; Criticality; safety; technical capability; operational consequences.

63. Withdrawal Enforcement Failure

Where the governed Access State has been withdrawn but technical Access remains possible, an enforcement failure exists.

64. Withdrawal Drift

Withdrawal Drift exists where:

governed withdrawal state

and:

actual or technically permitted Resource Access

diverge materially.

65. Example of Withdrawal Drift

Access Authorization — Revoked

Control State — Active

Actual Resource Access — Continuing

This represents material Withdrawal Drift.

66. Restoration

Some withdrawal states may be reversible.

Restoration should occur only where the governing conditions for restoration are satisfied.

67. Restoration Conditions

Conditions may include:

eligibility restored; compliance restored; temporary restriction ended; safety condition resolved; emergency ended; required evidence supplied; competent authority approval.

68. Restoration Authority

Technical ability to restore Access does not establish authority to restore it.

Restoration Capability ≠ Restoration Authority.

69. Reinstatement

Reinstatement restores a previously suspended or withdrawn Access position where the governing basis permits restoration.

70. Reinstatement vs New Authorization

Where the previous authorization has been fully terminated, future Access may require a new authorization rather than reinstatement.

Reinstatement ≠ New Authorization.

71. Withdrawal Finality

Withdrawal Finality establishes when the relevant withdrawal decision is complete and no longer pending operational propagation, review, restoration condition, or unresolved evidence.

72. Final Withdrawal

A final withdrawal should establish:

affected actor; Resource; affected right or authorization; final scope; effective time; authority; evidence; resulting Access State.

73. Closed Access Relationship

Where Access is permanently terminated, the relationship may become:

Closed

while remaining historically traceable.

74. Historical Preservation

Closed, revoked, expired, or terminated Resource Access relationships should not disappear from governance history where traceability is required.

75. Withdrawal Evidence

Evidence may include:

expiration records; revocation decisions; suspension decisions; termination notices; contract records; permit records; eligibility changes; compliance evidence; technical evidence; emergency orders; control propagation evidence.

76. Evidence Provenance

Material withdrawal evidence should preserve where relevant:

source; authority; actor; Resource; decision; scope; effective time; version.

77. Evidence Sufficiency

Evidence is sufficient when it supports the withdrawal determination required for the relevant governance purpose.

78. Withdrawal Conflict

Conflict may arise where:

different authorities issue incompatible decisions; rights evidence conflicts with revocation evidence; expiration is disputed; scope is disputed; restoration status is disputed; technical state conflicts with governance state.

79. Withdrawal Uncertainty

Uncertainty may concern:

authority; trigger;

basis; scope; effective time; rights continuity; restoration conditions; enforcement status.

80. Uncertainty Principle

Material uncertainty must remain visible.

Uncertain Withdrawal ≠ Final Withdrawal.

83. Withdrawal Not Required

Available evidence does not establish a current requirement or legitimate basis for withdrawal.

84. Withdrawal Under Review

A potential trigger exists and is being evaluated.

85. Access Restricted

Access remains valid but within reduced scope.

86. Access Suspended

Exercise of Access is temporarily prohibited.

87. Access Partially Withdrawn

Part of the existing Access position has been removed.

88. Access Revoked

The relevant Access authorization has been terminated.

89. Right Terminated

The underlying Resource right has ended.

90. Access Expired

The governed Access period has ended according to its existing conditions.

91. Restoration Pending

The Access position may be restorable, but restoration requirements are not yet fully satisfied or confirmed.

92. Access Reinstated

A previously suspended or legitimately restorable Access position has been restored.

93. Withdrawal Evidence-Limited

Evidence does not support a stronger withdrawal determination.

94. Withdrawal Conflicted

Credible evidence supports incompatible withdrawal states.

95. Withdrawal Unknown

Available evidence cannot establish the relevant withdrawal status.

96. Withdrawal Unresolved

Relevant evidence exists, but final determination remains unresolved.

97. Withdrawal Final

The governed withdrawal is complete, effective, sufficiently evidenced, and no longer pending implementation or required resolution.

98. State Separation

RRWM should preserve the distinction between:

Rights State

Access State

Withdrawal State

Control State

These may change at different times.

99. Example State Combination

A valid combination may be:

Resource Right — Established

Access Authorization — Suspended

Withdrawal State — Access Suspended

Control State — Suspended

The underlying right survives while its exercise is temporarily prevented.

100. Another State Combination

Another valid combination may be:

Resource Right — Terminated

Access Authorization — Revoked

Withdrawal State — Withdrawal Final

Control State — Revoked

This represents completed Access finality.

101. Withdrawal Traceability

A withdrawal should remain traceable through:

Resulting Access State → Withdrawal Decision → Withdrawal Basis → Withdrawal Trigger → Withdrawal Authority → Existing Right / Authorization

→ Resource & Actor → Evidence.

102. Withdrawal Explainability

A governed system should be able to explain materially:

what Access changed; whose Access changed; which Resource is affected; why it changed; who had authority to change it; when the change became effective; whether the change is temporary or final; what restoration conditions exist.

103. Withdrawal Auditability

Where required, historical state changes should remain reconstructable across:

restriction; suspension; partial withdrawal; revocation; termination; expiration; restoration; reinstatement; finality.

104. Withdrawal Versioning

Material withdrawal decisions should create updated governance versions where required for traceability.

105. Reassessment

Withdrawal should be reassessed where:

new evidence appears; authority changes; withdrawal basis changes; conditions are restored; conflict is resolved; technical enforcement fails; restoration conditions become satisfied.

106. Targeted Reassessment

Reassessment should focus on the dimensions capable of changing the current Withdrawal State.

116. Failure Conditions

RRWM should not be considered sufficiently completed where:

technical disconnection is treated as proof of legitimate withdrawal; Withdrawal Authority is missing; Authority Scope is exceeded; Withdrawal Basis is unsupported; a trigger is treated automatically as a withdrawal decision; restriction is confused with termination; suspension is confused with revocation; rights termination is confused with temporary Access suspension; partial withdrawal is represented as full withdrawal; expired Access is represented as active; continued Demand is treated as continued Access Authority; Criticality is treated as permanent Access immunity; lower Priority is treated automatically as Access revocation; zero Allocation is treated as Access withdrawal; non-consumption is treated as Access revocation; emergency authority is treated as unlimited authority; restoration occurs without legitimate authority; technical Access continues after effective withdrawal without being identified as drift; material conflict is hidden; material uncertainty is suppressed; historical withdrawal evidence is represented as current state without validation; finality is declared while required implementation or resolution remains incomplete.

117. Use Case 1 — AI Data Center Compute Access Suspension

An AI workload has:

valid eligibility; an established compute Access authorization; active GPU Access Controls.

A material security compliance condition required by the authorization is no longer satisfied.

RRWM identifies:

Withdrawal Trigger — Required compliance condition lost

Withdrawal Authority — Competent facility authority

Withdrawal Basis — Conditional authorization requirement

Withdrawal Scope — Defined workload GPU Access

The underlying contractual Resource right remains valid.

Therefore RRWM determines:

Access Suspended

rather than:

Right Terminated.

RACM receives the updated Access State and suspends the relevant compute controls.

Once compliance is restored and sufficiently evidenced, RRWM evaluates restoration conditions.

If satisfied:

Access Reinstated.

The module preserves the distinction between temporary suspension of Access and termination of the underlying Resource right.

118. Use Case 2 — Regional Water Access Partial Withdrawal

An industrial facility possesses a valid Resource right and Access authorization for water withdrawal within defined limits.

A governing condition permits temporary restriction during a specified infrastructure safety event.

RRWM validates:

event occurrence; competent authority; restriction basis; affected Resource Source; required scope; effective period.

The legitimate response requires reducing authorized withdrawal rather than terminating the entire Resource right.

RRWM determines:

Access Partially Withdrawn

with:

reduced flow limit; temporary duration; defined restoration condition.

RACM implements the resulting flow restriction.

The facility's underlying Resource right remains established.

When the safety condition ends, RRWM reassesses the withdrawal and may establish:

Access Reinstated.

120. Cross-Architecture Boundary

RRWM may consume:

RAEM — Eligibility State and evidence.

RRGM — Resource Rights Profile and Rights State.

RACM — Resource Access Control State and enforcement evidence.

RACS — Resource Availability context.

RDGS — Resource Demand context.

RDCS — Resource Dependency and Criticality context.

RPAS — Priority and Allocation evidence where relevant without converting Allocation changes into withdrawal automatically.

RSCS — Scarcity and Constraint states that may trigger legitimate Access restrictions.

GOA™ — Authority, Delegation, and governance-boundary evidence.

OBIDENITY® — Identity, Ownership, Authority, Permission, Transfer, and Continuity evidence.

ORA™ — evidence of actual continued or terminated Resource Access.

HAGA™ — Harm relevance where withdrawal or continued Access creates Harm consequences.

LIGA™ — Liability relevance where withdrawal decisions create liability questions.

RRWM does not absorb these mechanisms.

121. Cross-Architecture Mechanism Principle

An operational mechanism may produce inputs for multiple governance architectures, but it belongs only to the architecture whose governed domain includes that mechanism itself. Other architectures govern only its domain- specific relevance or consequences.

Scarcity may trigger Access reassessment.

Criticality may affect the consequences of withdrawal.

Authority: determines who may make the withdrawal decision.

RACM enforces the resulting Access State.

ORA™ may establish whether withdrawal occurred operationally.

RRWM governs the legitimacy, scope, state transition, restoration, and finality of the Resource Access withdrawal itself.

122. Canonical Boundary

RRWM governs how an existing Resource right, entitlement, authorization, or active Access State may legitimately be restricted, suspended, partially or fully withdrawn, revoked, terminated, expired, restored, reinstated, or brought to finality, including the applicable trigger, authority, basis, scope, timing, evidence, uncertainty, operational propagation, and restoration conditions.

RRWM does not establish initial Resource Access eligibility, create Resource rights or Access Authority, implement technical Access Controls, determine Resource Priority, allocate Resource capacity, govern Resource Consumption, determine Scarcity Governance as a whole, or establish long-term Resource Sustainability.

Its principal governed output is the Resource Access Withdrawal Profile .

123. Canonical Closing Statement

Legitimate Resource Access must be governable not only when it begins, but also when its conditions no longer justify its continuation.

RRWM governs that boundary — determining when Resource rights or Access may be restricted, suspended, withdrawn, terminated, restored, or brought to finality, while ensuring that technical disconnection never substitutes for legitimate withdrawal authority.
