1. Canonical Definition

RSCM — Resource Substitution Compatibility Module is the Core Module within RSFS that governs how identified alternative Resources, Resource capacities, Resource configurations, and operational pathways are evaluated against defined original Resource requirements to establish their functional compatibility, technical suitability, conditional equivalence, partial equivalence, incompatibility, and evidence-based compatibility boundaries before operational substitution decisions are made.

RSCM establishes whether an alternative is capable of satisfying the defined characteristics and functions of an original Resource requirement under specified conditions.

It distinguishes the existence of a candidate alternative from its validated compatibility.

It also distinguishes compatibility from actual availability, operational feasibility, authorization, activation, and effectiveness.

Candidate Alternative ≠ Compatible Substitute.

Functional Similarity ≠ Functional Equivalence.

Compatibility: Established ≠ Substitution Authorized.

2. Operational Role

RSCM governs the compatibility validation stage of Resource Substitution & Flexibility Governance.

Its role is to determine whether an alternative Resource or Resource configuration can satisfy the defined functional and technical requirements associated with the original Resource requirement.

The module establishes:

Compatibility: assessment identity. Original Resource requirement references. Candidate alternative identity. Functional compatibility criteria. Technical compatibility criteria. Quality requirements. Performance requirements. Interface requirements. Capacity compatibility conditions. Reliability compatibility conditions. Environmental and operating conditions. Compatibility limitations. Conditional compatibility. Partial equivalence. Incompatibility. Evidence sufficiency. Compatibility uncertainty. Conflicting compatibility evidence. Compatibility decision states. Compatibility reassessment. Handoff to operational transition and substitution governance.

RSCM does not grant operational permission to substitute Resources.

It does not independently determine Resource Access, Allocation, operational readiness, transition execution, or actual substitution effectiveness.

3. Operational Question

Under which defined functional, technical, quality, performance, reliability, capacity, interface, and operating conditions can an identified alternative Resource or configuration satisfy an original Resource requirement, and how should full, conditional, partial, unsupported, or failed compatibility be established without confusing compatibility with operational feasibility, authorization, or effective substitution?

5. Governed Space

Resource Substitution Compatibility & Equivalence Governance

This governed space concerns the evaluation and validation of whether a proposed alternative Resource or configuration can satisfy the defined functional and technical requirements of another Resource requirement.

It includes compatibility criteria, technical interfaces, performance conditions, quality thresholds, capacity suitability, reliability requirements, operating limitations, equivalence classifications, and compatibility evidence.

It does not independently govern operational activation, Resource Access, Allocation, or actual substitution execution.

6. Governed Object

The primary Governed Object is:

A Resource Substitution Compatibility Case connecting a defined original Resource requirement with an identified alternative Resource or configuration through explicit compatibility criteria, technical and functional evidence, equivalence conditions, limitations, and a governed compatibility state.

8. Foundational Governance Principles

8.1 Requirement-Relative Compatibility

Resource Compatibility Must Be Established Against a Defined Requirement.

An alternative may be compatible with one operational requirement and incompatible with another.

8.2 Functional Similarity Separation

Functional Similarity ≠ Functional Equivalence.

8.3 Technical Capability Separation

Technical Capability ≠ Operational Substitution Readiness.

8.4 Conditional Compatibility

Compatibility: may be valid only under defined operating conditions.

8.5 Partial Equivalence

An alternative may satisfy part of a requirement without satisfying the complete requirement.

8.6 Evidence-Based Validation

Claimed Compatibility ≠ Validated Compatibility.

8.7 Authority Separation

Compatibility: Validation ≠ Substitution Authorization.

56. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum operational RSCM implementation should contain nine governed layers.

Layer 1 — Requirement & Candidate Identity

Establish the original Resource requirement and candidate alternative identity.

Layer 2 — Compatibility Criteria

Define the functional, technical, quality, performance, capacity, reliability, and operating-condition requirements.

Layer 3 — Functional Assessment

Evaluate whether the candidate can perform the required function.

Layer 4 — Technical & Interface Assessment

Evaluate technical properties, interfaces, and applicable restrictions.

Layer 5 — Quality, Performance & Capacity Assessment

Evaluate whether the candidate satisfies the relevant quality, performance, and technical capacity conditions.

Layer 6 — Reliability & Operating Conditions

Evaluate reliability requirements, operating boundaries, and duration-dependent conditions.

Layer 7 — Evidence Validation

Assess testing evidence, certification references where applicable, provenance, sufficiency, uncertainty, and conflicts.

Layer 8 — Compatibility Classification

Establish full, conditional, partial, incompatible, evidence-limited, conflicted, or unknown compatibility states.

Layer 9 — Governed Output & Handoff

Produce the Resource Substitution Compatibility Profile and the applicable transition governance handoff.

64. Use Case 1 — Recovered Water Compatibility for Industrial Cooling

An industrial facility proposes replacing part of its freshwater supply with recovered water.

RSAM has identified recovered water as a candidate alternative.

RSCM evaluates:

Original Requirement: Water suitable for the defined industrial cooling function.

Candidate Alternative: Recovered water from an identified treatment process.

Functional Compatibility: Can the recovered water support the required cooling function?

Technical Compatibility: Are the relevant chemical, physical, and thermal properties suitable?

Quality Compatibility: Does the water meet the applicable quality thresholds?

Interface Compatibility: Is it compatible with the receiving cooling equipment and relevant technical interfaces?

Operating Conditions: Under which treatment, temperature, flow, and equipment conditions is compatibility supported?

Conditional Equivalence: Does suitability depend on additional treatment or restricted operating conditions?

The recovered water may be compatible for one cooling application but unsuitable for another.

RSCM preserves this distinction.

It does not independently authorize use of the recovered water or establish its operational availability.

Recovered Water Suitable for Application A ≠ Recovered Water Suitable for Every Application.

65. Use Case 2 — Alternative Material Compatibility in Manufacturing

A manufacturer proposes using a recovered secondary material instead of a primary material.

RSAM has identified the recovered material as a candidate.

RSCM evaluates:

Original Requirement: Material satisfying defined mechanical, chemical, dimensional, and operating requirements.

Candidate Alternative: Validated recovered secondary material.

Functional Compatibility: Can the material perform the intended function?

Technical Compatibility: Does it satisfy the applicable material properties?

Quality Compatibility: Are purity, composition, and other quality requirements met?

Performance Compatibility: Does it meet required strength, durability, or other relevant performance criteria?

Operating Conditions: Under which production and use conditions is compatibility established?

Partial Equivalence: Is the material suitable only for certain products, loads, or operating conditions?

The material may be fully compatible with one product and only partially compatible with another.

RSCM preserves requirement-specific equivalence.

It does not independently determine whether sufficient material is available for production or whether substitution has been authorized.

Validated Material Properties ≠ Universal Material Interchangeability.

68. Cross-Architecture Mechanism Principle

An operational mechanism may produce inputs for multiple governance architectures, but it belongs only to the architecture whose governed domain includes that mechanism itself. Other architectures govern only its domain- specific relevance or consequences.

RSCM may use technical testing, simulation, Resource characterization, and Recovery validation evidence.

It governs their relevance to Resource substitution compatibility.

It does not redefine the independent mechanisms that produced those inputs.

69. Canonical Boundary

RSCM governs how identified alternative Resources, Resource capacities, Resource configurations, and operational pathways are evaluated against defined original Resource requirements to establish their functional compatibility, technical suitability, conditional equivalence, partial equivalence, incompatibility, and evidence- based compatibility boundaries before operational substitution decisions are made.

It does not independently establish actual Resource availability, Access, Allocation, operational feasibility, transition readiness, authorization, activation, or effective substitution.

Its principal governed output is the Resource Substitution Compatibility Profile .

70. Canonical Closing Statement

An alternative Resource does not become a valid substitute merely because it resembles the original Resource or can perform a similar function. Compatibility must be established against the actual requirement, under defined technical, functional, quality, capacity, reliability, and operating conditions, with evidence sufficient to support the claimed degree of equivalence.

RSCM establishes the governance boundary between identifying a possible alternative and validating its compatibility — ensuring that Resource substitution decisions rest on requirement-specific evidence rather than assumed interchangeability, generalized similarity, or unsupported technical claims.
