1. Canonical Definition

RREM — Resource Reallocation & Exception Module governs how an existing or planned Resource Allocation may be legitimately modified, redistributed, reduced, increased, deferred, redirected, temporarily overridden, or otherwise transitioned when material changes, conflicts, emergencies, failures, new claims, changed priorities, changed capacity, or other governed conditions make the existing Allocation no longer sufficient, feasible, appropriate, or valid.

RREM also governs narrowly bounded exceptions to established Allocation rules or limits where competent authority and sufficient justification permit such deviation.

Its foundational distinction is:

Changed Conditions ≠ Automatic Reallocation.

And its exception boundary is:

Exception ≠ Governance Bypass.

2. Operational Role

Resource Allocation occurs within a changing operational environment.

After an Allocation has been established:

Resource Availability may change; Demand may change; Dependency may change; Criticality may change; Priority may change; Access may change; rights may change; infrastructure may fail; Scarcity may intensify; new legitimate claims may emerge; emergencies may occur.

An existing Resource distribution may therefore cease to represent the appropriate governed state.

RREM determines whether such change requires Reallocation and governs the transition from the existing Allocation to a revised Allocation state.

It also determines whether a justified exception to an established Allocation rule or limit may be considered, under what authority, for what scope, for what duration, and with what evidence.

3. Operational Question

When materially relevant conditions change or an exceptional condition arises, should an existing or planned Resource Allocation be maintained, modified, redistributed, reduced, increased, deferred, redirected, or exceptionally overridden, under what authority and boundaries, and with what evidence and reassessment requirements?

4. Position in RPAS Governance

Canonical progression:

Legitimate Competing Claims → Resource Priority Determination → Allocation Basis → Allocation Limits → Resource Allocation → Change / Conflict / Exceptional Condition → Reallocation or Exception Determination → Updated Allocation State → Reassessment

RREM governs:

Existing Allocation + Material Change / Exception Condition → Governed Allocation Transition

5. Governed Space

RREM governs:

Resource Reallocation & Exception Governance

This space concerns legitimate changes to established or planned Resource distributions and narrowly governed deviations from ordinary Allocation rules or limits.

6. Governed Object

The primary Governed Object is:

A proposed or required transition from an existing Resource Allocation State to a revised Resource Allocation State, including any governed exception associated with that transition.

Canonical relationship:

7. Scope

RREM governs:

Reallocation triggers; material change detection; Reallocation necessity; Reallocation authority; Reallocation scope; partial Reallocation; full Reallocation; temporary Reallocation; emergency Reallocation; scheduled Reallocation; claim addition; claim removal; capacity redistribution; Allocation reduction; Allocation increase; Allocation deferral; Allocation redirection; Allocation displacement; priority-driven Reallocation; capacity-driven Reallocation; constraint-driven Reallocation; exception requests; exception eligibility; exception authority; exception justification; exception scope; exception duration; exception conditions; exception evidence; temporary override; exception expiration; exception renewal; exception withdrawal; conflict; uncertainty; transition evidence; reassessment; versioning; traceability.

RREM does not independently:

determine Resource Demand; determine Dependency or Criticality;

determine original Resource Priority; establish the original Allocation Basis; establish ordinary Allocation Limits; create Resource rights; authorize Resource Access; implement technical Resource controls; measure Consumption; govern Scarcity as a whole; determine long-term Sustainability.

8. Reallocation Principle

An established Allocation should not be treated as permanent merely because it was once legitimate.

Previously Valid Allocation ≠ Permanently Valid Allocation.

When materially relevant conditions change, the Allocation may require reassessment.

9. Reallocation Trigger

A Reallocation Trigger is an event, state change, evidence change, or governance condition that may materially affect the validity or appropriateness of an existing Resource Allocation.

10. Trigger Boundary

Reallocation Trigger ≠ Reallocation Decision.

Detection of change initiates governance evaluation.

It does not automatically determine the outcome.

11. Materiality

Not every change requires Reallocation.

RREM should determine whether the change is materially relevant to:

Priority; Allocable Capacity; Allocation Basis; Allocation Limits; claim legitimacy; Resource feasibility; continuity; other Allocation conditions.

12. Material Change

A Material Change is a change capable of altering the legitimate Resource distribution.

13. Non-Material Change

A change that does not materially affect the governed Allocation may be recorded without triggering Resource redistribution.

14. Resource Availability Change

A change in Resource Availability may require Reallocation where existing assignments can no longer be supported or additional capacity becomes legitimately allocable.

15. Capacity Reduction

Loss of Resource capacity may require existing allocations to be reduced or redistributed.

16. Capacity Increase

Additional Resource capacity may permit:

previously deferred claims; partial allocations to increase; reserve restoration; new legitimate claims; other governed distribution changes.

17. Capacity Boundary

Additional Capacity ≠ Automatic Allocation Increase.

New capacity must still enter the governed Allocation process.

18. Demand Change

Material increases or decreases in validated Resource Demand may trigger Reallocation.

19. Demand Reduction

Reduced Demand may release Resource capacity for legitimate redistribution.

20. Demand Increase

Increased Demand does not automatically create increased Allocation.

Increased Demand ≠ Automatic Increased Allocation.

21. Dependency Change

A change in Resource Dependency may materially affect the basis for existing Allocation.

22. Criticality Change

A change in Criticality may trigger Priority reassessment and potentially Reallocation.

23. Criticality Boundary

Changed Criticality ≠ Automatic Reallocation Outcome.

RPDM may first need to reassess Priority.

24. Priority Change

A materially changed Resource Priority may require the existing Resource distribution to be reconsidered.

25. Priority Boundary

Changed Priority ≠ Automatic Resource Transfer.

The updated Allocation must remain within the current Allocation Basis and Allocation Limits or those must also be reassessed.

26. Access Change

Changes in Resource Access may require Allocation modification.

Examples include:

Access restriction; Access suspension; Access withdrawal; Access expiration; Access scope modification.

27. Access Boundary

Loss of Access may invalidate Allocation, but RREM does not itself revoke Access.

RAGS governs Access.

RREM governs the Allocation consequence.

28. Rights Change

Modification, expiration, suspension, transfer, or termination of a Resource right may affect an existing Allocation.

29. Rights Boundary

RREM does not determine the underlying Resource right.

It governs its Allocation consequence.

30. New Legitimate Claim

A new legitimate Resource claim may enter an existing Allocation context.

Its existence does not automatically displace existing allocations.

31. New Claim Boundary

New Legitimate Claim ≠ Automatic Existing-Allocation Reduction.

The new claim must enter the relevant Priority and Allocation governance process.

32. Claim Removal

Where a claim is legitimately removed from the Allocation context, its assigned capacity may become available for redistribution.

33. Infrastructure Change

Infrastructure failure, degradation, restoration, expansion, or topology change may alter feasible Resource distribution.

34. Local Constraint Change

A Resource system may require Reallocation even when aggregate capacity remains unchanged because a local delivery constraint changes.

35. Scarcity Change

A new or intensified Scarcity state may require tighter Resource distribution.

RSCS governs the Scarcity condition.

RREM governs its effect on existing allocations.

36. Scarcity Recovery

Reduced Scarcity may permit existing Allocation restrictions to be relaxed.

It does not automatically restore previous allocations.

37. Emergency Condition

An emergency may require rapid Reallocation to preserve:

life; safety; critical infrastructure; essential operations; system continuity; other legitimately protected functions.

38. Emergency Reallocation

Emergency Reallocation may use accelerated decision procedures where competent governance permits.

39. Emergency Boundary

Emergency ≠ Unlimited Reallocation Authority.

Authority, scope, duration, evidence, and affected claims should remain governed.

40. Reallocation Authority

Material Resource redistribution should remain traceable to competent authority.

41. Authority Origin

Where relevant:

Authority: Origin → Delegated Reallocation Authority → Reallocation Decision

should remain traceable.

42. Authority Scope

A Reallocation Authority should not modify Resource distributions outside its authorized scope.

43. Automated Reallocation

Automated systems may execute Reallocation under predefined governance rules.

44. Automation Boundary

Automated Reallocation Capability ≠ Reallocation Authority.

45. Reallocation Scope

RREM should determine how much of the existing Allocation context must be reconsidered.

46. Targeted Reallocation

A change affecting one claim or Resource segment may justify modification only of the affected Allocation dimensions.

47. Full Reallocation

A full Resource redistribution may be required where changes materially affect the overall Allocation context.

48. Reallocation Scope Principle

Local Change ≠ Automatic Full Reallocation.

Conversely:

Systemic Change ≠ Sufficiently Governed by Local Adjustment.

49. Partial Reallocation

Only part of an existing Resource Allocation may need to change.

50. Full Reallocation

All materially relevant Resource assignments within the Allocation context may be reconsidered.

51. Temporary Reallocation

Resource capacity may be redistributed for a limited period.

52. Permanent Reallocation

A revised Allocation may remain in effect until another governed change occurs.

53. Scheduled Reallocation

Some Resource systems may use predefined reassessment and Reallocation intervals.

54. Event-Driven Reallocation

Material events may trigger Reallocation outside scheduled review cycles.

55. Allocation Reduction

An existing Allocation may be reduced where current governance evidence no longer supports the previous level.

56. Reduction Boundary

Allocation Reduction ≠ Access Revocation.

An actor may retain legitimate Access while receiving less Resource capacity.

57. Allocation Increase

An existing Allocation may be increased where:

capacity becomes available; Priority changes; Demand changes; limits change; other legitimate conditions support increase.

58. Increase Boundary

Allocation Increase ≠ Expanded Access Authority.

The increased Allocation must remain within legitimate Access boundaries.

59. Allocation Deferral

A Resource Allocation may be postponed while the claim remains legitimate.

60. Deferral Boundary

Deferred Allocation ≠ Rejected Claim.

61. Allocation Redirection

Resource capacity may be redirected from one:

claim; location; Resource Source; time period; purpose

to another where governance permits.

62. Redirection Boundary

Redirection should preserve applicable:

rights; Access; Source; purpose; authority; technical constraints.

63. Allocation Displacement

A higher-priority or emergency claim may require reducing an existing lower-priority Allocation.

Such displacement should be explicit and evidenced.

64. Displacement Boundary

Higher Priority ≠ Automatic Total Displacement.

Only the Resource redistribution justified by the governed Allocation context should occur.

65. Reallocation Stability

Resource systems should avoid unnecessary redistribution where existing allocations remain legitimate and sufficient.

66. Stability Principle

Ability to Reallocate ≠ Need to Reallocate.

Unnecessary Reallocation may create:

operational instability; transition cost; Resource waste; continuity risk.

67. Reallocation Proportionality

The magnitude of Reallocation should be proportionate to the material governance change where appropriate.

68. Transition Feasibility

A desired Reallocation may require a transition period.

Immediate redistribution may be technically impossible or operationally harmful.

69. Transition Boundary

Reallocation Decision ≠ Instantaneous Resource Transition.

70. Transition Plan

Where required, RREM should establish:

current Allocation; target Allocation; transition sequence; effective time; temporary states; affected claims; technical dependencies.

71. Exception Principle

An Exception permits a defined deviation from an ordinary Allocation rule, limit, or condition where competent governance explicitly authorizes it.

72. Exception Boundary

Exception ≠ Governance Bypass.

An exception remains governed precisely because the ordinary rule is not being followed.

73. Exception Eligibility

An exception should be considered only where the relevant governance framework permits exceptions.

74. Exception Trigger

Potential triggers may include:

emergency; unforeseen operational condition;

critical continuity requirement; temporary infrastructure failure; exceptional safety requirement; other predefined or competently recognized condition.

75. Exception Request

An Exception Request should identify:

requested deviation; affected Resource; affected claim; ordinary rule or limit; reason; requested scope; requested duration; supporting evidence.

76. Exception Authority

An exception requires authority competent to authorize deviation from the relevant rule or limit.

77. Authority Boundary

Authority: to Allocate ≠ Automatically Authority to Grant Exceptions.

Exception authority may require a different or higher governance level.

78. Exception Justification

The justification should explain why ordinary Allocation governance cannot sufficiently address the exceptional condition.

79. Convenience Boundary

Operational Convenience ≠ Sufficient Exception Justification.

80. Exception Scope

An exception should be no broader than legitimately required.

It may be bounded by:

Resource; claim; quantity; capacity; rate; Source; location; purpose; time; condition.

81. Exception Duration

An exception should have a defined duration or termination condition where applicable.

82. Temporary Exception

A temporary exception expires when:

its defined period ends; the exceptional condition ends; its authority is withdrawn; its evidence becomes invalid; another specified termination condition occurs.

83. Permanent Exception Boundary

A repeated or indefinite exception may indicate that the underlying governance rule requires formal reassessment.

Repeated Exception ≠ Substitute for Rule Revision.

84. Exception Conditions

Exceptions may require conditions such as:

enhanced monitoring; reduced duration; additional evidence; higher authority; compensating controls; mandatory reassessment.

85. Limit Exception

A competent authority may permit deviation from a soft or otherwise exception-capable Allocation Limit.

86. Hard Limit Boundary

A limit defined as non-overridable within the current governance context cannot be bypassed merely by labeling the action an exception.

87. Physical Boundary

Exception Authority Cannot Override Physical Reality.

No governance exception can create Resource capacity that does not physically exist.

88. Access Boundary for Exceptions

An Allocation exception does not automatically expand Resource Access Authority.

Where Access modification is required, RAGS must govern that change.

89. Rights Boundary for Exceptions

An exception should not silently nullify or create Resource rights.

90. Priority Exception

Where competent governance permits, an exceptional condition may alter ordinary Priority treatment.

The deviation should remain explicit and temporary or otherwise formally governed.

91. Allocation Rule Exception

A Resource may be distributed using a different rule under an exceptional condition.

The alternate rule should be:

authorized; defined; bounded; evidenced; traceable.

92. Optimization Exception Boundary

Optimization output does not independently justify deviation from Allocation rules.

Better Optimized Outcome ≠ Exception Authority.

93. Value Exception Boundary

High economic or operational Value does not independently justify an exception.

High Value ≠ Exception Authority.

94. Market Exception Boundary

Ability to pay more does not independently authorize deviation from established Allocation governance.

95. Exception Conflict

An Exception Request may conflict with:

rights; Access; safety; continuity; other allocations; hard limits; Resource Availability; competent authority.

Such conflicts should remain visible.

96. Exception Uncertainty

Where evidence supporting an exception is materially uncertain, that uncertainty should remain part of the decision.

97. Exception Evidence

Exception evidence should be sufficient to establish:

why deviation is required; what is affected; who authorizes it; how far deviation extends; how long it applies; what consequences may result.

98. Exception Traceability

Canonical traceability:

Exception → Ordinary Rule / Limit → Exception Trigger → Justification → Exception Authority → Scope → Duration → Conditions → Evidence → Resulting Allocation State

99. Reallocation Evidence

Reallocation should preserve sufficient evidence to reconstruct:

previous Allocation; material change; affected claims; updated Priority where relevant; current Allocation Basis; current Allocation Limits; Reallocation Authority; new Allocation state.

100. Evidence Provenance

Material evidence should preserve where relevant:

source; time; Resource; claim; decision; authority; version.

101. Evidence Sufficiency

RREM should determine whether evidence is sufficient for the required Reallocation or exception decision.

102. Evidence Freshness

Evidence should remain sufficiently current for the Resource transition being governed.

103. Reallocation Conflict

Reallocation Conflict exists where legitimate governance inputs support materially incompatible Resource redistribution outcomes.

104. Reallocation Uncertainty

Uncertainty may concern:

Resource Availability; Demand; Priority; Access; rights; technical feasibility; transition consequence; exception conditions.

105. Uncertainty Principle

Uncertain Change ≠ Certain Reallocation.

Material uncertainty should remain visible.

106. Prediction Evidence

PGA™ may provide governed evidence concerning anticipated:

capacity loss; Demand change; Scarcity; infrastructure failure; future Resource requirements.

107. Prediction Boundary

Predicted Change ≠ Current Reallocation Requirement by definition.

Prediction may justify preparation or anticipatory governance where competent rules permit it.

108. Simulation Evidence

SIMULOS® may compare candidate Reallocation paths or exception consequences.

109. Simulation Boundary

Simulated Preferred Reallocation ≠ Governed Reallocation Decision.

119. Exception Expiration

An expired exception should not silently remain operational.

120. Exception Renewal

Where renewal is permitted, continuation should require renewed governance validation.

Previous Exception ≠ Automatic Exception Renewal.

121. Exception Withdrawal

A competent authority may withdraw an active exception where its basis no longer remains valid.

122. Exception Closure

Closure should preserve the final exception state and resulting Allocation state.

123. Reallocation Reassessment

RREM should reassess an active or planned Reallocation when material evidence changes before or during transition.

124. Exception Reassessment

Exceptions should be reassessed when:

125. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum operational RREM implementation should contain eight governed layers.

Layer 1 — Existing Allocation State

Establish:

Layer 2 — Change / Exception Detection

Identify:

Determine:

Evaluate:

Determine:

maintain; modify; reduce; increase; defer; redirect; reallocate; approve / deny exception.

Preserve:

evidence; conflict; uncertainty; expiration; reassessment triggers; version.

Produce:

132. Failure Conditions

RREM should not be considered sufficiently completed where:

every change automatically causes Reallocation; material changes are ignored; new capacity is automatically distributed; increased Demand automatically increases Allocation; Criticality change automatically determines the Reallocation outcome; Priority change automatically transfers Resource; Access consequences are confused with Access revocation; new claims automatically displace existing allocations; Scarcity automatically determines Reallocation without governance logic; emergency conditions create unlimited Reallocation Authority; technical capability is treated as Reallocation Authority; local changes automatically trigger unnecessary system-wide Reallocation; systemic changes are handled only through inadequate local adjustment; Allocation reduction is treated as Access revocation; Allocation increase expands Access without authority; deferred Allocation is treated as rejected claim; higher Priority automatically causes total displacement; Reallocation occurs where no material need exists; transition feasibility is ignored; exceptions are used as governance bypasses; exception authority is assumed from ordinary Allocation Authority; operational convenience is treated as sufficient exception justification; exceptions are broader or longer than justified; repeated exceptions substitute for formal rule revision; hard limits are bypassed by labeling an action an exception; exceptions are treated as capable of overriding physical reality; exceptions silently expand Access or create rights; optimization output creates exception authority; Value creates exception authority; material conflict is hidden; uncertainty is suppressed; predictions are treated automatically as current Reallocation requirements; simulation outputs are treated as decisions; expired exceptions continue silently; previous exceptions renew automatically; Reallocation and Exception states are conflated; material transitions cannot be traced to evidence and authority.

133. Use Case 1 — AI Data Center GPU Emergency Reallocation

A data center has active GPU allocations across:

safety-critical inference; customer production workloads; model training; experimental workloads.

A cooling-system failure suddenly reduces usable GPU capacity by 30%.

The existing allocations can no longer all be maintained.

RREM identifies:

capacity reduction as the Reallocation Trigger; the reduction as materially relevant; affected Allocation Profiles; updated Priority evidence; current Allocation Limits; Reallocation Authority.

The resulting transition may:

preserve the safety-critical minimum; reduce production capacity proportionally within governed limits; defer part of model training; temporarily suspend experimental Allocation.

If emergency rules permit temporary deviation from an ordinary Allocation rule, RREM separately records that deviation through a Resource Allocation Exception Profile .

When cooling capacity is restored, the emergency Allocation does not automatically become permanent.

A new governed reassessment determines the appropriate Resource distribution.

134. Use Case 2 — Regional Water Emergency Reallocation

A region has established water allocations for:

essential household supply; healthcare; agriculture; industrial production.

An unexpected Source contamination event removes a substantial portion of usable water capacity.

RREM evaluates:

the lost capacity; affected claims; updated Priority Profiles; current rights and Access boundaries; minimum Allocation Limits; alternative Sources; Reallocation Authority; transition feasibility.

The resulting Reallocation may temporarily:

protect minimum household supply; preserve healthcare requirements; reduce agricultural allocations; reduce or defer industrial allocations.

Where an emergency exception is required to temporarily modify an ordinary Allocation rule, the exception receives:

explicit authority; defined scope; defined duration; supporting evidence; termination conditions.

When the contaminated Source returns to service, the emergency distribution is reassessed rather than automatically continued.

136. Cross-Architecture Boundary

RREM may consume:

RCHS — current Resource identity, Source, state, and context.

RACS — changed Availability, usable capacity, reservation, and accessibility evidence.

RDGS — changed Demand and concurrency evidence.

RDCS — changed Dependency, Criticality, and consequence evidence.

RAGS — changed Resource rights, Access authorization, restrictions, withdrawal, and continuity evidence.

RPDM — current or reassessed Resource Priority Profiles.

RABM — current Resource Allocation Basis Profile.

RALM — current Resource Allocation Limit Profiles.

RSCS — Scarcity and Constraint states.

RSFS — substitution and flexibility evidence.

RIRS — interdependency and resilience evidence.

RCSS — continuity and sustainability constraints.

GOA™ — Reallocation Authority, Exception Authority, and Delegation evidence.

OBIDENITY® — identity, ownership, authority, permission, and provenance evidence.

ORA™ — actual operational changes and transition state.

PGA™ — governed prediction evidence.

SIMULOS® — simulated Reallocation and exception consequences.

VEGA™ — Value and efficiency evidence where legitimately relevant.

RREM does not absorb these mechanisms.

137. Cross-Architecture Mechanism Principle

An operational mechanism may produce inputs for multiple governance architectures, but it belongs only to the architecture whose governed domain includes that mechanism itself. Other architectures govern only its domain- specific relevance or consequences.

RACS determines Resource Availability and capacity.

RDCS determines Dependency and Criticality.

RAGS governs Access and rights.

RPDM determines Priority.

RABM establishes the Allocation Basis.

RALM establishes Allocation Limits.

RSCS determines Scarcity conditions.

RREM governs whether changes in those governed inputs require an existing Resource Allocation to transition and whether a legitimate exception is permitted.

138. Canonical Boundary

RREM governs the determination, authorization, scope, transition, evidence, maintenance, reassessment, and closure of Resource Reallocation and legitimate Allocation exceptions when material changes or exceptional conditions affect an existing or planned Resource distribution.

It does not determine original Resource Demand, Dependency, Criticality, Priority, ordinary Allocation Basis, ordinary Allocation Limits, Resource rights, Access authorization, physical Availability, technical Resource controls, Consumption, Scarcity Governance as a whole, or long-term Sustainability.

Its principal governed outputs are the Resource Reallocation Profile and, where applicable, the Resource Allocation Exception Profile .

139. Canonical Closing Statement

A Resource Allocation cannot remain governed if it is treated as permanent when the reality supporting it has materially changed, nor can governance remain credible if every difficult condition becomes an excuse to bypass its rules.

RREM governs both boundaries — enabling legitimate Resource redistribution when reality changes while ensuring that exceptional deviation remains authorized, bounded, evidenced, temporary where required, and fully traceable.
