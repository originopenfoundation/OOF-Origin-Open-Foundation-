1. Canonical Definition

SRRM — Secondary Resource Reintegration Module is the Core Module within RRCS that governs how validated recovered Resources are classified, prepared, traced, assessed for reintegration readiness, connected to legitimate subsequent Resource-use requirements, reintegrated under applicable governance conditions, monitored through subsequent use, and evidenced across successive Resource cycles.

SRRM establishes the governance continuity between validated Resource Recovery and actual secondary Resource use.

It distinguishes the existence of a validated recovered Resource from its availability, authorized reintegration, operational use, and demonstrated Circularity.

Its foundational distinctions are:

Validated Recovered Resource ≠ Reintegrated Resource.

Reintegration Ready ≠ Reintegration Authorized.

Reintegrated Resource ≠ Actually Utilized Resource.

One Successful Recovery Cycle ≠ Unlimited Circularity.

2. Operational Role

SRRM governs the final stage of Resource Recovery & Circularity Governance.

ERRM establishes what Resource remains after original use.

RPMM establishes whether Recovery is feasible.

RURPM governs actual Recovery process execution.

RRRGM establishes whether recovered output has been validated.

SRRM governs the transition from validated recovered Resource capability into a subsequent legitimate Resource-use cycle.

Its operational responsibilities include:

Establishing secondary Resource identity. Preserving Recovery and validation provenance. Determining reintegration readiness. Identifying applicable subsequent-use requirements. Preserving availability and Access conditions. Verifying applicable reintegration authorization. Recording actual Resource reintegration. Distinguishing reintegration from actual utilization. Monitoring subsequent Resource-use evidence. Preserving Resource-cycle continuity. Evaluating Circularity performance within defined boundaries.

Supporting repeated Recovery and reintegration cycles. Preventing unsupported or double-counted Circularity claims.

SRRM does not independently grant Access, Allocation, substitution, or operational execution authority.

3. Operational Question

How should validated recovered Resources be classified, prepared, authorized through competent governance mechanisms, reintegrated, and traced into subsequent legitimate Resource-use cycles so that actual Resource continuity and Circularity can be evidenced without confusing Recovery, validation, readiness, availability, allocation, reintegration, utilization, or long-term Sustainability?

4. Position in Architecture Governance Flow

RSGA™ Governance Flow

Resource Characterization → Availability & Capacity → Demand → Dependency & Criticality → Access → Prioritization & Allocation → Consumption & Utilization → Scarcity & Constraint → Recovery & Circularity → Substitution & Flexibility → Interdependency & Resilience → Continuity & Sustainability

RRCS Governance Flow

1. End-of-Use Resource Recognition — ERRM 2. Resource Recovery Potential — RPMM 3. Resource Recovery Process — RURPM 4. Recovered Resource Recognition & Validation — RRRGM 5. Secondary Resource Reintegration — SRRM

SRRM occupies the fifth and final governance position.

It establishes the evidence connecting validated recovered Resources with subsequent Resource use.

Its outputs may support Resource Availability, Consumption & Utilization, Substitution, Resilience, and Sustainability governance.

5. Governed Space

Secondary Resource Reintegration & Circularity Governance

This governed space concerns the classification, readiness, authorized reintegration, operational use evidence, and lifecycle continuity of validated recovered Resources.

It includes the traceable transition between Resource-use cycles.

The governed space does not assume that every recovered Resource must be reintegrated or that every reintegration produces a net sustainability benefit.

6. Governed Object

The primary Governed Object is:

A validated recovered Resource or Secondary Resource Candidate associated with an identifiable Recovery history, applicable subsequent-use requirements, reintegration conditions, and Resource-cycle continuity record.

Canonical relationship:

Validated Recovered Resource → Secondary Resource Classification → Reintegration Readiness → Subsequent- Use Requirements → Applicable Authority & Access References → Reintegration → Subsequent Resource Use → Circularity Evidence → Cycle Reassessment

7. Scope

SRRM governs:

Secondary Resource candidate identification. Secondary Resource classification. Recovered Resource provenance continuity. Reintegration readiness. Subsequent-use requirements. Resource availability references. Resource Access references. Allocation references. Substitution validity references. Reintegration authorization references. Reintegration execution evidence. Actual subsequent Resource use. Resource-cycle identity. Circularity measurement boundaries. Repeated Resource cycles. Resource quality degradation across cycles. Circularity evidence and uncertainty. Resource-cycle monitoring and reassessment.

SRRM does not independently determine Resource Access rights, Resource Allocation priority, substitution authorization, technical certification, operational safety, Value eligibility, Harm, Liability, or long-term Sustainability.

27. Resource-Cycle Continuity

Resource-cycle continuity establishes the traceable relationship between successive Resource-use cycles.

Canonical relationship:

Use Cycle 1 → End-of-Use → Recovery → Validation → Reintegration → Use Cycle 2

Where the Resource is subsequently recovered again:

Use Cycle 2 → End-of-Use → Recovery → Validation → Reintegration → Use Cycle 3

Each cycle requires its own evidence.

28. Repeated Recovery Cycles

A Resource may undergo multiple Recovery cycles.

However, the feasibility, quality, quantity, and supporting Resource requirements may change.

Recovery Feasible in Cycle 1 ≠ Recovery Feasible in Cycle 2.

35. Circularity Accounting Principle

Circularity Measurement ≠ Sustainability Measurement.

Circularity concerns evidenced Resource cycles.

Sustainability requires broader consideration of Resource dependencies, losses, constraints, regeneration, resilience, and long-term continuity.

36. Double Counting Prevention

SRRM must prevent the same Resource quantity from being incorrectly counted as multiple independent Resource quantities merely because it passes through several lifecycle stages.

For example:

Collected Quantity + Recovered Quantity + Reintegrated Quantity

must not automatically be reported as three separate quantities of newly created Resource.

37. Double Counting Principle

57. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum operational SRRM implementation should contain nine governed layers.

Layer 1 — Validated Resource Basis

Establish the recovered Resource identity, validation state, quantity, quality, provenance, and applicable restrictions.

Layer 2 — Secondary Resource Classification

Determine whether the recovered Resource satisfies the conditions for secondary Resource classification.

Layer 3 — Reintegration Readiness

Assess whether the Resource is prepared for the proposed subsequent use.

Layer 4 — Subsequent-Use Requirements

Identify the receiving Resource system and its applicable quality, quantity, compatibility, location, and timing requirements.

Layer 5 — Governance References

Preserve applicable Availability, Access, Allocation, substitution, and reintegration authorization references.

Layer 6 — Reintegration Execution

Record the actual reintegration event, Resource quantity, receiving system, time, location, and operational evidence.

Layer 7 — Subsequent-Use Evidence

Establish whether and how the secondary Resource was actually utilized.

Layer 8 — Circularity & Cycle Continuity

Preserve Resource-cycle identity, quality changes, quantity retention, losses, and Circularity evidence without double counting.

Layer 9 — Governed Output

Produce the Secondary Resource Reintegration & Circularity Profile .

65. Use Case 1 — Recovered AI Data Center Hardware Reintegration

An AI data center retires accelerator servers.

The hardware passes through Resource Recognition, Recovery Potential assessment, Recovery processing, and recovered Resource validation.

RRRGM validates selected refurbished accelerator cards for lower-demand computational applications.

SRRM governs their reintegration.

Secondary Resource: Refurbished Accelerator Card

Validated Use: Lower-Demand Compute

Receiving Resource System: Secondary Computing Infrastructure

Reintegration Requirements:

Applicable hardware compatibility. Validated functional performance. Available installation capacity. Authorized Resource Access. Applicable Allocation decision. Operational installation evidence. Actual subsequent-use evidence.

SRRM records the installation of the accelerator cards into the receiving system.

However, installation alone does not prove utilization.

The module preserves whether the cards actually performed subsequent computational work.

Hardware Installed ≠ Hardware Utilized.

Where subsequent use is evidenced, SRRM establishes the Resource-cycle continuity:

Original AI Compute Use → Hardware Retirement → Recovery → Validation → Secondary Compute Reintegration → Subsequent Compute Use

This establishes an evidenced additional Resource-use cycle without claiming indefinite hardware Circularity.

66. Use Case 2 — Recovered Industrial Water Reintegration

An industrial facility recovers wastewater through an authorized treatment process.

RRRGM validates the recovered water for a defined industrial cooling application.

SRRM governs the return of that water to the industrial system.

Secondary Resource: Recovered Industrial Water

Validated Use: Industrial Cooling

Receiving Resource System: Industrial Cooling Circuit

Reintegration Requirements:

Validated water quality. Available storage and distribution capacity. Applicable Access and operational authorization. Compatible cooling-system requirements. Measured reintegrated volume. Actual cooling-water utilization evidence.

SRRM distinguishes:

Water Recovered

Water Validated

Water Stored

Water Reintegrated

Water Actually Used

The module prevents the same water volume from being counted as several independent quantities of newly available water.

It establishes the traceable Resource cycle:

Industrial Water Use → Wastewater → Recovery → Validation → Cooling-System Reintegration → Subsequent Water Use

This demonstrates Resource continuity within a defined operational boundary.

It does not automatically establish that the complete water system is sustainable.

68. Cross-Architecture Mechanism Principle

An operational mechanism may produce inputs for multiple governance architectures, but it belongs only to the architecture whose governed domain includes that mechanism itself. Other architectures govern only its domain- specific relevance or consequences.

Secondary Resource reintegration may produce evidence relevant to Resource Availability, Consumption, substitution, resilience, and long-term Sustainability.

SRRM governs the Resource Recovery-to-reuse continuity and its Circularity evidence.

Other architectures govern their respective downstream decisions and consequences.

69. Canonical Boundary

SRRM governs how validated recovered Resources are classified, prepared, traced, assessed for reintegration readiness, connected to legitimate subsequent Resource-use requirements, reintegrated under applicable governance conditions, monitored through subsequent use, and evidenced across successive Resource cycles.

It does not independently determine Resource Access rights, Allocation priority, substitution authorization, technical certification, operational safety, Value eligibility, Harm, Liability, or long-term Sustainability.

Its principal governed output is the Secondary Resource Reintegration & Circularity Profile .

70. Canonical Closing Statement

A Resource does not become circular merely because it has been collected, processed, recovered, or validated. Circularity requires evidence that Resource capability has legitimately continued into another use cycle, with its identity, quantity, quality, restrictions, and losses remaining traceable.

SRRM establishes the governance continuity between validated Recovery and subsequent Resource use — ensuring that Circularity is demonstrated through actual Resource reintegration and utilization rather than inferred from Recovery potential, processing activity, or unsupported recycling claims.
