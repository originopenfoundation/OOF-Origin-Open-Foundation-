1. Canonical Definition

RCASM — Resource Continuity Assessment Module is the Core Module within RCSS that governs how the continued availability, accessibility, adequacy, and operational usability of Resources required to sustain defined operational functions are identified, evaluated, evidenced, classified, monitored, and reassessed across specified temporal, geographic, operational, capacity, demand, dependency, and disturbance conditions.

RCASM establishes the methodological conditions under which Resource continuity may be evaluated as a bounded operational capability rather than inferred from present Resource availability, installed capacity, contractual supply arrangements, historical performance, or documented contingency plans.

It evaluates whether the Resources supporting required operational functions can remain available and usable throughout the applicable assessment horizon, including periods of changing demand, supply variability, capacity degradation, dependency disruption, and Resource constraints.

RCASM distinguishes continuity of Resource supply from continuity of required operational functions.

It also distinguishes projected Resource continuity from demonstrated operational continuity and preserves the conditions under which either conclusion remains applicable.

Resource Available Now ≠ Resource Continuously Available.

Resource Supply Maintained ≠ Required Operational Function Maintained.

Installed Resource Capacity ≠ Continuously Usable Resource Capacity.

Resource Continuity Demonstrated ≠ Resource Sustainability Demonstrated.

RCASM governs the assessment of Resource continuity across defined operational and temporal conditions.

Its operational role is to establish:

Resource capacity continuity. Resource demand continuity. Resource access continuity. Resource dependency continuity. Supporting infrastructure continuity. Resource quality continuity. Resource delivery continuity. Resource timing requirements. Resource supply variability. Resource capacity degradation. Resource demand growth. Resource competition. Resource scarcity exposure. Resource disturbance exposure. Resource resilience references. Resource continuity gaps. Resource continuity uncertainty. Resource continuity classification. Resource continuity monitoring. RCASM does not independently establish Resource Sustainability, determine Resource allocation priorities, grant Resource Access, execute operational interventions, or guarantee future Resource availability.

3. Core Question

How should Resource continuity be assessed so that the Resources required to sustain defined operational functions remain demonstrably available, accessible, adequate, and usable across the relevant assessment horizon without confusing present availability, installed capacity, contractual supply, theoretical backup arrangements, or short-term operational success with validated future Resource continuity?

76. Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum operational RCASM implementation should contain ten governed layers.

Layer 1 — Resource System & Continuity Identity

Establish the Resource System, assessment identity, operational context, and continuity boundary.

Layer 2 — Required Operational Functions

Identify the functions that must remain supported and their Resource requirements.

Layer 3 — Assessment Horizon

Define the temporal period across which continuity is evaluated.

Layer 4 — Availability & Capacity Continuity

Evaluate whether expected Resource availability and usable capacity remain sufficient.

Layer 5 — Demand & Adequacy Continuity

Evaluate current and projected demand, peak requirements, and temporal adequacy.

Layer 6 — Access, Dependency & Delivery Continuity

Evaluate Resource Access, supporting dependencies, infrastructure, quality, delivery, and timing.

Layer 7 — Constraint & Disturbance Exposure

Evaluate scarcity, variability, degradation, competing demand, disturbance exposure, and combined constraints.

Layer 8 — Continuity Evidence & Classification

Evaluate evidence sufficiency, uncertainty, continuity gaps, and the resulting continuity state.

Layer 9 — Monitoring & Reassessment

Establish monitoring signals, material change conditions, and reassessment triggers.

Layer 10 — Governed Output & Handoff

Produce the Resource Continuity Assessment Profile and preserve the downstream handoff to Resource Consumption & Replenishment Sustainability Governance.

Resource Requirements: Electricity, cooling capacity, water where applicable, and supporting infrastructure.

Availability Continuity: Expected electricity and cooling Resource availability.

Capacity Continuity: Usable electrical and cooling capacity over time.

Demand Continuity: Current and projected computing demand.

Dependency Continuity: Electricity distribution, cooling infrastructure, water supply, and control systems.

Access Continuity: Applicable Resource access and supply conditions.

Delivery Continuity: Required electricity and cooling delivery.

Constraint Exposure: Demand growth, capacity degradation, supply variability, and shared dependencies.

Evidence Basis: Availability records, capacity assessments, demand projections, infrastructure evidence, and relevant resilience profiles.

Output: Resource Continuity Assessment Profile.

The assessment may establish that current electricity capacity is adequate while projected demand exceeds future usable capacity.

Current Computing Operations Supported ≠ Future Computing Expansion Continuity Established.

85. Use Case 2 — Industrial Water Supply Continuity

An industrial facility depends on water for production and plans to maintain operations through seasonal supply variation.

RCASM establishes:

Resource System: Industrial production facility.

Required Function: Water-supported production.

Assessment Horizon: Defined operating period.

Resource Requirements: Required water quantity, quality, delivery rate, and timing.

Availability Continuity: Expected water availability across seasonal conditions.

Capacity Continuity: Storage, treatment, pumping, and distribution capacity.

Demand Continuity: Production water requirements and expected variability.

Dependency Continuity: Electricity, pumping infrastructure, treatment systems, and delivery pathways.

Access Continuity: Applicable water access conditions.

Constraint Exposure: Seasonal scarcity, infrastructure degradation, and competing water demand.

Buffer Contribution: Usable water storage and its duration.

Alternative Resource Arrangements: Alternative water sources where available and operationally compatible.

Output: Resource Continuity Assessment Profile.

A facility may possess sufficient water storage for short interruptions while remaining exposed to persistent water scarcity.

Short-Term Water Supply Continuity ≠ Long-Term Water Sustainability.

87. Cross-Architecture Boundary

RCASM may consume governed evidence from:

RCHS — Resource Characterization Standard

RACS — Resource Availability & Capacity Standard

RDGS — Resource Demand Governance Standard

RDCS — Resource Dependency & Criticality Standard

RAGS — Resource Access Governance Standard

RPAS — Resource Prioritization & Allocation Standard

RCUS — Resource Consumption & Utilization Standard

RSCS — Resource Scarcity & Constraint Standard

RRCS — Resource Recovery & Circularity Standard

RSFS — Resource Substitution & Flexibility Standard

RIRS — Resource Interdependency & Resilience Standard

Its principal downstream relationship is to the remaining RCSS Core Modules governing Resource Sustainability.

RCASM evaluates Resource continuity using the outputs of these mechanisms.

It does not replace their independent governance functions.

88. Cross-Architecture Mechanism Principle

An operational mechanism may produce inputs for multiple governance architectures, but it belongs only to the architecture whose governed domain includes that mechanism itself. Other architectures govern only its domain- specific relevance or consequences.

RCASM may use Resource availability, capacity, demand, dependency, access, scarcity, recovery, substitution, and resilience evidence.

It governs the relevance of those inputs to Resource continuity across the assessment horizon.

It does not redefine the mechanisms that produced them.

89. Canonical Boundary

RCASM governs how the continued availability, accessibility, adequacy, and operational usability of Resources required to sustain defined operational functions are identified, evaluated, evidenced, classified, monitored, and reassessed across specified temporal, geographic, operational, capacity, demand, dependency, and disturbance conditions.

It does not independently create Resource supply, grant Resource Access, execute Resource Allocation, authorize operational interventions, determine Harm or Liability, or establish long-term Resource Sustainability.

Its principal governed output is the Resource Continuity Assessment Profile .

90. Canonical Closing Statement

Resource continuity cannot be established merely because Resources are currently available, infrastructure capacity exists, supply contracts remain active, or operations have historically continued without interruption. Continuity becomes a supportable conclusion only when the Resource quantities, capacities, qualities, access conditions, delivery pathways, supporting dependencies, and timing requirements necessary for defined operational functions remain adequately evidenced across the relevant assessment horizon.

RCASM establishes the first governance layer of RCSS — ensuring that present Resource availability is never mistaken for future adequacy, installed capacity is not confused with continuously usable capacity, and operational continuity remains a bounded, evidence-dependent conclusion rather than an unsupported promise of Resource Sustainability.
