Energy Governance Architecture — Physical Energy Lifecycle Governance Layer

Official Energy Governance Architecture Map for Energy Sources, Generation, Conversion, Transmission, Distribution, Storage, Delivery, Consumption, Balancing, Measurement, Flow Attribution, Efficiency, Reliability, Environmental Impacts, Energy Claims, and Future Energy Systems

This document serves as the official OOF® Energy Governance architecture map and structural index used for Energy Governance design, Physical Energy Lifecycle navigation, energy governance diagnostics, architecture development, Energy Governance assessment, energy system state analysis, energy measurement and reconciliation, physical flow attribution, efficiency and loss governance, supply reliability, environmental impact assessment, energy claims and disclosure governance, historical continuity, and interoperability across physical energy systems and future energy environments.

EGA™ consists of 13 Parent Standards and 65 Core Modules.

Additional standards and modules may be added when new governable Physical Energy Lifecycle spaces are identified. However, the standards and modules listed below form the core structural backbone of EGA™.

Architectural Position

EGA™ governs the Physical Energy Lifecycle from Energy Source & Origin through Generation, Conversion, Transmission, Distribution, Storage, Delivery, and Consumption, with System Balancing, Measurement & Reconciliation, Flow Attribution, Efficiency & Loss, Supply Reliability, Environmental Impact, and Claims & Disclosure operating as applicable cross-cutting governance spaces.

The primary physical lifecycle is represented by:

Energy Source & Origin → Energy Generation → Energy Conversion → Energy Transmission & Distribution ↔ Energy Storage → Energy Delivery & Consumption

Cross-Cutting Governance: Energy System Balancing · Energy Measurement & Reconciliation · Energy Flow Attribution · Energy Efficiency & Loss · Energy Supply Reliability · Energy Environmental Impact · Energy Claims & Disclosure

This representation is a governance reference, not a mandatory physical sequence.

Energy may be converted multiple times, stored before or after transmission, distributed through different networks, recovered, reused, or consumed without passing through every illustrated stage.

Together these standards govern what physical energy source exists, where its origin and relevant properties are established, how energy is generated and converted, how it is transported and stored, what is delivered and consumed, how physical supply and demand remain balanced, how energy quantities are measured and reconciled, how flows and attributes are allocated, how efficiency and losses are assessed, how supply reliability is established, how environmental impacts are evaluated, and whether energy-related claims and disclosures are sufficiently substantiated.

The applicable governance progression may be expressed as:

Source Identification & Origin Assessment → Generation Capability & Performance → Conversion Input-Output Accountability → Transport Infrastructure & Flow Governance → Storage and/or Delivery & Consumption → Applicable Cross-Cutting Assessments → Evidence-Supported Governance Determinations

Energy generation does not establish that energy was delivered.

Energy Generated ≠ Energy Delivered.

Energy delivered does not establish that energy was consumed in the manner claimed.

Energy Delivered ≠ Verified Energy Consumption.

Energy measured does not automatically establish its physical origin or contractual attribution.

Energy Measured ≠ Energy Origin Verified.

Where physical configurations, source characteristics, generation conditions, conversion performance, network flows, storage conditions, consumption, balancing requirements, or supporting evidence materially change:

Governed Energy Object → Material Change Identification → Impact Assessment → Earliest Materially Affected EGA™ Governance Space → Applicable Reassessment → Updated Governance Determination → Preserved Historical Continuity

Where measurement inconsistencies arise:

Measurement Identification → Data Integrity Assessment → Reconciliation → Discrepancy Classification → Correction or Unresolved Exception → Updated Measurement Determination

Where an energy-related claim becomes unsupported:

Claim Identification → Evidence Reassessment → Substantiation Deficiency → Correction, Qualification, Withdrawal, or Other Applicable Disposition → Historical Claim Continuity

Where a material reliability deficiency is identified:

Reliability Assessment → Availability or Continuity Deficiency → Applicable Corrective Governance → Renewed Reliability Assessment → Updated Reliability State

This recursive structure allows EGA™ to remain responsive to changing physical energy systems, measurement corrections, attribution changes, energy losses, environmental evidence, reliability conditions, and published claims.

Re-enter the earliest materially affected governance space.

Reassess what materially changed. Preserve what remains valid.

Energy governance reassessment must preserve physical object identity, evidence provenance, and historical traceability.

Cross-Architecture Boundary

EGA™ ↔ GOA™

GOA™ governs the constitutional structure, authority, boundaries, delegation, validation, and continuity of governance. EGA™ governs physical energy lifecycle conditions and determinations under applicable governance requirements and legitimate authority.

General Governance Authority ≠ Energy Governance Authorization.

EGA™ ↔ ORA™

ORA™ governs what actually exists and occurs in Operational Reality. EGA™ governs the energy-specific identities, conditions, measurements, relationships, and assessments applicable to that reality.

Declared Energy State ≠ Actual Physical Energy State.

EGA™ ↔ AGA™

AGA™ governs responsibility, accountability, attribution of responsibility, authority, and related accountability relationships. EGA™ governs energy-specific responsibilities and decision conditions within its physical lifecycle scope.

Energy Responsibility Assigned ≠ Energy Requirement Satisfied.

EGA™ ↔ ADIT®

ADIT® governs evidential observation, preservation, correlation, verification, examination, and auditability. EGA™ governs the energy-specific requirements, assessments, and determinations to which relevant evidence applies.

Energy Evidence Recorded ≠ Energy Claim Substantiated.

EGA™ ↔ VALIDOS™

VALIDOS™ governs whether a validation basis sufficiently supports a Validation Determination. EGA™ governs energy-specific requirements and decisions that may depend on validated information.

Validation Determination ≠ Energy System Authorization.

EGA™ ↔ INTEGROS®

INTEGROS® governs integrity across objects, states, evidence, and processes. EGA™ governs the physical energy lifecycle and its domain-specific integrity requirements.

General Integrity Governance ≠ Physical Energy Governance.

EGA™ ↔ OBIDENITY®

OBIDENITY® governs origin, identity, ownership, authority, permission, responsibility, provenance, transfer, continuity, and auditability. EGA™ governs energy source, asset, process, flow, measurement, attribution, and claim relationships.

Energy Source Identity ≠ Energy Flow Identity.

Energy Ownership Claim ≠ Verified Physical Energy Origin.

EGA™ ↔ AIG®

AIG® governs AI behavioral governance and behavioral integrity. EGA™ governs physical energy conditions and assessments, including those supported by AI-enabled energy systems.

AI Energy Prediction ≠ Verified Physical Energy Measurement.

EGA™ ↔ ASGA™

ASGA™ governs autonomous-system authority, execution, constraints, coordination, intervention, escalation, and operational governability. EGA™ governs energy-specific conditions and requirements applicable to autonomous energy operations.

Autonomous Energy Control Capability ≠ Legitimate Operational Authority.

EGA™ ↔ SIMULOS®

SIMULOS® governs simulation purpose, structure, execution, outputs, and lifecycle. EGA™ governs actual physical energy systems and the energy-specific interpretation of modeled or simulated information.

Simulated Energy Flow ≠ Measured Physical Energy Flow.

Simulated Reliability ≠ Verified Supply Reliability.

EGA™ ↔ SLGA™

SLGA™ governs the lifecycle of software products, components, configurations, releases, deployments, changes, and retirement. EGA™ governs physical energy systems, including energy systems monitored or controlled by software.

Software Lifecycle Verification ≠ Physical Energy Performance Verification.

EGA™ ↔ Energy Engineering Systems

EGA™ governs the methodological conditions for physical energy lifecycle governance. Energy engineering systems perform technical design, construction, operation, optimization, maintenance, protection, and physical intervention.

Energy Engineering Execution ≠ Energy Governance Completion.

Energy System Operating ≠ Energy System Governed.

EGA™ ↔ Energy Markets & Contractual Systems

EGA™ governs physical energy quantities, source relationships, attribution conditions, and substantiation of energy-related claims. Energy markets and contractual systems govern transactions, commercial rights, contractual deliveries, financial settlements, and market participation under their applicable rules.

Contractual Energy Allocation ≠ Physical Energy Pathway.

Energy Certificate ≠ Physical Delivery Proof by default.

Physical Energy Identity & Configuration Structure

EGA™ does not assume that one energy system corresponds to one source, one generation asset, one energy carrier, one network pathway, one storage condition, or one consumption state.

A single energy system may contain:

multiple energy sources

multiple feedstocks

multiple generation assets

multiple conversion processes

multiple energy carriers

multiple transmission pathways

multiple distribution networks

multiple storage assets

multiple delivery points

multiple consumption points

multiple measurement systems

multiple balancing regions

multiple attribution and allocation relationships

multiple environmental impact pathways

multiple reliability conditions

multiple claims and disclosure records

or:

multiple independently changing physical energy subsystems

Each object may possess distinct physical, operational, measured, calculated, attributed, and historical attributes while remaining connected to the wider energy system.

Therefore:

Energy Source ≠ Energy Generation Asset.

Generation Asset ≠ Generated Energy Quantity.

Energy Carrier ≠ Energy Source Origin.

Physical Flow Pathway ≠ Contractual Delivery Pathway.

Measured Quantity ≠ Calculated Quantity.

Calculated Quantity ≠ Verified Quantity.

Energy Storage Capacity ≠ Stored Energy Quantity.

Stored Energy Quantity ≠ Usable Discharge Quantity.

Energy Delivered ≠ Energy Consumed.

Energy Allocation ≠ Physical Energy Transfer.

One Energy System ≠ One Operational State.

Evidence, Authority, and Historical Continuity

EGA™ does not require every material change to trigger reassessment of every Parent Standard.

The applicable change path is:

Material Change Identification → Impact Assessment → Earliest Materially Affected Governance Space → Applicable Reassessment → Verification of Changed Conditions → Updated Determination → Historical Continuity Preservation

A change to a generation asset may require reassessment of generation capability, conversion performance, measurement, reliability, or environmental impacts.

A changed meter may require renewed measurement integrity assessment and reconciliation without automatically invalidating unrelated physical source records.

A changed allocation method may require renewed attribution and disclosure assessment without implying that physical energy flows have changed.

A changed environmental factor may affect impact assessments and claims without requiring a new generation asset identity.

Energy System Change ≠ Complete Governance Restart by default.

Physical Change ≠ Measurement Change by default.

Measurement Correction ≠ Physical Energy Reversal.

Attribution Change ≠ Physical Flow Change by default.

Reassessment ≠ New Energy Source Origin by default.

They are not mandatory sequential physical stages after energy consumption.

→ governs environmental pressures, impact pathways, attribution, assessment, verification, and historical environmental continuity.

→ governs energy claim identification, substantiation, attribution, disclosure integrity, verification, corrections, and claim continuity.

Energy Measurement, Attribution & Evidence Structure

Physical energy governance requires explicit separation between physical reality and the information used to describe it.

An energy quantity may be:

physically generated

physically converted

physically transmitted

physically stored

physically delivered

physically consumed

directly measured

indirectly measured

calculated

estimated

modeled

reconciled

allocated

contractually attributed

or:

represented in a published claim

These are not interchangeable evidential states.

A meter may establish a measured quantity within a defined boundary and uncertainty.

A model may estimate an unmeasured quantity.

A reconciliation may identify discrepancies between available records.

An allocation method may assign quantities or attributes to participants.

A contractual certificate may establish rights or accounting attributes under applicable rules.

None of these mechanisms automatically establishes the complete physical pathway or origin of the energy concerned.

Therefore:

Physical Energy Flow ≠ Energy Accounting Entry.

Meter Reading ≠ Complete Energy Balance.

Measured Quantity ≠ Uncertainty-Free Quantity.

Estimated Quantity ≠ Directly Measured Quantity.

Reconciled Quantity ≠ Independently Verified Physical Origin.

Contractual Attribution ≠ Physical Flow Attribution.

Verification Performed ≠ Independent Assurance Completed.

Independent Assurance ≠ Regulatory Authorization.

Energy Efficiency, Reliability & Environmental Structure

EGA™ distinguishes energy efficiency, supply reliability, and environmental impact as separate governance spaces.

An energy system may operate efficiently while remaining unreliable.

An energy system may provide reliable supply while producing substantial environmental pressures.

An energy system may reduce energy consumed per unit of output while increasing its total energy consumption.

An environmental improvement claim may be based on modeled assumptions rather than directly observed environmental outcomes.

Therefore:

Energy Efficiency ≠ Energy Supply Reliability.

Energy Efficiency ≠ Environmental Sustainability.

Relative Efficiency Improvement ≠ Absolute Energy Reduction.

Installed Capacity ≠ Available Capacity.

Available Capacity ≠ Delivered Energy.

Supply Adequacy ≠ Uninterrupted Supply.

Environmental Pressure ≠ Demonstrated Environmental Impact.

Modeled Environmental Benefit ≠ Verified Environmental Outcome.

These distinctions preserve the independence of the relevant EGA™ assessments while allowing their evidence and findings to inform one another.

Energy Claims, Disclosure & Historical Continuity Structure

Energy claims may concern:

Source Origin

Generation Method

Renewable Attributes

Energy Quantity

Energy Delivery

Energy Consumption

Energy Efficiency

Energy Loss Reduction

Supply Reliability

Environmental Performance

Emissions

Resource Use

Certification or Assurance Status

or:

Historical Energy Performance

A claim may rely on physical measurements, calculations, allocation methodologies, contractual instruments, environmental assessments, or independent verification.

The claim must remain distinguishable from the underlying evidence and from the authority of the organization issuing it.

A later correction must not silently replace the original historical claim.

EGA™ therefore preserves the distinction between:

the time at which a physical condition existed

the time at which supporting evidence became available

the time at which an assessment was issued

the time at which a claim was published

and:

the time at which a correction or reassessment occurred

Therefore:

Claim Issued ≠ Claim Verified.

Certificate Issued ≠ Physical Energy Origin Proven by default.

Evidence Available Today ≠ Evidence Available at Historical Decision Time.

Historical Reassessment ≠ Original Historical Determination.

Claim Corrected ≠ Original Claim Erased.

Disclosure Completed ≠ Disclosure Integrity Established.

Canonical Principle

EGA™ does not prescribe energy engineering methods, replace energy system operators, perform physical energy generation, conversion, transmission, distribution, storage, delivery, or consumption, independently authorize energy operations, execute market transactions, establish contractual ownership rights, perform independent certification, replace specialized environmental or safety requirements, or substitute for general evidence, validation, identity, accountability, integrity, regulatory, technical, or operational decision systems. EGA™ governs the methodological conditions under which physical energy sources, generation assets, conversion processes, transport infrastructure, storage systems, delivery and consumption relationships, balancing conditions, measurements, reconciliations, flow attributions, efficiency and loss assessments, supply reliability, environmental impacts, and energy claims remain identifiable, distinguishable, assessable, evidence-supported, change-controlled, and historically traceable.

Physical energy systems may involve multiple energy sources, conversion stages, network pathways, storage configurations, delivery points, consumption processes, measurement methods, allocation arrangements, environmental conditions, and contractual claims. These elements may change independently, operate concurrently, or require targeted reassessment. EGA™ provides the governance architecture for those relationships while preserving the distinction between physical reality, measurement, calculation, attribution, verification, assurance, disclosure, and legitimate authority.

EGA™ governs Physical Energy from Source & Origin through Generation, Conversion, Transmission, Distribution, Storage, Delivery, and Consumption — with cross-cutting governance of Balancing, Measurement, Attribution, Efficiency, Reliability, Environmental Impact, and Claims across the complete energy lifecycle.
