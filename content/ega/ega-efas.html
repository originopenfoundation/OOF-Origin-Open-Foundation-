1. Canonical Definition

Energy Flow Attribution Standard (EFAS) is the ninth Parent Standard within EGA™ — Energy Governance

Architecture.

EFAS establishes the methodological governance requirements for attributing physical energy flows, quantities,

sources, pathways, storage relationships, conversion processes, and destinations across interconnected energy

systems.

It governs the conditions under which an energy quantity may be associated with an identifiable origin, physical

transfer relationship, intermediate process, allocation method, or consumption destination.

EFAS recognizes that physical energy flow attribution is not equivalent to contractual ownership, commercial

settlement, certificate allocation, or the existence of an energy purchase agreement.

An electricity consumer may purchase energy associated with a particular generator without receiving an individually

identifiable physical stream from that generator.

A storage system may receive energy from several sources before discharging energy into a shared network.

A conversion facility may transform one energy carrier into another while introducing losses and changing the physical

form of energy.

A distribution network may combine energy from multiple sources, making exact source-to-consumer attribution

physically indeterminate without additional assumptions or a defined allocation methodology.

A measurement system may accurately record energy entering and leaving a network while remaining unable to

establish the exact origin of every delivered energy unit.

EFAS therefore distinguishes:

Physical Flow Evidence

Physical Source Attribution

Methodological Allocation

Contractual Attribution

Certificate-Based Attribution

Unresolved Attribution

These categories must not be treated as interchangeable.

EFAS establishes the methodological conditions for recognizing, limiting, allocating, or rejecting energy attribution

claims according to physical evidence, topology, time, carrier characteristics, transformation history, uncertainty, and

the applicable attribution model.

Its principal governance output is the Energy Flow Attribution Governance Profile.

3. Core Question

How can energy quantities be attributed to their physical sources, transfer pathways, conversion processes,

storage histories, and destinations without confusing actual physical flow evidence with model-based

allocation, commercial arrangements, certificates, or unsupported origin claims?

4. Foundational Principle

Energy flow attribution must be governed according to physically supported relationships, explicit attribution

boundaries, applicable operating intervals, energy transformations, measurement evidence, and documented

allocation methods—not merely contractual association, numerical matching, or declared origin.

Contractual Energy Association ≠ Verified Physical Energy Flow.

Energy Certificate ≠ Physical Pathway Evidence.

Measured Delivery ≠ Verified Source Attribution.

Allocated Energy ≠ Individually Traced Energy.

Energy Mixing ≠ Preservation of Individually Identifiable Source Units.

Numerical Allocation Balance ≠ Physical Causation Proof.

8. Physical Attribution Versus Contractual Attribution

Physical attribution concerns evidence-supported relationships between physical energy sources, transfers, and

destinations.

Contractual attribution concerns rights, obligations, or commercial associations established through agreements.

A contractual relationship may exist without demonstrating an individually traceable physical flow.

EFAS requires these relationships to remain separately classified.

9. Physical Attribution Versus Certificate Attribution

Energy certificates may represent specified energy attributes or accounting claims under an applicable scheme.

They do not automatically demonstrate the physical pathway taken by energy delivered to a particular consumer.

EFAS must preserve the distinction between:

Physical Flow Evidence

and:

Certificate-Based Attribute Allocation

The validity of a certificate under its own scheme does not independently establish physical source-to-destination

tracing.

10. Physical Flow Versus Allocation

Physical flow concerns energy transfer governed by the characteristics of the physical system.

Allocation assigns quantities or attributes according to a defined methodology.

An allocation may be methodologically valid without representing a uniquely identifiable physical pathway.

EFAS requires the allocation basis to remain explicit.

45. Double-Counting Prevention

46. Physical Versus Attribute Double Counting

EFAS governs the physical attribution and allocation consistency of energy quantities.

Certificate issuance, transfer, cancellation, and attribute exclusivity may depend on external scheme rules.

EFAS must preserve the distinction between these governance domains.

55. Attribution Confidence Classification

EFAS applies the EGA™ confidence framework.

Confirmed — Sufficient applicable evidence supports the specific attribution conclusion.

Reconciled — The conclusion is supported through justified evidence reconciliation.

Estimated — The attribution relies materially on explicit allocation assumptions, estimation, or modeling.

Conflicted — Material evidence supports incompatible attribution conclusions.

Unknown — Available evidence is insufficient.

A confirmed methodological allocation must not be mislabeled as confirmed direct physical tracing.

Confidence and attribution classification must remain separate.

56. Governance Determination

EFAS supports:

PASS — Applicable attribution governance requirements are satisfied.

CONDITIONAL — Attribution is supported subject to explicit limitations.

FAIL — One or more mandatory attribution requirements are not satisfied.

INDETERMINATE — Evidence is insufficient or materially conflicting.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed requirement does not apply.

A PASS establishes conformity with the defined attribution methodology and evidence requirements.

It does not automatically establish direct physical source-to-destination tracing.

69. Attribution Quantity Accounting

For a defined attribution boundary, the total attributed quantity must not exceed the physically supported quantity

available for attribution under the applicable accounting method.

A simplified relationship is:

Available Energy = Attributed Energy + Unattributed Energy

This relationship applies only after the relevant physical accounting, losses, storage changes, and boundary

conditions have been addressed.

It does not independently establish a physical source-to-destination pathway.

1. Purpose of the Standard

The Energy Flow Attribution Standard (EFAS) establishes a methodological framework for determining how

physical energy quantities may be associated with identifiable sources, transfer pathways, conversion processes,

storage systems, and destinations.

Its purpose is to prevent unsupported attribution conclusions arising from incomplete physical evidence, ambiguous

energy pathways, numerical allocations, or contractual relationships.

EFAS governs not only whether an energy quantity has been attributed, but also what that attribution actually

demonstrates.

A measured energy delivery, a model-based source allocation, and a contractual energy purchase represent different

evidentiary relationships.

EFAS requires those relationships to remain distinguishable.

2. Why EFAS Is Necessary

Modern energy systems increasingly combine multiple generation sources, interconnected networks, storage facilities,

conversion technologies, and distributed consumption.

This complexity creates a fundamental attribution problem.

A consumer may receive a measured quantity of electricity without being able to identify the exact physical source of

every delivered unit.

A battery may store energy received from several generators.

A hydrogen production facility may consume electricity associated with multiple sources.

An energy supplier may allocate renewable attributes through certificates without establishing a dedicated physical

delivery pathway.

These situations require a methodology that separates physical flow evidence from attribution conventions.

EFAS provides that distinction.

3. Core Governance Problem

The central problem addressed by EFAS is the possibility of presenting an energy attribution relationship as physically

established when the available evidence supports only a contractual association, a model, or an allocation.

The existence of a numerical attribution does not independently demonstrate the physical origin of energy delivered to

a destination.

Similarly, the existence of a physically connected pathway does not prove the actual flow magnitude or source

contribution.

EFAS requires every material attribution to identify its evidentiary basis.

4. Core Question

How can energy quantities be attributed to their physical sources, transfer pathways, conversion processes,

storage histories, and destinations without confusing actual physical flow evidence with model-based

allocation, commercial arrangements, certificates, or unsupported origin claims?

5. Foundational Principle

Energy flow attribution must be governed according to physically supported relationships, explicit attribution

boundaries, applicable operating intervals, energy transformations, measurement evidence, and documented

allocation methods—not merely contractual association, numerical matching, or declared origin.

7. Physical Flow Attribution

Physical flow attribution concerns the relationship between an identifiable energy source, a physical transfer process,

and a destination.

The strength of an attribution conclusion depends on the available evidence.

A dedicated physical connection may support a stronger source-to-destination attribution than a shared network

containing numerous sources and withdrawals.

However, connectivity alone does not establish actual flow.

EFAS requires the physical pathway, operating interval, relevant measurements, and material alternative inputs to be

evaluated.

8. Attribution in Interconnected Networks

Interconnected energy networks create attribution limitations.

In an electricity network, multiple generators inject energy while consumers withdraw energy from the shared system.

The network's physical behavior is governed by electrical conditions rather than contractual source-to-customer

assignments.

A consumer may therefore have a valid commercial relationship with a particular generator without possessing

evidence of a uniquely traceable physical energy stream from that generator.

EFAS permits defined allocation methodologies while requiring their limitations to remain explicit.

9. Energy Mixing and Source Identity

Energy mixing may occur in electricity networks, gas infrastructure, thermal distribution systems, and shared storage

facilities.

Once energy from several sources enters a common system, source-specific physical attribution may become limited.

EFAS distinguishes identifiable source contributions from individually traceable physical units.

It does not assume that a source identity remains physically recoverable after mixing.

Where direct attribution is unavailable, an explicit allocation method may be used without representing the result as

direct physical tracing.

10. Storage-Mediated Attribution

A storage system may receive energy from different sources during different intervals and discharge energy later.

Attributing discharged energy to particular charging sources may require a documented allocation model.

Examples include:

Electricity → Heat

Electricity → Hydrogen

EFAS requires source attribution across conversion to preserve the relevant input-output relationship.

Conversion losses, measurement boundaries, and applicable operating intervals must be considered.

The output of a conversion process must not be treated as physically identical to the input without accounting for the

transformation.

12. Attribution Methodologies

EFAS recognizes several attribution approaches.

Direct Physical Attribution is supported by sufficiently constrained physical transfer evidence.

Measurement-Supported Network Attribution uses relevant network and measurement evidence to establish

supported relationships.

Model-Based Attribution uses explicit models and assumptions.

Methodological Allocation assigns energy quantities according to defined rules.

Contractual Association identifies commercial relationships without independently establishing physical delivery.

Certificate-Based Attribute Allocation identifies claims governed by applicable certificate arrangements.

These classifications must remain separate.

13. Measurement and Reconciliation Dependencies

EFAS depends on reliable measurement and physical accounting evidence.

EMRS — Energy Measurement & Reconciliation Standard governs measurement identity, instrument integrity,

data acquisition, and reconciliation.

EFAS uses that evidence to evaluate attribution.

A valid energy measurement does not automatically establish source identity.

A reconciled network energy balance does not automatically demonstrate which generator supplied a particular

consumer.

The standards therefore address different governance questions.

14. Attribution Quantity Conservation

Energy attribution must remain consistent with physically supported available quantities.

A source quantity must not be allocated repeatedly in a manner that creates additional physical energy.

Storage charging and discharging must be represented without unsupported duplication.

Internal transfers must not be counted as new external supply.

EFAS requires attribution totals and unresolved quantities to remain explicit within the defined accounting scope.

15. Double-Counting Prevention

Double counting may arise when one energy quantity is attributed to multiple destinations or counted repeatedly

across interconnected processes.

It may also arise when a storage system's charging energy and subsequent discharge are treated as separate source

production.

EFAS requires the attribution method to identify and control such risks.

Physical quantity double counting must remain distinguishable from duplicate claims to contractual or certificate-

based energy attributes.

16. Attribution Uncertainty

Attribution uncertainty may result from incomplete measurements, shared network pathways, energy mixing, storage

histories, model assumptions, or unresolved physical accounting differences.

EFAS requires uncertainty to be represented according to its source.

A mathematically precise allocation does not necessarily establish a physically certain source relationship.

The standard therefore distinguishes measurement uncertainty, attribution method uncertainty, and governance

confidence.

19. Practical Use Case 2 — Mixed-Source Battery Storage Attribution

Situation

A battery storage system receives:

40 MWh from an identifiable solar generation source

60 MWh from a shared electricity network

The total charging input is:

100 MWh

After accounting for the storage process, the system delivers:

90 MWh

The operator attributes all 90 MWh of discharged energy to the solar source.

No source-specific physical segregation or justified allocation method is documented.

EFAS Assessment

EFAS identifies:

Solar Charging Contribution: 40 MWh

Shared Network Charging Contribution: 60 MWh

Total Charging Energy: 100 MWh

Discharged Energy: 90 MWh

Source-Specific Storage Attribution Method: Missing

The operator cannot justify attributing the entire discharged quantity to the solar source solely because solar energy

was one charging input.

Under an explicitly adopted proportional allocation with uniformly allocated losses, the illustrative result would be:

Solar-Allocated Discharge: 36 MWh

These quantities represent a methodological allocation, not proof that individual discharged energy units retained

identifiable source origins.

Governance Determination

FAIL — The Claimed Solar Attribution Is Unsupported by the Charging Evidence and an Explicit Allocation

Method.

Methodological Lesson

Energy stored from multiple sources cannot be attributed entirely to one source without a physically

supported relationship or justified allocation methodology.

1. Canonical Definition

FPAM — Flow Pathway Assessment Module is the second Core Module within Energy Flow Attribution Standard

(EFAS), operating under EGA™ — Energy Governance Architecture.

FPAM establishes the methodological governance requirements for evaluating the physical pathways through which

energy may be transferred, converted, stored, distributed, or delivered between identifiable sources and destinations.

It governs the distinction between a physically connected pathway, an operationally available pathway, an observed

energy transfer, a model-supported flow relationship, and an unsupported source-to-destination claim.

FPAM recognizes that physical connectivity does not independently establish actual energy flow.

A generator may be connected to a network without producing energy during the assessed interval.

A transmission connection may exist while a switching device prevents transfer.

An electricity network may contain several possible pathways without permitting a unique physical source-to-

consumer route to be established.

A battery may receive energy from multiple sources and later discharge into a shared system.

A conversion facility may transform an energy carrier while changing the physical conditions governing the

downstream transfer.

These situations require pathway assessments to distinguish physical possibility from observed operation and source-

specific attribution.

FPAM therefore establishes a structured assessment of network topology, physical interfaces, operating states,

transfer direction, conversion relationships, storage intermediation, energy mixing, measurement evidence, and

pathway uncertainty.

Its principal governance sequence is:

Identified Source & Destination → Applicable Physical Topology → Interface Connectivity → Operating-State

Assessment → Transfer Direction → Pathway Evidence → Mixing & Transformation Assessment →

Uncertainty → Pathway Determination

FPAM receives canonical source, destination, boundary, carrier, interface, and interval identities from EFAIM.

It provides evidence-supported pathway classifications to EAAM and AVAM, while ACCM preserves their historical

continuity.

Its principal governance output is the Physical Flow Pathway & Evidence Profile.

Estimated — The conclusion relies materially on modeling, inference, or reconstruction.

Conflicted — Material evidence supports incompatible pathway conclusions.

Unknown — Available evidence is insufficient.

Confidence must apply to the specific pathway claim.

67. Practical Use Case 1 — Connected Generator Without an Operational

Transfer Pathway

Situation

A solar generation facility is physically connected to an industrial facility through a dedicated electrical connection.

The connection has a rated transfer capability of:

5 MW

The industrial facility reports:

20 MWh of electricity consumption during a defined operating interval

The operator claims that the solar facility physically supplied the full 20 MWh.

However, the connection was isolated by an open switching device throughout the assessed interval.

The industrial facility received electricity through an alternative network connection.

FPAM Assessment

FPAM identifies:

Physical Connectivity: Established

Rated Transfer Capability: 5 MW

Operational Pathway Availability: Not Established

Switching State: Open

Observed Transfer Through Dedicated Connection: Not Supported

The dedicated connection existed physically but was unavailable for energy transfer during the assessed interval.

Its rated capacity does not establish actual delivery.

Governance Determination

FAIL — The Claimed Physical Transfer Pathway Was Not Operationally Available During the Assessed

Interval.

Methodological Lesson

A physical connection and rated transfer capability do not establish actual energy flow when operating-state

evidence contradicts the claimed transfer.

Situation

Three electricity generators inject power into an interconnected network.

The operator has measurements of generator injections and consumer withdrawals.

Consumer Withdrawals: Measured

Network Connectivity: Established

Multiple Physical Pathways: Present

Unique Source-Specific Physical Route: Not Established

The measured injections and withdrawals support network-level physical energy relationships.

They do not establish that electricity from one generator traveled exclusively along the claimed route to the selected

consumer.

A defined attribution or allocation methodology may subsequently be evaluated under EAAM.

Governance Determination

FAIL — A Unique Physical Source-to-Destination Route Is Claimed Without Sufficient Supporting Evidence.

Methodological Lesson

Measured network injections and withdrawals do not independently establish a uniquely traceable physical

72. Canonical Closing Statement

A physical energy pathway cannot be considered verified merely because a connection exists between an

energy source and a destination.

A generator may be connected to a network without operating.

1. Canonical Definition

EAAM — Energy Attribution & Allocation Module is the third Core Module within Energy Flow Attribution

Standard (EFAS), operating under EGA™ — Energy Governance Architecture.

EAAM establishes the methodological governance requirements for attributing physical energy quantities to

identifiable sources, transfer relationships, conversion processes, storage histories, and destinations.

It governs the distinction between directly supported physical attribution, measurement-supported attribution, model-

based attribution, methodological allocation, contractual association, certificate-based attribute allocation, and

unresolved attribution.

EAAM recognizes that assigning an energy quantity to a source does not independently demonstrate that the quantity

physically originated from that source or followed an individually identifiable pathway to its destination.

An interconnected electricity network may contain multiple simultaneous injections and withdrawals.

A shared thermal network may combine heat from several production facilities.

A storage system may receive energy from multiple sources before discharging energy during a later interval.

A conversion process may transform an energy carrier while introducing losses and changing the physical relationship

between input and output.

An allocation methodology may produce mathematically consistent source shares without establishing unique

physical source-to-destination tracing.

EAAM therefore requires attribution methods to be explicitly identified, physically bounded, temporally applicable,

reproducible, uncertainty-aware, and consistent with available energy quantities.

Its principal governance sequence is:

Identified Attribution Objects → Physical Pathway Evidence → Available Energy Quantity → Attribution

Method → Source & Destination Allocation → Loss & Storage Treatment → Residual Assessment →

Uncertainty → Attribution Determination

EAAM receives identity and boundary references from EFAIM and physical pathway evidence from FPAM.

It provides attribution and allocation records for verification under AVAM and historical preservation under ACCM.

Its principal governance output is the Energy Attribution & Allocation Profile.

8. Foundational Principle

Energy attribution and allocation must be governed by identifiable physical quantities, explicit source and

destination relationships, applicable operating intervals, documented methods, physical quantity constraints,

and supporting evidence—not merely contractual association, declared origin, or numerical assignment.

Allocated Energy ≠ Individually Traced Energy.

Methodological Consistency ≠ Physical Source Certainty.

Contractual Source Association ≠ Verified Physical Delivery.

Energy Mixing ≠ Preservation of Individually Identifiable Source Units.

Complete Allocation ≠ Complete Physical Evidence.

9. Attribution Versus Allocation

EAAM distinguishes attribution from allocation.

Attribution establishes an evidence-supported relationship between an energy quantity and an identified source,

process, or destination.

Allocation assigns an energy quantity according to an explicit methodological rule.

An allocation may support an attribution conclusion within a defined methodological scope.

However, an allocation does not automatically establish a directly observed physical transfer relationship.

23. Proportional Allocation

Proportional allocation assigns energy according to defined contribution shares.

For source contributions (E_i), the proportional share of source (i) is:

[ p_i = \frac{E_i}{\sum_j E_j} ]

Where:

(p_i) — Source allocation fraction.

(E_i) — Eligible energy contribution from source (i).

The denominator must represent the defined eligible source population.

The method must preserve its assumptions and limitations.

24. Proportional Quantity Assignment

For an eligible output quantity (E_{\text{out}}), proportional allocation may be expressed as:

[ A_i = p_i E_{\text{out}} ]

Where:

(A_i) — Quantity allocated to source (i).

(p_i) — Applicable source fraction.

(E_{\text{out}}) — Eligible output quantity after relevant physical accounting.

This equation describes a methodological allocation.

It does not independently establish direct physical tracing.

43. Attribution Quantity Conservation

For a defined attribution scope:

[ E_{\text{available}} = E_{\text{attributed}} + E_{\text{unattributed}} ]

Where:

(E_{\text{available}}) — Physically supported energy available for attribution.

(E_{\text{attributed}}) — Energy assigned under the applicable attribution method.

(E_{\text{unattributed}}) — Energy not assigned under that method.

This relationship applies after relevant physical accounting, losses, storage changes, and boundary adjustments.

It does not independently establish source-specific physical tracing.

58. Confidence Classification

EAAM applies the EGA™ confidence framework.

Confirmed — Sufficient applicable evidence supports the specific attribution conclusion.

Reconciled — The conclusion is supported through justified evidence reconciliation.

Estimated — The attribution relies materially on explicit allocation assumptions, modeling, or estimation.

Conflicted — Material evidence supports incompatible attribution conclusions.

Unknown — Available evidence is insufficient.

Confidence must apply to the actual attribution classification.

A confirmed calculation under a proportional allocation method must not be represented as confirmed direct physical

tracing.

NOT APPLICABLE — The assessed requirement does not apply.

68. Practical Use Case 1 — Mixed-Source Battery Discharge Allocation

Situation

A battery storage system receives:

40 MWh from Solar Generation Facility A

60 MWh from Shared Electricity Network B

The total charging input is:

100 MWh

After storage losses, the battery delivers:

90 MWh

The operator claims:

“All 90 MWh of discharged energy originated from the solar facility.”

No physically segregated storage inventory or source-specific discharge evidence supports this claim.

EAAM Assessment

EAAM identifies:

Solar Charging Contribution: 40 MWh

Network Charging Contribution: 60 MWh

Total Charging Input: 100 MWh

Eligible Discharged Energy: 90 MWh

Physical Source Mixing: Present

Direct Source-Specific Discharge Tracing: Not Established

If the operator explicitly adopts a proportional allocation method with uniformly allocated storage losses:

[ p_{\text{solar}}=\frac{40}{100}=0.40 ]

[ A_{\text{solar}}=0.40\times90=36\text{ MWh} ]

[ A_{\text{network}}=0.60\times90=54\text{ MWh} ]

The resulting allocation is:

Solar-Allocated Discharge: 36 MWh

Network-Allocated Discharge: 54 MWh

These quantities represent a methodological allocation rather than individually traced physical energy units.

Governance Determination

FAIL — The Original Claim Attributes More Discharged Energy to the Solar Source Than the Evidence and

Defined Allocation Method Support.

A separately documented proportional allocation may be assessed for conformity under the applicable requirements.

Methodological Lesson

Energy discharged from mixed-source storage must be attributed through a physically supported

relationship or an explicit allocation method, not by assigning the entire discharge to a preferred charging

source.

Simultaneous Physical Delivery

Situation

An industrial facility consumes:

1,000 MWh during the same year

The facility and generator are connected to an interconnected electricity network.

EAAM Assessment

Annual Numerical Matching: Established

Continuous Temporal Matching: Not Established

Unique Physical Source-to-Destination Tracing: Not Established

The annual quantities may support an allocation or contractual matching conclusion under a defined methodology.

They do not establish simultaneous physical delivery during every consumption interval.

Governance Determination

Methodological Lesson

Equal annual generation and consumption quantities do not establish simultaneous physical energy delivery

or unique source-to-destination tracing.

1. Canonical Definition

AVAM — Attribution Verification & Assurance Module is the fourth Core Module within Energy Flow Attribution

AVAM establishes the methodological requirements for verifying whether energy attribution and allocation conclusions

23. Source Quantity Verification

AVAM verifies that the quantity attributed to each source remains within its eligible physically supported contribution.

For source (i):

[ \sum_j A_{ij} \leq E_{i,\mathrm{eligible}} ]

Where:

(A_{ij}) — Energy attributed from source (i) to destination (j).

(E_{i,\mathrm{eligible}}) — Eligible source quantity within the defined scope.

The relationship must be interpreted according to applicable physical boundaries, storage treatment, and loss

accounting.

57. Assurance Confidence Classification

AVAM applies the EGA™ confidence framework.

Confirmed — Sufficient applicable evidence supports the specific verification conclusion.

Reconciled — The conclusion is supported through justified evidence reconciliation.

Estimated — The conclusion depends materially on explicit modeling, allocation assumptions, or reconstruction.

Conflicted — Material evidence supports incompatible conclusions.

Unknown — Available evidence is insufficient.

Confidence must be attached to the actual assurance statement.

A confirmed calculation does not automatically establish confirmed physical source tracing.

67. Practical Use Case 1 — Mathematically Correct Allocation With an

Unsupported Physical Origin Claim

Situation

An industrial facility consumes:

1,000 MWh of electricity during one year

A renewable generator produces:

1,000 MWh during the same year

A reporting system allocates the generator's annual production to the facility's annual consumption.

The calculation is correct:

[ 1{,}000\text{ MWh}=1{,}000\text{ MWh} ]

The facility claims:

“Every MWh physically consumed by our facility originated directly from this renewable generator.”

The generator and facility are connected through an interconnected electricity network.

No evidence establishes a unique physical route or simultaneous source-specific delivery during every consumption

interval.

AVAM Assessment

AVAM identifies:

Annual Quantity Matching: Verified

Calculation Reproducibility: Verified

Contractual or Methodological Association: Potentially Supported

Unique Physical Source-to-Destination Tracing: Not Established

Continuous Temporal Matching: Not Established

The numerical equality supports the calculation.

It does not independently establish the claimed physical source-to-destination relationship.

Governance Determination

FAIL — The Physical Origin Claim Exceeds the Evidence-Supported Attribution Scope.

A separately scoped annual allocation verification may receive a different determination.

Methodological Lesson

A mathematically correct allocation must not receive assurance of direct physical source tracing when the

available evidence supports only annual quantity matching.

68. Practical Use Case 2 — Double-Counted Energy Across Storage and

Consumption

Situation

A solar generation facility produces:

100 MWh

Of this quantity:

60 MWh is delivered directly to an industrial load

40 MWh is used to charge a battery

The battery later discharges:

36 MWh

The difference represents:

4 MWh of storage losses

A reporting system records:

100 MWh of Solar Generation

40 MWh of Battery Charging

36 MWh of Battery Discharge

It then reports:

176 MWh of Total Solar Energy Supplied

The reporting system adds generation, charging, and discharging quantities as though they were independent energy

production.

AVAM Assessment

AVAM identifies:

Solar Generation: 100 MWh

Direct Load Delivery: 60 MWh

Battery Charging: 40 MWh

Battery Discharge: 36 MWh

Storage Loss: 4 MWh

The physical accounting relationship is:

[ 100=60+36+4 ]

The 40 MWh used for battery charging is an internal transfer of previously generated energy.

The 36 MWh discharged from the battery is not additional generation.

The reported 176 MWh therefore duplicates physical quantities across different lifecycle stages.

Governance Determination

FAIL — The Reported Total Double Counts Energy Transfers and Exceeds the Physically Supported

Generation Quantity.

Methodological Lesson

Generation, storage charging, and storage discharging must not be added as independent energy production

quantities when they represent successive transfers of the same physical energy inventory.

72. Canonical Closing Statement

Energy attribution assurance must verify not only whether a calculation is correct, but whether the

conclusion drawn from that calculation is physically and evidentially justified.

A network energy balance may reconcile without establishing the origin of individual withdrawals.

A proportional allocation may be mathematically correct without preserving physically identifiable source units.

A storage system may deliver measured energy while its charging source history remains mixed.

A contractual purchase may associate energy with a generator without demonstrating direct physical delivery.

And a certificate may support an energy attribute claim without establishing the physical pathway followed by

delivered energy.

AVAM establishes the methodological structure required to verify these distinctions.

It evaluates physical identities, pathway evidence, attribution methods, eligible quantities, conservation relationships,

storage and conversion histories, temporal matching, double counting, uncertainty, and evidence sufficiency.

It provides the assurance foundation required before an attribution conclusion is accepted within a defined

governance scope.

AVAM ensures that energy attribution conclusions receive assurance only to the extent justified by their

physical evidence, methodological consistency, quantitative limits, temporal validity, and documented

uncertainty—not merely because their calculations balance, their contracts exist, or their declared origins

appear plausible.

ACCM establishes the methodological governance requirements for preserving the integrity, traceability, and historical

It governs the lifecycle of attribution identities, physical pathway references, allocation methods, source and

A consumer may move to a different delivery boundary.

41. Temporal Integrity Principle

ACCM distinguishes three mandatory temporal dimensions.

Physical Valid Time

When the physical energy relationship, configuration, quantity, or attribution condition applied.

Evidence Availability Time

When supporting evidence became available for assessment.

Assessment Issuance Time

When the governance determination was issued.

Physical Valid Time ≠ Evidence Availability Time ≠ Assessment Issuance Time.

These dimensions must not be silently collapsed.

67. Practical Use Case 1 — Historical Electricity Attribution Rewritten

After a Network Configuration Change

Situation

An industrial facility receives electricity through a distribution network.

During January, the facility's attribution assessment uses:

Network Topology Version 1

Source Population A + B

Attribution Method Version 1

January Measurement Evidence

The January attribution receives a documented governance determination.

In March, the distribution network is reconfigured.

A new generation source is connected.

The network operator updates its topology database to:

Network Topology Version 2

Source Population A + B + C

A reporting system subsequently recalculates January attribution using the March network topology and includes

Source C as though it had been connected during January.

The original January assessment is overwritten.

ACCM Assessment

ACCM identifies:

January Physical Configuration: Version 1

March Physical Configuration: Version 2

Source C January Eligibility: Not Supported

Historical Topology Substitution: Detected

Original January Attribution Record: Not Preserved

The March configuration must not be applied to January merely because it is the latest available topology.

The January assessment must remain linked to the physical configuration and evidence applicable to its historical

interval.

Governance Determination

FAIL — Historical Attribution Was Rewritten Using a Later Physical Configuration, and the Original

Assessment Was Not Preserved.

Methodological Lesson

A later network configuration must not silently replace the physical conditions or attribution conclusions

applicable to an earlier operating interval.

72. Canonical Closing Statement

Energy attribution cannot remain trustworthy over time if changes to physical systems, allocation methods,

measurements, or evidence silently alter the historical record.

A network may be reconfigured after an attribution assessment.

A generation source may become unavailable.

A storage system may receive energy from additional charging sources.

An allocation method may be revised.

A measurement error may be discovered months after the original assessment.

And new evidence may demonstrate that an earlier source attribution was incomplete or unsupported.

These events may justify new conclusions, but they do not justify erasing the conditions under which the original

conclusions were established.

ACCM establishes the methodological structure required to preserve attribution identity, physical configuration history,

method versions, original calculations, evidence availability, reassessment lineage, authorization, verification, and

governance determinations.

It provides the continuity foundation required for independent historical reconstruction and evidence-based attribution

governance.

ACCM ensures that energy attribution evolves through explicit, versioned, evidence-supported changes while

preserving the original physical, methodological, and governance history—rather than allowing later

information to silently rewrite what was previously known, calculated, verified, or determined.
