EGA™ serves as the official OOF® Physical Energy Lifecycle Governance reference map used to identify,

define, classify, navigate, govern, evaluate, and understand governable Physical Energy Lifecycle spaces

across energy resources, electricity systems, thermal systems, fuel-based energy systems, renewable and

non-renewable energy infrastructure, industrial facilities, transmission and distribution networks, storage

assets, distributed energy resources, integrated energy systems, and future energy technologies.

The purpose of EGA™ is not to prescribe energy engineering methodologies, replace physical energy

science, perform generation, conversion, transmission, distribution, storage, delivery, consumption,

balancing, measurement, or environmental assessment activities, independently grant operational authority,

replace specialized technical, environmental, safety, regulatory, contractual, or operational decision systems,

or substitute for general evidence, validation, integrity, identity, accountability, software lifecycle, AI

behavioral, or autonomous-system governance.

The purpose of EGA™ is to identify, define, separate, and map the governable spaces through which Energy

Source & Origin is established, Energy Generation is governed, Energy Conversion remains traceable,

Energy Transmission & Distribution remains identifiable and controlled, Energy Storage remains measurable

and governable, Energy Delivery & Consumption remains attributable, and cross-cutting Energy System

Balancing, Measurement & Reconciliation, Flow Attribution, Efficiency & Loss, Supply Reliability,

Environmental Impact, and Claims & Disclosure remain evidence-supported, bounded, historically

consistent, and verifiable.

Core Architectural Principle

Every Physical Energy Lifecycle Governance space answers one primary governance question.

No Physical Energy Lifecycle Governance space duplicates the primary purpose of another Physical Energy Lifecycle

Governance space.

Every Physical Energy Lifecycle Governance space has a defined applicability trigger, a defined operational purpose,

and a defined governance boundary.

Every Physical Energy Lifecycle Governance space becomes applicable when its own governance conditions arise

and the necessary evidence, physical system identity, or preceding inputs have been sufficiently established.

Physical Energy Lifecycle Governance spaces may operate concurrently, recur, overlap in physical infrastructure, or

require targeted reassessment.

Physical Energy Lifecycle Governance does not require every energy quantity to pass through every

architecture stage.

Energy System Balancing, Measurement & Reconciliation, Flow Attribution, Efficiency & Loss, Supply

Reliability, Environmental Impact, and Claims & Disclosure operate as cross-cutting governance spaces

rather than mandatory sequential operational stages after Energy Consumption.

Together, these thirteen Physical Energy Lifecycle Governance spaces form the official Energy Governance reference

map of EGA™.

1. Energy Source & Origin Standard (ESOS)

Governed Space: Energy Source Identity, Origin, Classification, Provenance & Continuity Governance

Core Question

How can the source, origin, identity, classification, provenance, and continuity of energy resources remain

identifiable, evidence-supported, and governable before and throughout their use in physical energy

systems?

Canonical Definition

Energy Source & Origin Standard (ESOS) defines the governance conditions under which energy sources and

resources are identified, their origins and classifications are established, relevant provenance evidence is preserved,

source relationships are distinguished from energy carriers and contractual attributes, and source continuity remains

traceable across changes in supply, ownership, configuration, and use.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an energy resource, source, or claimed origin requires identification or classification for a physical

energy lifecycle activity.

Ends for an individual source-origin determination when source identity, classification, provenance, applicable

boundaries, uncertainty, and supporting evidence have received a sufficiently governed disposition.

Continues whenever source provenance, classification, supply relationships, or historical identity materially change.

Energy Source Identified ≠ Energy Origin Verified.

Renewable Classification ≠ Zero Environmental Impact.

2. Energy Generation Standard (EGS)

Governed Space: Energy Generation Governance

Core Question

How can energy generation facilities, processes, inputs, outputs, operating conditions, performance, and

generation records remain identifiable, measurable, and governable throughout generation activities?

Canonical Definition

Energy Generation Standard (EGS) defines the governance conditions under which generation facilities and

processes remain identifiable, physical energy inputs and outputs are bounded, generation conditions and

performance are assessed, generation quantities are supported by applicable evidence, and generation-related

changes remain historically traceable.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an identifiable facility or process produces, or is intended to produce, an energy output through an

applicable generation process.

Ends for a defined generation assessment when the relevant facility, generation process, operating interval, physical

inputs, outputs, performance conditions, and evidence limitations have been sufficiently established.

Continues while generation activity, operating conditions, or generation-related obligations remain materially relevant.

Installed Generation Capacity ≠ Actual Generation.

Energy Generated ≠ Energy Delivered.

3. Energy Conversion Standard (ECS)

Governed Space: Energy Conversion Governance

Core Question

How can physical energy conversion processes remain identifiable, input-output traceable, performance-

assessable, and governable across changing technologies, operating conditions, and conversion

boundaries?

Canonical Definition

Energy Conversion Standard (ECS) defines the governance conditions under which energy transformations

between forms or carriers remain identifiable, conversion boundaries and input-output relationships are established,

applicable conservation relationships and losses are examined, conversion performance is assessed, and changes in

conversion conditions remain traceable.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an identifiable physical process transforms energy between relevant forms or carriers and requires

explicit conversion governance.

Ends for a defined conversion assessment when input quantities, output quantities, operating conditions, conversion

boundaries, losses, and applicable uncertainty have received a sufficiently governed disposition.

Remains applicable to repeated conversions and materially changed operating conditions.

Energy Input ≠ Useful Energy Output.

Conversion Performed ≠ Conversion Efficiency Verified.

4. Energy Transmission & Distribution Standard

(ETDS)

Governed Space: Energy Transmission & Distribution Governance

Core Question

How can energy transport networks, physical configurations, capacities, flows, operating conditions, losses,

changes, and historical continuity remain identifiable, controlled, and governable throughout energy

transmission and distribution?

Canonical Definition

Energy Transmission & Distribution Standard (ETDS) defines the governance conditions under which physical

energy transport networks, infrastructure components, network configurations, transport capacities, operating states,

flows, losses, infrastructure changes, and historical configuration continuity remain identifiable and evidence-

supported.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an identifiable physical network, transport path, or distribution infrastructure conveys energy between

relevant physical boundaries.

Ends for a defined transport assessment when the applicable network configuration, capacity, physical flow, operating

conditions, transport losses, and evidence limitations have been sufficiently established.

Continues through network reconfiguration, capacity changes, maintenance, operational transitions, and historical

reassessment.

Network Capacity ≠ Actual Physical Flow.

Network Configuration Recorded ≠ Operational Configuration Verified.

5. Energy Storage Standard (ESS)

Governed Space: Energy Storage Governance

Core Question

How can energy storage systems, stored quantities, charging and discharging, usable capacity, performance,

degradation, operating conditions, and continuity remain identifiable and governable throughout the storage

lifecycle?

Canonical Definition

Energy Storage Standard (ESS) defines the governance conditions under which storage assets and stored energy

states remain identifiable, charging and discharging relationships are traceable, rated and usable capacities are

distinguished, storage losses and performance are assessed, degradation and operating constraints are governed,

and historical storage continuity is preserved.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an identifiable system receives, retains, or releases energy through a storage process requiring explicit

governance.

Ends for a defined storage assessment when the relevant storage identity, state, charging or discharge quantities,

capacity, performance, operating constraints, and evidence limitations have been sufficiently established.

Continues through storage cycles, degradation, maintenance, configuration changes, and materially altered storage

capability.

Rated Storage Capacity ≠ Usable Storage Capacity.

Stored Energy ≠ Deliverable Energy under Every Condition.

6. Energy Delivery & Consumption Standard

(EDCS)

Governed Space: Energy Delivery & Consumption Governance

Core Question

How can delivered and consumed energy remain identifiable, measurable, attributable, and governable

across delivery points, consumers, energy services, operating conditions, and changing consumption

patterns?

Canonical Definition

Energy Delivery & Consumption Standard (EDCS) defines the governance conditions under which energy delivery

points, delivered quantities, consumption boundaries, consumers, energy services, operating conditions, and

consumption changes remain identifiable, measurable, attributable, and historically traceable.

Governance Boundary

Begins when energy reaches an identifiable delivery boundary or enters an identifiable consumption process requiring

explicit governance.

Ends for a defined delivery or consumption assessment when the relevant delivery point, consumer or consuming

process, energy quantity, assessment period, measurement basis, and evidence limitations have received a

sufficiently governed disposition.

Continues through changing delivery arrangements, consumption patterns, measurement conditions, and related

historical corrections.

Energy Contracted ≠ Energy Delivered.

Energy Delivered ≠ Energy Consumed.

7. Energy System Balancing Standard (ESBS)

Governed Space: Energy System Balancing Governance

Core Question

How can energy supply-demand balancing, reserve conditions, balancing actions, operating constraints, and

system stability relationships remain identifiable, evidence-supported, and governable across changing

energy system conditions?

Canonical Definition

Energy System Balancing Standard (ESBS) defines the governance conditions under which energy supply-demand

relationships, balancing requirements, reserve availability, balancing actions, operational constraints, and resulting

system balancing conditions remain identifiable, assessable, and traceable.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an energy system requires the assessment or governance of supply-demand equilibrium, balancing

capability, reserve conditions, or balancing actions.

Ends for an individual balancing assessment when the relevant balancing requirement, available capability, action,

operating constraints, evidence, and resulting determination have received a sufficiently governed disposition.

Operates continuously or recurrently wherever balancing conditions arise across the energy lifecycle.

Balancing Reserve Declared ≠ Balancing Reserve Available.

Balancing Action Executed ≠ System Stability Guaranteed.

8. Energy Measurement & Reconciliation

Standard (EMRS)

Governed Space: Energy Measurement & Reconciliation Governance

Core Question

How can energy measurements, calculated quantities, measurement boundaries, data quality, reconciliation

differences, uncertainty, and corrections remain identifiable, verifiable, and historically consistent?

Canonical Definition

Energy Measurement & Reconciliation Standard (EMRS) defines the governance conditions under which energy

measurement objects, measurement methods, instruments, quantities, data quality, calculated values, reconciliation

relationships, residual differences, uncertainty, corrections, and historical measurement continuity remain identifiable

and evidence-supported.

Governance Boundary

Begins when a physical energy quantity is measured, calculated, estimated, or reconciled for a governed energy

lifecycle purpose.

Ends for a defined measurement or reconciliation assessment when its quantity, measurement boundary, method,

data quality, reconciliation status, uncertainty, and applicable corrections have received a sufficiently governed

disposition.

Continues where measurement conditions, evidence, calibration status, reconciliation results, or historical records

materially change.

Energy Measured ≠ Energy Reconciled.

Measurement Validated ≠ All Derived Claims Validated.

9. Energy Flow Attribution Standard (EFAS)

Governed Space: Energy Flow Attribution Governance

Core Question

How can physical energy flows, modeled flows, accounting allocations, and attribution relationships remain

distinguishable, traceable, methodologically justified, and governable across complex energy systems?

Canonical Definition

Energy Flow Attribution Standard (EFAS) defines the governance conditions under which energy flows and their

attribution relationships remain identifiable, physical measurements are distinguished from modeled or inferred flows,

accounting allocations and contractual attributes are separately represented, and attribution conclusions remain

bounded by applicable evidence, methodology, and uncertainty.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an energy quantity, flow, origin, destination, or attribute is assigned or attributed to a physical system

object, actor, transaction, process, or reporting boundary.

Ends for a defined attribution assessment when the relevant attribution basis, physical or accounting classification,

method, evidence, exclusions, uncertainty, and resulting conclusion have been sufficiently established.

Continues where underlying flows, measurement evidence, accounting arrangements, or attribution methods

materially change.

Physical Energy Flow ≠ Accounting Allocation.

Contractual Energy Attribute ≠ Physical Electricity Delivery.

10. Energy Efficiency & Loss Standard (EELS)

Governed Space: Energy Efficiency & Loss Governance

Core Question

How can energy efficiency, useful output, energy losses, performance comparisons, operating conditions,

and improvement claims remain measurable, comparable, and governable without confusing relative

performance with absolute energy reduction?

Canonical Definition

Energy Efficiency & Loss Standard (EELS) defines the governance conditions under which energy inputs, useful

outputs, losses, efficiency indicators, performance baselines, functional units, operating conditions, and comparative

performance assessments remain identifiable, measurable, and methodologically comparable.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an energy process, system, service, or comparison requires efficiency or loss assessment.

Ends for a defined assessment when the relevant input-output boundaries, functional unit, baseline, operating

conditions, loss categories, uncertainty, and resulting efficiency or loss determination have received a sufficiently

governed disposition.

Continues where performance conditions, energy quantities, system configurations, or comparison assumptions

materially change.

Improved Energy Efficiency ≠ Reduced Absolute Energy Consumption.

Reduced Energy Losses ≠ Zero Energy Losses.

Energy Supply Reliability Standard (ESRS) defines the governance conditions under which energy supply reliability

Governance Boundary

Begins when an identifiable energy supply arrangement, system, service, or operating condition requires reliability

Ends for a defined reliability assessment when the applicable adequacy, availability, continuity, resilience, recovery,

Continues through material changes in demand, generation capability, network conditions, storage capability,

contingencies, and operational evidence.

Historical Reliability ≠ Guaranteed Future Availability.

Resilience Test Passed ≠ Universal Resilience.

12. Energy Environmental Impact Standard

(EEIS)

Governed Space: Energy Environmental Impact Governance

Core Question

How can environmental pressures, impact pathways, affected receptors, environmental assessments,

attribution, uncertainty, and environmental change remain identifiable, evidence-supported, and governable

throughout the physical energy lifecycle?

Canonical Definition

Energy Environmental Impact Standard (EEIS) defines the governance conditions under which environmental

pressures associated with energy activities are identified, pathways and affected receptors are examined,

environmental impacts are assessed and attributed within defined boundaries, evidence and uncertainty are

Governance Boundary

Begins when an identifiable physical energy activity, system, or lifecycle stage creates, may create, or is claimed to

affect an environmental pressure or impact requiring assessment.

Ends for a defined environmental assessment when its physical activity boundary, pressure, pathway, receptor, impact

method, attribution basis, evidence, uncertainty, and resulting determination have received a sufficiently governed

disposition.

Continues when energy activities, environmental conditions, assessment methods, or materially relevant evidence

change.

Environmental Pressure ≠ Environmental Impact.

Low Operational Emissions ≠ Low Lifecycle Emissions.

13. Energy Claims & Disclosure Standard

(ECDS)

Governed Space: Energy Claims, Substantiation, Attribution, Disclosure Integrity, Verification & Historical Continuity

Governance

Core Question

How can claims and disclosures about energy systems, energy origin, operational performance, reliability,

efficiency, environmental impacts, and sustainability-related characteristics be substantiated, verified,

communicated, and historically governed so that their wording and implied meaning remain consistent with

physical reality, accounting boundaries, evidence, uncertainty, and applicable methodological limitations?

Canonical Definition

Energy Claims & Disclosure Standard (ECDS) defines the governance conditions under which energy-related

claims are identified and classified, supporting physical and accounting evidence is attributed, disclosure wording and

implied meaning are examined, material omissions and uncertainty are governed, verification and assurance

conclusions remain bounded, and claim corrections and historical continuity are preserved.

Governance Boundary

Begins when an energy-related statement, representation, claim, label, disclosure, or communicated conclusion

requires explicit governance.

Ends for an individual claim or disclosure assessment when claim identity, supported meaning, evidence, attribution,

disclosure integrity, verification status, authorization conditions, and unresolved limitations have received a sufficiently

governed disposition.

Continues when the underlying physical reality, evidence, accounting arrangements, wording, communication context,

or verification status materially changes.

Claim Substantiated ≠ Disclosure Integrity Established.

Disclosure Published ≠ Independent Certification.

Complete Physical Energy Lifecycle

EGA™ does not treat Energy Generation, Energy Delivery, or Energy Consumption as the completion of all energy-

related governance.

The primary physical lifecycle is:

Energy Source & Origin → Generation → Conversion → Transmission & Distribution ↔ Storage → Delivery &

Consumption

The cross-cutting governance spaces are:

Balancing · Measurement & Reconciliation · Flow Attribution · Efficiency & Loss · Supply Reliability ·

Environmental Impact · Claims & Disclosure

An energy system may therefore remain subject to governance after an individual energy quantity has been

consumed.

A generation record establishes an identifiable generation event or quantity within its applicable scope.

It does not establish physical delivery to a particular consumer.

A delivery record establishes a delivery relationship.

It does not automatically establish actual consumption or useful energy service.

An environmental assessment establishes a bounded conclusion concerning applicable environmental conditions.

It does not automatically authorize an unrestricted environmental claim.

Energy Generated ≠ Energy Delivered.

Energy Delivered ≠ Energy Consumed.

Energy Consumed ≠ Environmental Consequences Resolved.

Environmental Assessment Completed ≠ Claim Governance Completed.

Recursive Physical Energy Lifecycle

EGA™ does not assume that physical energy governance progresses through its lifecycle only once.

Physical energy systems and their representations may continue to change:

Energy Sources may change.

Generation Conditions may change.

Conversion Processes may change.

Network Configurations may change.

Storage Capacity and Performance may change.

Delivery Arrangements may change.

Consumption Patterns may change.

Balancing Requirements may change.

Measurement Methods may change.

Reconciliation Results may change.

Flow Attribution may change.

Efficiency and Loss Conditions may change.

Reliability Conditions may change.

Environmental Evidence may change.

Energy Claims and Disclosures may change.

The architecture therefore becomes recursive:

Governed Energy System → Material Change → Impact Assessment → Applicable Energy Governance

Reassessment → Updated Determination → Continued Lifecycle Governance

Where physical energy conditions change:

Existing Energy State → Material Physical Change → Affected EGA™ Governance Space → Reassessment →

Updated Energy State

Where measurement evidence changes:

Original Measurement → New Evidence or Correction → Measurement Reassessment → Dependent

Attribution / Performance / Environmental / Claim Reassessment where applicable

Where environmental conditions change:

Environmental Change → Affected Impact Pathway or Receptor → Environmental Reassessment → Updated

Impact Determination → Dependent Claim Review

Where a claim becomes unsupported:

Existing Claim → Material Evidence or Meaning Change → Claim Reassessment → Correction / Qualification /

Suspension / Withdrawal → Historical Preservation

A materially changed source or provenance relationship may require renewed governance within Energy Source &

Origin Standard.

A changed generation facility, operating condition, or generation record may require renewed governance within

Energy Generation Standard.

A changed conversion process, energy input-output relationship, or conversion efficiency condition may require

renewed governance within Energy Conversion Standard.

A changed transport network configuration, capacity, physical flow, or infrastructure continuity condition may require

renewed governance within Energy Transmission & Distribution Standard.

A changed storage state, usable capacity, degradation condition, or charging and discharge relationship may require

renewed governance within Energy Storage Standard.

A changed delivery boundary, consumer, consumption process, or consumption quantity may require renewed

governance within Energy Delivery & Consumption Standard.

A changed supply-demand relationship, balancing reserve, operating constraint, or balancing response may require

renewed governance within Energy System Balancing Standard.

A measurement correction, instrument deficiency, unresolved reconciliation difference, or revised uncertainty estimate

may require renewed governance within Energy Measurement & Reconciliation Standard.

A changed physical flow, modeled flow, accounting allocation, or attribution method may require renewed governance

within Energy Flow Attribution Standard.

A changed input-output boundary, efficiency baseline, operating condition, or loss calculation may require renewed

governance within Energy Efficiency & Loss Standard.

A changed supply adequacy condition, interruption, resilience capability, or recovery performance may require

renewed governance within Energy Supply Reliability Standard.

A new environmental pressure, changed pathway, affected receptor, impact method, or environmental evidence may

require renewed governance within Energy Environmental Impact Standard.

A changed claim, supporting evidence, accounting attribute, communication context, verification status, or disclosure

limitation may require renewed governance within Energy Claims & Disclosure Standard.

The architecture therefore supports targeted Physical Energy Governance Reassessment rather than

uncontrolled reconstruction of every energy lifecycle assessment.

Reassess what materially changed. Preserve what remains valid.

Governance reassessment must preserve physical identity, original evidence, and historical traceability.

Physical Energy Identity & Configuration

Structure

EGA™ does not assume that one energy system represents one stable source, energy carrier, configuration, quantity,

or operational state.

A single energy system may contain:

multiple Energy Sources

multiple Generation Facilities

multiple Conversion Processes

multiple Energy Carriers

multiple Transmission Paths

multiple Distribution Networks

multiple Storage Systems

multiple Delivery Points

multiple Consumers

multiple Measurement Systems

multiple Accounting Arrangements

multiple simultaneous Operating States

or:

a wider interconnected Physical Energy System.

Each physical energy object may possess its own identity, state, boundary, and historical continuity while remaining

connected to the broader system.

A generation facility may produce energy into several network paths.

Different storage assets may operate under different charge states and constraints.

One consumer may receive energy through multiple contractual and physical arrangements.

A single accounting attribute may be associated with a reporting boundary without identifying a unique physical

delivery path.

Therefore:

One Energy System ≠ One Energy Source.

One Energy Source ≠ One Energy Carrier.

One Generation Facility ≠ One Generated Quantity.

One Network ≠ One Physical Flow.

One Storage Asset ≠ One Constant Usable Capacity.

One Contract ≠ One Physical Energy Path.

Declared Operating State ≠ Verified Physical Operating State.

Architectural Position

EGA™ governs the complete Physical Energy Lifecycle from energy source identification through generation,

conversion, transmission, distribution, storage, delivery, and consumption, with cross-cutting governance of

balancing, measurement, attribution, efficiency, reliability, environmental impacts, and claims.

The architecture progresses through:

Energy Source & Origin → Energy Generation → Energy Conversion → Energy Transmission & Distribution

↔ Energy Storage → Energy Delivery & Consumption

Cross-Cutting Governance: Energy System Balancing · Energy Measurement & Reconciliation · Energy Flow

Attribution · Energy Efficiency & Loss · Energy Supply Reliability · Energy Environmental Impact · Energy

Claims & Disclosure

Together, these Physical Energy Lifecycle Governance spaces govern where energy originates, how it is generated

and transformed, how it moves through infrastructure, how it is stored, how it reaches consumers, how consumption is

established, how balancing conditions are governed, how measurements and reconciliations remain reliable, how

energy flows are attributed, how efficiency and losses are assessed, how supply reliability is evaluated, how

environmental impacts are established, and how resulting claims remain substantiated and appropriately

communicated.

EGA™ therefore provides a governance path from the first identification of what physical energy source or

resource is involved and what evidence establishes its origin to the determination of what physical energy

activities occurred, what quantities and impacts can be supported, and which conclusions may legitimately

be communicated.

Cross-Architecture Governance Boundaries

EGA™ ↔ GOA™ — Governance Architecture

GOA™ — Governance Architecture governs the constitutional structure, authority, boundaries, delegation,

validation, and continuity of governance.

EGA™ — Energy Governance Architecture governs physical energy lifecycle conditions under applicable

governance requirements and legitimate authority.

General Governance Authority ≠ Energy Operational Authorization.

EGA™ ↔ ORA™ — Operational Reality Architecture

ORA™ — Operational Reality Architecture governs what exists and occurs in actual Operational Reality.

EGA™ — Energy Governance Architecture governs the physical energy processes, quantities, flows, conditions,

and lifecycle relationships applicable to that reality.

Declared Energy State ≠ Actual Physical Energy State.

EGA™ ↔ AGA™ — Accountability Governance Architecture

AGA™ — Accountability Governance Architecture governs responsibility, accountability, attribution, authority, and

related accountability relationships.

EGA™ — Energy Governance Architecture governs energy lifecycle requirements and controls involving those

relationships.

Responsibility Assigned ≠ Physical Control Capability Established.

EGA™ ↔ ADIT® — Continuous Audit & Evidence Governance Architecture

ADIT® governs evidential observation, preservation, correlation, verification, examination, and auditability.

EGA™ governs physical energy conditions, measurements, assessments, and claims supported by relevant

evidence.

Evidence Recorded ≠ Energy Claim Substantiated.

EGA™ ↔ VALIDOS™ — Validation Governance Architecture

Canonical Principle

EGA™ does not prescribe energy engineering methods, replace physical energy science, operate energy

infrastructure, perform generation, conversion, transmission, storage, delivery, consumption, balancing,

measurement, environmental assessment, or verification activities itself, independently grant operational

authority, replace specialized technical requirements, govern general accountability, perform general

evidence governance, or substitute for contractual, regulatory, safety, environmental, or other domain-

specific decision systems. EGA™ governs the methodological conditions under which identifiable physical

energy systems, quantities, flows, transformations, operating states, measurements, attribution

relationships, efficiency and loss assessments, reliability conditions, environmental impacts, and associated

claims become bounded, evidence-supported, historically traceable, and governable.

Canonical Closing Statement

EGA™ serves as the official Physical Energy Lifecycle Governance reference map for energy sources,

generation, conversion, transmission, distribution, storage, delivery, consumption, balancing, measurement,

attribution, efficiency, reliability, environmental impacts, energy claims, and future energy systems. By

defining the thirteen primary governable spaces of the Physical Energy Lifecycle, EGA™ provides the

architecture used to establish energy source identity and provenance, govern generation and conversion,

preserve network configuration and physical flow traceability, govern storage and delivery, identify

consumption, assess balancing conditions, establish measurement and reconciliation integrity, distinguish

physical flow from accounting attribution, evaluate efficiency and losses, assess supply reliability, govern

environmental impact evidence, and preserve the substantiation, disclosure integrity, verification, and

historical continuity of energy claims. EGA™ therefore establishes Physical Energy Lifecycle Governance as

a distinct methodological layer around physical energy systems themselves, enabling energy activities,

quantities, flows, performance conditions, environmental consequences, and communicated claims to

remain identifiable, bounded, evidence-supported, historically traceable, and governable regardless of

whether energy systems are conventional, renewable, distributed, integrated, automated, AI-enabled,

autonomously controlled, or based on future energy technologies.

EGA™ governs Physical Energy from Source & Origin through Generation, Conversion, Transmission,

Storage, Delivery, and Consumption — with continuous governance of Balancing, Measurement, Attribution,

Efficiency, Reliability, Environmental Impact, and Claims throughout the entire lifecycle.
