Governed Space

Integrity Deviation

Core Question

When and how does integrity begin to deviate from its governed state?

Canonical Definition

Integrity Deviation Standard (IDS) defines the governance conditions required
to detect, classify, evaluate, and manage deviations from an approved
integrity state. It establishes the criteria, deviation models, severity
levels, and governance rules required to identify integrity deviations before
they develop into integrity breaches throughout the complete lifecycle of a
governed entity.

Governance Flow Position

Definition → Structure → Conditions → Measurement → State → Deviation → Breach
→ Response → Restoration → Assurance

Standard Abstract

Integrity rarely fails instantly.

In most governed systems, integrity gradually moves away from its approved
operational state through observable deviations that can be detected before a
complete integrity breach occurs.

Building upon the integrity states established by ISS, Integrity Deviation
Standard defines how departures from approved integrity conditions are
identified and governed.

IDS enables early detection, risk reduction, and proactive governance before
operational integrity is compromised.

Operational Role

Integrity Deviation Standard governs the identification and management of
integrity deviations. It establishes the governance framework required to
recognize, classify, monitor, and evaluate departures from approved integrity
states before escalation into integrity breaches.

Operational Space

IDS governs:

  • deviation detection
  • deviation classification
  • deviation severity
  • deviation analysis
  • deviation tracking
  • deviation reporting
  • deviation governance.


This governed space ensures that integrity deviations are consistently
identified and managed before they threaten operational integrity.

Why This Standard Exists

Governance requires the ability to recognize deterioration before failure
occurs.

Without a standardized deviation model, organizations cannot distinguish
between normal operational variation and the early signs of integrity
degradation.

IDS exists because Integrity Deviation represents the sixth governed space of
INTEGROS® — Integrity Governance Architecture, creating the governance layer
that separates operational integrity management from integrity breach
management.

Foundational Principle

Integrity breaches are rarely the beginning of failure. Most failures begin as
governed deviations that remain undetected or unmanaged.

Canonical Closing Statement

Integrity Deviation Standard establishes the canonical governance framework
for identifying, classifying, evaluating, and managing departures from
approved integrity states. By governing integrity deviations before they
evolve into integrity breaches, IDS enables proactive governance, early
intervention, and continuous preservation of integrity throughout the complete
lifecycle of every governed entity.
