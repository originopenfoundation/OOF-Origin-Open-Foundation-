Governed Space

Integrity Breach

Core Question

When does an integrity deviation become an integrity breach?

Canonical Definition

Integrity Breach Standard (IBS) defines the governance conditions required to
identify, verify, classify, assess, and govern integrity breaches. It
establishes the criteria, breach determination rules, evidence requirements,
severity models, and governance controls required to recognize when integrity
has been materially compromised throughout the complete lifecycle of a
governed entity.

Governance Flow Position

Definition → Structure → Conditions → Measurement → State → Deviation → Breach
→ Response → Restoration → Assurance

Standard Abstract

Not every integrity deviation results in an integrity breach.

A breach represents the point at which integrity is no longer considered
preserved according to approved governance conditions. Recognizing this
transition consistently is essential for effective governance, operational
resilience, regulatory compliance, and accountability.

Building upon the integrity deviations established by IDS, Integrity Breach
Standard defines when integrity has been materially compromised and breach
governance becomes necessary.

IBS transforms integrity failure from subjective interpretation into a
standardized governance determination supported by objective evidence.

Operational Role

Integrity Breach Standard governs the identification and management of
integrity breaches. It establishes the governance framework required to
determine when integrity has been materially violated, classify the breach,
evaluate its impact, and initiate formal breach governance procedures.

Operational Space

IBS governs:

  • breach identification
  • breach verification
  • breach classification
  • breach severity
  • breach evidence
  • breach documentation
  • breach governance.


This governed space ensures that integrity breaches are recognized,
documented, and governed consistently across all operational environments.

Why This Standard Exists

Governance requires a clear distinction between an integrity deviation and an
integrity breach.

Without standardized breach criteria, organizations cannot determine when
integrity has actually failed, when emergency governance should begin, or when
formal accountability mechanisms must be activated.

IBS exists because Integrity Breach represents the seventh governed space of
INTEGROS® — Integrity Governance Architecture, providing the canonical
governance framework for recognizing material integrity failures before
response and restoration activities begin.

Foundational Principle

An integrity breach is not the existence of a problem—it is the governed
determination that integrity has been materially compromised according to
approved governance conditions.

Canonical Closing Statement

Integrity Breach Standard establishes the canonical governance framework for
identifying, verifying, classifying, and governing material integrity
failures. By defining when integrity deviations become integrity breaches, IBS
enables objective breach determination, accountable governance, timely
intervention, and structured recovery throughout the complete lifecycle of
every governed entity.
