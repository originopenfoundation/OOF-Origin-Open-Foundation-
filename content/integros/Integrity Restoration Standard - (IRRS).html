Governed Space

Integrity Restoration

Core Question

How can integrity be restored after a verified integrity breach and governed
response?

Canonical Definition

Integrity Restoration Standard (IRRS) defines the governance conditions
required to restore integrity following a verified integrity breach and
completed response activities. It establishes the restoration principles,
recovery procedures, validation requirements, accountability mechanisms,
evidence requirements, and governance controls required to return a governed
entity to an approved state of integrity while ensuring that restoration is
objective, verifiable, and fully auditable throughout the complete lifecycle
of the governed entity.

Governance Flow Position

Definition → Structure → Conditions → Measurement → State → Deviation → Breach
→ Response → Restoration → Assurance

Standard Abstract

Responding to an integrity breach does not automatically restore integrity.

After containment and response, governance must ensure that integrity has been
objectively re-established through controlled restoration activities supported
by evidence, validation, and accountability.

Building upon the governed responses established by IRRS, Integrity
Restoration Standard defines how integrity is systematically restored before
long-term integrity assurance begins.

IRRS transforms operational recovery into a standardized governance process
that confirms integrity has been re-established according to approved
governance conditions.

Operational Role

Integrity Restoration Standard governs the restoration and recovery of
integrity following verified breaches. It establishes the governance framework
required to plan restoration activities, recover governed entities, validate
restored integrity, document recovery, and prepare the entity for continuous
integrity assurance.

Operational Space

IRRS governs:

  • restoration planning
  • restoration execution
  • recovery validation
  • integrity re-establishment
  • restoration evidence
  • restoration documentation
  • restoration governance.


This governed space ensures that integrity restoration is performed
consistently, objectively, and under approved governance across all
operational environments.

Why This Standard Exists

Containing an integrity breach is not equivalent to restoring integrity.

Without standardized restoration governance, organizations cannot objectively
demonstrate that integrity has been fully re-established, operational risks
have been eliminated, and governance conditions have been satisfied.

IRRS exists because Integrity Restoration represents the ninth governed space
of INTEGROS® — Integrity Governance Architecture, providing the canonical
governance framework for restoring integrity before continuous assurance
activities begin.

Foundational Principle

Integrity is not restored when the incident ends—it is restored when governed
evidence objectively demonstrates that approved integrity conditions have been
re-established.

Canonical Closing Statement

Integrity Restoration Standard establishes the canonical governance framework
for restoring integrity following verified breaches and governed responses. By
defining how integrity is objectively re-established, validated, documented,
and governed, IRRS enables consistent recovery, accountable restoration,
operational resilience, and structured transition to continuous integrity
assurance throughout the complete lifecycle of every governed entity.
