About Integrity Breach Standard - (IBS)

Determining When Integrity Has Been Materially Compromised

Not every integrity deviation constitutes an integrity breach. Governance
requires a clear and objective determination of the point at which integrity
is no longer considered preserved. This distinction enables consistent
governance decisions, timely intervention, regulatory compliance, and
organizational accountability.

What Is Integrity Breach Standard?

Integrity Breach Standard (IBS) is the seventh Parent Standard of INTEGROS® —
Integrity Governance Architecture. It establishes the canonical governance
conditions required to identify, verify, classify, assess, and govern
integrity breaches throughout the complete lifecycle of a governed entity.

Canonical Definition

Integrity Breach Standard (IBS) defines the governance conditions required to
identify, verify, classify, assess, and govern integrity breaches by
establishing breach determination criteria, evidence requirements, severity
models, and governance rules throughout the complete lifecycle of a governed
entity.

Why IBS Exists

Detecting integrity deviations is only the beginning of governance.

Organizations must also determine when a deviation has crossed the governance
threshold and become a material integrity breach requiring formal governance
action.

Building upon the integrity deviation framework established by IDS, IBS
defines the governance criteria used to determine when integrity has been
materially compromised.

This provides a standardized foundation for initiating incident management,
accountability processes, regulatory reporting, and recovery activities.

What IBS Governs

IBS governs the complete lifecycle of integrity breaches, including:

  • breach identification
  • breach verification
  • breach classification
  • breach severity assessment
  • breach evidence
  • breach documentation
  • breach governance.


This governed space ensures that integrity breaches are recognized, evaluated,
and governed consistently using objective evidence and standardized governance
rules.

Architectural Position

Within INTEGROS® — Integrity Governance Architecture, IBS is the seventh
Parent Standard in the governance flow:

Definition → Structure → Conditions → Measurement → State → Deviation → Breach
→ Response → Restoration → Assurance

IBS establishes the canonical transition from integrity deterioration to
formal integrity failure, creating the governance foundation upon which
response, restoration, and long-term assurance are built.

Canonical Closing Statement

Integrity Breach Standard establishes the canonical governance framework for
identifying, verifying, classifying, assessing, and governing material
integrity failures. By defining the point at which integrity is no longer
considered preserved, IBS enables objective breach determination, accountable
governance, structured response, and resilient recovery throughout the
complete lifecycle of every governed entity.
