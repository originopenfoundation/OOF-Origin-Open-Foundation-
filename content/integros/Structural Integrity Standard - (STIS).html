Governed Space

Structural Integrity

Core Question

Is the structure capable of preserving integrity?

Canonical Definition

Structural Integrity Standard (STIS) defines the structural conditions
required for a governed entity to preserve integrity throughout its complete
lifecycle. It governs the organization, relationships, dependencies, and
structural coherence necessary to ensure that integrity can be maintained
under normal operation, change, and evolution.

Structure determines whether integrity can endure.

STIS governs that structure.

Governance Flow Position

Definition → Structure → Conditions → Measurement → State → Deviation → Breach
→ Response → Restoration → Assurance

Standard Abstract

After integrity has been defined, the next governance question is whether the
underlying structure is capable of preserving it.

A well-defined integrity model cannot remain valid if the structure supporting
it is inconsistent, incomplete, or unstable.

STIS establishes the architectural foundations that enable integrity to
persist across changing operational environments.

It defines how components, relationships, dependencies, and organizational
structures contribute to—or undermine—the preservation of integrity.

Operational Role

Structural Integrity Standard governs the structural architecture required to
maintain integrity. It establishes the organizational framework that enables
integrity definitions to remain consistent, resilient, and governable as
systems evolve.

Operational Space

STIS governs:

  • structural integrity
  • structural organization
  • component integrity
  • relationship integrity
  • dependency integrity
  • structural coherence
  • integrity-preserving architecture.


This governed space ensures that integrity is supported by a structure capable
of sustaining it throughout the complete lifecycle of a governed entity.

Why This Standard Exists

Integrity cannot survive without a structure capable of preserving it.

Even when integrity is clearly defined, structural weaknesses may introduce
inconsistency, fragmentation, hidden dependencies, or systemic failure.

STIS exists because Structural Integrity represents the second governed space
of INTEGROS® — Integrity Governance Architecture, ensuring that integrity is
not only defined but also structurally sustainable.

Foundational Principle

Integrity depends not only on definition but also on structure. A governed
