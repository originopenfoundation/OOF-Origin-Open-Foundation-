Canonical Definition

The Liability Connection Relationship Module (LCXRM) governs how the relationship between the identified elements of each Liability Connection Candidate is explicitly structured, distinguished, bounded, directed, typed, and represented before those relationships are assembled into complete Liability Connection Chains.

LCXRM transforms an Identified Liability Connection Set into a governed set of explicit Liability Connection Relationships by determining what elements are related, the nature of their relationship, the direction in which that relationship operates where relevant, the scope and boundaries of the relationship, and how multiple or overlapping relationships remain distinct.

LCXRM does not determine the complete Connection Chain, Connection Qualification, evidentiary sufficiency, Liability Attachment, or Liability.

It governs the transition:

Identified Liability Connection Set → Relationship Formation → Relationship Type → Direction & Boundary → Relationship Distinction → Governed Liability Connection Relationship Set Its foundational distinction is:

Connection Candidate ≠ Governed Connection Relationship.

Operational Role

The operational role of LCXRM is:

to convert identified potential Connections into explicit governed relationships whose meaning, endpoints, direction, type, scope, and boundaries are sufficiently structured for subsequent Connection Chain governance.

LCXIM asks:

Which potential Connections require governance?

LCXRM asks:

What exactly is the governed relationship between the identified elements of each Connection Candidate?

LCXCM subsequently asks:

How do those governed Connection Relationships combine into complete Connection Chains?

The internal LCNS progression is:

Liability Connection Identification → Liability Connection Relationship → Liability Connection Chain → Liability Connection Qualification → Liability Connection Evidence Association LCXRM governs:

Central Operational Question

What is the governed relationship between the identified elements of each Liability Connection Candidate, and how must that relationship be structured so that it can form part of a Liability Connection Chain?

Connection Relationship

A Connection Relationship is an explicitly governed relationship between two or more identified elements within a potential Liability Connection structure.

It defines:

how the elements are related without determining:

what the complete Connection Chain establishes.

Therefore:

Connection Relationship ≠ Connection Chain.

Relationship Formation

Relationship Formation converts an identified Connection Candidate into an explicit relational structure.

The process should establish, where materially relevant:

the connected elements, relationship identity, relationship type, direction, scope, boundary, temporal relevance, and applicable associations.

Relationship Identity

Each materially distinct Connection Relationship should retain a stable governed identity.

Relationship Identity allows the same relationship to be:

referenced, combined into chains, qualified, corrected, reassessed, versioned, or historically reconstructed.

Therefore:

Connection Relationship Identity ≠ Endpoint Identity.

Relationship Identity and Candidate Identity

A Connection Candidate may become one governed Connection Relationship.

It may also reveal:

multiple relationships, a composite relationship, or no sufficiently governable relationship.

Therefore:

Connection Candidate Identity ≠ Connection Relationship Identity by default.

Relationship Endpoints

Each Connection Relationship should identify its materially relevant endpoints.

Canonical representation:

Endpoint A ↔ Endpoint B

or, where direction matters:

Endpoint A → Endpoint B Endpoints should reference governed objects rather than ambiguous descriptive labels wherever possible.

Endpoint Integrity

The identity of each endpoint should remain stable enough to prevent the Relationship from silently changing meaning.

Therefore:

Endpoint Change ≠ Same Relationship by default.

A materially changed endpoint may require a different Relationship identity.

Relationship Direction

Where direction affects Liability relevance, LCXRM should establish explicit Relationship direction.

Examples:

Subject → Action Authority → Decision Action → Object State Event → Consequence Condition → Subject Applicability Direction must not be assumed from presentation order alone.

Bidirectional Relationship

Some relationships may legitimately operate in both directions.

Where this is materially relevant, they may be represented as:

A ↔ B

A bidirectional representation should not be used merely because direction is unknown.

Therefore:

Bidirectional Relationship ≠ Unknown Direction.

Unknown Direction

Where a Relationship exists but its direction cannot yet be determined, direction should remain explicitly unknown.

Therefore:

Unknown Direction ≠ Bidirectional Relationship.

Relationship Type

Each Relationship should be typed where type materially affects Connection governance.

Potential types may include:

Operational, Control, Authority, Dependency, Conduct, Event, State, Temporal, Contribution, Identity, Ownership, Permission, Maintenance, Design, Production, Deployment, Supervision, Reliance, Communication, or another explicitly governed type.

Type Does Not Determine Liability

A Relationship type describes the Connection relationship.

It does not determine Liability.

Therefore:

Relationship Type ≠ Liability Type.

Operational Relationship

An Operational Relationship may connect elements through actual operational activity or state.

ORA™ may govern the underlying Operational Reality.

LCXRM governs only its Liability-specific relational representation.

Therefore:

Operational Relationship ≠ Liability.

Control Relationship

A Control Relationship may connect a Subject to:

Object, system, process, activity, or another Subject.

The existence of control may be materially relevant.

However:

Control Relationship ≠ Liability Determination.

Authority Relationship

An Authority Relationship may connect a Subject to:

decision, action, system, Object, or another Subject.

GOA™ may govern the underlying Authority state.

Therefore:

Authority Relationship ≠ Liability.

Responsibility Relationship

A Responsibility state may create a materially relevant Connection Relationship.

AGA™ governs Responsibility.

LCXRM governs its Liability-specific relational relevance.

Therefore:

Responsibility Relationship ≠ Liability.

Accountability Relationship

An Accountability structure may become relevant to the Connection.

However:

Accountability Relationship ≠ Liability Relationship Determination.

Accountability and Liability remain distinct governance objects.

Dependency Relationship

A Dependency Relationship exists where one element materially depends upon another.

Dependency may concern:

operation, state, authority, information, resource, decision, or another governed dependency.

Therefore:

Dependency Relationship ≠ Liability by itself.

Conduct Relationship

A Conduct Relationship connects a Subject or other relevant element to governed conduct.

The Relationship may concern:

performance, authorization, control, instruction, supervision, execution, or another materially relevant role.

Therefore:

Conduct Relationship ≠ Wrongdoing.

Action Relationship

An Action Relationship identifies how an action relates to another governed element.

Examples:

Subject → Action Action → Object Action → Event The Relationship does not establish the complete downstream chain.

Omission Relationship

An Omission Relationship may exist where governed non-action is materially connected to another element.

LCXRM must preserve the Requirement or Condition that makes the non-action relevant.

Therefore:

Non-Action ≠ Omission Relationship by default.

Event Relationship

An Event Relationship connects an event to another materially relevant element.

Examples:

Action → Event Event → State Event → Consequence Event Relationship does not establish causation by default.

State Relationship

A State Relationship connects a governed state to another relevant element.

The state may originate from:

ORA™, HAGA™, AGA™, GOA™, OBIDENITY®, AIG®, ASGA™, or another relevant architecture.

LCXRM governs only its Liability-specific relationship.

Consequence Relationship

A Consequence Relationship connects a governed consequence to another element.

The existence of a consequence does not establish how it arose or who bears Liability.

Therefore:

Consequence Relationship ≠ Liability.

Harm Relationship

A governed Harm state may participate in a Connection Relationship where Harm is relevant to the Liability Basis.

HAGA™ retains governance ownership of Harm.

Therefore:

Harm Relationship ≠ Liability Relationship.

Identity Relationship

Identity may establish that two records, actions, states, or representations relate to the same governed Subject or Object.

OBIDENITY® may govern that identity.

LCXRM may preserve the identity relationship where necessary for Liability Connection governance.

Therefore:

Identity Relationship ≠ Liability Connection by itself.

Ownership Relationship

Ownership may create a materially relevant relationship between Subject and Object.

Ownership does not automatically establish:

control, conduct, fault, or Liability.

Therefore:

Ownership Relationship ≠ Liability.

Permission Relationship

Permission may connect a Subject to an action, system, capability, or Object.

The existence of Permission does not establish execution.

Therefore:

Permission ≠ Action.

and:

Permission Relationship ≠ Liability.

Supervision Relationship

Supervision may connect one Subject to another Subject, process, system, or activity.

The existence of supervision does not determine whether the supervised conduct occurred or whether Liability attaches.

Maintenance Relationship

A Maintenance Relationship may connect:

maintenance provider, Object, maintenance action, Condition, or operational state.

LCXRM preserves the relevant relational structure without determining whether maintenance was adequate or Liability exists.

Design Relationship

A Design Relationship may connect a designer or design authority to an Object, component, system, or specification.

Therefore:

Design Relationship ≠ Design Defect.

Production Relationship

A Production Relationship may connect a producer to a product, component, process, or output.

Therefore:

Production Relationship ≠ Product Liability.

Deployment Relationship

A Deployment Relationship may connect a deploying Subject to:

system, configuration, environment, or operational activity.

Therefore:

Deployment Relationship ≠ Deployment Liability.

Reliance Relationship

Reliance may become materially relevant under certain Liability Bases.

A Reliance Relationship should identify:

the relying Subject, the relevant representation or state, and the direction of reliance.

Therefore:

Reliance Relationship ≠ Liability by itself.

Communication Relationship

A Communication Relationship may connect:

sender, message, recipient, decision, action, or consequence.

The existence of communication does not establish reliance or Liability.

Therefore:

Communication ≠ Reliance.

Contribution Relationship

A Contribution Relationship may represent that an element contributed to another state, event, or consequence.

Contribution should not be treated automatically as complete causation.

Therefore:

Contribution ≠ Complete Causation.

and:

Contribution ≠ Liability.

Relationship Scope

Each Connection Relationship should have a scope sufficient to determine what the Relationship includes.

Scope may be limited by:

Subject, Object, Condition, Basis, activity, time, location, system, event, or another relevant boundary.

Relationship Boundary

The Relationship Boundary determines where a Connection Relationship begins and ends as a governed relational object.

A boundary prevents adjacent relationships from being silently merged.

Therefore:

Relationship Proximity ≠ Relationship Identity.

Basis-Specific Relationship

A Connection Relationship may possess relevance only under a particular Liability Basis.

Therefore:

Relationship under Basis A ≠ Relationship under Basis B by default.

Basis association should remain explicit.

Condition-Specific Relationship

A Relationship may be relevant to one Liability Condition but not another.

Therefore:

Relationship relevant to Condition A ≠ Relationship relevant to Condition B by default.

Subject-Specific Relationship

Different Subjects may possess materially different Relationships to the same Object, Condition, event, or consequence.

Therefore:

Subject A Relationship ≠ Subject B Relationship.

Object-Specific Relationship

A Subject may possess different Connection Relationships to different Liability Objects.

Therefore:

Relationship to Object A ≠ Relationship to Object B.

Relationship-Specific Relationship

A Connection Relationship may itself depend upon an upstream Liability Relationship established under LRS.

The two remain distinct.

Therefore:

Liability Relationship ≠ Liability Connection Relationship.

The first defines relational participation in the potential Liability structure.

The second defines the Liability-specific Connection between relevant elements.

Direct Connection Relationship

A Direct Connection Relationship links two governed elements without requiring a materially necessary intermediate element for that specific relational step.

Canonical form:

A → B Directness applies to the individual Relationship.

It does not establish that the complete Connection is direct.

Indirect Connection Reference

Where the identified candidate depends upon intermediate elements, LCXRM should not falsely represent the endpoints as directly related.

Instead, it may preserve an indirect Connection reference for subsequent Chain governance.

Therefore:

Indirect Connection ≠ Direct Relationship.

Intermediate Relationship Requirement

Where A and C appear connected only through B:

A → B → C LCXRM should govern:

A → B and:

B → C as distinct Relationships where both are materially necessary.

LCXCM later governs their combination into a Chain.

Relationship Sequence

Individual Relationships may possess a preliminary sequence where order is known.

However, LCXRM does not own complete Connection Chain sequencing.

Therefore:

Relationship Sequence ≠ Complete Connection Chain.

Temporal Relationship

A Connection Relationship may be valid only during a defined period.

LCXRM should preserve materially relevant:

start, end, activation, suspension, expiration, or unknown temporal state.

Historical Relationship

A Connection Relationship may have existed historically but no longer exist.

Therefore:

Current Relationship Absence ≠ Historical Relationship Absence.

Relationship Continuity

A Relationship may change while retaining its governed identity.

Therefore:

Relationship Change ≠ New Relationship by default.

Continuity should preserve material historical states.

Relationship Activation

A Relationship may exist structurally before becoming operationally active.

Therefore:

Relationship Existence ≠ Relationship Activation.

Relationship Suspension

A Relationship may temporarily cease active operation without losing identity.

Therefore:

Relationship Suspension ≠ Relationship Termination.

Relationship Termination

A Relationship may terminate while remaining historically relevant.

Therefore:

Relationship Termination ≠ Historical Erasure.

Multiple Relationships Between Same Endpoints

The same two endpoints may possess several materially distinct Relationships.

Example:

Subject A → Object B: Ownership Subject A → Object B: Operational Control Subject A → Object B: Maintenance Responsibility These should not be collapsed merely because their endpoints are identical.

Therefore:

Same Endpoints ≠ Same Relationship.

Parallel Relationships

Multiple Relationships may operate in parallel.

Parallelism should preserve:

separate identities, types, scopes, and temporal states.

Therefore:

Parallel Relationships ≠ One Composite Relationship by default.

Overlapping Relationships

Relationships may partially overlap in:

scope, time, Subjects, Objects, or function.

Overlap does not establish equivalence.

Therefore:

Relationship Overlap ≠ Relationship Equivalence.

Composite Relationship

A Composite Relationship may be represented where several inseparable relational components must be governed together.

Its internal components should remain identifiable where materially relevant.

Therefore:

Composite Relationship ≠ Unstructured Relationship Aggregation.

Multi-Subject Relationship

A Connection Relationship may involve multiple Subjects.

Where individual Subject roles differ, those differences should remain explicit.

Therefore:

Multi-Subject Relationship ≠ Equal Subject Connection.

Multi-Object Relationship

A Connection Relationship may involve multiple Objects.

Object-specific distinctions should remain visible where they affect downstream Connection governance.

Relationship Network

LCXRM may produce multiple interconnected Relationships.

This does not yet constitute the complete governed Connection Network.

LCXCM determines how Relationships combine into Chains and network structures.

Therefore:

Relationship Set ≠ Complete Connection Network.

Relationship Distinction

LCXRM must distinguish materially different Relationships even where they appear similar.

Distinction may depend on:

identity, type, direction, scope, Basis, Condition, Subject, Object, time, or role.

Relationship Similarity

Two Relationships may appear similar without being equivalent.

Therefore:

Relationship Similarity ≠ Relationship Identity.

Relationship Equivalence

Relationships should be treated as equivalent only where the applicable methodology establishes that their materially relevant characteristics represent the same governed relationship.

Equivalence should not be inferred from shared labels alone.

Relationship Duplication

Multiple records may represent the same Connection Relationship.

Duplicate reconciliation should preserve provenance.

Therefore:

Multiple Relationship Records ≠ Multiple Relationships by default.

Relationship Conflict

Governed inputs may support conflicting descriptions of the same potential Relationship.

LCXRM should preserve the conflict.

Therefore:

Relationship Conflict ≠ Authority to Manufacture Relational Certainty.

Relationship Uncertainty

Uncertainty may concern:

Relationship existence, type, direction, scope, endpoint identity, temporal state, or Basis relevance.

Uncertainty should remain explicit.

Therefore:

Relationship Uncertainty ≠ Relationship Absence.

Unknown Relationship

Where a Connection Candidate is relevant but the nature of the Relationship cannot yet be established, it may remain:

Unknown Relationship

rather than being forced into an unsupported type.

Therefore:

Unknown Relationship ≠ No Relationship.

Disputed Relationship

A Relationship may remain disputed where competing governed sources support materially different representations.

Therefore:

Disputed Relationship ≠ Established Relationship.

Inferred Relationship

A Relationship may be inferred where methodology permits.

The inference should preserve:

inputs, logic, assumptions, and uncertainty.

Therefore:

Inferred Relationship ≠ Directly Established Relationship.

Assumed Relationship

An assumption may be recorded for analytical purposes.

It must not be represented as an established Relationship.

Therefore:

Assumed Relationship ≠ Governed Established Relationship.

Relationship Change

A Connection Relationship may change because:

underlying reality changes, Subject roles change, Object states change, Conditions change, authority changes, control changes, or new information becomes available.

Change should remain traceable.

Relationship Correction

An earlier Relationship representation may be found incorrect.

Correction should preserve:

the earlier representation, corrected representation, reason, effective relevance, and provenance.

Therefore:

Relationship Correction ≠ Historical Erasure.

Relationship Reassessment

New information may require reassessment of:

type, direction, scope, identity, or relevance.

Therefore:

Relationship Reassessment ≠ Historical Rewrite.

Relationship Versioning

Material Relationship changes may require versioning.

Therefore:

New Relationship Version ≠ New Relationship by default.

The distinction between continuity and new identity must be governed.

Relationship Re-Entry

Later LCNS stages may reveal that a Relationship was incorrectly structured.

LCXRM should support targeted re-entry.

Therefore:

Relationship Re-Entry ≠ New Liability Origin by default.

Upstream Re-Entry

Relationship governance may reveal that the problem lies in Connection Identification.

Where a relevant Connection Candidate is missing or incorrectly identified, governance should return to LCXIM.

Likewise, defective upstream LIGA™ structures should return to their owning Parent Standard.

No Liability Relationship Substitution

LCXRM must not substitute the Liability Relationship established by LRS for the Connection Relationship governed here.

For example:

a contractual relationship may establish that two Subjects participate in the Liability structure.

LCXRM must still determine how a relevant action, Condition, Object, event, or consequence connects within that structure.

Therefore:

Participation Relationship ≠ Connection Relationship.

No Causation Substitution

An operational causal relationship may become a Connection Relationship input.

It does not automatically become the complete Liability Connection Relationship.

Therefore:

Operational Causal Relationship ≠ Liability Connection Relationship by default.

No Harm Substitution

A relationship to Harm does not automatically establish the Liability-specific relationship required by LCNS.

Therefore:

Relationship to Harm ≠ Liability Connection Relationship by default.

No Accountability Substitution

An Accountability relationship does not automatically become a Liability Connection Relationship.

Therefore:

Accountability Relationship ≠ Liability Connection Relationship by default.

No Control Substitution

A Control Relationship may be materially relevant.

It must not be converted automatically into Liability.

Therefore:

Control Relationship ≠ Liability Connection Sufficiency.

No Ownership Substitution

Ownership may form one Relationship within the Connection structure.

It does not establish the complete Liability Connection.

Therefore:

Ownership Relationship ≠ Complete Liability Connection.

No Temporal Shortcut

Two events occurring close together may justify a Relationship inquiry.

Temporal proximity alone does not establish the Relationship type or meaning.

Therefore:

Temporal Proximity ≠ Governed Connection Relationship.

No Spatial Shortcut

Spatial proximity may be relevant but does not establish the governed Relationship.

Therefore:

Spatial Proximity ≠ Governed Connection Relationship.

No Label Shortcut

Two records carrying the same relationship label may represent materially different Relationships.

Therefore:

Same Label ≠ Same Relationship.

Governed characteristics determine identity.

Relationship Sufficiency

A Connection Relationship is sufficiently governed for Chain formation when materially relevant aspects of its:

identity, endpoints, type, direction, scope, boundary, Basis association, Condition association, Subject association, Object association, temporal state, and uncertainty are explicit enough for LCXCM to determine how it connects with other Relationships.

Relationship Sufficiency and Qualification

Relationship sufficiency does not mean the overall Connection has been qualified.

Therefore:

Relationship Sufficiency ≠ Connection Qualification.

LCXQM owns Qualification.

Relationship Sufficiency and Evidence

A Relationship may be structurally sufficient for Chain governance while its evidentiary support remains incomplete or disputed.

Therefore:

Relationship Structural Sufficiency ≠ Evidence Sufficiency.

LCXEAM governs Connection Evidence Association.

Governed Liability Connection Relationship Set

The set may contain:

direct Relationships, directional Relationships, bidirectional Relationships, unknown-direction Relationships, operational Relationships, control Relationships, authority Relationships, dependency Relationships, conduct Relationships, event Relationships, state Relationships, consequence Relationships, and other explicitly governed relational types.

Handoff to Connection Chain Governance

LCXRM hands the:

Governed Liability Connection Relationship Set

to:

LCXCM — Liability Connection Chain Module

The next governance question becomes:

How do the governed Liability Connection Relationships combine into complete, ordered, traceable, and materially sufficient Connection Chains?

LCXRM must not answer that question prematurely.

Minimum Implementation Framework

A conforming implementation of LCXRM must provide exactly the following eight capabilities:

Connection Relationship Formation & Identity

Capability to transform Connection Candidates into explicitly governed Connection Relationships with persistent identities sufficient for subsequent Chain governance.

Endpoint, Direction & Role Governance

Capability to establish Relationship endpoints, direction, bidirectionality, unknown direction, and the materially relevant roles of connected elements.

Relationship Type Governance

Capability to distinguish operational, control, authority, dependency, conduct, event, state, consequence, contribution, identity, ownership, permission, supervision, maintenance, design, production, deployment, reliance, communication, and other materially relevant Relationship types.

Scope, Boundary & Association Governance

Capability to govern Relationship scope and boundaries and preserve relevant Basis, Subject, Object, Condition, and upstream Liability Relationship associations.

Multiple, Parallel, Overlapping & Composite Relationship Governance

Capability to preserve materially distinct Relationships among the same or overlapping endpoints without inappropriate consolidation and to represent composite or multi-subject structures where justified.

Temporal, Uncertain, Disputed & Inferred Relationship Governance

Capability to preserve temporal states, historical Relationships, uncertainty, disputes, conflicts, assumptions, and governed inference without manufacturing certainty.

Relationship Change, Correction, Versioning & Re-Entry

Capability to preserve legitimate Relationship change, correction, reassessment, versioning, continuity, and targeted lifecycle re-entry without historical erasure.

Relationship Sufficiency & Handoff

Capability to determine whether the Governed Liability Connection Relationship Set is sufficiently structured for Connection Chain governance without prematurely determining Connection Qualification, evidentiary sufficiency, or Liability.

Cross-Architecture Boundary

LCXRM may use relational states governed by other OOF® architectures as inputs.

ORA™ governs Operational Reality and operational relationships.

GOA™ governs Authority, Scope, and Delegation.

AGA™ governs Responsibility, Accountability, and Attribution.

HAGA™ governs Harm and its lifecycle.

ADIT® governs evidence and auditability.

VALIDOS™ governs validation.

OBIDENITY® governs Identity, Ownership, Authority, Permission, Provenance, Transfer, and Continuity.

AIG® and ASGA™ govern AI and autonomous-system behavior and operational governance states.

LCXRM does not duplicate those governance domains.

It determines how their relevant governed states participate in a Liability Connection Relationship.

The canonical interoperability principle is:

An externally governed relationship may become a component of a Liability Connection Relationship without transferring governance ownership of its original domain to LIGA™.

Canonical Boundary

LCXRM begins with an:

Identified Liability Connection Set

and asks:

What exactly is the governed relationship between the identified elements of each Connection Candidate?

LCXRM governs:

Relationship Formation, Relationship Identity, Endpoints, Direction, Type, Role, Scope, Boundary, Basis Association, Subject Association, Object Association, Condition Association, upstream Liability Relationship Association, Temporal State, Direct Relationships, Indirect Relationship References, Multiple Relationships, Parallel Relationships, Overlapping Relationships, Composite Relationships, Multi-Subject Relationships, Relationship Distinction, Relationship Conflict, Relationship Uncertainty, Inference, Change, Correction, Reassessment, Versioning, Re-Entry, and Relationship Sufficiency.

LCXRM does not determine:

the complete Connection Chain, final Connection Network structure, Connection Qualification, Connection Evidence Sufficiency, Operational Causation itself, Harm, Accountability, Liability Attachment, Liability Determination, Liability Scope, Liability Allocation, Liability State, Liability Transfer, Recourse, Resolution, Discharge, or Finality.

LCXRM ends when a:

Governed Liability Connection Relationship Set

is sufficiently structured for Liability Connection Chain governance.

Use Case 1 — Autonomous System Control Relationship

An autonomous industrial system performs an operational action involving a governed Liability Object.

LCXIM has identified potential Connections among:

the system operator, the autonomous system, the action, the Object, and an operational event.

LCXRM determines the distinct Relationships among those elements.

It may distinguish:

operator → system as an operational-control Relationship, system → action as an execution Relationship, and action → Object as an operational Relationship.

The module does not collapse those Relationships into one assumed causal or Liability chain.

It produces explicit governed Relationships that LCXCM can subsequently assemble and test as a Connection Chain.

Use Case 2 — Multi-Party Maintenance Relationships

A technical installation is owned by one organization, operated by another, and maintained by a third provider.

A later incident makes several Connection Candidates relevant.

LCXRM distinguishes:

owner → equipment as an Ownership Relationship, operator → equipment as an Operational-Control Relationship, maintenance provider → maintenance activity as a Conduct Relationship, and maintenance activity → equipment state as an Operational Relationship.

The shared Object does not make these Relationships equivalent.

Each Relationship retains its own identity, type, direction, scope, and temporal state.

LCXCM can then determine how the relevant Relationships combine into one or more Liability Connection Chains.

Canonical Closing Statement

Identifying the elements of a potential Liability Connection is not enough.

The relationship between those elements must itself be explicit: what is related, in what direction, through what role, within what scope, and under which governed context.

LCXRM converts potential Connections into governed relational structure without prematurely constructing the complete chain or determining Liability.

A Connection Candidate identifies what may be related. A Connection Relationship defines how those elements are related.

Define the relationship before constructing the chain.
