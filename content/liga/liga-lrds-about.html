About Liability Resolution & Discharge Standard (LRDS)

Standard (LRDS)

What Is the Liability Resolution & Discharge Standard?

The Liability Resolution & Discharge Standard (LRDS) is the LIGA™ Parent Standard for governing how an active Liability Relationship or defined Liability requirement may legitimately cease requiring the same continued performance or active governance.

It governs the transition from active Liability toward Resolution, Satisfaction, Settlement, Discharge, Extinguishment, or another legitimate Resolution state while preserving unresolved Liability, secondary Relationships, historical integrity, and downstream Finality requirements.

LRDS does not determine jurisdiction-specific legal outcomes.

It provides the methodological structure for determining whether a Liability requirement has actually reached a governed Resolution or Discharge state.

Canonical Meaning

LRDS establishes the methodological boundary between an active Liability requirement and a Liability requirement that has been sufficiently resolved or discharged.

Its central distinction is:

Liability Resolution ≠ Liability Finality.

Resolution may end an active Liability requirement.

Finality determines whether the Liability lifecycle itself may legitimately reach terminal closure.

What Does LRDS Govern?

LRDS governs:

Resolution initiation, Resolution Objects, affected Liability Relationships, affected Subjects, Resolution Basis, Resolution Scope, Resolution Boundary, Satisfaction, payment relevance, performance relevance, Settlement, Discharge, Extinguishment, release or waiver where applicable, Resolution conditions, Resolution States, remaining active Liability, Resolution dependencies, verification, Reopening relevance, Resolution history, and the transition toward Continuity & Finality governance.

Its principal output is:

Governed Liability Resolution & Discharge State

Why Is LRDS Necessary?

Liability-related systems frequently treat operational events as if they automatically ended Liability.

A payment may be recorded as closure.

A completed performance may be interpreted as Satisfaction.

A Settlement may be interpreted as an admission.

A Claim may be closed while Liability remains unresolved.

One Relationship may be discharged while another secondary Relationship remains active.

Without a dedicated Resolution governance layer, these different events can be collapsed into a single ambiguous state such as “closed.” LRDS prevents that collapse.

It asks a more precise question:

What exactly has ended, under what Basis, for which Subject, within which Liability Relationship, and what remains active?

Position in the LIGA™ Lifecycle

LRDS occupies the eleventh position in the LIGA™ Governance Flow:

Liability Context → Liability Object → Liability Basis → Liability Relationships → Liability Conditions → Liability Connection → Liability Determination → Liability Scope & Allocation → Liability State & Change → Liability Transfer & Recourse → Liability Resolution & Discharge → Liability Continuity & Finality Its upstream governance space is:

Liability Transfer & Recourse Standard (LTRS)

Its downstream governance space is:

Liability Continuity & Finality Standard (LCFS)

LRDS therefore sits at the boundary between active Liability governance and potential lifecycle completion.

Resolution Object

Before Liability can be described as resolved, the object of Resolution must be explicit.

The Resolution Object may be:

an entire Liability Relationship, part of a Liability Relationship, a specific obligation, a payment requirement, a performance requirement, a shared Liability component, a Recourse Relationship, or another defined Liability requirement.

This prevents Resolution of one component from being incorrectly interpreted as Resolution of the entire Liability structure.

Resolution of a Liability Component ≠ Resolution of the Complete Liability Structure by default.

Resolution Basis

A governed Resolution requires an identifiable Basis.

Resolution may arise through:

Satisfaction, performance, Settlement, Discharge, Extinguishment, release, waiver where applicable, authoritative Resolution, or another legitimate mechanism.

The Basis that originally established Liability and the Basis through which Liability later resolves may differ.

Therefore:

Liability Basis ≠ Resolution Basis by default.

Satisfaction

Satisfaction concerns fulfillment of a defined Liability requirement.

A requirement may be:

fully satisfied, partially satisfied, conditionally satisfied, or remain unsatisfied.

Satisfaction may support Resolution but is not universally identical to Resolution.

Satisfaction ≠ Liability Resolution by default.

Payment and Performance

Payment and performance are important Resolution-relevant events, but neither should automatically be interpreted as Resolution.

A payment may satisfy only part of a monetary requirement.

Performance may complete one obligation while another remains active.

A third party may make payment without becoming the primary Liability-bearing Subject.

Therefore:

Payment ≠ Liability Resolution by default.

Performance ≠ Satisfaction by default.

Settlement

Settlement is a distinct Resolution mechanism.

It may establish how a Liability dispute or requirement will be addressed, but Settlement should not automatically be interpreted as an admission that every underlying Liability position was correct.

It may also require future performance before Resolution is complete.

Therefore:

Settlement ≠ Liability Determination.

Settlement ≠ Admission of Liability.

Settlement Agreement ≠ Settlement Performance.

Discharge

Discharge governs the state in which an applicable Liability requirement no longer requires continued performance from the relevant Subject under the applicable Resolution structure.

Discharge may be:

complete, partial, conditional, Subject-specific, or Relationship-specific.

Discharge changes the active Liability state.

It does not erase the Liability history.

Discharge ≠ Historical Erasure.

Partial Resolution

Liability does not always resolve as one indivisible structure.

One requirement may be satisfied while another remains active.

One Subject may be discharged while another remains liable.

One Recourse Relationship may resolve while another continues.

LRDS therefore preserves both:

Resolved Liability

and

Remaining Active Liability

within the same wider Liability structure where necessary.

Primary and Secondary Liability Resolution

Primary Liability and secondary Liability Relationships may possess independent Resolution lifecycles.

A primary Liability Relationship may resolve while Recourse continues.

A Recourse Relationship may resolve while another Recourse Relationship remains active.

Therefore:

Primary Liability Resolution ≠ Recourse Liability Resolution.

Resolution Across a Liability Network

One occurrence may create multiple Liability Relationships.

Those Relationships may later produce:

Transfers, Sharing structures, Contribution, Indemnification, Insurance-related relationships, and Recourse chains.

Each Relationship may reach Resolution independently.

Therefore:

Local Liability Resolution ≠ Liability Network Resolution.

A network should not be represented as resolved merely because one constituent Relationship has ended.

Resolution Verification

A claimed Resolution should be verified against the actual Resolution requirements.

Verification may consider:

the Resolution Object, Basis, Scope, conditions, payment, performance, Settlement, Discharge, authority, effective time, and remaining Liability.

A generic administrative closure signal is insufficient.

Administrative Closure ≠ Verified Liability Resolution.

Resolution State and History

LRDS preserves both the current Resolution State and the history through which that state emerged.

A Liability Relationship may move through states such as:

Initiated, Pending, Partially Satisfied, Satisfied, Settled, Partially Discharged, Discharged, Resolved, Disputed, or Reopened.

The current state should not replace the historical sequence.

Current Resolution State ≠ Complete Resolution History.

Reopening

Resolution does not necessarily make a Liability Relationship permanently incapable of Re-entry.

New materially relevant information, failed Resolution conditions, reversal, invalid Resolution inputs, or another legitimate governance condition may require Reopening.

Reopening does not automatically create a new Liability.

Liability Reopening ≠ New Liability by default.

The prior Resolution remains part of the history.

Reopening must not rewrite history.

Resolution vs Harm Resolution

HAGA™ governs the lifecycle of Harm.

LRDS governs Liability Resolution.

Harm may be resolved while Liability remains active.

Liability may be resolved while some Harm consequences remain.

Therefore:

Harm Resolution ≠ Liability Resolution.

Resolution vs Claim Closure

A Claim and Liability are different governed objects.

A Claim may be:

paid, rejected, withdrawn, settled, or closed without independently determining the complete Liability state.

Therefore:

Claim Closure ≠ Liability Resolution.

Claim Payment ≠ Liability Admission.

Resolution vs Operational Termination

The mechanism or Relationship associated with the original Liability may end while Liability remains active.

A contract may terminate.

An operational process may stop.

A system may be decommissioned.

A commercial Relationship may end.

None automatically resolves existing Liability.

Operational Termination ≠ Liability Resolution.

Resolution vs Finality

This is the principal downstream boundary of LRDS.

Resolution answers:

Has this active Liability requirement legitimately ceased requiring the same continued performance or governance?

Finality asks:

May this Liability Relationship or Liability Network legitimately reach terminal lifecycle closure while remaining historically reconstructable and subject to governed Re-entry where necessary?

These are different governance questions.

Therefore:

Liability Resolution ≠ Liability Finality.

What LRDS Does Not Govern

LRDS does not independently determine:

whether Liability originally existed, the original Liability Basis, initial Liability Relationships, Liability Conditions, Liability Connection, initial Liability Determination, initial Scope or Allocation, general Liability State & Change, Transfer formation, Recourse formation, Harm Resolution, Claims governance, jurisdiction-specific legal adjudication, or Liability Finality.

Those matters remain with their respective governance spaces.

Historical Integrity

Resolution changes the active state of Liability.

It does not erase:

the Liability Origin, original Determination, Scope, Allocation, Transfers, Sharing, Recourse, prior States, payments, performance, Settlement history, or the Resolution process itself.

This distinction is foundational:

Liability Resolution ≠ Liability History Erasure.

Cross-Architecture Relationship

LRDS may receive inputs from other OOF® architectures.

ORA™ may establish actual payment, performance, termination, or other Operational Reality.

HAGA™ may establish Harm Recovery or Harm Resolution.

AGA™ may establish Responsibility, Accountability, or Authority structures.

ADIT® may provide evidence and auditability.

VALIDOS™ may validate Resolution-relevant inputs.

OBIDENITY® may support identity, authority, provenance, succession, and continuity.

VFM® may govern economic or Value Flow.

AIG® and ASGA™ may provide relevant AI or autonomous-system operational events.

These inputs may support Liability Resolution governance.

They do not replace it.

An external mechanism may satisfy, evidence, enable, or affect a Liability Resolution condition, but LRDS governs whether the Liability requirement itself has reached a governed Resolution or Discharge state.

Use Case 1 — Partial Satisfaction of a Multi-Requirement

Liability

A Liability Relationship contains a monetary requirement and a separate corrective performance requirement.

The monetary requirement is fully paid.

The corrective performance remains incomplete.

LRDS identifies the payment as Satisfaction of the monetary component while preserving the corrective requirement as active Liability.

The Liability Relationship is therefore not incorrectly represented as completely resolved merely because payment occurred.

The resulting Resolution structure distinguishes:

the resolved component, the unresolved component, the relevant Subjects, the applicable Resolution Basis, and the current Resolution State.

Use Case 2 — Primary Liability Resolved While Recourse

Continues

A Subject satisfies an established primary Liability requirement.

A separate Recourse Relationship exists because another Subject may be required to reimburse part of the economic burden.

LRDS may establish that the primary Liability Relationship has reached Resolution while preserving the Recourse Relationship as active.

The two Relationships retain separate Resolution States.

The primary Resolution therefore does not collapse or erase the secondary Recourse structure.

Primary Liability Resolution ≠ Recourse Liability Resolution.

Canonical Output

The principal output of LRDS is:

Governed Liability Resolution & Discharge State

This state identifies what has been resolved, satisfied, settled, discharged, extinguished, or otherwise legitimately ended; what remains active; the Basis and Scope of Resolution; affected Subjects and Relationships; verification status; relevant dependencies; and the history necessary for downstream Continuity & Finality governance.

Canonical Meaning in One Sentence

LRDS governs when and how an active Liability requirement may legitimately cease requiring continued performance or active governance while preserving unresolved Liability, secondary Relationships, Resolution history, and the distinction between Resolution and Finality.

Canonical Closing Statement

A Liability matter is not resolved simply because something stopped, something was paid, or a system marked it closed.

Resolution must identify exactly what ended, why it ended, for whom it ended, and what remains active.

And even when active Liability has legitimately ended, its lifecycle has not necessarily reached Finality.

Liability Resolution ≠ Liability Finality.

Resolve what has legitimately ended. Preserve what remains active. Carry the complete Liability history forward to Finality governance.
