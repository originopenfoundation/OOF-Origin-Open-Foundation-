Canonical Definition

The Liability Connection Evidence Association Module (LCXEAM) governs how governed evidence, evidentiary references, validated inputs, observations, records, measurements, statements, system outputs, and other admissible evidentiary objects are associated with specific Liability Connections, Connection Relationships, Connection Chains, endpoints, intermediate elements, qualification states, uncertainties, and conflicts.

LCXEAM establishes a traceable association between the governed Liability Connection structure and the evidence relevant to that structure.

It determines what evidence supports, contradicts, limits, contextualizes, or leaves unresolved each materially relevant Liability Connection element.

LCXEAM does not create evidence, validate evidence, determine evidentiary admissibility under jurisdiction-specific law, or determine Liability.

It governs the transition:

Qualified Liability Connection Set → Evidence Identification → Connection Evidence Association → Support & Contradiction Mapping → Evidence Gap & Uncertainty Association → Connection Evidence Traceability →

Governed Liability Connection Structure

Its foundational distinction is:

Evidence ≠ Liability Connection.

Operational Role

The operational role of LCXEAM is:

to make the evidentiary relationship of each materially relevant Liability Connection explicit, traceable, differentiated, and reconstructable before the Connection structure is handed to Liability Determination.

LCXQM asks:

Is the Connection sufficiently qualified?

LCXEAM asks:

What governed evidence supports, contradicts, limits, contextualizes, or leaves unresolved that Connection and its material components?

The module does not ask:

Does the evidence prove Liability?

That question exceeds the LCXEAM boundary.

Position in the Parent Standard Flow

The internal LCNS progression is:

Liability Connection Identification → Liability Connection Relationship → Liability Connection Chain → Liability Connection Qualification → Liability Connection Evidence Association LCXEAM governs:

Liability Connection Evidence Association

Its principal input is:

Qualified Liability Connection Set

Its principal output is:

Governed Liability Connection Structure

Its canonical handoff is:

Governed Liability Connection Structure → Liability Determination

Central Operational Question

What governed evidence supports, contradicts, limits, contextualizes, or leaves unresolved each materially relevant Liability Connection and its constituent elements?

Governed Space

LCXEAM governs:

Evidence Association Identification, Evidence-to-Connection Association, Evidence-to-Relationship Association, Evidence-to-Chain Association, Evidence-to-Endpoint Association, Evidence-to-Intermediate-Element Association, Evidence-to-Qualification Association, Supporting Evidence Association, Contradicting Evidence Association, Limiting Evidence Association, Contextual Evidence Association, Neutral Evidence Association, Mixed Evidence Association, Evidence Gap Association, Evidence Conflict Association, Evidence Uncertainty Association, Evidence Temporal Association, Evidence Provenance Reference, Evidence Validation Reference, Evidence Scope, Evidence Relevance, Evidence Coverage, Evidence Duplication, Evidence Dependency, Evidence Change, Evidence Correction, Evidence Association Reassessment, Evidence Association History, and Connection Evidence Traceability.

Evidence Association

Evidence Association is the governed linkage between an evidentiary object and a specific element of the Liability Connection structure.

The evidentiary object remains governed by its appropriate evidence governance framework.

LCXEAM governs only its Liability Connection association.

Therefore:

Evidence Governance ≠ Evidence Association.

Evidence Object

An Evidence Object may include a governed:

record, document, measurement, observation, statement, system log, sensor output, transaction record, technical result, validated output, image, communication, audit record, or another evidence-bearing object.

LCXEAM should reference the Evidence Object without silently redefining it.

Evidence Identity

Each evidence association should reference a sufficiently identifiable Evidence Object.

Where evidence identity is uncertain, that uncertainty should remain explicit.

Therefore:

Evidence Label ≠ Evidence Identity.

Connection Identity Association

Evidence should be associated with the specific Connection identity to which it is relevant.

Evidence relevant to Connection A should not automatically be transferred to Connection B.

Therefore:

Evidence for Connection A ≠ Evidence for Connection B by default.

Relationship Evidence Association

Evidence may support or contradict a specific Connection Relationship.

Example:

Subject → Action may be associated with:

system logs, instructions, records, or other governed evidence.

The association should identify which Relationship the evidence concerns.

Chain Evidence Association

Evidence may concern a complete or partial Connection Chain.

Example:

A → B → C → D An evidentiary object may support:

one Relationship, several Relationships, an intermediate element, or the overall sequence.

These scopes should remain distinguishable.

Endpoint Evidence Association

Evidence may concern the identity, state, or relevance of a Connection endpoint.

Therefore:

Evidence of Endpoint ≠ Evidence of Relationship by default.

Intermediate Element Evidence Association

Evidence may support an Intermediate Element without supporting the Relationships on either side of that element.

Therefore:

Evidence of Intermediate Element ≠ Evidence of Complete Chain.

Qualification Evidence Association

Evidence may support or challenge a Qualification State.

LCXEAM should preserve which Qualification criteria or Connection characteristics the evidence affects.

Therefore:

Evidence Supporting Qualification ≠ Evidence Establishing Liability.

Evidence Scope

Every evidence association should identify the scope of what the Evidence Object actually supports or contradicts.

Evidence should not be generalized beyond its governed scope.

Therefore:

Evidence Scope ≠ Entire Liability Matter by default.

Evidence Relevance

Evidence is relevant to a Connection where it materially informs:

Connection existence, identity, endpoint, Relationship, direction, sequence, intermediate element, timing, qualification, uncertainty, or another Connection characteristic.

Relevance and Support

Relevant evidence does not necessarily support the Connection.

It may:

support, contradict, limit, contextualize, or remain neutral.

Therefore:

Relevant Evidence ≠ Supporting Evidence.

Supporting Evidence

Supporting Evidence is evidence associated with a Connection element in a manner that supports the represented Connection state.

Support should specify what exactly is supported.

Therefore:

Supporting Evidence ≠ Liability Proof.

Contradicting Evidence

Contradicting Evidence materially conflicts with a represented Connection element or state.

It should remain visible rather than being excluded merely because it weakens the Connection.

Therefore:

Contradicting Evidence ≠ Irrelevant Evidence.

Limiting Evidence

Limiting Evidence does not necessarily contradict a Connection but restricts the scope, strength, temporal applicability, or interpretation of that Connection.

Therefore:

Limitation ≠ Contradiction.

Contextual Evidence

Contextual Evidence provides information necessary to interpret the Connection correctly without independently supporting or contradicting it.

Therefore:

Contextual Evidence ≠ Supporting Evidence by default.

Neutral Evidence

Evidence may be materially relevant while neither supporting nor contradicting the Connection.

Neutral evidence should not be forced into a directional category.

Therefore:

Neutral Evidence ≠ No Evidence.

Mixed Evidence

An Evidence Object may support one Connection element while contradicting or limiting another.

LCXEAM should allow evidence association at the appropriate granular level.

Therefore:

One Evidence Object ≠ One Universal Evidentiary Effect.

Evidence Association Direction

The association should identify whether the Evidence Object:

supports, contradicts, limits, contextualizes, or neutrally informs the relevant Connection element.

Association direction must not be inferred solely from who submitted or produced the evidence.

Evidence Producer

The producer of evidence may be relevant to provenance.

Producer identity does not determine evidentiary effect.

Therefore:

Evidence Producer ≠ Evidence Meaning.

Evidence Source

Evidence Source should remain distinguishable from:

Evidence Object, Evidence Producer, Evidence Custodian, and Evidence User.

These roles may belong to different Subjects.

Evidence Provenance

LCXEAM should preserve a reference to governed evidence provenance where available.

It should not recreate evidence provenance governance.

ADIT® or another appropriate evidence governance mechanism retains ownership of that domain.

Evidence Provenance and Connection

Strong provenance does not automatically establish a Connection.

Therefore:

Strong Evidence Provenance ≠ Established Liability Connection.

Evidence Validation Reference

Where an Evidence Object has undergone validation, LCXEAM may preserve the applicable Validation reference.

VALIDOS™ retains governance ownership of validation.

Therefore:

Validated Evidence ≠ Liability Determination.

Evidence Audit Reference

Where evidence possesses an audit record, LCXEAM may preserve the relevant ADIT® reference.

The audit state should not be converted into a Liability conclusion.

Evidence Temporal Association

Evidence should be associated with the time period to which it materially relates.

The time at which evidence was created may differ from the time of the state it represents.

Therefore:

Evidence Creation Time ≠ Represented Event Time by default.

Evidence Discovery Time

The time evidence becomes known may differ from:

creation time, event time, or effective time.

Therefore:

Evidence Discovery Time ≠ Evidence Event Time.

Later Evidence

Evidence discovered later may alter the governed understanding of a Connection.

It should not rewrite what was known earlier.

Therefore:

Later Evidence ≠ Earlier Knowledge.

Historical Evidence Association

LCXEAM should support reconstruction of which evidence was associated with a Connection at materially relevant lifecycle moments.

Therefore:

Current Evidence Set ≠ Historical Evidence Set.

Evidence Coverage

Evidence Coverage describes which portions of the Connection structure possess associated evidence.

Coverage may be:

complete, partial, uneven, unknown, or absent.

Coverage is distinct from evidence quality.

Partial Evidence Coverage

A Connection Chain may possess evidence for some Relationships but not others.

Example:

A → B → C → D may contain strong evidence for:

A → B and:

C → D while:

B → C remains unsupported.

LCXEAM should preserve that gap.

Evidence Gap

An Evidence Gap exists where evidence necessary for a materially relevant Connection element is absent, unavailable, inaccessible, unknown, or insufficiently associated.

Therefore:

Evidence Gap ≠ Connection Absence.

Material Evidence Gap

An Evidence Gap is material where resolving it could affect:

Connection Qualification, Connection interpretation, Connection completeness, or downstream Liability Determination.

Materiality should remain explicit.

Evidence Gap State

A gap may be represented as:

Missing, Unavailable, Unknown, Pending, Inaccessible, Not Yet Identified, or another governed state.

These states should not be collapsed.

Missing Evidence

Missing Evidence means evidence expected or required for the defined association is not presently available.

It does not establish that the relevant Connection did not exist.

Therefore:

Missing Evidence ≠ No Connection.

Unknown Evidence

Unknown Evidence means it is not presently known whether relevant evidence exists.

Therefore:

Unknown Evidence ≠ Missing Evidence.

Unavailable Evidence

Evidence may be known to exist but unavailable.

Therefore:

Unavailable Evidence ≠ Nonexistent Evidence.

Inaccessible Evidence

Evidence may exist and be available to another authority while inaccessible to the current governance process.

Therefore:

Inaccessible Evidence ≠ Absent Evidence.

Pending Evidence

Evidence may be expected from a pending:

audit, measurement, record retrieval, technical analysis, validation, or another process.

Therefore:

Pending Evidence ≠ Supporting Evidence.

Evidence Conflict

Evidence Conflict exists where different Evidence Objects materially support incompatible representations of the same Connection element.

The conflict should remain explicit.

Therefore:

Evidence Conflict ≠ Authority to Select Preferred Evidence.

Conflict Scope

Evidence conflict should identify what exactly is disputed.

Conflict may concern:

endpoint identity, Relationship existence, direction, sequence, time, intermediate element, Connection state, or Qualification.

Evidence Conflict and Qualification

A conflict may require Qualification reassessment.

LCXEAM should route the affected Connection back to LCXQM where necessary.

Therefore:

Evidence Conflict ≠ Automatic Connection Rejection.

Evidence Consistency

Multiple Evidence Objects may consistently support the same Connection element.

Consistency may strengthen the governed association.

It does not by itself establish Liability.

Therefore:

Evidence Consistency ≠ Liability Determination.

Evidence Quantity

Evidence quantity should remain separate from evidentiary relevance and coverage.

Therefore:

More Evidence ≠ Better Connection Evidence by default.

Evidence Duplication

Multiple records may represent copies, derivatives, or repeated representations of the same underlying evidence.

LCXEAM should avoid treating duplication as independent evidentiary support.

Therefore:

Evidence Duplication ≠ Independent Corroboration.

Evidence Dependency

One Evidence Object may depend upon another.

Examples include:

derived reports, summaries, analytical outputs, or replicated records.

Dependency should remain visible where materially relevant.

Therefore:

Multiple Dependent Evidence Objects ≠ Multiple Independent Evidence Sources.

Evidence Corroboration

Independent Evidence Objects may corroborate the same Connection element.

Corroboration should identify the relevant scope.

Therefore:

Corroboration of One Element ≠ Corroboration of Entire Chain.

Evidence Independence

Where independence matters, the governance process should distinguish independent evidence from evidence derived from a common source.

Therefore:

Different Evidence Records ≠ Independent Evidence by default.

Evidence Granularity

Evidence should be associated at the lowest meaningful Connection level where practical.

This may be:

endpoint, Relationship, intermediate element, Chain segment, complete Chain, or Qualification characteristic.

Granularity prevents evidence relevant to one part from being generalized to the whole Connection.

Chain-Level Evidence

Some Evidence Objects may legitimately concern the complete Connection Chain.

Chain-level association should be used only where the evidence actually supports that scope.

Therefore:

Evidence Touching Multiple Elements ≠ Chain-Level Evidence by default.

Relationship-Level Evidence

Evidence may support a specific Connection Relationship without supporting the complete Chain.

This distinction should remain explicit.

Node-Level Evidence

Evidence may establish the identity or state of a node without establishing any connecting Relationship.

Therefore:

Node Evidence ≠ Edge Evidence.

Network-Level Evidence

In a Connection Network, evidence may concern:

one node, one edge, one Chain, several Chains, or the broader Network.

Network-level association should not erase path-specific evidentiary differences.

Multi-Subject Evidence

One Evidence Object may concern multiple Subjects.

Its effect on each Subject-specific Connection should be represented separately where materially different.

Therefore:

Shared Evidence ≠ Equal Subject Connection Support.

Multi-Basis Evidence

The same Evidence Object may possess different relevance under different Liability Bases.

Therefore:

Evidence Relevant under Basis A ≠ Evidence Relevant under Basis B by default.

Multi-Condition Evidence

Evidence may relate differently to different Liability Conditions.

Therefore:

Evidence Supporting Condition A ≠ Evidence Supporting Condition B by default.

Evidence and Operational Causation

Evidence may support an operational causal relationship.

ORA™ retains governance ownership of Operational Reality.

LCXEAM governs only how that evidence-supported state associates with the Liability Connection.

Therefore:

Evidence of Operational Causation ≠ Evidence of Liability by itself.

Evidence and Harm

Evidence may support a Harm state governed by HAGA™.

That Harm state may then participate in a Liability Connection.

Therefore:

Evidence of Harm ≠ Evidence of Liability by itself.

Evidence and Responsibility

Evidence may support Responsibility governed by AGA™.

Therefore:

Evidence of Responsibility ≠ Evidence of Liability by itself.

Evidence and Accountability

Evidence may support Accountability.

Therefore:

Evidence of Accountability ≠ Evidence of Liability by itself.

Evidence and Control

Evidence may establish Control.

Control may be relevant to a Connection.

However:

Evidence of Control ≠ Evidence of Liability by itself.

Evidence and Ownership

Evidence may establish Ownership.

Therefore:

Evidence of Ownership ≠ Evidence of Liability by itself.

Evidence and Conduct

Evidence may establish that conduct occurred.

It does not automatically establish the complete Connection from Subject to consequence.

Therefore:

Evidence of Conduct ≠ Evidence of Complete Liability Connection.

Evidence and Event

Evidence may establish that an event occurred.

Therefore:

Evidence of Event ≠ Evidence of Event Connection by default.

Evidence and Consequence

Evidence may establish a consequence.

Therefore:

Evidence of Consequence ≠ Evidence of Connection to a Particular Subject.

Evidence and Relationship

Evidence may establish an upstream Liability Relationship.

That does not automatically establish the Liability Connection Relationship.

Therefore:

Evidence of Liability Relationship ≠ Evidence of Liability Connection.

Evidence and Condition

Evidence may support a Liability Condition State.

That does not automatically support the Connection between the Condition and a particular Subject or event.

Therefore:

Evidence of Condition State ≠ Evidence of Condition Connection.

Evidence and Qualification

A Qualification State should remain distinguishable from the Evidence Objects associated with it.

Therefore:

Qualified Connection ≠ Fully Evidenced Connection by default.

LCXEAM may reveal evidentiary limitations requiring Qualification reassessment.

Evidence Association and Qualification Re-Entry

Where evidence association reveals a material defect in Qualification, LCXEAM should route the affected Connection to LCXQM.

Examples include:

previously unknown contradiction, material evidence gap, incorrect evidence scope, or invalid evidence association.

Evidence Association and Chain Re-Entry

Where evidence reveals that a Chain is incomplete or incorrectly structured, governance should return to LCXCM.

LCXEAM must not repair the Chain by silently modifying it.

Evidence Association and Relationship Re-Entry

Where evidence reveals that a Connection Relationship was incorrectly represented, governance should return to LCXRM.

Evidence Association and Identification Re-Entry

Where evidence reveals a previously unidentified Connection Candidate, governance should return to LCXIM.

Therefore:

Evidence Discovery ≠ Automatic Connection Creation.

The Connection must pass through its proper governance lifecycle.

Upstream LIGA™ Re-Entry

Evidence may reveal defects in:

Liability Object, Liability Basis, Liability Relationships, or Liability Conditions.

The inquiry should return to the earliest materially affected governance space.

LCXEAM should not absorb those upstream functions.

Evidence Association Identity

Each materially significant Evidence Association should possess sufficient identity to be:

referenced, changed, corrected, reviewed, and reconstructed.

Evidence Association identity is distinct from Evidence Object identity.

Therefore:

Evidence Identity ≠ Evidence Association Identity.

One Evidence Object, Multiple Associations

One Evidence Object may legitimately possess multiple Evidence Associations.

Example:

a system log may support:

an action time, an actor identity, and an event sequence.

Each association should preserve its own scope.

One Association, Multiple Evidence Objects

One Connection element may be supported or contradicted by multiple Evidence Objects.

Those objects should remain individually traceable.

Evidence Association Change

An Evidence Association may change because:

the Connection changes, evidence interpretation changes, evidence is corrected, scope is refined, or new information becomes available.

Change should remain traceable.

Evidence Association Correction

An earlier Evidence Association may be incorrect.

Correction should preserve:

the earlier association, corrected association, reason, time, and provenance.

Therefore:

Evidence Association Correction ≠ Historical Erasure.

Evidence Object Correction

The Evidence Object itself may be corrected by its governing evidence system.

LCXEAM should preserve the resulting impact on Connection associations.

It should not silently overwrite the historical evidence state.

Evidence Withdrawal

Evidence may be withdrawn, invalidated, superseded, or otherwise cease to be relied upon.

Historical association should remain reconstructable.

Therefore:

Evidence Withdrawal ≠ Historical Evidence Erasure.

Evidence Supersession

A later Evidence Object may supersede an earlier representation.

The earlier evidence should remain historically traceable where materially relevant.

Therefore:

Evidence Supersession ≠ Evidence Deletion.

Evidence Association Reassessment

A Connection Evidence Association may require reassessment after:

new evidence, Connection change, Qualification change, or correction.

Therefore:

Evidence Association Reassessment ≠ Historical Rewrite.

Evidence Association Versioning

Material association changes may require a new version.

Therefore:

New Evidence Association Version ≠ New Evidence Object by default.

Evidence Association Continuity

Evidence associations should remain continuous across:

system migration, record transformation, Connection versioning, and lifecycle re-entry.

Therefore:

System Migration ≠ New Evidence Association Origin by default.

Historical Reconstruction

LCXEAM should support reconstruction of:

what evidence existed, what evidence was known, what evidence was associated, how it was classified, and what Connection state it informed at materially relevant times.

Evidence Reality, Knowledge, and Record

The following should remain distinct:

Evidence Reality

Evidence Knowledge

Evidence Record

Evidence Association

An Evidence Object may exist before it becomes known.

It may become known before it is correctly associated.

Therefore:

Evidence Reality ≠ Evidence Knowledge ≠ Evidence Association by default.

Evidence Association Uncertainty

The association between evidence and a Connection may itself be uncertain.

Examples include uncertainty concerning:

which Subject the record concerns, which event it represents, whether timing aligns, or which Chain segment it supports.

This uncertainty should remain explicit.

Evidence Interpretation Uncertainty

An Evidence Object may be undisputed while its meaning for the Connection remains uncertain.

Therefore:

Evidence Certainty ≠ Association Certainty.

Evidence Relevance Uncertainty

Evidence may plausibly relate to the Connection without its relevance being fully established.

Therefore:

Potential Evidence Relevance ≠ Established Evidence Relevance.

No Evidence-to-Liability Shortcut

LCXEAM prohibits direct conversion of evidence into Liability.

Therefore:

Evidence → Liability is not a valid LCXEAM governance shortcut.

The governed progression remains:

Evidence → Connection Association → Governed Liability Connection Structure → Liability Determination

No Evidence-Volume Shortcut

A large Evidence Set should not substitute for a missing Connection.

Therefore:

More Evidence ≠ Missing Connection.

Evidence cannot repair a structurally absent Connection.

No Documentary Shortcut

A document stating that a Subject is responsible or liable does not automatically establish the governed Connection or Liability.

The document itself must be governed according to its identity, authority, scope, provenance, and relevance.

Therefore:

Statement of Liability ≠ Governed Liability Determination.

No System-Output Shortcut

An AI, algorithmic, automated, or analytical system output may become evidence or an evidentiary input where appropriately governed.

It does not become Liability truth automatically.

Therefore:

System Output ≠ Liability Determination.

No Expert-Output Shortcut

An expert assessment may become a governed evidentiary input.

Its existence does not eliminate the need for Connection governance.

Therefore:

Expert Assessment ≠ Liability Connection by itself.

No Claim Shortcut

A Claim may contain evidence or reference evidence.

The Claim itself does not establish the Connection.

Therefore:

Claim Evidence ≠ Liability Connection by default.

Evidence Association Sufficiency

Evidence Association is sufficient for LCNS completion when materially relevant Connection elements possess:

explicit evidence associations where evidence exists, explicit contradiction where evidence conflicts, explicit gaps where evidence is absent or unavailable, explicit uncertainty where association is unresolved, and sufficient traceability for downstream Liability Determination.

Evidence Association Sufficiency and Proof

Association sufficiency does not mean the evidence proves the Connection conclusively.

Therefore:

Evidence Association Sufficiency ≠ Evidentiary Proof.

Connection Evidence Completeness

Connection Evidence Completeness concerns whether all materially necessary Connection elements have an explicit evidentiary state.

That state may be:

supported, contradicted, limited, neutral, unknown, missing, unavailable, pending, or disputed.

Completeness therefore does not require universal supporting evidence.

Evidence Completeness and Liability

A completely mapped Evidence Association structure does not establish Liability.

Therefore:

Complete Evidence Mapping ≠ Liability Determination.

Governed Liability Connection Structure

The principal output of LCXEAM and the completed LCNS module sequence is:

Governed Liability Connection Structure

This structure may contain:

Connection identities, Connection Relationships, Connection Chains, Connection Networks, Connection Qualification States, evidence associations, supporting evidence references, contradicting evidence references, limiting evidence references, contextual evidence references, neutral evidence references, Evidence Gaps, evidence conflicts, uncertainties, provenance references, validation references, temporal associations, association history, and reconstruction information.

Meaning of the Output

A Governed Liability Connection Structure means:

the Liability-specific connective structure has been identified, its Relationships have been structured, its Chains have been constructed, its Connections have been qualified, and the relevant evidence has been explicitly associated.

It does not mean:

Liability exists.

Therefore:

Governed Liability Connection Structure ≠ Liability Determination.

Handoff to Liability Determination

The canonical architecture handoff is:

Governed Liability Connection Structure → Liability Determination LDS may then evaluate whether Liability can legitimately attach and be determined on the established governed basis.

LCXEAM does not perform that Determination.

Minimum Implementation Framework

A conforming implementation of LCXEAM must provide exactly the following eight capabilities:

Evidence Object & Association Identification

Capability to reference governed Evidence Objects and create explicit, traceable Evidence Associations without duplicating the governance ownership of evidence itself.

Connection Element & Scope Association

Capability to associate evidence at the appropriate Connection level — endpoint, Relationship, intermediate element, Chain segment, complete Chain, Network path, or Qualification characteristic — with explicit scope.

Evidentiary Effect Governance

Capability to distinguish supporting, contradicting, limiting, contextual, neutral, and mixed evidentiary effects without converting evidence into Liability conclusions.

Evidence Coverage, Gap & Dependency Governance

Capability to represent partial coverage, Evidence Gaps, missing, unknown, unavailable, inaccessible and pending evidence, duplication, dependency, independence, and corroboration.

Conflict, Uncertainty & Multi-Path Evidence Governance

Capability to preserve conflicting evidence, uncertain associations, alternative interpretations, multi-Subject effects, multiBasis relevance, and path-specific evidentiary differences.

Provenance, Validation, Temporal & Traceability Association

Capability to preserve references to evidence provenance, validation, audit states, temporal relevance, discovery time, and sufficient association traceability without taking ownership of those external governance functions.

Association Change, Correction, Continuity & Re-Entry

Capability to preserve Evidence Association changes, corrections, withdrawals, supersession, reassessment, versioning, historical states, lifecycle continuity, and targeted re-entry without historical erasure.

Evidence Association Sufficiency & Liability Determination Handoff

Capability to determine whether the Liability Connection evidence structure is sufficiently explicit, differentiated, and reconstructable to form the final Governed Liability Connection Structure for handoff to Liability Determination.

Cross-Architecture Boundary

LCXEAM is intentionally dependent upon, but separate from, evidence governance.

ADIT® governs evidence, provenance, auditability, evidence lifecycle, and related evidentiary structures.

VALIDOS™ governs validation.

ORA™ may provide evidence-supported Operational Reality states.

HAGA™ may provide evidence-supported Harm states.

AGA™ may provide evidence-supported Responsibility, Accountability, Authority, and Attribution states.

GOA™ may provide evidence-supported Authority, Scope, and Delegation structures.

OBIDENITY® may provide evidence-supported Identity, Ownership, Permission, Provenance, Transfer, and Continuity states.

AIG® and ASGA™ may provide evidence-supported AI and autonomous-system governance states.

LCXEAM does not duplicate these governance domains.

It governs only:

how governed evidence and externally governed states are associated with the Liability Connection structure.

The canonical interoperability principle is:

Evidence remains governed by its evidence authority; LCXEAM governs only the explicit association between that evidence and the Liability-specific Connection structure.

Canonical Boundary

LCXEAM begins with a:

Qualified Liability Connection Set

and asks:

What governed evidence supports, contradicts, limits, contextualizes, or leaves unresolved each materially relevant Liability Connection?

LCXEAM governs:

Evidence Association Identification, Connection-Level Evidence Association, Relationship-Level Evidence Association, Chain-Level Evidence Association, Endpoint Evidence Association, Intermediate Element Evidence Association, Qualification Evidence Association, Supporting Evidence, Contradicting Evidence, Limiting Evidence, Contextual Evidence, Neutral Evidence, Mixed Evidence, Evidence Scope, Evidence Relevance, Evidence Coverage, Evidence Gaps, Evidence Conflict, Evidence Duplication, Evidence Dependency, Evidence Corroboration, Evidence Independence, Provenance References, Validation References, Audit References, Temporal Association, Discovery Time, Evidence Association Uncertainty, Association Change, Correction, Withdrawal, Supersession, Reassessment, Versioning, Historical Reconstruction, Continuity, Re-Entry, and Evidence Association Sufficiency.

LCXEAM does not govern:

evidence creation itself, evidence validation itself, evidence provenance itself, jurisdiction-specific evidentiary admissibility, Operational Reality itself, Harm itself, Responsibility itself, Accountability itself, Fault, Claim validity, Liability Attachment, Liability Determination, Liability Scope, Liability Allocation, Liability State, Liability Transfer, Recourse, Resolution, Discharge, or Finality.

LCXEAM ends when a:

Governed Liability Connection Structure

is sufficiently explicit, traceable, qualified, evidence-associated, and reconstructable for Liability Determination.

Use Case 1 — Autonomous System Evidence Association

An autonomous industrial system performs an action followed by an equipment incident.

LCXQM has qualified a Connection Chain:

Operator → Autonomous System → Execution Decision → Machine Action → Operational Event → Equipment

State

LCXEAM associates evidence with individual elements of that Chain.

A system log may support the Execution Decision.

A machine record may support the Machine Action.

A sensor record may support the Operational Event.

An operator statement may contextualize the operator-to-system Relationship.

A separate technical record may contradict the assumed timing of one intermediate step.

LCXEAM preserves each association at its proper scope rather than treating all available evidence as general proof of the complete Chain.

The resulting structure is ready for Liability Determination without asserting that Liability exists.

Use Case 2 — Multi-Party Maintenance Evidence Network

A technical incident involves an owner, operator, maintenance provider, and component producer.

LCXQM has produced different Qualification States for several Connection paths.

LCXEAM associates maintenance records with the maintenance-provider path, operational logs with the operator path, production records with the producer path, and technical measurements with the relevant equipment states.

Some evidence supports individual Relationships.

Some evidence contradicts the proposed sequence of events.

One material Chain segment contains an Evidence Gap.

LCXEAM preserves those differences instead of combining all records into one undifferentiated evidence pool.

The completed Governed Liability Connection Structure allows LDS to evaluate each relevant Connection using an explicit, traceable, and differentiated evidentiary structure.

Canonical Closing Statement

A Liability Connection should not enter Determination as an unsupported diagram, an assumed narrative, or a conclusion assembled from an undifferentiated evidence pool.

The relationship between evidence and Connection must itself be governed.

Evidence may support a Connection.

Evidence may contradict it.

Evidence may limit it.

Evidence may leave part of it unresolved.

None of those states is equivalent to Liability.

LCXEAM completes the Liability Connection lifecycle by making the evidentiary structure of the Connection explicit and reconstructable.

Evidence supports the Connection. It does not become the Connection, and it does not determine the Liability.

Associate the evidence with the Connection before determining the Liability.
