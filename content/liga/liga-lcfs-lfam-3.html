Canonical Definition

The Liability Finality Assessment Module (LFAM) governs the assessment of whether an explicitly bounded Liability requirement, Liability Relationship, group of Liability Relationships, or Liability Network has satisfied the conditions necessary to legitimately reach a governed Finality state.

LFAM evaluates the applicable Finality Object, Scope, Basis, authority, Resolution and Discharge status, remaining active Liability, secondary Liability Relationships, Recourse, network dependencies, unresolved conditions, uncertainty, disputes, historical reconstructability, and potential Re-entry requirements before Finality may be established.

It prevents Resolution, Discharge, payment, Settlement, Claim closure, administrative closure, or another terminal-looking event from being treated automatically as completion of the Liability lifecycle.

Its principal output is:

Governed Liability Finality Assessment

Its foundational distinction is:

Liability Resolution ≠ Liability Finality.

Operational Role

The operational role of LFAM is:

to determine whether a defined Liability structure is sufficiently resolved, bounded, historically reconstructable, free from material unresolved active requirements within the assessed Scope, and otherwise qualified to leave active Liability governance and enter a governed Finality state.

Position in the LCFS Internal Flow

The LCFS internal module progression is:

Liability Continuity Identification → Liability Historical Record → Liability Finality Assessment → Liability ReEntry → Liability Finality Record LFAM governs:

Liability Finality Assessment

Its principal inputs are:

Governed Liability Continuity Identification Structure

and

Governed Liability Historical Record

together with the applicable:

Governed Liability Resolution & Discharge State

Its principal output is:

Governed Liability Finality Assessment

The output proceeds to:

LREM — Liability Re-Entry Module

where Re-entry requirements are governed before terminal Finality recording.

Central Operational Question

Has the defined Liability structure satisfied the governed conditions necessary to leave active Liability governance and legitimately reach Finality within its specified Scope, or does active governance or Re-entry remain necessary?

Finality Object

Every Finality assessment requires an explicit Finality Object.

The Finality Object may be:

a Liability requirement, a Liability Relationship, a defined group of Liability Relationships, a Recourse Relationship, or a Liability Network.

Finality Object vs Liability Object

The Finality Object is not necessarily identical to the original Liability Object.

Therefore:

Finality Object ≠ Liability Object by default.

Finality Object Identity

The Finality Object should remain traceable to the Liability lifecycle from which it emerged.

Finality assessment should not be performed against an unidentified or historically disconnected terminal record.

Finality Scope

Every Finality assessment should establish the exact Scope within which Finality is being considered.

The Scope may be:

requirement-specific, Subject-specific, Relationship-specific, multi-Relationship, Recourse-specific, or network-wide.

Claimed Finality vs Assessed Finality

A claim that Liability is final should not determine the assessment result.

Therefore:

Claimed Finality ≠ Governed Finality.

Finality Scope Boundary

The assessment should identify what falls inside and outside the proposed Finality Scope.

This prevents local Resolution from being represented as wider lifecycle completion.

Partial Finality

A bounded component of a Liability structure may qualify for Finality while other components remain active.

Therefore:

Partial Finality ≠ Complete Liability Finality.

Relationship Finality

A Liability Relationship may qualify for Finality independently from other Relationships where its own lifecycle conditions are satisfied.

Network Finality

Liability Network Finality requires assessment of the relevant connected Liability Relationships rather than one Relationship alone.

Therefore:

Liability Relationship Finality ≠ Liability Network Finality.

Local Finality

A local Finality state should remain bounded to its legitimate Scope.

Therefore:

Local Liability Finality ≠ Liability Network Finality.

Subject-Specific Finality

One Subject's Liability may qualify for Finality while another Subject's Liability remains active.

Therefore:

Subject A Finality ≠ Subject B Finality by default.

Primary Liability Finality

Primary Liability may qualify for Finality independently from secondary Liability.

Recourse Finality

Recourse Relationships possess their own lifecycle.

Therefore:

Primary Liability Finality ≠ Recourse Liability Finality.

Shared Liability Finality

Where Liability is distributed across multiple Subjects, LFAM should assess the applicable Relationship and Subject Scope rather than assuming one shared terminal state.

Finality Basis

Every positive Finality assessment should possess an identifiable Finality Basis.

The Basis explains why active Liability governance may legitimately end for the assessed Finality Object.

Resolution Basis vs Finality Basis

Resolution and Finality answer different governance questions.

Therefore:

Resolution Basis ≠ Finality Basis by default.

Finality Basis Sufficiency

A Finality Basis should be sufficient for the Scope of Finality claimed.

Therefore:

Finality-Relevant Basis ≠ Sufficient Finality Basis by default.

Finality Preconditions

LFAM should assess whether the relevant prerequisites for Finality have been sufficiently established.

These may include:

verified Resolution or Discharge where required, defined Finality Object, defined Finality Scope, known remaining active Liability, secondary Relationship reconciliation, Recourse reconciliation, network reconciliation where applicable, required conditions, sufficient authority, historical reconstructability, and absence of an unresolved material Re-entry requirement.

Resolution Verification

LFAM should use the governed Resolution & Discharge state produced through LRDS.

It should not treat a Resolution claim as sufficient merely because a matter appears closed.

Resolution vs Finality

The foundational rule is:

Liability Resolution ≠ Liability Finality.

Resolution answers whether an active Liability requirement has legitimately ended.

Finality answers whether the applicable Liability lifecycle may legitimately leave active governance.

Discharge vs Finality

Discharge may establish that a requirement no longer requires active performance.

It does not automatically establish lifecycle Finality.

Therefore:

Liability Discharge ≠ Liability Finality.

Satisfaction vs Finality

Satisfaction may support Resolution without establishing Finality.

Therefore:

Liability Satisfaction ≠ Liability Finality.

Payment vs Finality

Payment may satisfy a monetary requirement.

It does not establish Finality by itself.

Therefore:

Payment ≠ Liability Finality.

Full Payment vs Finality

Even full payment may leave:

non-monetary requirements, secondary Liability, Recourse, conditional obligations, or other Liability Relationships active.

Therefore:

Full Payment ≠ Complete Liability Finality by default.

Performance vs Finality

Completion of performance does not independently establish Finality.

Therefore:

Performance Completion ≠ Liability Finality.

Settlement vs Finality

Settlement may support Resolution.

It does not independently establish Finality.

Therefore:

Settlement ≠ Liability Finality.

Settlement Completion vs Finality

Completion of a Settlement should be assessed within its Scope.

Therefore:

Settlement Completion ≠ Liability Finality by default.

Release vs Finality

A release may affect the applicable Liability requirement but should not automatically be interpreted as Finality beyond its Scope.

Therefore:

Release ≠ Universal Liability Finality.

Waiver vs Finality

A waiver does not erase Liability history or automatically establish complete Finality.

Therefore:

Waiver ≠ Liability Finality by default.

Extinguishment vs Finality

Extinguishment of an active requirement may support Finality assessment.

It does not independently establish Finality of all connected Liability structures.

Therefore:

Liability Extinguishment ≠ Liability Finality by default.

Administrative Closure

Administrative systems may mark a matter closed for operational purposes.

Therefore:

Administrative Closure ≠ Liability Finality.

Claim Closure

A Claim may close independently from Liability.

Therefore:

Claim Closure ≠ Liability Finality.

Claim Payment

Payment of a Claim does not establish Liability Finality.

Therefore:

Claim Payment ≠ Liability Finality.

Operational Termination

Termination of the operational mechanism or process from which Liability relevance arose does not establish Finality.

Therefore:

Operational Termination ≠ Liability Finality.

Contract Completion

Completion or termination of a contract does not automatically establish Finality of Liability associated with it.

Therefore:

Contract Completion ≠ Liability Finality.

Harm Finality

Harm and Liability possess separate governance lifecycles.

Therefore:

Harm Finality ≠ Liability Finality.

Accountability Closure

Completion of Accountability governance does not establish Liability Finality.

Therefore:

Accountability Closure ≠ Liability Finality.

Audit Completion

Completion of an audit does not establish Liability Finality.

Therefore:

Audit Completion ≠ Liability Finality.

Validation Completion

Validation of relevant inputs or outcomes does not establish Liability Finality.

Therefore:

Validation Completion ≠ Liability Finality.

Remaining Active Liability

LFAM should identify whether any active Liability remains inside the proposed Finality Scope.

A material active requirement ordinarily prevents unconditional Finality for that same Scope.

Residual Active Requirement

A remaining requirement should not disappear because another requirement has resolved.

Therefore:

Resolved Component ≠ Resolved Liability Structure.

Active Liability Outside Finality Scope

Liability may remain active outside the proposed Finality Scope without necessarily preventing bounded Finality inside that Scope.

The boundary should remain explicit.

Secondary Liability

Secondary Liability should be identified before complete Finality is represented.

Recourse Continuation

Recourse may continue after primary Liability has resolved.

Therefore:

Primary Liability Resolution ≠ Recourse Resolution.

Primary Finality with Active Recourse

Primary Liability may qualify for Relationship Finality while Recourse remains active.

The resulting assessment should not represent the complete Liability Network as Final.

Liability Network Completeness

Network Finality requires that the relevant Relationships within the assessed network have been sufficiently accounted for.

Network Gap

An unidentified or unresolved material Relationship may prevent reliable network-wide Finality.

Therefore:

Incomplete Liability Network ≠ Verified Network Finality.

Finality Conditions

Finality may depend on explicit conditions.

LFAM should identify whether each material condition is:

satisfied, unsatisfied, pending, unknown, uncertain, disputed, or no longer applicable.

Unresolved Finality Condition

Therefore:

Unresolved Required Finality Condition ≠ Unconditional Finality.

Conditional Finality

Where Finality depends on continuing conditions, the assessment should preserve that conditionality.

Therefore:

Conditional Finality ≠ Unconditional Finality.

Finality Dependencies

Dependencies necessary for Finality should be identified explicitly.

Unresolved Dependency

A material unresolved dependency may prevent Finality or require a conditional or pending assessment.

Finality Authority

Where Finality requires authority, LFAM should identify:

the relevant authority, its Scope, its effective period, and its relationship to the Finality Object.

Authority to Resolve vs Authority to Establish Finality

Authority to resolve a Liability requirement does not automatically establish authority over lifecycle Finality.

Therefore:

Resolution Authority ≠ Finality Authority by default.

Local Authority

Authority over one Relationship does not automatically extend to the entire Liability Network.

Therefore:

Local Finality Authority ≠ Universal Liability Finality Authority.

Authority Conflict

Where authority is materially disputed or conflicting, the conflict should remain explicit.

Therefore:

Authority Conflict ≠ Governed Finality.

Historical Reconstructability

Finality assessment should use the Governed Liability Historical Record.

A Liability lifecycle proposed for Finality should remain sufficiently reconstructable to explain how it reached the terminal state.

Finality Without History

A terminal status unsupported by sufficient lifecycle history should not automatically be treated as governed Finality.

Therefore:

Terminal Status ≠ Historically Supported Finality.

Historical Gap

A historical gap should be assessed for materiality.

Not every missing record prevents Finality.

A gap that prevents reconstruction of a material Finality dependency may do so.

Historical Completeness vs Record Volume

Finality does not require every available record.

It requires sufficient material history.

Therefore:

Record Volume ≠ Finality Readiness.

Continuity Sufficiency

LFAM should determine whether Liability identity and Relationship continuity are sufficiently established for the proposed Finality Object.

Broken Continuity

Where the Finality Object cannot be reliably connected to the Liability lifecycle, Finality assessment should remain unresolved.

Finality Readiness

LFAM should distinguish whether the Liability structure is ready for Finality assessment from whether Finality itself is established.

Therefore:

Finality Readiness ≠ Liability Finality.

Finality Assessment State

LFAM may represent assessment states such as:

Not Ready for Finality, Assessment Pending, Finality Conditions Not Met, Partially Qualified, Conditionally Qualified, Qualified for Finality, Re-Entry Assessment Required, Unknown, Uncertain, Disputed, or another explicitly governed state.

These are methodological governance states, not universal legal classifications.

Qualified for Finality

A Qualified for Finality assessment means that the assessed Liability structure has satisfied the applicable methodological conditions for Finality within the defined Scope.

It does not erase history.

Partially Qualified

Where only part of the Finality Object satisfies Finality conditions, the assessment should remain bounded.

Therefore:

Partial Qualification ≠ Complete Finality Qualification.

Unknown Finality Assessment

Where available information is insufficient, the assessment should remain Unknown.

Therefore:

Unknown Finality ≠ No Finality by assumption.

Uncertain Finality Assessment

Where materially plausible alternatives remain, uncertainty should remain explicit.

Therefore:

Uncertain Finality ≠ Authority to Select a Preferred Finality State.

Disputed Finality Assessment

Where Finality is materially disputed, the dispute should remain represented.

Therefore:

Disputed Finality ≠ Governed Finality.

Finality Effective Time

LFAM should identify the proposed or established effective time relevant to Finality where sufficiently supported.

Resolution Time vs Finality Time

Therefore:

Resolution Effective Time ≠ Finality Effective Time by default.

Discharge Time vs Finality Time

Therefore:

Discharge Effective Time ≠ Finality Effective Time by default.

Assessment Time vs Finality Time

The time at which Finality is assessed may differ from the time at which Finality becomes effective.

Therefore:

Finality Assessment Time ≠ Finality Effective Time by default.

Recording Time vs Finality Time

Therefore:

Finality Recording Time ≠ Finality Effective Time by default.

Finality Evidence

Evidence may support Finality assessment.

Evidence does not independently establish Finality.

Therefore:

Evidence of Finality-Relevant Facts ≠ Liability Finality.

Evidence Sufficiency

Evidence sufficiency should be assessed against the specific Finality Object, Scope, Basis, conditions, and dependencies.

Provenance

The assessment should remain traceable to the governed inputs upon which it relies.

Finality Rationale

LFAM should preserve sufficient rationale to explain:

what was assessed, within what Scope, against what conditions, what remained active, what dependencies were considered, and why the resulting assessment state was reached.

Finality Assessment vs Finality Rationale

Therefore:

Finality Assessment State ≠ Finality Rationale.

Re-Entry Relevance

LFAM should identify whether information discovered during Finality assessment creates a potential requirement for Reentry.

Re-Entry Trigger vs Finality Failure

Not every failed Finality assessment requires upstream Re-entry.

A Liability structure may simply remain active.

Therefore:

Finality Not Established ≠ Re-Entry Required by default.

Re-Entry Trigger

Where the assessment identifies a material defect or changed condition in an earlier governance space, it should produce a Re-entry requirement for downstream governance by LREM.

Re-Entry vs New Liability

A potential Re-entry should remain associated with the existing Liability lifecycle unless a genuinely new Liability Origin is established.

Therefore:

Re-entry ≠ New Liability by default.

Historical Integrity

Finality assessment should not rewrite earlier states.

If an earlier state is later found incorrect, correction or Re-entry should preserve the prior historical record.

Finality Reassessment

A Finality assessment may later require reassessment if materially relevant circumstances change.

Therefore:

Finality Reassessment ≠ Finality Correction.

Finality Correction

Where the earlier assessment itself was incorrect based on information available at the time, a correction may be required.

The prior assessment should remain reconstructable.

Therefore:

Finality Correction ≠ Historical Erasure.

Finality Supersession

A later assessment may supersede an earlier assessment.

Therefore:

Finality Supersession ≠ Historical Deletion.

Finality Reversal

Where a previously established Finality State later legitimately re-enters active governance, the historical Finality should remain preserved.

Therefore:

Finality Reversal ≠ Finality History Deletion.

Multiple Finality Assessments

One Liability lifecycle may contain multiple Finality assessments over time.

Each should remain historically distinguishable.

Relationship Assessment vs Network Assessment

LFAM should maintain separate assessment levels where necessary.

A positive Relationship-level assessment should not be silently elevated into a positive Network-level assessment.

Finality Propagation

Finality should not automatically propagate between connected Liability Relationships.

Therefore:

Relationship Connection ≠ Automatic Finality Propagation.

Cross-Architecture Finality

Finality in another governance architecture may provide relevant input but does not establish Liability Finality.

Harm Finality Input

HAGA™ may establish Harm Finality.

LFAM determines its relevance, if any, to Liability Finality.

Therefore:

Harm Finality ≠ Liability Finality.

Operational Closure Input

ORA™ may establish termination of an operational mechanism or process.

Therefore:

Operational Closure ≠ Liability Finality.

Accountability Closure Input

AGA™ may establish completion of Accountability governance.

Therefore:

Accountability Closure ≠ Liability Finality.

Audit and Validation Inputs

ADIT® and VALIDOS™ may provide evidence and validation relevant to Finality assessment.

Their outputs do not replace the Liability Finality assessment.

Automated Finality Assessment

AI or automated systems may assist with:

Finality Object identification, Scope reconciliation, remaining Liability detection, Relationship and network reconciliation, condition checking, dependency checking, historical reconstruction, Recourse detection, conflict detection, and Finality readiness assessment.

Automation should preserve uncertainty and authority boundaries.

Therefore:

Automated Finality Assessment ≠ Authoritative Liability Finality by default.

Finality Assessment Failure

Where Finality cannot be sufficiently assessed, LFAM should preserve the reason.

Possible reasons include:

insufficient continuity, material historical gap, unresolved active Liability, unknown secondary Relationship, unresolved Recourse, unsatisfied condition, authority conflict, uncertainty, dispute, or potential Re-entry requirement.

No Forced Finality

LFAM should never convert insufficient information into Finality merely to close a lifecycle.

The canonical principle is:

Absence of visible active Liability ≠ Evidence of Finality.

Finality Assessment Completeness

A Finality assessment is sufficiently complete when the material requirements relevant to the defined Finality Object and Scope have been accounted for.

Governed Liability Finality Assessment

The principal output of LFAM is:

Governed Liability Finality Assessment

It should identify, where applicable:

Finality Object, Finality Scope, affected Subjects, affected Liability Relationships, Liability Network Scope, Finality Basis, Resolution status, Discharge status, remaining active Liability, secondary Liability, Recourse status, conditions, dependencies, authority, continuity sufficiency, historical reconstructability, historical gaps, Finality readiness, assessment State, proposed Finality Effective Time, evidence associations, uncertainty, disputes, conflicts, potential Re-entry requirements, rationale, and provenance.

Meaning of the Output

A Governed Liability Finality Assessment establishes whether the defined Liability structure is methodologically qualified to proceed toward Finality, remain active, remain conditional or uncertain, or undergo Re-entry assessment.

It is not the terminal Finality Record.

It does not erase the Liability history.

It provides the governed assessment required before Re-entry and terminal Finality recording.

Minimum Implementation Framework

A conforming implementation of LFAM must provide exactly the following eight capabilities:

Finality Object, Subject, Relationship, Network & Scope Identification

Capability to identify the exact Finality Object, affected Subjects and Liability Relationships, relevant Liability Network boundary, and the Scope within which Finality is being assessed.

Finality Basis, Resolution, Discharge & Active Liability Assessment

Capability to assess the Finality Basis, verified Resolution and Discharge states, remaining active Liability, and whether the applicable active requirements have sufficiently ended.

Secondary Liability, Transfer, Sharing, Recourse & Network Assessment

Capability to identify secondary Liability Relationships, transferred or shared structures, Recourse, and network dependencies and to prevent local Finality from being represented as wider Finality.

Finality Condition, Dependency, Authority & Temporal Assessment

Capability to assess required Finality conditions, dependencies, authority, authority Scope, effective time, and conditional or pending Finality states.

Continuity, Historical Reconstructability, Evidence & Provenance Assessment

Capability to assess whether Liability identity, historical continuity, material lifecycle history, evidence associations, and provenance are sufficient to support Finality assessment.

Uncertainty, Dispute, Conflict, Gap & Finality Qualification Governance

Capability to preserve Unknown, Uncertain, Disputed, Partial, Conditional, and Not-Ready states and prevent unsupported Finality where material gaps or conflicts remain.

Re-Entry Relevance, Correction, Reassessment & Historical Integrity Governance

Capability to identify potential Re-entry requirements, distinguish active continuation from Re-entry, govern correction and reassessment, and preserve prior Finality assessments without rewriting history.

Governed Finality Assessment Formation & Re-Entry Handoff Governance

Capability to produce a Governed Liability Finality Assessment sufficiently bounded, reasoned, traceable, historically reconstructable, and explicit for Liability Re-Entry governance and subsequent Finality recording.

Cross-Architecture Boundary

LFAM may use governed inputs from adjacent OOF® architectures when those inputs are relevant to Liability Finality.

ORA™ may establish Operational Reality relevant to whether performance, termination, or another event occurred.

HAGA™ may establish Harm Resolution or Harm Finality.

AGA™ may establish Responsibility, Accountability, attribution, or authority states.

ADIT® may provide evidence and auditability.

VALIDOS™ may validate Finality-relevant inputs.

OBIDENITY® may provide identity, Origin, provenance, succession, and authority information.

VFM® may provide payment or Value Flow information.

AIG® and ASGA™ may provide relevant AI or autonomous-system events.

Claims governance may establish Claim closure or Claim Finality.

These inputs may inform LFAM.

They do not determine Liability Finality merely because their own lifecycle has ended.

The canonical interoperability principle is:

Finality in an adjacent governance domain may be relevant to Liability Finality, but only LFAM assesses whether the Liability lifecycle itself satisfies the conditions necessary to proceed toward governed Finality.

Canonical Boundary

LFAM begins when a Liability structure has sufficient continuity, historical reconstruction, and Resolution or Discharge information to require Finality assessment.

LFAM governs:

Finality Objects, Finality Scope, Subjects, Liability Relationships, Liability Network boundaries, Finality Basis, Resolution status, Discharge status, remaining active Liability, secondary Liability, Recourse, Finality conditions, dependencies, authority, effective time, continuity sufficiency, historical reconstructability, evidence associations, uncertainty, disputes, conflicts, Finality readiness, Finality qualification, potential Re-entry requirements, correction, reassessment, and assessment provenance.

LFAM does not independently execute Re-entry.

LFAM does not produce the terminal Finality Record.

LFAM ends when the applicable Finality assessment is sufficiently represented as a:

Governed Liability Finality Assessment

for Re-entry governance and subsequent Finality recording.

Use Case 1 — Primary Liability Resolved but Recourse

Remains Active

A primary Liability Relationship has been fully satisfied, resolved, and discharged.

A secondary Recourse Relationship remains active between other Subjects.

LFAM may determine that the primary Liability Relationship is qualified for Relationship-level Finality while the wider Liability Network is not qualified for Network Finality.

The active Recourse Relationship remains visible and independently governed.

Primary Liability Finality ≠ Recourse Liability Finality.

Use Case 2 — Matter Marked Closed with an Unresolved

Requirement

An administrative system marks a Liability matter as closed after payment.

Historical reconstruction shows that a required corrective action remains incomplete.

LFAM identifies the unresolved active requirement and determines that the complete Liability Relationship is not yet qualified for Finality.

The administrative closure therefore does not become a methodological Finality determination.

Administrative Closure ≠ Liability Finality.

Canonical Closing Statement

Finality is not the absence of visible activity.

It is not a payment.

It is not a Settlement.

It is not a Discharge.

It is not a closed Claim or a closed administrative record.

Finality requires a defined object, a bounded Scope, a sufficient Basis, resolved material requirements, reconciled Relationships, preserved history, and no unresolved governance condition that prevents the applicable Liability lifecycle from legitimately ending.

Liability Resolution ≠ Liability Finality.

Assess what has actually completed before allowing the Liability lifecycle to leave active governance.
