Liability Governance Architecture — Liability Governance Layer

Official Liability Governance Architecture Map for Individuals, Groups, Organizations, Physical and Digital Objects, AI Systems, Autonomous Systems,

Infrastructure, Environments, Institutions, Networks, and Future Operational

This document serves as the official OOF® Liability Governance architecture map and structural index used for Liability Governance design, Liability Lifecycle navigation, Liability diagnostics, architecture development, Liability Governance assessment, lifecycle governance, Liability state analysis, Liability Resolution and Finality governance, and interoperability across individuals, groups, organizations, physical and digital objects, AI systems, autonomous systems, infrastructure, environments, institutions, networks, and future operational systems.

LIGA™ consists of 12 Parent Standards and 60 Core Modules.

Additional standards and modules may be added when new governable Liability spaces are identified. However, the standards and modules listed below form the core structural backbone of LIGA™.

Architectural Position

LIGA™ governs the complete Liability Governance lifecycle from Liability-relevant Context and Liability Object formation through Liability Resolution, Discharge, Continuity, Re-Entry, and Finality.

The architecture progresses:

Liability Context → Liability Object → Liability Basis → Liability Relationships → Liability Conditions → Liability Connection → Liability Determination → Liability Scope & Allocation → Liability State & Change → Liability Transfer & Recourse → Liability Resolution & Discharge → Liability Continuity & Finality Together these standards govern the context in which Liability may become relevant, what exact matter forms the Liability Object, on what Basis Liability may potentially exist, which Subjects and Relationships participate in the Liability structure, what Conditions must exist or be satisfied, what Liability-Relevant Connection exists, whether Liability can legitimately attach and be determined, for what and to what extent Liability applies, how Liability is allocated, what its current State is and how it changes, how Liability or its consequences may move or create secondary and Recourse Relationships, when active Liability may be resolved or discharged, and when the Liability lifecycle can legitimately reach Finality while remaining historically reconstructable and capable of governed Re-Entry.

Its complete lifecycle progresses:

Potential Liability → Attached / Determined Liability → Active Liability Governance → Liability Change → Transfer / Recourse where applicable → Resolution / Discharge → Continuity / Re-Entry where required → Finality Liability Determination therefore does not complete the Liability lifecycle.

Liability Determination ≠ Liability Lifecycle Completion.

Where material Liability conditions or information change:

Governed Liability → Liability State Change → Scope / Allocation Change where applicable → Transfer / Recourse where applicable → Resolution / Discharge → Finality Assessment Where Resolution or Discharge is sufficient and terminal conditions are established:

Resolution / Discharge → Continuity & Historical Reconstruction → Finality Assessment → Finality Record →

Governed Liability Finality State

Where material information, conditions, events, corrections, reversals, conflicts, or other developments require renewed governance:

Resolution / Finality → Re-Entry → Earliest Materially Affected LIGA™ Governance Space → Renewed Liability Governance → Resolution / Discharge → Continuity & Finality This recursive structure allows Liability Governance to remain responsive to Liability State change, Scope change, Allocation change, new or changed Relationships, Transfer, Sharing, succession, Recourse, failed Resolution conditions, payment reversal, Settlement failure, new materially relevant information, correction, Reassessment, Reopening, and legitimate post-Finality Re-Entry.

Re-enter the earliest materially affected governance space.

Reassess what materially changed. Preserve what remains valid.

Re-entry must not rewrite history.

Cross-Architecture Boundary

LIGA™ ↔ ORA™

ORA™ governs what is actually occurring in Operational Reality. LIGA™ governs whether and how that Operational Reality becomes relevant to Liability and what Liability state results from the complete governed Liability lifecycle.

Operational Reality ≠ Liability.

Operational Causation ≠ Liability-Relevant Connection.

LIGA™ ↔ HAGA™

HAGA™ governs the Harm lifecycle. LIGA™ governs the Liability lifecycle.

Harm may provide Liability-relevant input where applicable, but Harm is not a universal prerequisite for Liability.

Harm ≠ Liability.

Harm Determination ≠ Liability Determination.

Harm Resolution ≠ Liability Resolution.

Harm Finality ≠ Liability Finality.

LIGA™ ↔ AGA™

LIGA™ governs Liability. AGA™ governs authority, responsibility, accountability, attribution, and related accountability relationships surrounding actions, decisions, omissions, systems, and consequences.

Responsibility ≠ Liability.

Accountability ≠ Liability.

Accountability Attribution ≠ Liability Allocation.

LIGA™ ↔ ADIT®

LIGA™ governs Liability and its lifecycle states. ADIT® governs evidential observation, preservation, verification, correlation, examination, and auditability.

Evidence of Liability-Relevant Facts ≠ Liability Determination.

LIGA™ ↔ VALIDOS™

LIGA™ governs Liability Determination and subsequent Liability lifecycle governance. VALIDOS™ governs whether a validation basis sufficiently supports a Validation Determination and intended reliance.

Validation Determination ≠ Liability Determination.

LIGA™ ↔ INTEGROS®

LIGA™ governs Liability as a distinct lifecycle object. INTEGROS® governs integrity across objects, states, evidence, and processes.

Liability Governance ≠ General Integrity Governance.

LIGA™ ↔ OBIDENITY®

LIGA™ governs Liability Subjects and Liability Relationships. OBIDENITY® governs Origin, Identity, Ownership, Authority, Permission, Responsibility, Provenance, Transfer, Continuity, and Auditability.

Identity ≠ Liability Subject Determination.

Identity Continuity ≠ Liability Continuity by itself.

LIGA™ ↔ AIG®

LIGA™ governs Liability that may arise in relation to AI behavior. AIG® governs AI behavioral governance and behavioral integrity.

AI Behavior ≠ Liability.

AI Behavioral Failure ≠ Liability Determination.

LIGA™ ↔ ASGA™

LIGA™ governs Liability that may arise in relation to autonomous operation. ASGA™ governs autonomous-system authority, execution, constraints, coordination, intervention, escalation, and operational governability.

Autonomous Action ≠ Liability.

Autonomous-System Failure ≠ Liability Determination.

LIGA™ ↔ VFM®

LIGA™ governs what Liability exists and what happens to that Liability throughout its lifecycle. VFM® governs what happens to Value as a consequence of events and conditions.

Value Loss ≠ Liability.

Payment ≠ Liability Admission.

Value Transfer ≠ Liability Transfer by default.

LIGA™ ↔ SIMULOS®

LIGA™ governs actual Liability. SIMULOS® governs simulation purpose, structure, execution, outputs, and lifecycle.

Simulated Liability ≠ Actual Liability.

Simulated Liability Determination ≠ Governed Liability Determination.

LIGA™ ↔ Claims Systems

LIGA™ governs Liability. Claims systems govern Claims, Claim processing, Claim decisions, Claim administration, and related Claim lifecycle activities.

Claim ≠ Liability.

Claim Acceptance ≠ Liability Determination.

Claim Rejection ≠ Absence of Liability.

Claim Payment ≠ Liability Admission.

Claim Closure ≠ Liability Finality.

LIGA™ ↔ Liability-Relevant Mechanisms

LIGA™ governs the Liability lifecycle. Human actions, AI behavior, autonomous actions, physical interactions, organizational processes, contractual events, system failures, environmental conditions, Harm, loss, and composite operations may constitute or contribute to inputs through which Liability relevance may arise.

Liability-Relevant Mechanism ≠ Liability.

Liability Relationship & Network Structure

LIGA™ does not assume that one Liability-relevant event creates one Liability Relationship.

A single occurrence may produce:

multiple Liability Subjects multiple Liability Relationships different Liability Bases different Liability Conditions different Liability-Relevant Connections different Liability Determinations different Liability Scopes different Liability Allocations shared Liability transferred Liability secondary Liability Relationships

Recourse Relationships

or:

a wider Liability Network Each Relationship may possess its own lifecycle while remaining structurally connected to the wider Liability Network.

Therefore:

One Occurrence ≠ One Liability Relationship.

One Harm ≠ One Liability Relationship.

One Liability Subject ≠ One Liability Relationship.

One Liability Relationship ≠ Complete Liability Network.

Local Liability Resolution ≠ Liability Network Resolution.

Liability Relationship Finality ≠ Liability Network Finality.

The architecture therefore preserves the distinction:

Harm-Producing Actor ≠ Liability-Bearing Party ≠ Paying Party.

A Transfer or Recourse Relationship does not automatically rewrite the original Liability Relationship.

Transfer ≠ Historical Replacement.

Primary Liability ≠ Recourse Liability.

Primary Liability Resolution ≠ Recourse Resolution.

Primary Liability Finality ≠ Recourse Finality.

Canonical Principle

LIGA™ does not determine jurisdiction-specific legal liability, replace courts, regulators, arbitrators, insurers, contractual authorities, Claims systems, or legal decision systems, govern Harm itself, allocate general accountability, perform general incident or causation investigation, perform evidence governance, make validation determinations, determine Value consequences, or substitute for legal, regulatory, insurance, contractual, safety, medical, or other domain-specific decision systems. LIGA™ governs the methodological conditions under which Liability becomes contextually identifiable, object-bounded, basis-grounded, relationally structured, condition-aware, sufficiently connected, determinable and attachable where justified, scoped, allocatable, state-governed, traceable through change, transferable or recoverable through Recourse where applicable, legitimately resolvable and dischargeable, historically continuous, reopenable where materially required, and capable of reaching governed Finality throughout its complete lifecycle.

Liability may become relevant without Harm, may involve multiple Relationships arising from one occurrence, may change after Determination, may move between Subjects, may create secondary and Recourse Relationships, may remain active after payment or Settlement, may reach Relationship Finality while the wider Liability Network remains active, and may require Re-Entry after previous Resolution or Finality. LIGA™ provides the governance architecture for that entire lifecycle.

LIGA™ governs Liability from relevance to Finality.
