Canonical Definition

The Liability Finality Record Module (LFRM) governs the formation, preservation, maintenance, and controlled evolution of the canonical record through which a Liability requirement, Liability Relationship, defined group of Relationships, or Liability Network is represented as having reached a governed Finality state.

LFRM records what reached Finality, the Scope and Basis of that Finality, the affected Subjects and Relationships, relevant Resolution and Discharge states, remaining Liability outside the Finality boundary, authority, conditions, effective time, historical continuity, provenance, rationale, and the conditions under which future Re-entry may legitimately occur.

It establishes the terminal active-governance representation of the LIGA™ lifecycle without erasing the Liability history that produced it.

Its principal output is:

Governed Liability Finality State

Its foundational distinction is:

Liability Finality ≠ Liability Erasure.

Operational Role

The operational role of LFRM is:

to establish and preserve the canonical terminal Liability governance record through which active governance may legitimately end within a defined Scope while the complete Liability history, Finality rationale, remaining external or connected Liability states, and future Re-entry conditions remain reconstructable.

Position in the LCFS Internal Flow

The LCFS internal module progression is:

Liability Continuity Identification → Liability Historical Record → Liability Finality Assessment → Liability ReEntry → Liability Finality Record LFRM governs:

Liability Finality Record

Its principal inputs include:

Governed Liability Finality Assessment

Governed Liability Historical Record

Governed Liability Continuity Identification Structure

and, where applicable:

Governed Liability Re-Entry Structure

Its principal output is:

Governed Liability Finality State

This is the terminal active-governance output of LCFS and the LIGA™ Liability lifecycle for the applicable Finality Scope.

Central Operational Question

What must the canonical Finality Record preserve so that active Liability governance may legitimately end within a defined Scope without erasing history, obscuring remaining Liability, or preventing legitimate future Re-entry?

Finality Record Object

Every Finality Record should identify the exact structure that has reached Finality.

The Finality Record Object may be:

a Liability requirement, a Liability Relationship, a defined group of Liability Relationships, a Recourse Relationship, or a Liability Network.

Finality Record Object vs Liability Object

The Finality Record Object is not necessarily identical to the original Liability Object.

Therefore:

Finality Record Object ≠ Liability Object by default.

Finality Object Identity

The Finality Record should preserve the identity of the Finality Object and its association with the applicable Liability Origin.

Liability Origin

Finality does not create a new Origin.

The Finality Record should remain connected to the Origin of the Liability lifecycle it completes.

Therefore:

Liability Finality ≠ New Liability Origin.

Finality Scope

The Finality Record should explicitly identify the Scope within which active Liability governance has ended.

Scope Precision

Finality should not be recorded more broadly than the governed assessment supports.

Therefore:

Bounded Finality ≠ Universal Finality.

Requirement-Level Finality

A specific Liability requirement may reach Finality independently from other requirements.

Relationship-Level Finality

A Liability Relationship may reach Finality while other connected Relationships remain active.

Network-Level Finality

Network Finality should be recorded only where the applicable Liability Network has itself satisfied the relevant Finality conditions.

Therefore:

Liability Relationship Finality ≠ Liability Network Finality.

Subject-Specific Finality

One Subject may reach Finality within a defined Liability Relationship while another Subject remains within active governance elsewhere.

Therefore:

Subject Finality ≠ Universal Liability Finality.

Primary Liability Finality

Primary Liability Finality should remain distinguishable from Finality of secondary Relationships.

Recourse Finality

Recourse Relationships should retain independent Finality states.

Therefore:

Primary Liability Finality ≠ Recourse Liability Finality.

Partial Finality

Where only part of the relevant Liability structure reaches Finality, the record should preserve that limited Scope.

Therefore:

Partial Finality ≠ Complete Liability Finality.

Conditional Finality

Where Finality remains dependent on continuing conditions, those conditions should remain explicit.

Therefore:

Conditional Finality ≠ Unconditional Finality.

Finality State

The Finality Record should identify the applicable governed Finality State.

Possible methodological states may include:

Relationship Final, Network Final, Partially Final, Conditionally Final, Final, Re-entry Required, Reopened, Superseded, or another explicitly governed terminal or post-terminal state.

Finality State vs Finality Scope

The state describes the governance condition.

The Scope identifies where that condition applies.

Therefore:

Finality State ≠ Finality Scope.

Finality Basis

The record should preserve the Basis upon which Finality was established.

Finality Basis vs Resolution Basis

The Basis supporting Resolution may contribute to Finality but should not automatically replace the Finality Basis.

Therefore:

Resolution Basis ≠ Finality Basis by default.

Finality Conditions

The record should preserve material conditions upon which Finality depends.

Satisfied Conditions

Where Finality required conditions to be satisfied, their governed status should remain reconstructable.

Continuing Conditions

Where a Finality State remains conditional, continuing conditions should remain identifiable after active governance ends.

Finality Authority

Where authority is relevant, the Finality Record should preserve:

the applicable authority, authority Scope, authority Basis, and relevant effective period.

Authority Scope

Authority to establish Finality for one Liability Relationship should not be represented as authority over unrelated Liability.

Therefore:

Local Finality Authority ≠ Universal Finality Authority.

Finality Effective Time

The record should identify when Finality became effective where sufficiently established.

Finality Recording Time

The time at which Finality is recorded may differ from the time at which it became effective.

Therefore:

Finality Recording Time ≠ Finality Effective Time by default.

Resolution Time vs Finality Time

Therefore:

Liability Resolution Time ≠ Liability Finality Time by default.

Discharge Time vs Finality Time

Therefore:

Liability Discharge Time ≠ Liability Finality Time by default.

Finality Assessment Time vs Effective Time

Therefore:

Finality Assessment Time ≠ Finality Effective Time by default.

Resolution Association

The Finality Record should remain associated with the governed Resolution state that preceded Finality where applicable.

Discharge Association

Where Discharge contributed to Finality, the relevant Discharge state should remain associated.

Satisfaction Association

Relevant Satisfaction should remain traceable without being represented as Finality itself.

Therefore:

Satisfaction Record ≠ Finality Record.

Payment Association

Payment information may form part of the Finality history.

Therefore:

Payment Record ≠ Liability Finality Record.

Settlement Association

Settlement information may support Finality.

Therefore:

Settlement Record ≠ Liability Finality Record.

Administrative Closure

An administrative closure may be associated with Finality where relevant.

It does not replace the Finality Record.

Therefore:

Administrative Closure Record ≠ Liability Finality Record.

Claim Closure

Claim closure may be associated with the Liability history.

Therefore:

Claim Closure Record ≠ Liability Finality Record.

Remaining Liability

The Finality Record should explicitly identify material Liability that remains active outside the Finality Scope.

No Implied Universal Closure

The existence of a Finality Record should not imply that all connected Liability has ended.

Therefore:

Recorded Finality ≠ Universal Liability Closure.

Secondary Liability

Secondary Liability Relationships should remain visible where they continue beyond primary Finality.

Recourse Continuity

Active Recourse should remain represented even where primary Liability has reached Finality.

Liability Network State

Where the Finality Record concerns only one Relationship within a network, the wider network state should remain identifiable.

Local Finality vs Network State

Therefore:

Local Finality ≠ Network Finality.

Historical Continuity

The Finality Record should remain connected to the complete governed Liability history.

Historical Record Association

LFRM should preserve association with the:

Governed Liability Historical Record

rather than duplicating the entire history within the Finality Record.

Current Finality State vs Complete History

Therefore:

Current Finality State ≠ Complete Liability History.

Finality Rationale

The record should preserve sufficient rationale to explain why Finality was established.

Finality Rationale Elements

The rationale should be capable of explaining, where applicable:

what reached Finality, within what Scope, under what Basis, which requirements ended, what remained active, what dependencies were reconciled, what authority applied, and why active governance could legitimately end.

Rationale vs State

Therefore:

Finality Rationale ≠ Finality State.

Finality Provenance

The Finality Record should preserve sufficient provenance to reconstruct where the Finality determination came from.

Evidence Association

Evidence supporting Finality should remain associated where applicable.

Evidence governance remains within the relevant evidence architecture.

Therefore:

Finality Record ≠ Evidence Repository by default.

Finality Record Integrity

The record should protect against:

silent alteration, unsupported Scope expansion, historical deletion, untraceable correction, untraceable supersession, false authority representation, and loss of Re-entry conditions.

Finality Record Versioning

A Finality Record may require versioning where later information changes its representation.

Version Change vs New Finality

A technical or representational update should not automatically create a new Finality event.

Therefore:

Finality Record Version Change ≠ New Liability Finality by default.

Finality Correction

An incorrect Finality Record may be corrected.

The prior record should remain historically reconstructable where materially relevant.

Therefore:

Finality Correction ≠ Historical Erasure.

Finality Reassessment

A previously valid Finality may later require reassessment because materially relevant circumstances changed.

Therefore:

Finality Reassessment ≠ Finality Correction.

Finality Supersession

A later Finality Record may supersede an earlier representation.

Therefore:

Finality Supersession ≠ Historical Deletion.

Finality Reversal

Where Final Liability legitimately returns to active governance, the earlier Finality Record should remain part of the lifecycle.

Therefore:

Finality Reversal ≠ Finality History Deletion.

Re-Entry Conditions

The Finality Record should preserve conditions or trigger classes under which future Re-entry may be required.

Re-Entry Capability

Finality should not destroy the ability to route the same Liability lifecycle back into active governance where materially required.

Finality vs Irreversibility

Methodological Finality does not mean that future materially relevant developments are impossible.

Therefore:

Liability Finality ≠ Absolute Irreversibility.

Re-Entry Trigger Association

Where known, the Finality Record may identify categories of events capable of triggering Re-entry assessment.

Re-Entry vs New Origin

If Re-entry occurs within the same Liability lifecycle:

Re-entry ≠ New Origin by default.

Reopened State

A Finality Record should be capable of representing that the Final Liability structure later entered a Reopened state.

Prior Finality Preservation

Reopening should not delete the earlier Finality State.

Therefore:

Reopening ≠ Prior Finality Erasure.

Multiple Finality Cycles

A Liability lifecycle may contain:

Finality, Re-entry, renewed governance, and later Finality.

Each Finality cycle should remain independently reconstructable.

Finality Sequence

Where multiple Finality states exist, the record should preserve their sequence.

Original Finality vs Later Finality

A later Finality does not make the earlier Finality historically nonexistent.

Therefore:

Later Finality ≠ Historical Replacement of Earlier Finality.

Finality History

The Finality Record should preserve or associate:

initial Finality, later correction, reassessment, supersession, Re-entry, Reopening, and subsequent Finality.

Relationship Finality History

Each Liability Relationship may possess its own Finality history.

Network Finality History

A Liability Network may reach Finality after individual Relationships reach Finality at different times.

Finality Propagation

Finality should not automatically propagate across connected Relationships.

Therefore:

Relationship Connection ≠ Automatic Finality Propagation.

Finality Dependency

A Relationship may depend on another Liability structure without sharing its Finality state.

Therefore:

Liability Dependency ≠ Shared Finality State.

Finality and System Migration

A Finality Record may migrate between systems.

The underlying Finality identity should remain continuous.

Therefore:

System Migration ≠ New Liability Finality.

Finality Record Identifier Change

A technical identifier change should not alter the governed Finality identity.

Therefore:

Finality Record Identifier Change ≠ Finality Identity Change by default.

Multiple Finality Representations

Multiple systems may represent the same Finality State.

Therefore:

Multiple Finality Records ≠ Multiple Finality Events by default.

Duplicate Finality Record

Duplicate records should not be interpreted as independent Finality determinations without sufficient Basis.

Record Absence

Absence of an accessible Finality Record does not prove that Finality never occurred.

Likewise, absence of an active record does not prove Finality.

Therefore:

Record Absence ≠ Liability Finality.

Data Deletion

Deletion of a technical Finality record does not erase the historical Finality event.

Therefore:

Data Deletion ≠ Finality Historical Non-Existence.

Retention End

End of a technical retention period does not define Liability Finality.

Therefore:

Retention End ≠ Liability Finality.

Archival State

A Finality Record may move into archival storage without changing its governed Finality meaning.

Therefore:

Archival State ≠ New Finality State.

Unknown Finality Record Element

Where a material record element is unavailable, it should remain Unknown rather than being invented.

Uncertain Finality Record Element

Where a record element remains uncertain, uncertainty should remain explicit.

Disputed Finality Record

Where Finality is disputed, the Finality Record should preserve that dispute.

Therefore:

Recorded Finality ≠ Undisputed Finality.

Conflicting Finality Records

Conflicting Finality representations should not be silently merged.

Therefore:

Conflicting Finality Records ≠ Authority to Select a Preferred Finality History.

Conflict Resolution

Where a conflict is legitimately resolved, the record should preserve:

the conflict, the Basis of resolution, the resulting state, and relevant provenance.

Finality Record Completeness

A Finality Record should contain sufficient information to understand the terminal governance state without requiring every lifecycle detail to be duplicated.

Completeness vs Data Volume

Therefore:

Finality Record Completeness ≠ Maximum Data Volume.

Reconstructability

The Finality Record should be sufficient to navigate back to the relevant Liability history and reconstruct why Finality was established.

Standalone Terminal Label

A standalone status such as “closed” or “final” without Scope, Basis, history, or provenance is insufficient as a governed Finality Record.

Therefore:

Terminal Label ≠ Governed Liability Finality State.

Finality Record Preservation

The Finality Record should remain preservable for as long as required by the applicable governance environment without implying a universal retention period.

Preservation vs Legal Retention

LFRM does not establish jurisdiction-specific legal record-retention periods.

It establishes methodological requirements for preserving Finality reconstructability.

Automated Finality Recording

AI or automated systems may assist with:

Finality Record formation, field population, Relationship reconciliation, network-state association, historical linking, provenance association, condition monitoring, Re-entry trigger monitoring, version comparison, and conflict detection.

Automation should not create Finality where no governed Finality assessment exists.

Therefore:

Automated Finality Recording ≠ Liability Finality Determination.

Automated Closure Signal

A system-generated closure signal should not independently create a Finality Record.

Therefore:

Automated Closure Signal ≠ Governed Liability Finality State.

Finality Record Validation

The record should be checked for consistency with the applicable Finality Assessment before terminal Finality is established.

Scope Consistency

The recorded Finality Scope should not exceed the assessed Finality Scope.

Relationship Consistency

The Relationships recorded as Final should correspond to the Relationships actually assessed for Finality.

Network Consistency

Network Finality should not be recorded where only Relationship-level Finality was established.

Remaining Liability Consistency

Any known Liability remaining outside the Finality Scope should remain represented.

Historical Consistency

The Finality Record should remain consistent with the Governed Liability Historical Record or explicitly identify any unresolved conflict.

Re-Entry Consistency

Known Re-entry conditions should remain associated with the Finality Record.

Finality Record Failure

A terminal Finality Record should not be established where material deficiencies prevent reliable representation of:

the Finality Object, Scope, Basis, State, authority where applicable, remaining active Liability, or historical continuity.

Finality Record Readiness

Record readiness should follow a sufficiently governed Finality assessment.

Therefore:

Finality Record Readiness ≠ Liability Finality by itself.

Governed Liability Finality State

The principal output of LFRM is:

Governed Liability Finality State

It should identify, where applicable:

Liability Origin, Finality Record Object, Finality Scope, affected Subjects, affected Liability Relationships, Liability Network Scope, Finality State, Finality Basis, Finality conditions, Finality authority, Finality Effective Time, Finality Recording Time, Resolution association, Discharge association, remaining active Liability, secondary Liability, Recourse state, network state, historical record association, Finality rationale, Finality provenance, evidence associations, Re-entry conditions, Re-entry trigger associations, prior Finality states, Reopening state, corrections, reassessments, supersession, uncertainty, disputes, and conflicts.

Meaning of the Output

A Governed Liability Finality State means that active LIGA™ governance has legitimately ended for the explicitly defined Finality Scope.

It does not mean:

that Liability never existed, that Liability history may be erased, that all connected Liability Relationships are Final, that the entire Liability Network is Final unless explicitly established, that Harm, Accountability, Claims, or another external lifecycle is Final, or that future Re-entry is impossible.

It means that the defined Liability structure no longer requires active Liability lifecycle governance under the established Finality conditions.

Minimum Implementation Framework

A conforming implementation of LFRM must provide exactly the following eight capabilities:

Finality Object, Scope, Subject, Relationship & State Recording

Capability to identify and preserve the Finality Record Object, Finality Scope, affected Subjects, affected Liability Relationships, Liability Network relevance, and exact governed Finality State.

Finality Basis, Condition, Authority & Effective Time Recording

Capability to preserve the Finality Basis, applicable conditions, authority and authority Scope where relevant, Finality Effective Time, and Finality Recording Time.

Resolution, Discharge, Remaining Liability, Secondary Liability & Recourse

Reconciliation

Capability to associate the Finality State with relevant Resolution and Discharge outcomes while preserving remaining active Liability, secondary Relationships, Transfer, Sharing, and Recourse states.

Relationship, Network & Finality Boundary Preservation

Capability to distinguish requirement, Subject, Relationship, group, Recourse, and Network Finality and prevent local or partial Finality from being represented as universal Finality.

Historical Continuity, Provenance, Rationale & Evidence Association

Preservation

Capability to associate the Finality Record with the complete governed Liability history and preserve sufficient provenance, rationale, and evidence associations to make Finality reconstructable.

Re-Entry Condition, Reopening & Multi-Finality-Cycle Governance

Capability to preserve Re-entry conditions and trigger associations, represent Reopening without deleting prior Finality, and maintain multiple Finality cycles within one continuing Liability lifecycle.

Versioning, Correction, Reassessment, Supersession, Uncertainty & Conflict

Governance

Capability to govern changes to the Finality Record without historical erasure and preserve Unknown, Uncertain, Disputed, conflicting, corrected, reassessed, and superseded states.

Governed Liability Finality State Formation

Capability to reconcile the complete terminal governance structure and establish the canonical Governed Liability Finality State through which active LIGA™ governance legitimately ends within the defined Scope.

Cross-Architecture Boundary

LFRM may preserve references to terminal or continuing states produced by adjacent OOF® architectures where they are relevant to Liability Finality.

ORA™ may preserve relevant Operational Reality.

HAGA™ may preserve Harm Resolution or Harm Finality.

AGA™ may preserve Responsibility, Accountability, attribution, and authority states.

ADIT® may preserve evidence and auditability structures.

VALIDOS™ may preserve validation states.

OBIDENITY® may preserve identity, Origin, succession, provenance, and authority structures.

VFM® may preserve payment and Value Flow states.

AIG® and ASGA™ may preserve relevant AI or autonomous-system governance states.

Claims governance may preserve Claim Resolution or Claim Finality.

These states may remain associated with the Liability Finality Record.

They do not become Liability Finality merely because their own lifecycle has ended.

The canonical interoperability principle is:

An external lifecycle may end, continue, or reopen independently; LFRM records whether the Liability lifecycle itself has legitimately reached Finality within its defined Scope.

Canonical Boundary

LFRM begins when a Liability structure has undergone sufficient Finality assessment and any required Re-entry governance has been resolved or explicitly represented.

LFRM governs:

Finality Record Objects, Finality Scope, Subjects, Liability Relationships, Liability Network boundaries, Finality States, Finality Basis, conditions, authority, effective time, Resolution and Discharge associations, remaining Liability, secondary Liability, Recourse, historical continuity, Finality rationale, provenance, evidence associations, Re-entry conditions, Reopening, multiple Finality cycles, versioning, correction, reassessment, supersession, uncertainty, disputes, conflicts, and terminal Finality record integrity.

LFRM does not erase Liability history.

LFRM does not automatically finalize connected Liability Relationships outside the established Scope.

LFRM does not prevent legitimate future Re-entry.

LFRM ends when the applicable Liability lifecycle has been sufficiently represented through a:

Governed Liability Finality State

within its legitimate Finality Scope.

Use Case 1 — Relationship Finality While Recourse Remains

Active

A primary Liability Relationship has been resolved, discharged, and assessed as qualified for Finality.

A separate Recourse Relationship remains active within the wider Liability Network.

LFRM records Finality for the primary Relationship, preserves the active Recourse Relationship and network state, and prevents the Relationship-level Finality from being represented as Network Finality.

Primary Liability Finality ≠ Recourse Liability Finality.

Use Case 2 — Complete Network Finality with Future ReEntry Capability

All materially relevant Liability Relationships within a defined Liability Network have been resolved or otherwise legitimately completed, secondary and Recourse structures have been reconciled, and the Network has been assessed as qualified for Finality.

LFRM establishes the Governed Liability Finality State for the complete network while preserving its Origin, history, Finality Basis, rationale, provenance, and Re-entry conditions.

If materially relevant information later emerges, the same Liability lifecycle can re-enter governance without deleting the historical Finality State.

Liability Finality ≠ Liability Erasure.

Canonical Closing Statement

Finality is the end of active Liability governance.

It is not the end of Liability history.

A Final Liability structure must remain connected to its Origin, Determination, Scope, Allocation, State changes, Transfers, Recourse, Resolution, Discharge, and every material transition that explains how Finality was reached.

And where materially relevant circumstances later require renewed governance, that history must remain intact.

Liability Finality ≠ Liability Erasure.

Finality ends active Liability governance. It does not erase Liability history.

LIGA™ governs Liability from relevance to Finality.
