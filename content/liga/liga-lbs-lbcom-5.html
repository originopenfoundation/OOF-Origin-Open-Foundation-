Canonical Definition

The Liability Basis Continuity Module (LBCOM) governs how a Liability Basis and its governed association with a Liability Object retain traceable, reconstructable, and historically coherent identity across time, state transitions, source changes, amendments, qualification changes, association changes, supersession, correction, reassessment, system migration, lifecycle progression, and lifecycle re-entry.

LBCOM ensures that subsequent Liability governance can determine not only what the current Liability Basis is, but also how that Basis reached its current state and which Basis state governed at any materially relevant lifecycle moment.

LBCOM governs the transition:

Governed Object–Basis Association → Continuity Establishment → Basis State Lineage → Change & Version Preservation → Historical Reconstruction → Governed Liability Basis Its foundational distinction is:

Liability Basis Continuity ≠ Liability Basis Immutability.

Operational Role

The operational role of LBCOM is:

to preserve the identity, lineage, state history, and reconstructability of the Liability Basis as the Liability lifecycle progresses.

A Liability Basis may:

change, be amended, be reclassified, be requalified, become inactive, be superseded, be corrected, be reassessed, or re-enter active governance.

These changes must not destroy the ability to determine:

what the Basis was, which state applied, when that state applied, how it changed, and why later governance relied upon it.

Central Operational Question

How can a Liability Basis remain identifiable, traceable, historically coherent, and reconstructable while allowing legitimate change throughout the Liability lifecycle?

Continuity does not require the Basis to remain unchanged.

It requires change to remain governable.

Governed Space

LBCOM governs:

Basis Identity Continuity, Basis Origin Continuity, Basis Source Continuity, Basis State Continuity, Basis Version Continuity, Classification Continuity, Qualification Continuity, Object–Basis Association Continuity, Basis Dependency Continuity, Basis Temporal Continuity, Basis Change Lineage, Basis Amendment, Basis Supersession, Basis Correction, Basis Reassessment, Basis Migration, Basis Re-entry, Historical Preservation, Basis Reconstruction, and Continuity Sufficiency.

Continuity Input

LBCOM receives a:

Governed Object–Basis Association

This input should preserve:

Liability Object Identity, Liability Basis Identity, Basis Origin, Basis Source, Basis Classification, Qualification State, Object–Basis Association, association scope, temporal state, dependencies, uncertainty, and material history.

LBCOM converts these governed states into a continuity structure capable of surviving later lifecycle change.

Continuity Purpose

A Liability Basis may remain relevant for years after its initial identification.

During that period:

sources may change, agreements may be amended, rules may be superseded, Object states may change, qualification may be reassessed, associations may expand or contract, and new information may emerge.

Without continuity governance, later systems may see only the current Basis state.

That can destroy the ability to reconstruct historical Liability governance.

LBCOM prevents this loss.

Basis Identity Continuity

A Liability Basis should retain a persistent governed identity across legitimate changes where the underlying foundation remains methodologically continuous.

Therefore:

Basis Change ≠ New Liability Basis by default.

The determination must depend on whether the governed foundation remains continuous.

Persistent Basis Identity

Where continuity exists, the Basis should retain a persistent identity independent of changes to:

name, label, representation, source location, system identifier, version, or storage environment.

The governing principle is:

Representation Change ≠ Basis Identity Change.

Basis Origin Continuity

The original formation or identification of the Basis should remain traceable throughout its lifecycle.

Later changes should not replace the Basis Origin.

Therefore:

Current Basis State ≠ Basis Origin.

Basis Origin remains part of the permanent historical lineage.

Basis Source Continuity

The source underlying a Basis may change representation or version.

LBCOM should preserve the relationship among:

original source, subsequent source versions, amendments, replacement sources, and superseding sources.

Therefore:

Current Basis Source ≠ Complete Basis Source History.

Source Relocation

A source may move between:

repositories, systems, organizations, registries, document platforms, or technical environments.

Relocation should not create a new Liability Basis merely because the source location changes.

Therefore:

Source Relocation ≠ New Liability Basis.

Source Replacement

A source may be replaced by another source.

The governance question becomes whether the replacement:

continues the same Basis, modifies the Basis, supersedes the Basis, or creates a new Basis.

LBCOM must preserve this distinction.

Therefore:

Source Replacement ≠ Basis Continuity by default.

Basis State Continuity

Every material Basis State should remain connected to preceding and subsequent states.

A simplified lineage may be:

Basis State A → Basis State B → Basis State C The current state should not exist as an isolated record.

Its lineage should remain reconstructable.

Basis State Lineage

Basis State Lineage should preserve:

state identity, effective time, preceding state, subsequent state, change trigger, change authority where relevant, supporting governance input, and change rationale.

The objective is not merely version storage.

The objective is governed historical meaning.

Current State and Historical State

LBCOM distinguishes:

Current Liability Basis State

from:

Historical Liability Basis State

Both may remain relevant.

Therefore:

Current Liability Basis State ≠ Complete Liability Basis History.

Basis Versioning

A Liability Basis may have multiple versions.

Each material version should remain connected to the same persistent Basis Identity where continuity remains intact.

The structure may be:

Basis Origin → Version 1 → Version 2 → Version 3 Therefore:

New Basis Version ≠ New Liability Basis by default.

Version Identity

Each material Basis version should be independently identifiable while remaining linked to the persistent Basis Identity.

This permits governance to distinguish:

the Basis as a continuing methodological object from:

a specific state or version of that Basis.

Therefore:

Basis Identity ≠ Basis Version Identity.

Effective Version

The current version may not be the version relevant to the Liability inquiry.

LBCOM must preserve which version was effective at each materially relevant time.

Therefore:

Current Basis Version ≠ Applicable Historical Basis Version by default.

Classification Continuity

A Basis classification may change.

For example, later governance may:

refine, expand, correct, or replace an earlier classification.

LBCOM should preserve both the old and new classifications and the transition between them.

Therefore:

Classification Change ≠ Basis Identity Change by default.

Qualification Continuity

Qualification State may change throughout the Basis lifecycle.

Possible progression may include:

Provisionally Qualified → Qualified → Suspended → Requalified Each state must remain historically traceable.

Therefore:

Current Qualification State ≠ Complete Qualification History.

Qualification Withdrawal

A Basis that was previously qualified may later lose qualification.

The earlier Qualification State must not be erased.

It may remain relevant to decisions made while that state was operative.

Therefore:

Qualification Withdrawal ≠ Historical Non-Qualification.

Requalification

A previously unqualified, suspended, or conditionally qualified Basis may later become qualified.

Requalification should create a new Qualification State in the same lineage where Basis Identity remains continuous.

Therefore:

Requalification ≠ New Liability Basis by default.

Object–Basis Association Continuity

The association between a Liability Object and a Liability Basis may change while both identities remain stable.

LBCOM should preserve:

initial association, scope changes, boundary changes, temporary suspension, removal, restoration, and reassessment.

Therefore:

Association Change ≠ Basis Identity Change.

Association Lineage

Object–Basis Association history should remain reconstructable as:

Association State A → Association State B → Association State C This allows later governance to determine which Basis was associated with which Object state at a particular time.

Object Change and Basis Continuity

The Governed Liability Object may change.

Such change may:

leave the Basis association unchanged, modify the association, require Basis reassessment, terminate the association, or create a new Basis requirement.

Therefore:

Liability Object Change ≠ Liability Basis Change by default.

The two governance identities must remain independent.

Basis Change and Object Continuity

Likewise, the Basis may change while the Liability Object remains stable.

Therefore:

Liability Basis Change ≠ Liability Object Change.

LBCOM preserves the independent lineage of both.

Dependency Continuity

A Basis may depend on another Basis or external governed structure.

LBCOM should preserve changes in those dependencies.

A dependency may:

arise, change, become inactive, be replaced, or terminate.

Therefore:

Dependency Change ≠ Basis Identity Change by default.

But a material dependency change may require reassessment.

Dependency Lineage

Where Basis B depends on Basis A, the relevant historical state of Basis A must remain identifiable.

The relationship should not be represented merely as:

Basis B depends on current Basis A.

It may need to represent:

Basis B State 2 depended on Basis A State 4 during Time T.

This enables historical reconstruction.

Temporal Continuity

LBCOM preserves the temporal sequence of Basis states.

Relevant temporal markers may include:

effective time, amendment time, suspension time, supersession time, termination time, requalification time, association change time, and re-entry time.

The timeline must preserve both sequence and meaning.

Retroactive Change

A Basis change may sometimes be represented as having retroactive effect.

Where such a state is governed by an appropriate authority or source, LBCOM should preserve:

the original historical state, the later retroactive determination, the claimed effective period, and the relationship between them.

Therefore:

Retroactive Effect ≠ Historical Record Erasure.

Basis Amendment

A Basis may be amended without becoming a new Basis.

LBCOM should determine whether the amendment represents:

continuity, material transformation, partial replacement, or formation of a new Basis.

Therefore:

Basis Amendment ≠ New Liability Basis by default.

Material Transformation

A change may be so substantial that the earlier and later foundations should no longer be treated as the same Liability Basis.

Where this occurs, LBCOM should preserve a successor relationship.

The structure may be:

Basis A → terminated / transformed → Basis B Therefore:

Continuity must not be artificially preserved where Basis Identity has materially changed.

Basis Supersession

A Basis may be superseded by:

a later version, a replacement rule, a new agreement, a revised obligation, or another foundation.

Supersession should preserve:

the superseded Basis, the superseding Basis, effective timing, relationship between them, and relevant transition state.

Therefore:

Basis Supersession ≠ Historical Deletion.

Partial Supersession

A Basis may be superseded only in part.

The unaffected portion may remain active.

LBCOM should preserve the split.

Therefore:

Partial Supersession ≠ Complete Basis Termination.

Basis Termination

A Basis may cease to operate prospectively.

Termination should preserve:

the termination event, effective time, authority or source, historical states, and continuing relevance where applicable.

Therefore:

Basis Termination ≠ Basis Erasure.

Continuing Historical Relevance

A terminated or superseded Basis may remain relevant to Liability concerning an earlier period.

Therefore:

Inactive Basis ≠ Historically Irrelevant Basis.

LBCOM preserves the state necessary to reconstruct historical Liability governance.

Basis Correction

A Basis record may contain an error.

Correction should preserve:

the incorrect representation, the corrected representation, the correction reason, the correction time, and the relationship between them.

Therefore:

Basis Correction ≠ Historical Erasure.

Correction and Basis Identity

A correction does not create a new Basis where it merely repairs an inaccurate representation of the same underlying foundation.

Therefore:

Corrected Basis Representation ≠ New Liability Basis by default.

However, if the correction reveals that a different foundation was actually involved, a separate Basis Identity may be required.

Basis Reassessment

Later information may require a Liability Basis to be reassessed.

Reassessment may affect:

classification, qualification, association, dependency, temporal state, or continuity itself.

LBCOM preserves both the pre-reassessment and post-reassessment states.

Therefore:

Basis Reassessment ≠ Historical Rewrite.

Reassessment Trigger

Potential triggers include:

new evidence, new validation, source discovery, source correction, Object change, classification correction, qualification change, association change, authority change, dependency change, or lifecycle re-entry.

The trigger should remain traceable to the resulting reassessment.

Lifecycle Re-Entry

A Liability matter may re-enter Basis governance after later information materially affects an earlier Basis state.

Re-entry should return governance to the earliest materially affected point.

For example:

new information concerning the source may require re-entry into Basis Qualification, while a newly discovered foundation may require re-entry into Basis Identification.

Therefore:

Re-entry ≠ Full Lifecycle Restart by default.

Re-Entry and Origin Identity

Lifecycle re-entry must not create a new Origin merely because governance has resumed.

Therefore:

Re-entry ≠ New Origin by default.

The existing Basis lineage should continue unless the re-entry reveals a genuinely different Liability Basis.

Re-Entry and Historical Integrity

Re-entry may change the current understanding of a Basis.

It must not erase the governance state that existed before re-entry.

Therefore:

Re-entry must not rewrite history.

The architecture must preserve:

what was known, what was governed, what changed, and why the current state differs.

System Migration

A Liability Basis may move between:

information systems, organizations, registries, platforms, technical infrastructures, or governance environments.

Migration should preserve:

Basis Identity, Basis Origin, Basis history, version lineage, qualification history, association history, dependencies, and temporal states.

Therefore:

System Migration ≠ New Liability Basis.

Organizational Transfer

Responsibility for administering or recording a Liability Basis may move between organizations.

Such transfer should not automatically change the Basis Identity.

Therefore:

Administrative Ownership Change ≠ Liability Basis Change by default.

Governance ownership of the methodological object should remain traceable independently from administrative custody.

Representation Change

The Basis may be represented differently across:

human-readable documents, machine-readable structures, registries, databases, knowledge graphs, AI systems, or other representations.

Therefore:

Representation Change ≠ Methodological Object Change.

The persistent Basis Identity should survive representation changes.

Cross-System Identity

Where the same Basis appears in several systems, LBCOM should support cross-system identity mapping.

The objective is to determine whether records represent:

the same Basis, different versions of the same Basis, related Bases, or distinct Bases.

Therefore:

Multiple System Records ≠ Multiple Liability Bases by default.

Duplicate Basis Records

Duplicate technical records may exist.

LBCOM should preserve methodological identity independently of record count.

Therefore:

Record Duplication ≠ Basis Duplication.

A duplicate record should not create a new Liability foundation.

Basis Separation

A Basis previously represented as one foundation may later be determined to contain multiple distinct Bases.

LBCOM should preserve:

the earlier combined representation, the separation event, the resulting Basis identities, and their relationship to the original record.

Therefore:

Basis Separation ≠ Independent Historical Origin.

The separated Bases retain traceability to the earlier governance state.

Basis Consolidation

Several records or Basis Candidates may later be determined to represent one underlying Liability Basis.

Consolidation should preserve:

all prior identities or references, the consolidation rationale, the resulting persistent Basis Identity, and the historical relationships.

Therefore:

Basis Consolidation ≠ Historical Identity Erasure.

Basis Successor Relationship

Where one Basis legitimately succeeds another without being identical to it, LBCOM should preserve a successor relationship.

The structure may be:

Basis A → succeeded by → Basis B This is different from ordinary version continuity.

Therefore:

Basis Successor ≠ Basis Version by default.

Historical Reconstruction

A central LBCOM requirement is the ability to reconstruct the Liability Basis at a materially relevant point in time.

Reconstruction should answer:

What Basis existed?

What was its identity?

What source governed it?

Which version applied?

How was it classified?

What was its Qualification State?

Which Object was it associated with?

What was the association scope?

Which dependencies existed?

What uncertainty existed?

What changes had already occurred?

Reconstruction and Current State

Historical reconstruction must not rely solely on current data.

Therefore:

Current Basis State ≠ Historical Reconstruction.

The current state may be materially different from the state relevant to the Liability inquiry.

Reconstruction Sufficiency

Historical reconstruction is sufficient when a competent downstream governance process can understand the materially relevant Basis state without inventing missing transitions.

Where reconstruction remains incomplete, the uncertainty must be explicit.

Therefore:

Incomplete Reconstruction ≠ Certain Historical Basis State.

Continuity Across the Liability Lifecycle

Liability Basis continuity does not end when LBS hands the Basis to LRS.

The governed Basis may remain relevant throughout:

Liability Relationships, Liability Conditions, Liability Connection, Liability Determination, Liability Scope & Allocation, Liability State & Change, Liability Transfer & Recourse, Liability Resolution & Discharge, and Liability Continuity & Finality.

LBCOM establishes the continuity structure that later governance can reference.

Basis Continuity and Liability Determination

A later Liability Determination may depend on the Basis state that existed at a specific time.

LBCOM preserves that state.

It does not make the Liability Determination.

Therefore:

Liability Basis Continuity ≠ Liability Determination.

Basis Continuity and Liability Change

Liability may later change while the underlying Basis remains unchanged.

Conversely, the Basis may change while an existing Liability state remains subject to separate governance.

Therefore:

Liability Basis Change ≠ Liability Change by default.

and:

Liability Change ≠ Liability Basis Change by default.

LSCS owns Liability State & Change.

Basis Continuity and Resolution

Resolution of a Liability Relationship does not erase the Basis upon which that Liability was governed.

Therefore:

Liability Resolution ≠ Liability Basis Erasure.

The Basis history may remain necessary for:

audit, reconstruction, recourse, re-entry, or finality.

Basis Continuity and Finality

Liability Finality does not require destruction of the Liability Basis history.

Therefore:

Liability Finality ≠ Liability Basis Erasure.

The Basis may remain part of the reconstructable governance record even after active Liability governance ends.

Continuity Failure

A continuity failure exists where the governance system can no longer reliably determine:

whether two Basis records represent the same Basis, which version applied, how a Basis changed, which Qualification State existed, which Object association applied, or how the current state derives from the historical state.

Continuity failure should be explicit.

It must not be hidden through unsupported reconstruction.

Continuity Uncertainty

Possible continuity states may include:

Continuous

Continuous with Recorded Change

Continuity Uncertain

Continuity Disputed

Continuity Interrupted

Successor Basis Established

Historical Reconstruction Incomplete

These states allow downstream governance to understand the reliability of the Basis lineage.

Continuity Conflict

Different records may produce conflicting Basis histories.

LBCOM should preserve:

the competing histories, their sources, the conflicting transition, and unresolved uncertainty.

Therefore:

Continuity Conflict ≠ Authority to Manufacture Historical Certainty.

Continuity Correction

A continuity record may itself require correction.

The correction must preserve:

the earlier continuity representation, the corrected representation, the reason, the evidence or governed input, and the effective relationship between states.

Therefore:

Continuity Correction ≠ Historical Deletion.

Continuity Sufficiency

Liability Basis continuity is sufficient when subsequent governance can determine:

the persistent Basis Identity, Basis Origin, relevant source history, state lineage, version history, classification history, qualification history, Object–Basis Association history, dependency history, material changes, supersession, corrections, reassessment, re-entry, and the historically relevant Basis state.

Continuity sufficiency does not establish Liability.

Minimum Implementation Framework

A conforming implementation of LBCOM must provide at least the following eight capabilities:

Persistent Basis Identity Governance

Capability to preserve a stable Liability Basis Identity across legitimate changes in name, representation, source location, version, state, and system environment.

Basis State & Version Lineage

Capability to preserve materially relevant Basis states, versions, effective periods, transitions, and relationships between historical and current states.

Classification, Qualification & Association Continuity

Capability to preserve changes in Basis Classification, Qualification State, and Object–Basis Association without replacing historical states with current states.

Change, Amendment, Supersession & Successor Governance

Capability to distinguish ordinary Basis change, amendment, partial or complete supersession, material transformation, termination, and successor Basis formation.

Correction, Reassessment & Re-Entry Governance

Capability to govern corrections, later reassessment, and lifecycle re-entry while preserving the Basis Origin and historical record.

Cross-System & Organizational Continuity

Capability to preserve Basis Identity and lineage across system migration, organizational transfer, representation change, duplicate records, and cross-system mapping.

Historical Reconstruction

Capability to reconstruct the materially relevant Liability Basis state at a defined lifecycle moment, including source, version, classification, qualification, association, dependency, and uncertainty.

Continuity Sufficiency & Handoff

Capability to determine whether Basis continuity is sufficiently preserved to produce a Governed Liability Basis and support reliable handoff into Liability Relationship governance.

LBCOM may depend on continuity information governed elsewhere within the OOF® architecture ecosystem.

GOA™ may provide continuity of Authority, Scope, Delegation, or other governance structures relevant to the Basis.

ORA™ may preserve Operational Reality necessary for historical Basis reconstruction.

AGA™ may preserve Responsibility, Accountability, Authority, and Attribution states relevant to historical Basis states.

HAGA™ may provide historical Harm states where the Basis depends on Harm.

ADIT® may preserve evidence necessary to reconstruct Basis sources, versions, changes, and historical states.

VALIDOS™ may validate continuity-relevant information.

OBIDENITY® may provide persistent Identity, Ownership, Authority, Permission, Provenance, Transfer, and Continuity structures.

Other OOF® architectures may preserve governed states that remain dependencies of a Liability Basis.

The governing principle is:

Continuity of an external governed state may support Liability Basis continuity without transferring governance ownership of that state to LIGA™.

Canonical Boundary

LBCOM begins with a:

Governed Object–Basis Association

and asks:

How can this Liability Basis remain identifiable, traceable, and reconstructable throughout legitimate change?

LBCOM governs:

Persistent Basis Identity, Basis Origin Continuity, Basis Source Continuity, Basis State Lineage, Basis Versioning, Classification Continuity, Qualification Continuity, Object–Basis Association Continuity, Dependency Continuity, Temporal Continuity, Basis Amendment, Basis Transformation, Basis Supersession, Basis Termination, Basis Correction, Basis Reassessment, Basis Re-entry, System Migration, Organizational Transfer, Representation Change, Cross-System Identity, Basis Separation, Basis Consolidation, Successor Relationships, Historical Reconstruction, Continuity Uncertainty, Continuity Conflict, and Continuity Sufficiency.

LBCOM does not determine:

Liability Subjects, Liability Relationships, Liability Conditions, Condition Satisfaction, Liability-Relevant Connection, Liability Attachment, Liability Determination, Liability Scope, Liability Allocation, Liability State, Transfer, Recourse, Resolution, Discharge, or Finality.

LBCOM ends when a:

Governed Liability Basis

is sufficiently continuous and reconstructable for Liability Relationship governance.

Use Case 1 — Contractual Basis Across Multiple Versions

A Liability Basis concerning an AI-enabled service derives from a contractual agreement.

The agreement is later amended several times.

A Liability-relevant matter concerns activity that occurred under an earlier version.

LBCOM preserves the persistent Basis Identity while maintaining the complete version lineage and identifies which Basis state was effective during the relevant period.

The current agreement does not overwrite the historical Basis.

Later Liability governance can therefore evaluate the matter against the correct historical foundation.

Use Case 2 — Industrial Basis After Supersession and

Reassessment

A Liability Basis associated with industrial equipment was originally derived from a maintenance arrangement.

The arrangement was later superseded, and new information subsequently requires reassessment of an earlier Liability matter.

LBCOM preserves the superseded Basis, its Qualification State, its Object association, the superseding Basis, and the relationship between them.

The matter re-enters Basis governance at the earliest materially affected point without creating a false new Origin or deleting the original governance history.

Subsequent Liability Relationship governance can reconstruct exactly which Basis existed at the relevant time.

Canonical Closing Statement

A Liability Basis can change without losing its identity, and it can cease to operate without losing its history.

Continuity preserves the governed path between what the Basis was, what it became, and which state mattered at each point in the Liability lifecycle.

Without that path, later Liability governance may rely on the present while misunderstanding the past.

Change does not require loss of Basis identity. Continuity does not require absence of change.

Preserve the Basis so the Liability lifecycle can always reconstruct its foundation.
