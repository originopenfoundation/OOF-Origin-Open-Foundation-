Canonical Definition

The Liability Re-Entry Module (LREM) governs when, why, where, and how an existing Liability lifecycle must return to active governance after a material change, newly available information, identified defect, failed condition, reversal, conflict, or other Re-entry-relevant development.

LREM determines whether Re-entry is required, identifies the earliest materially affected LIGA™ governance space, defines the Scope of reassessment, preserves unaffected prior governance, and maintains the historical continuity of the Liability lifecycle through the Re-entry process.

Re-entry does not automatically create a new Liability.

It does not erase prior Resolution, Discharge, or Finality.

It continues governance of the existing Liability lifecycle where materially required.

Its principal output is:

Governed Liability Re-Entry Structure

Its foundational rule is:

Re-entry must not rewrite history.

Operational Role

The operational role of LREM is:

to reactivate only the materially affected parts of an existing Liability lifecycle when renewed governance becomes necessary, while preserving the original Liability identity, valid prior governance, complete historical record, and explicit relationship between the pre-Re-entry and post-Re-entry states.

Central Operational Question

Has a material development occurred that requires renewed Liability governance, and if so, what is the earliest materially affected governance space, what must be reassessed, what remains valid, and how must the existing Liability history be preserved?

Re-Entry Object

Every Re-entry assessment should identify the exact Liability structure potentially requiring renewed governance.

The Re-Entry Object may be:

a Liability requirement, a Liability Relationship, a defined part of a Liability Relationship, a Recourse Relationship, a group of Relationships, or a Liability Network.

Re-Entry Object vs Liability Object

The Re-Entry Object is not necessarily identical to the original Liability Object.

Therefore:

Re-Entry Object ≠ Liability Object by default.

Re-Entry Trigger

A Re-entry Trigger is a development sufficiently relevant to require assessment of whether prior Liability governance remains valid.

Potential triggers may include:

new materially relevant information, new evidence, incorrect earlier information, changed conditions, new Subject information, new Relationship information, changed Liability-Relevant Connection, payment reversal, performance failure, Settlement failure, Discharge reversal, authority correction, new Transfer, new Recourse, or another material lifecycle development.

Trigger vs Re-Entry

A trigger does not automatically establish Re-entry.

Therefore:

Re-Entry Trigger ≠ Liability Re-Entry.

Materiality

LREM should determine whether the trigger is materially capable of affecting an existing Liability governance result.

Immaterial developments should not reopen governance unnecessarily.

Material Change vs Any Change

Therefore:

Change ≠ Material Re-Entry Trigger by default.

Re-Entry Requirement

Re-entry is required only where the trigger materially affects an existing Liability governance state or a dependency upon which that state relies.

Re-Entry vs Active Continuation

A Liability lifecycle that remains active does not necessarily require Re-entry.

Normal continuation through an active lifecycle should remain distinct from return to an earlier governance space.

Therefore:

Continued Liability Governance ≠ Re-Entry.

Re-Entry vs Reassessment

Reassessment is the renewed evaluation of a governance state.

Re-entry is the routing mechanism that determines where such reassessment must occur.

Therefore:

Re-Entry ≠ Reassessment.

Re-Entry vs Correction

Correction addresses an earlier governance representation that was incorrect.

Re-entry may also occur because circumstances legitimately changed after an earlier valid state.

Therefore:

Re-Entry ≠ Correction by default.

Re-Entry vs New Liability

Where the same Liability lifecycle continues, Re-entry should preserve its existing identity.

Therefore:

Re-entry ≠ New Liability by default.

Re-Entry vs New Origin

Re-entry should not create a new Origin merely because governance becomes active again.

Therefore:

Re-entry ≠ New Origin by default.

Reopening

Reopening occurs where a previously resolved, discharged, or Final Liability structure returns to active governance.

Re-entry provides the governance mechanism for that return.

Reopening vs New Liability

Therefore:

Liability Reopening ≠ New Liability by default.

Reopening vs Re-Entry

Not every Re-entry involves reopening a Final Liability.

A still-active Liability may re-enter an earlier governance space.

Therefore:

Re-Entry ≠ Post-Finality Reopening only.

Pre-Finality Re-Entry

Re-entry may occur before Finality where a material issue is discovered during active governance or Finality assessment.

Post-Finality Re-Entry

A Liability structure that previously reached Finality may legitimately return to active governance where a sufficient Reentry Basis exists.

Prior Finality Preservation

A later Re-entry should not erase the historical fact that Finality had previously been established.

Therefore:

Post-Finality Re-Entry ≠ Historical Finality Deletion.

Re-Entry Basis

Every governed Re-entry should possess an identifiable Basis explaining why renewed governance is necessary.

Re-Entry Basis Sufficiency

The Basis should be sufficient for the Scope and destination of Re-entry.

Therefore:

Re-Entry-Relevant Information ≠ Sufficient Re-Entry Basis by default.

Re-Entry Scope

LREM should identify exactly which part of the Liability lifecycle requires renewed governance.

The Scope may be:

requirement-specific, Subject-specific, Relationship-specific, state-specific, Recourse-specific, or network-wide.

Targeted Re-Entry

Re-entry should be limited to what materially requires renewed governance.

The canonical principle is:

Reassess what materially changed. Preserve what remains valid.

Full Lifecycle Restart

A material change does not automatically require restarting the complete LIGA™ lifecycle.

Therefore:

Re-entry ≠ Full Lifecycle Restart by default.

Earliest Materially Affected Governance Space

LREM should identify the earliest LIGA™ governance space whose output is materially affected by the Re-entry Basis.

The canonical routing rule is:

Re-enter the earliest materially affected governance space.

Why Earliest Matters

Returning too late may preserve a materially invalid upstream assumption.

Returning too early may unnecessarily reconstruct valid governance.

LREM therefore identifies the earliest point at which renewed governance is materially required.

LCFS Internal Reassessment

Where the material change affects only continuity, historical reconstruction, Finality assessment, or Finality recording, reassessment may remain within LCFS.

Re-Entry Destination

The Re-entry Destination should identify the Parent Standard and, where sufficiently known, the relevant governance object requiring reassessment.

Re-Entry Boundary

LREM should establish a boundary identifying:

what changed, what prior output is affected, what remains valid, what must be reassessed, which downstream states depend on the affected output, and where reassessment may legitimately stop.

Preserved Validity

Prior governance unaffected by the Re-entry Basis should remain valid unless a material dependency requires reassessment.

Therefore:

Re-entry ≠ Automatic Invalidation of All Prior Governance.

Dependency Propagation

A changed upstream output may require reassessment of downstream outputs that materially depend upon it.

Propagation should follow actual governance dependencies.

Dependency vs Automatic Cascade

Therefore:

Upstream Change ≠ Automatic Full Downstream Invalidation.

Downstream Reassessment

LREM should identify which downstream governance outputs require reassessment because their Basis materially depends on the changed state.

Reassessment Stop Point

Reassessment may stop where the changed information no longer materially affects downstream governance.

Liability Identity Continuity

Re-entry should preserve the existing Liability identity where the same lifecycle continues.

Relationship Identity Continuity

A reopened Liability Relationship should remain linked to its earlier Relationship identity unless a genuinely new Relationship is formed.

Network Continuity

Where Re-entry affects one Relationship within a Liability Network, LREM should preserve the network association while limiting reassessment to materially affected Relationships.

Local Re-Entry

One Liability Relationship may re-enter governance while other Relationships remain Final.

Therefore:

Relationship Re-Entry ≠ Liability Network Re-Entry by default.

Network Re-Entry

Network-level Re-entry should occur only where the material change affects the wider network structure.

Primary Liability Re-Entry

Primary Liability may reopen without automatically reopening Recourse.

Recourse Re-Entry

Recourse may re-enter governance independently from primary Liability.

Therefore:

Primary Liability Re-Entry ≠ Recourse Re-Entry by default.

Shared Liability Re-Entry

Where Liability is shared, Re-entry should identify which Subjects and Relationships are materially affected.

Re-Entry Authority

Where renewed governance requires authority, LREM should identify the applicable authority and its Scope.

Authority to Reopen vs Authority to Determine

Authority to initiate Re-entry does not necessarily equal authority to perform the resulting Liability Determination.

Therefore:

Re-Entry Authority ≠ Liability Determination Authority by default.

Local Re-Entry Authority

Authority to reopen one Relationship should not automatically extend to the complete Liability Network.

Therefore:

Local Re-Entry Authority ≠ Universal Network Re-Entry Authority.

Authority Conflict

Where Re-entry authority is disputed or conflicting, the conflict should remain explicit.

Re-Entry Conditions

Re-entry may depend on specific conditions.

LREM should represent whether those conditions are:

satisfied, unsatisfied, pending, unknown, uncertain, or disputed.

Conditional Re-Entry

Where Re-entry depends on unresolved conditions, its status should remain conditional.

Re-Entry State

LREM may represent states such as:

Not Required, Assessment Pending, Conditionally Required, Required, Initiated, In Progress, Completed, Unknown, Uncertain, Disputed, Superseded, or another explicitly governed state.

These are methodological governance states, not universal legal classifications.

Unknown Re-Entry State

Where available information is insufficient to determine whether Re-entry is required, the state should remain Unknown.

Therefore:

Unknown Re-Entry State ≠ No Re-Entry Required by assumption.

Uncertain Re-Entry

Where materially plausible alternatives remain, uncertainty should remain explicit.

Disputed Re-Entry

A disputed Re-entry requirement should remain represented as disputed until legitimately resolved.

Re-Entry Effective Time

LREM should identify when renewed governance becomes effective where applicable.

Trigger Time vs Re-Entry Time

The triggering event may occur before Re-entry is identified or initiated.

Therefore:

Re-Entry Trigger Time ≠ Re-Entry Effective Time by default.

Discovery Time vs Trigger Time

Information about an earlier event may become known later.

Therefore:

Discovery Time ≠ Re-Entry Trigger Event Time by default.

Reopening Time vs Original Finality Time

A reopened Liability lifecycle should preserve both times.

The later Reopening does not rewrite the earlier Finality Effective Time.

New Evidence

New evidence may justify Re-entry where it materially affects prior governance.

Evidence alone does not automatically establish the new Liability state.

Therefore:

New Evidence ≠ New Liability Determination.

Evidence vs Re-Entry

New evidence may create a Re-entry Basis without determining the outcome of the reassessment.

Therefore:

Evidence Supporting Re-Entry ≠ Predetermined Reassessment Outcome.

Incorrect Earlier Information

Where prior governance relied on materially incorrect information, LREM should identify the earliest governance space affected.

Correction vs Changed Reality

LREM should distinguish:

an earlier state that was incorrect when established, from an earlier state that was valid but later became outdated because reality changed.

Historical Correction

Correction should preserve what was previously recorded and why it was corrected.

Therefore:

Correction ≠ Historical Erasure.

Changed Reality

Where reality changed after a valid earlier governance state, Re-entry should preserve the validity of the earlier state for its historical period.

Payment Reversal

A reversed payment may require renewed Satisfaction, Resolution, State, or other Liability governance.

It does not automatically recreate every earlier Liability state.

Settlement Failure

Failure or termination of a Settlement may require renewed governance where it materially affects Resolution or Discharge.

Therefore:

Settlement Failure ≠ Automatic Restoration of Every Prior Liability State.

Discharge Reversal

Where a Discharge state becomes invalid or is legitimately reversed, LREM should identify the affected requirement and appropriate Re-entry Destination.

Resolution Reversal

Resolution reversal should preserve the prior Resolution as part of the history.

Therefore:

Resolution Reversal ≠ Resolution History Deletion.

Finality Reversal

Where a Final Liability legitimately re-enters active governance:

Finality Reversal ≠ Finality History Deletion.

New Subject Information

New information about a Subject may require Relationship, Scope, Allocation, Transfer, Recourse, or other reassessment depending on material effect.

New Relationship Information

Discovery of a previously unidentified Liability Relationship may require local or network-level Re-entry.

New Recourse Relationship

A newly formed or newly identified Recourse Relationship does not automatically reopen the primary Liability Relationship.

Therefore:

New Recourse Relationship ≠ Primary Liability Reopening by default.

External Lifecycle Change

Changes in Harm, Accountability, Claims, Operational Reality, identity, or another adjacent governance domain may create Re-entry-relevant inputs.

They do not automatically require Liability Re-entry.

Harm Change

A change in Harm governance may be relevant to Liability.

Therefore:

Harm Reopening ≠ Liability Reopening by default.

Claim Reopening

A reopened Claim does not automatically reopen Liability.

Therefore:

Claim Reopening ≠ Liability Reopening.

Accountability Reopening

Renewed Accountability governance does not automatically require Liability Re-entry.

Therefore:

Accountability Reopening ≠ Liability Reopening.

Operational Change

A new operational event may create a new Liability issue or affect an existing one.

LREM should distinguish these cases.

Therefore:

New Operational Event ≠ Existing Liability Re-Entry by default.

Re-Entry vs New Liability Formation

Where a new event creates a genuinely separate Liability Origin, it should enter LIGA™ as a new lifecycle rather than being forced into Re-entry of an unrelated prior Liability.

Same Origin Test

LREM should assess whether the renewed governance concerns:

the same Liability Origin, the same continuing Liability Relationship, a secondary Relationship, or a genuinely new Liability lifecycle.

Historical Integrity

Re-entry should preserve:

the pre-Re-entry State, Resolution, Discharge, Finality assessment, Finality State where applicable, Trigger, Basis, Destination, reassessment path, and resulting new State.

Re-Entry History

Each Re-entry should become part of the Governed Liability Historical Record.

No Retroactive Rewrite

A later state should not be represented as though it had always existed.

Therefore:

Later Liability State ≠ Historical State by retroactive substitution.

Re-Entry Provenance

LREM should preserve sufficient provenance to explain:

what triggered Re-entry, where the information came from, what governance state was affected, why the chosen destination was selected, and what reassessment followed.

Re-Entry Rationale

A governed Re-entry should contain sufficient rationale to explain why renewed governance was necessary.

Re-Entry Conflict

Conflicting information concerning whether Re-entry is required should remain explicit.

Therefore:

Conflicting Re-Entry Inputs ≠ Authority to Force Re-Entry.

Re-Entry Gap

Where material information necessary to determine Re-entry is unavailable, the gap should remain visible.

Automated Re-Entry Detection

AI or automated systems may assist with:

change detection, new evidence detection, condition monitoring, payment reversal detection, Settlement failure detection, relationship change detection, historical inconsistency detection, dependency analysis, and Re-entry routing.

Automation may identify potential triggers.

It should not silently rewrite Liability states.

Therefore:

Automated Re-Entry Trigger Detection ≠ Liability Re-Entry.

Automated Routing

Automated systems may propose the earliest materially affected governance space.

The routing should remain explainable and preserve uncertainty where material ambiguity exists.

Re-Entry Completion

Re-entry is complete when the materially affected governance spaces have been reassessed and a sufficiently coherent updated Liability state has been established.

Re-Entry Completion vs Finality

Completion of Re-entry does not automatically establish Finality.

Therefore:

Re-Entry Completion ≠ Liability Finality.

Return to LCFS

After upstream reassessment, the updated Liability structure should return to LCFS where continuity, historical record, Finality assessment, or Finality recording requires renewed governance.

Finality Reassessment After Re-Entry

Where Re-entry materially affects a previous Finality Basis, Finality should be reassessed.

Finality Restoration

A reopened Liability may later qualify for Finality again.

The new Finality assessment should remain connected to the earlier Finality and Re-entry history.

Multiple Re-Entry Cycles

One Liability lifecycle may contain multiple Re-entry cycles.

Each should remain distinguishable and historically reconstructable.

Governed Liability Re-Entry Structure

The principal output of LREM is:

Governed Liability Re-Entry Structure

It should identify, where applicable:

Re-Entry Object, Liability Origin, affected Liability requirement, affected Subjects, affected Liability Relationships, Liability Network relevance, Re-Entry Trigger, Trigger Time, Discovery Time, Re-Entry Basis, materiality assessment, Re-Entry Scope, affected prior governance output, earliest materially affected governance space, Re-Entry Destination, preserved valid governance, downstream dependencies, required reassessment, Re-Entry authority, conditions, Re-Entry State, effective time, uncertainty, disputes, conflicts, historical continuity, prior Resolution, prior Discharge, prior Finality, provenance, and Re-Entry rationale.

Meaning of the Output

A Governed Liability Re-Entry Structure means that renewed Liability governance has been sufficiently bounded and routed.

It identifies:

what must be reconsidered, where governance must return, what prior governance remains valid, and how the existing Liability history must be preserved.

It does not predetermine the result of the reassessment.

It does not create Finality.

Minimum Implementation Framework

A conforming implementation of LREM must provide exactly the following eight capabilities:

Re-Entry Object, Trigger, Basis & Materiality Identification

Capability to identify the Re-Entry Object, Re-entry Trigger, supporting Basis, relevant times, and whether the development is materially capable of affecting prior Liability governance.

Liability Origin, Subject, Relationship & Network Continuity Governance

Capability to preserve the existing Liability Origin and identity, distinguish affected Subjects and Relationships, maintain Liability Network associations, and separate Re-entry from genuinely new Liability formation.

Re-Entry Scope, Boundary & Earliest Affected Governance Space Identification

Capability to define the Scope of Re-entry, establish what prior governance is affected, and identify the earliest materially affected LIGA™ governance space.

Dependency, Preserved Validity & Reassessment Propagation Governance

Capability to identify which prior outputs remain valid, which downstream outputs materially depend on the affected state, where reassessment must propagate, and where it may legitimately stop.

Resolution, Discharge, Finality, Transfer & Recourse Re-Entry Governance

Capability to govern Re-entry involving Resolution, Discharge, prior Finality, Transfer, Sharing, secondary Liability, Recourse, and Liability Network structures without collapsing their independent lifecycle states.

Authority, Condition, State, Temporal, Uncertainty & Conflict Governance

Capability to represent Re-entry authority, conditions, states, effective time, uncertainty, disputes, conflicts, and unresolved information without forcing unsupported Re-entry.

Historical Integrity, Provenance, Correction & Reopening Governance

Capability to preserve pre-Re-entry states, distinguish correction from changed reality, maintain Re-entry provenance and rationale, govern Reopening, and ensure that Re-entry does not rewrite history.

Re-Entry Routing, Completion & LCFS Return Governance

Capability to route reassessment to the correct LIGA™ governance space, determine when Re-entry is complete, and return the updated Liability structure to LCFS for renewed continuity, historical, Finality, and Finality Record governance.

Cross-Architecture Boundary

LREM may receive Re-entry-relevant inputs from adjacent OOF® architectures.

ORA™ may establish new or changed Operational Reality.

HAGA™ may establish new Harm, Harm change, Harm Reopening, or revised Harm states.

AGA™ may establish changes in Responsibility, Accountability, attribution, or authority.

ADIT® may provide newly available evidence or audit findings.

VALIDOS™ may identify validation changes or failures.

OBIDENITY® may provide new identity, Origin, succession, provenance, or authority information.

VFM® may provide payment reversal, Transfer, or other Value Flow developments.

AIG® and ASGA™ may provide new AI or autonomous-system events.

Claims governance may identify Claim Reopening or Claim lifecycle changes.

These developments may create Liability Re-entry relevance.

They do not automatically reopen Liability.

The canonical interoperability principle is:

A change in an adjacent governance domain may create a Re-entry-relevant input, but LREM determines whether the existing Liability lifecycle itself requires renewed governance and where that governance must resume.

Canonical Boundary

LREM begins when a material development creates a legitimate question about whether an existing Liability governance state remains sufficient.

LREM governs:

Re-Entry Objects, Re-entry Triggers, materiality, Re-entry Basis, Liability Origin continuity, Subjects, Relationships, Liability Network relevance, Re-entry Scope, Re-entry Boundary, earliest materially affected governance space, Re-entry Destination, preserved validity, dependency propagation, reassessment Scope, Resolution and Discharge Re-entry, Finality Re-entry, Transfer and Recourse Re-entry, authority, conditions, Re-entry State, time, uncertainty, disputes, conflicts, historical integrity, Reopening, provenance, and Re-entry rationale.

LREM does not predetermine the outcome of the governance space to which the matter returns.

LREM does not automatically create a new Liability Origin.

LREM does not erase prior Resolution, Discharge, or Finality.

LREM ends when renewed governance has been sufficiently bounded and routed through a:

Governed Liability Re-Entry Structure

or when Re-entry has been assessed as not required.

Use Case 1 — New Evidence After Finality

A Liability Relationship previously reached a Governed Liability Finality State.

New materially relevant evidence later emerges that may affect the original Liability Determination.

LREM preserves the earlier Finality State, identifies the new evidence as a potential Re-entry Trigger, assesses its materiality, and determines that Liability Determination is the earliest materially affected governance space.

The matter is routed back to LDS for reassessment.

The prior Finality remains part of the historical record.

Re-entry must not rewrite history.

Use Case 2 — Settlement Failure Requires Limited Re-Entry

A Liability Relationship entered Resolution through a Settlement and was represented as conditionally discharged.

A material Settlement condition later fails.

The original Liability Basis, Relationships, Connection, Determination, Scope, and Allocation remain valid.

LREM therefore does not restart the complete LIGA™ lifecycle.

It routes the matter back to Liability Resolution & Discharge governance for targeted reassessment while preserving unaffected upstream governance.

Reassess what materially changed. Preserve what remains valid.

Canonical Closing Statement

Re-entry is not a failure of Finality governance.

It is the mechanism that prevents Finality from becoming an irreversible methodological error.

New information may emerge.

Conditions may fail.

Payments may reverse.

Settlements may collapse.

Relationships may become visible later.

And a previously valid Finality State may become insufficient under materially changed circumstances.

The answer is not to erase the earlier lifecycle.

The answer is to continue it.

Re-enter the earliest materially affected governance space.

Reassess what materially changed. Preserve what remains valid.

Re-entry must not rewrite history.
