Canonical Definition

The Liability Continuity & Finality Standard (LCFS) governs the continuity, historical reconstructability, Re-entry, and legitimate Finality of Liability throughout and beyond its active governance lifecycle.

LCFS establishes how a Liability Relationship, Liability requirement, or Liability Network remains continuously traceable from its Origin through Determination, Scope, Allocation, State changes, Transfer, Sharing, Recourse, Resolution, and Discharge; how its complete governance history is preserved; when a previously governed Liability matter must re-enter an earlier lifecycle space; and under what governed conditions active Liability governance may legitimately reach Finality.

LCFS distinguishes the end of an active Liability requirement from the end of the complete Liability governance lifecycle.

Resolution may end an active requirement.

Discharge may end continuing performance.

Neither automatically establishes Finality.

Finality requires a governed determination that the applicable Liability structure has reached a legitimate terminal lifecycle state while remaining historically reconstructable and capable of governed Re-entry where materially required.

LCFS does not erase Liability.

It governs when active Liability governance may end without destroying the continuity of what occurred before that end.

Its foundational distinction is:

Liability Resolution ≠ Liability Finality.

Operational Role

The operational role of LCFS is:

to preserve the continuous identity, history, reconstructability, and lifecycle integrity of Liability and to determine when a Liability Relationship or Liability Network may legitimately leave active governance while remaining capable of governed Re-entry where materially required.

LCFS is the terminal Parent Standard of LIGA™.

Core Question

How must Liability remain continuous and reconstructable, when may a Liability Relationship or Liability Network legitimately reach finality, and under what conditions must governed re-entry occur?

Position in the LIGA™ Governance Flow

The canonical LIGA™ Governance Flow is:

Liability Context → Liability Object → Liability Basis → Liability Relationships → Liability Conditions → Liability Connection → Liability Determination → Liability Scope & Allocation → Liability State & Change → Liability Transfer & Recourse → Liability Resolution & Discharge → Liability Continuity & Finality LCFS governs:

Liability Continuity & Finality

It is the twelfth and terminal governance space of the LIGA™ lifecycle.

Complete Liability Governance Lifecycle

The complete Liability Governance Lifecycle extends beyond Liability Determination and beyond Resolution.

The canonical lifecycle progression is:

Potential Liability → Attached / Determined Liability → Active Liability Governance → Liability Change → Transfer / Recourse where applicable → Resolution / Discharge → Continuity / Re-Entry where required → Finality Therefore:

Liability Determination ≠ Liability Lifecycle Completion.

Liability Resolution ≠ Liability Lifecycle Completion.

Liability Discharge ≠ Liability Lifecycle Completion.

Complete Lifecycle Principle

LIGA™ governs the complete Liability Governance Lifecycle from Liability-relevant context, Liability Object formation, and potential Liability formation through Liability Basis, Relationships, Conditions, Connection, Attachment and Determination, Scope and Allocation, active Liability State and Change, Transfer and Recourse, Resolution and Discharge, Continuity, Re-entry where required, and Finality.

The governing principle is:

LIGA™ governs Liability from relevance to Finality.

Governed Space

LCFS governs:

Liability continuity, Liability identity continuity, Liability Relationship continuity, Liability Network continuity, historical preservation, historical reconstructability, lifecycle provenance, state continuity, Resolution continuity, Discharge continuity, Finality readiness, Finality assessment, Relationship Finality, Network Finality, Re-entry, Reopening, post-Finality Re-entry where legitimately required, and Finality record preservation.

Continuity Begins Before Finality

Liability continuity does not begin only after Resolution.

Continuity exists throughout the Liability lifecycle.

LCFS owns the terminal governance of that continuity and the preservation necessary for Finality.

Therefore:

Liability Continuity ≠ Post-Resolution Recordkeeping.

Liability Identity Continuity

A Liability structure should remain traceable to its Origin even when its:

State, Scope, Allocation, Subjects, Relationships, performance status, Transfer structure, Recourse structure, Resolution state, or representation changes.

Origin Continuity

The Origin of Liability should remain historically identifiable.

A later state should not silently create a false new Origin.

Therefore:

Liability Change ≠ New Liability Origin by default.

Identity Through Change

A methodological object may change its name, acronym, version, location, or representation without changing its Origin Identity.

The same principle applies to Liability governance objects.

Therefore:

Representation Change ≠ Liability Identity Change by default.

Relationship Continuity

A Liability Relationship should remain traceable across its lifecycle states.

Its history should preserve:

formation, Determination, Scope, Allocation, State changes, Transfer, Sharing, Recourse, Resolution, Discharge, and Finality.

Network Continuity

Where one matter creates multiple Liability Relationships, LCFS should preserve the wider Liability Network.

The canonical principle is:

One occurrence may create multiple Liability Relationships.

Therefore:

One Liability Relationship ≠ Complete Liability Network.

Liability Network Identity

A Liability Network should preserve the relationships between:

primary Liability, shared Liability, transferred Liability, secondary Liability, Recourse, Indemnification, insurance-related structures, and other connected Liability Relationships.

Current State vs History

The current Liability State is only one point in the lifecycle.

Therefore:

Current Liability State ≠ Complete Liability History.

Historical Integrity

Historical Liability states should not be overwritten merely because the current state changed.

The governance record should preserve:

what was known, what was determined, what changed, when it changed, why it changed, under what authority, and what replaced the earlier state.

Historical Reconstructability

A Liability lifecycle should remain sufficiently reconstructable to explain how the current or final state was reached.

Reconstructability should not depend solely on the latest record.

Liability Provenance

LCFS should preserve provenance across material lifecycle transitions.

Provenance may include:

Origin, source governance objects, Determination, authority, state transitions, Transfer, Resolution, Discharge, Re-entry, and Finality decisions.

Supersession

A later Liability state may supersede an earlier state.

The earlier state should remain historically preserved.

Therefore:

Supersession ≠ Historical Deletion.

Correction

An incorrect Liability record may be corrected.

Correction should preserve the prior record and the Basis for correction where materially relevant.

Therefore:

Correction ≠ Historical Erasure.

Reassessment

A previously valid Liability state may require reassessment because relevant circumstances changed.

Therefore:

Reassessment ≠ Correction.

Resolution Continuity

A resolved Liability requirement should remain connected to:

its Origin, Determination, Scope, Allocation, active State history, Resolution pathway, and Resolution Basis.

Discharge Continuity

A discharged Liability requirement should remain historically reconstructable.

Therefore:

Discharge ≠ Historical Erasure.

Resolution vs Finality

Resolution governs whether an active Liability requirement has legitimately ended or changed into a resolved state.

Finality governs whether the applicable Liability lifecycle may legitimately leave active governance.

Therefore:

Liability Resolution ≠ Liability Finality.

Discharge vs Finality

Discharge may remove a continuing active requirement.

It does not automatically establish lifecycle Finality.

Therefore:

Liability Discharge ≠ Liability Finality.

Extinguishment vs Finality

Extinguishment of an active Liability requirement does not necessarily establish Finality of the complete Liability structure.

Therefore:

Liability Extinguishment ≠ Liability Finality by default.

Settlement vs Finality

Settlement may contribute to Resolution.

It does not independently establish Finality.

Therefore:

Settlement ≠ Liability Finality.

Payment vs Finality

Payment does not establish Finality.

Therefore:

Payment ≠ Liability Finality.

Administrative Closure vs Finality

Administrative closure does not establish Liability Finality.

Therefore:

Administrative Closure ≠ Liability Finality.

Claim Closure vs Finality

Closure of a Claim does not establish Liability Finality.

Therefore:

Claim Closure ≠ Liability Finality.

Finality

Liability Finality is the governed terminal state in which the applicable Liability structure no longer requires active lifecycle governance, subject to preserved history and legitimate Re-entry conditions.

Finality does not mean that the Liability never existed.

Finality Object

Every Finality assessment should identify exactly what is proposed to reach Finality.

The Finality Object may be:

a Liability requirement, a Liability Relationship, a group of Relationships, a Recourse Relationship, or a complete Liability Network.

Finality Object vs Liability Object

The Finality Object should not automatically be treated as identical to the original Liability Object.

Therefore:

Finality Object ≠ Liability Object by default.

Finality Scope

Finality should possess an explicit Scope.

The Scope may be:

requirement-specific, Subject-specific, Relationship-specific, network-specific, or another explicitly bounded structure.

Partial Finality

Finality may exist for one bounded Liability Relationship while other Relationships remain active.

Therefore:

Local Liability Finality ≠ Complete Liability Network Finality.

Relationship Finality

A Liability Relationship may reach Finality when its lifecycle has legitimately completed and no further active governance is required for that Relationship, subject to preserved Re-entry conditions.

Network Finality

Liability Network Finality requires assessment of the complete relevant network rather than one Relationship alone.

Therefore:

Liability Relationship Finality ≠ Liability Network Finality.

Primary Liability Finality

Primary Liability may reach Finality while secondary Liability remains active.

Therefore:

Primary Liability Finality ≠ Recourse Liability Finality.

Recourse Finality

Recourse Relationships possess their own lifecycle and may reach Finality independently.

Shared Liability Finality

Where multiple Subjects possess separate Liability Relationships, Finality should be evaluated at the appropriate Subject and Relationship level.

Therefore:

Subject A Finality ≠ Subject B Finality by default.

Finality Readiness

A Liability structure may become ready for Finality assessment after Resolution and Discharge.

Readiness does not establish Finality.

Therefore:

Finality Readiness ≠ Liability Finality.

Finality Preconditions

Finality assessment should consider whether:

the relevant Liability Object and Relationship remain identifiable, the applicable Determination is known, Scope and Allocation are reconstructable, material State changes are preserved, Transfer and Recourse structures are accounted for, Resolution and Discharge are sufficiently verified, remaining active Liability is identified, relevant secondary Relationships are accounted for, material disputes are resolved or explicitly governed, and the historical record is sufficiently reconstructable.

Finality Basis

Every governed Finality State should possess an identifiable Basis.

The Basis should explain why active Liability governance may legitimately end.

Finality Basis vs Resolution Basis

The Basis supporting Resolution and the Basis supporting Finality may overlap but are not automatically identical.

Therefore:

Resolution Basis ≠ Finality Basis by default.

Finality Authority

Where Finality requires an authority determination, the authority and its Scope should remain explicit.

Local Finality Authority

Authority over one Liability Relationship should not automatically extend to the complete Liability Network.

Therefore:

Local Finality Authority ≠ Universal Liability Finality Authority.

Finality Conditions

Finality may depend upon conditions such as:

verified Resolution, verified Discharge, completion of relevant secondary Relationships, absence of unresolved active requirements, required preservation of records, completion of applicable waiting or review states, or another legitimate governance condition.

Conditional Finality

Where required conditions remain outstanding, Finality should not be represented as unconditional.

Therefore:

Conditional Finality ≠ Unconditional Finality.

Finality State

LCFS may represent Finality States such as:

Not Ready for Finality, Finality Assessment Pending, Partially Final, Relationship Final, Network Final, Conditionally Final, Final, Re-entry Required, Reopened, Unknown, Uncertain, Disputed, Superseded, or another explicitly governed state.

These are methodological governance states, not universal legal classifications.

Unknown Finality

Where available information cannot establish Finality, the state should remain Unknown.

Therefore:

Unknown Finality ≠ No Finality by assumption.

Uncertain Finality

Where materially plausible interpretations remain, uncertainty should remain explicit.

Therefore:

Uncertain Finality ≠ Authority to Select a Preferred Finality State.

Disputed Finality

Where Finality is disputed, the dispute should remain part of the governed state.

Therefore:

Disputed Finality ≠ Governed Finality.

Finality Effective Time

LCFS should identify when Finality becomes effective.

Resolution Time vs Finality Time

Resolution may precede Finality.

Therefore:

Resolution Effective Time ≠ Finality Effective Time by default.

Discharge Time vs Finality Time

Discharge may precede Finality.

Therefore:

Discharge Effective Time ≠ Finality Effective Time by default.

Recording Time vs Finality Time

Finality may be recorded after it becomes effective.

Therefore:

Finality Recording Time ≠ Finality Effective Time by default.

Finality Verification

Finality should be verified against the complete applicable lifecycle structure.

A closure label alone is insufficient.

Therefore:

Closure Signal ≠ Liability Finality.

Finality Evidence

Evidence may support Finality assessment.

Evidence does not independently create Finality.

Therefore:

Evidence of Resolution ≠ Evidence of Finality by default.

Finality Rationale

A Finality State should possess sufficient rationale to explain:

what reached Finality, why, for which Subjects, within what Scope, at what time, under what authority, and what related Liability remains outside that Finality.

Finality State vs Rationale

Therefore:

Finality State ≠ Finality Rationale.

Finality Record

LCFS should preserve a canonical Finality Record sufficient to reconstruct the terminal governance decision.

The record should not replace the underlying lifecycle history.

Finality ≠ Erasure

Finality does not authorize deletion of the Liability's methodological history merely because active governance has ended.

Therefore:

Liability Finality ≠ Liability Erasure.

Post-Finality Historical Continuity

A Final Liability structure should remain reconstructable after active governance ends.

The terminal state remains part of the Liability history.

Finality and Archival State

A Liability structure may transition from active governance into historical or archival continuity.

Archival status should not be confused with non-existence.

Therefore:

Archived Liability ≠ Non-Existent Liability.

Re-Entry

Re-entry is the governed return of a Liability matter to an earlier lifecycle governance space when materially relevant information, conditions, events, corrections, conflicts, or changes require renewed governance.

Re-Entry Principle

Re-entry should occur only as far upstream as materially required.

The canonical rule is:

Re-enter the earliest materially affected governance space.

Targeted Reassessment

Re-entry should not automatically reconstruct the entire Liability lifecycle.

The canonical principle is:

Reassess what materially changed. Preserve what remains valid.

Re-Entry Trigger

Potential Re-entry Triggers may include:

new materially relevant evidence, incorrect earlier governance input, changed condition, failed Resolution condition, reversed payment, Settlement failure, Discharge reversal, new Relationship information, previously unknown Subject, Recourse development, authority correction, or another material lifecycle change.

Re-Entry Trigger vs Reopening

A Re-entry Trigger creates a reason to assess Re-entry.

It does not automatically establish that the Liability has reopened.

Therefore:

Re-Entry Trigger ≠ Liability Reopening.

Reopening

Reopening occurs when a previously resolved, discharged, or Final Liability structure legitimately returns to active governance.

Reopening vs New Liability

Reopening should preserve the original Liability identity where the same Liability lifecycle is continuing.

Therefore:

Liability Reopening ≠ New Liability by default.

Reopening vs New Origin

Re-entry should not create a new Origin merely because the matter becomes active again.

Therefore:

Re-entry ≠ New Origin by default.

Historical Integrity of Re-Entry

Re-entry should preserve:

the prior State, the prior Resolution, the prior Discharge, the prior Finality State, the Re-entry Trigger, the Re-entry Basis, and subsequent governance.

The foundational rule is:

Re-entry must not rewrite history.

Finality Reversal

Where a previously Final Liability structure legitimately re-enters governance, the historical fact that Finality had previously been established should remain preserved.

Therefore:

Finality Reversal ≠ Finality History Deletion.

Reopened Liability State

A reopened Liability should distinguish:

historical Finality, current active state, Re-entry Basis, and the lifecycle space to which governance returned.

Re-Entry Routing

Re-entry may route to:

Liability Context, Liability Object, Liability Basis, Liability Relationships, Liability Conditions, Liability Connection, Liability Determination, Liability Scope & Allocation, Liability State & Change, Liability Transfer & Recourse,

or Liability Resolution & Discharge

depending on what materially changed.

Context Re-Entry

Where the material change concerns the relevant environment or circumstances, governance may return to Liability Context.

Object Re-Entry

Where the governed matter itself was incorrectly identified or bounded, governance may return to Liability Object governance.

Basis Re-Entry

Where the underlying Liability Basis materially changes or was incorrectly represented, governance may return to Liability Basis.

Relationship Re-Entry

Where new or corrected Subject or Relationship information arises, governance may return to Liability Relationships.

Conditions Re-Entry

Where applicable Liability Conditions materially change, governance may return to Liability Conditions.

Connection Re-Entry

Where Liability-Relevant Connection changes or new relevant connection information emerges, governance may return to Liability Connection.

Determination Re-Entry

Where new information materially affects whether Liability attached, governance may return to Liability Determination.

Scope & Allocation Re-Entry

Where the extent or distribution of established Liability changes, governance may return to Liability Scope & Allocation.

State & Change Re-Entry

Where the current Liability State changes without invalidating earlier governance, governance may return to Liability State & Change.

Transfer & Recourse Re-Entry

Where a new Transfer, Sharing, succession, insurance, Indemnification, contribution, or Recourse structure emerges, governance may return to Liability Transfer & Recourse.

Resolution & Discharge Re-Entry

Where Satisfaction, Settlement, Discharge, Resolution, or related conditions materially change, governance may return to Liability Resolution & Discharge.

No Automatic Full Lifecycle Restart

Re-entry should not automatically restart LIGA™ from Liability Context.

Therefore:

Re-entry ≠ Full Lifecycle Restart by default.

Preserved Validity

Governance results unaffected by the Re-entry Trigger should remain valid unless a material dependency requires reassessment.

Re-Entry Dependency Propagation

Where a changed governance object materially affects downstream states, those states should be reassessed.

Propagation should follow actual dependencies rather than assumptions.

Re-Entry Boundary

The Re-entry Boundary should identify:

what changed, which governance space is affected, which prior outputs remain valid, which downstream outputs require reassessment, and where the reassessment may stop.

Re-Entry Authority

Where Re-entry requires authority, the relevant authority and Scope should remain explicit.

Re-Entry State

LCFS may represent Re-entry States such as:

Not Required, Assessment Pending, Required, Initiated, In Progress, Completed, Unknown, Uncertain, Disputed, or another explicitly governed state.

Re-Entry Completion

Re-entry is complete when the materially affected governance spaces have been reassessed and the resulting Liability lifecycle state is again sufficiently coherent.

Re-Entry and Finality Reassessment

After Re-entry, Finality should be reassessed where the change materially affects the previous Finality Basis.

Finality Restoration

A reopened Liability may later reach Finality again.

The new Finality State should remain linked to the prior Finality and Re-entry history.

Multiple Finality Cycles

A Liability lifecycle may contain:

Resolution, Finality, Re-entry, renewed active governance, and later Finality.

Therefore:

One Liability Origin may contain more than one Finality assessment over time.

Finality Versioning

Where Finality is reassessed, the current Finality State should remain distinguishable from earlier Finality States.

Historical Finality

An earlier Finality State remains historically true as a governance state that existed at that time even if later Re-entry occurs.

Finality and Liability Network Evolution

A Liability Network may evolve after one Relationship reaches Finality.

New secondary or Recourse Relationships should not silently rewrite the Finality history of earlier Relationships.

Network Finality Assessment

Network Finality should consider whether any materially connected Liability Relationship remains active or requires governance.

Local Finality vs Network Finality

The canonical distinction is:

Local Liability Finality ≠ Liability Network Finality.

Finality Propagation

Finality should not automatically propagate between Relationships.

Therefore:

Relationship Connection ≠ Automatic Finality Propagation.

Claim Finality Distinction

Claims governance and Liability governance remain separate.

Therefore:

Claim Finality ≠ Liability Finality.

A Claim may close while Liability remains active.

Liability may reach Finality independently of a Claim lifecycle.

Harm Finality Distinction

Harm and Liability possess separate lifecycle Finality.

Therefore:

Harm Finality ≠ Liability Finality.

Accountability Finality Distinction

Completion of Accountability governance does not establish Liability Finality.

Therefore:

Accountability Closure ≠ Liability Finality.

Operational Finality Distinction

Termination of an operational process does not establish Liability Finality.

Therefore:

Operational Termination ≠ Liability Finality.

Contractual Finality Distinction

Termination or completion of a contract does not automatically establish Finality of Liability associated with that contract.

Therefore:

Contract Completion ≠ Liability Finality.

Audit Finality Distinction

Audit completion does not establish Liability Finality.

Therefore:

Audit Completion ≠ Liability Finality.

Validation Finality Distinction

Validation completion does not establish Liability Finality.

Therefore:

Validation Completion ≠ Liability Finality.

Continuity Across Systems

Where Liability information exists across multiple systems, continuity should preserve the ability to associate records belonging to the same governed Liability structure.

System Migration

Migration between systems should not create a new Liability Origin merely because the technical representation changes.

Therefore:

System Migration ≠ New Liability Origin.

Record Fragmentation

Fragmented records should not automatically be treated as separate Liability structures.

Identity and provenance should determine whether they belong to the same lifecycle.

Duplicate Records

Multiple records may represent the same Liability Relationship.

Therefore:

Multiple Liability Records ≠ Multiple Liability Relationships by default.

Record Loss

Loss of a record does not establish that Liability ceased to exist or reached Finality.

Therefore:

Record Absence ≠ Liability Finality.

Archival Continuity

Historical Liability records should remain associated with their Origin and lifecycle structure after archival transition.

Retention vs Finality

The existence or expiration of a record-retention period should not itself define methodological Liability Finality.

Therefore:

Record Retention End ≠ Liability Finality by default.

Data Deletion vs Liability Erasure

Deletion of a technical record does not methodologically erase the historical Liability event.

Therefore:

Data Deletion ≠ Liability Historical Non-Existence.

Continuity Under Subject Change

A Liability structure may persist through changes in:

identity representation, organizational form, succession, representation, or other Subject changes.

Continuity should preserve the historical relationship between earlier and later states.

Succession

Succession may alter the Subject associated with continuing Liability without rewriting the earlier Liability history.

Therefore:

Succession ≠ Historical Subject Replacement.

Authority Change

A change in governing authority should not erase prior valid governance states.

Jurisdictional or Framework Change

Where the applicable external framework changes, LCFS preserves the methodological history while allowing targeted reassessment where materially required.

Automated Continuity Governance

AI and automated systems may assist with:

identity linking, history reconstruction, dependency mapping, Finality readiness detection, Re-entry trigger detection, duplicate detection, network reconciliation, and provenance preservation.

Automation should not independently erase history or declare Finality without the required governance Basis and authority.

Therefore:

Automated Finality Classification ≠ Authoritative Liability Finality by default.

Automated Re-Entry Detection

Automated systems may identify potential Re-entry Triggers.

Detection should initiate governance rather than silently rewrite prior states.

Therefore:

Automated Re-Entry Trigger Detection ≠ Liability Reopening.

Continuity Failure

A continuity failure exists where the current Liability state cannot be reliably connected to the history necessary to explain it.

Continuity failure should be made explicit rather than reconstructed through unsupported assumptions.

Historical Gap

A gap in the Liability record should remain identifiable.

Therefore:

Historical Gap ≠ Continuity by Assumption.

Conflicting History

Where historical records conflict, the conflict should remain visible until legitimately reconciled.

Therefore:

Conflicting Liability History ≠ Authority to Select a Preferred History.

Finality Conflict

Where evidence or governance states conflict concerning Finality, LCFS should preserve the conflict.

Therefore:

Conflicting Finality Inputs ≠ Governed Finality.

Finality Correction

An incorrectly represented Finality State may be corrected.

The earlier state and correction should remain reconstructable.

Therefore:

Finality Correction ≠ Historical Erasure.

Finality Reassessment

A previously valid Finality State may require reassessment where materially relevant circumstances change.

Therefore:

Finality Reassessment ≠ Finality Correction.

Finality Supersession

A later Finality assessment may supersede an earlier assessment.

Therefore:

Finality Supersession ≠ Historical Deletion.

Governed Liability Continuity Structure

LCFS should preserve a governed continuity structure connecting the Liability lifecycle from Origin to current or Final state.

The structure should identify, where applicable:

Liability Origin, Liability Objects, Subjects, Relationships, Basis, Conditions, Connections, Determinations, Scope, Allocation, State history, Transfers, Sharing, Recourse, Resolution, Discharge, Re-entry, Finality assessments, and Finality records.

Governed Liability Finality State

The terminal active-governance output of LCFS is:

Governed Liability Finality State

It should identify, where applicable:

Finality Object, Finality Scope, affected Subjects, affected Liability Relationships, Finality Basis, Finality authority, Finality conditions, Resolution association, Discharge association, remaining active Liability, secondary Liability status, Recourse status, Liability Network status, Finality State, effective time, Re-entry conditions, historical continuity status, provenance, rationale, and Finality history.

Meaning of the Finality State

A Governed Liability Finality State means that the applicable Liability structure has been sufficiently governed to determine whether active lifecycle governance may legitimately end.

It does not mean:

the Liability never existed, the history may be erased, all connected Liability Relationships necessarily reached Finality, Re-entry can never occur, or every external process has ended.

Liability Lifecycle Completion

A Liability lifecycle reaches methodological completion when the applicable Finality Object has reached a governed Finality State and its history remains sufficiently preserved for continuity and legitimate Re-entry.

Therefore:

Lifecycle Completion requires Finality, not merely Resolution.

Canonical Finality Rule

The canonical Finality rule is:

Finality ends active Liability governance. It does not erase Liability history.

Canonical Re-Entry Rule

The canonical Re-entry rule is:

Re-enter the earliest materially affected governance space.

Canonical Historical Integrity Rule

The canonical historical integrity rule is:

Re-entry must not rewrite history.

Canonical Reassessment Rule

The canonical reassessment rule is:

Reassess what materially changed. Preserve what remains valid.

Governance Requirements

A conforming implementation of LCFS should:

preserve Liability Origin and lifecycle identity; maintain continuity across material State changes; preserve Relationship and Liability Network history; distinguish current State from complete history; preserve Resolution and Discharge history; identify Finality Objects and Scope; assess Relationship Finality independently from Network Finality; identify remaining active Liability; preserve secondary and Recourse Relationships; establish Finality Basis and authority where applicable; represent Finality conditions and uncertainty; preserve Finality rationale and provenance; support targeted Re-entry; preserve prior states during Re-entry; avoid false new Origins; support historical reconstructability; and preserve the Liability lifecycle after active governance ends.

Cross-Architecture Boundary

LCFS may receive continuity, historical, and terminal-state inputs from adjacent OOF® architectures.

ORA™ governs Operational Reality and may establish events relevant to continuing or reopening Liability.

HAGA™ governs Harm continuity and Harm Finality independently from Liability Finality.

AGA™ governs Responsibility, Accountability, authority, and attribution without determining Liability Finality.

ADIT® governs evidence and auditability.

VALIDOS™ governs Validation.

OBIDENITY® may provide identity, provenance, ownership, authority, succession, and continuity inputs.

VFM® may preserve relevant economic and Value Flow.

AIG® and ASGA™ may provide AI or autonomous-system events relevant to Liability continuity or Re-entry.

Claims governance may govern Claim closure or Claim Finality separately from Liability Finality.

These external governance states may affect LCFS.

They do not replace LCFS Finality governance.

The canonical interoperability principle is:

An external lifecycle may end, continue, or reopen independently; LCFS governs whether the Liability lifecycle itself remains active, requires Re-entry, or has legitimately reached Finality.

Canonical Boundary

LCFS governs the terminal continuity and Finality layer of LIGA™.

Its continuity responsibility spans the Liability lifecycle, while its terminal governance becomes decisive when Resolution, Discharge, or another lifecycle state creates a potential Finality condition.

LCFS begins terminal Finality assessment when the applicable Liability structure is sufficiently mature to determine whether active Liability governance may end.

It governs:

Liability continuity, Origin continuity, historical reconstructability, Relationship continuity, network continuity, Resolution continuity, Discharge continuity, Finality readiness, Finality Objects, Finality Scope, Finality Basis, Finality authority, Finality conditions, Relationship Finality, Network Finality, remaining active Liability, Re-entry, Reopening, Finality reversal, historical integrity, provenance, Finality records, and post-Finality continuity.

LCFS does not erase Liability history.

LCFS does not treat Resolution as Finality.

LCFS does not treat Relationship Finality as automatic Network Finality.

LCFS does not create a new Liability Origin merely because Re-entry occurs.

LCFS ends active LIGA™ governance when the applicable Liability structure reaches a:

Governed Liability Finality State

while remaining historically reconstructable and subject to legitimate Re-entry where materially required.

Canonical Closing Statement

Liability does not reach Finality merely because payment occurred.

It does not reach Finality merely because performance ended.

It does not reach Finality merely because a Settlement was completed, a Liability requirement was discharged, a Claim was closed, or an administrative system marked the matter complete.

Resolution answers whether an active Liability requirement has legitimately ended.

Finality answers whether the applicable Liability lifecycle may legitimately leave active governance.

Even then, the history remains.

The Origin remains.

The Determination remains part of the record.

The Scope, Allocation, State changes, Transfers, Recourse, Resolution, and Discharge remain part of the record.

And where materially relevant circumstances require renewed governance, Re-entry must preserve that history rather than rewrite it.

Liability Resolution ≠ Liability Finality.

Finality ends active Liability governance. It does not erase Liability history.

LIGA™ governs Liability from relevance to Finality.
