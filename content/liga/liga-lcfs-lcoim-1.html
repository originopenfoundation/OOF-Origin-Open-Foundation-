Canonical Definition

The Liability Continuity Identification Module (LCOIM) governs the identification of the Liability structures, identities, Relationships, states, dependencies, lifecycle transitions, and historical associations that must remain continuously traceable across the Liability lifecycle and into Finality governance.

LCOIM establishes what constitutes the continuing Liability governance object, which lifecycle elements belong to that continuity structure, how current and prior representations remain associated, and where continuity boundaries exist between distinct Liability Relationships, Liability requirements, and Liability Networks.

It prevents changes in State, Subject representation, system, record, Scope, Allocation, Transfer, Resolution, Discharge, or other lifecycle characteristics from silently fragmenting one continuing Liability structure into false separate objects or collapsing separate Liability structures into one.

Its principal output is:

Governed Liability Continuity Identification Structure

Its foundational distinction is:

Representation Change ≠ Liability Identity Change by default.

Operational Role

The operational role of LCOIM is:

to identify what must remain continuously traceable as the same Liability governance structure across lifecycle change so that history, Resolution, Re-entry, and Finality can be governed without loss of identity or false lifecycle fragmentation.

Central Operational Question

What Liability structure must remain continuously identifiable across lifecycle changes, which records, Subjects, Relationships, states, and transitions belong to that structure, and where must continuity boundaries be maintained?

Continuity Object

Every continuity assessment requires an identifiable Continuity Object.

The Continuity Object may be:

a Liability requirement, a Liability Relationship, a group of connected Liability Relationships, a Recourse Relationship, or a Liability Network.

Continuity Object vs Liability Object

The Continuity Object is not necessarily identical to the original Liability Object.

Therefore:

Continuity Object ≠ Liability Object by default.

Continuity Object Identity

The Continuity Object should remain traceable across material lifecycle transitions without requiring identical representation at every point.

Liability Origin Association

Continuity should preserve association with the relevant Liability Origin.

The Origin provides the historical anchor from which the continuing lifecycle may be reconstructed.

Origin vs Current Representation

The current representation of Liability may differ substantially from its earlier representation.

Therefore:

Current Representation ≠ Liability Origin.

Origin Continuity

A change in record, system, Subject representation, or governance State should not automatically create a new Origin.

Therefore:

Liability Change ≠ New Liability Origin by default.

Continuity Identity

LCOIM should establish the identifiers and relationships necessary to determine whether lifecycle records represent:

the same continuing Liability structure, a changed state of that structure, a secondary Liability Relationship, a successor structure, a reopened structure, or a genuinely distinct Liability lifecycle.

Identity Continuity vs State Continuity

A Liability may remain the same governance object while its State changes.

Therefore:

Liability State Change ≠ Liability Identity Change by default.

Identity Continuity vs Scope Change

A change in Liability Scope does not automatically create a new Liability identity.

Therefore:

Liability Scope Change ≠ New Liability by default.

Identity Continuity vs Allocation Change

Liability may be reallocated while remaining connected to the same underlying lifecycle.

Therefore:

Liability Allocation Change ≠ New Liability Origin by default.

Identity Continuity vs Subject Change

A change in Subject association may occur through succession, Transfer, substitution, representation, or another governed mechanism.

Such change should not automatically erase continuity.

Identity Continuity vs Transfer

Transfer may alter who bears, performs, administers, or economically carries an aspect of Liability.

Therefore:

Liability Transfer ≠ New Liability Origin by default.

Identity Continuity vs Recourse

A Recourse Relationship may arise from an existing Liability structure.

It should remain connected to that structure while preserving its own Relationship identity.

Therefore:

Recourse Relationship ≠ Primary Liability Relationship.

Relationship Continuity

Each Liability Relationship should remain independently identifiable throughout its lifecycle.

Relationship continuity may span:

formation, Determination, Scope, Allocation, State changes, Transfer, Resolution, Discharge, Re-entry, and Finality.

Relationship Identity

Two Relationships should not be merged merely because they share:

an Origin, Subject, Liability Object, payment, event, or Resolution pathway.

Therefore:

Shared Origin ≠ Relationship Identity.

Multiple Relationships

One Liability-relevant matter may create multiple distinct Liability Relationships.

Therefore:

One Occurrence ≠ One Liability Relationship.

Liability Network Continuity

Where multiple Relationships are materially connected, LCOIM should identify the Liability Network while preserving each Relationship's independent identity.

Network Identity

A Liability Network is not merely a collection of records.

It is the governed structure of materially connected Liability Relationships.

Relationship vs Network Continuity

Continuity of one Relationship should not be mistaken for continuity of the entire network.

Therefore:

Liability Relationship Continuity ≠ Liability Network Continuity.

Primary and Secondary Relationships

LCOIM should distinguish primary Liability Relationships from:

secondary Liability, shared Liability, transferred Liability, Recourse, Indemnification, insurance-related, or other connected Relationships.

Primary vs Recourse Identity

A Recourse Relationship may depend on primary Liability without becoming identical to it.

Therefore:

Primary Liability Relationship ≠ Recourse Liability Relationship.

Continuity Through Determination

Liability Determination should remain connected to the Liability structure it established.

Later State changes should not detach the current Liability from the Determination that produced it.

Continuity Through Scope & Allocation

Scope and Allocation outputs should remain traceable to the same governed Liability structure through later lifecycle stages.

Continuity Through State Change

State transitions should preserve:

prior State, new State, transition Basis, effective time, and continuity association.

State Transition vs New Object

Therefore:

Liability State Transition ≠ New Liability Object by default.

Continuity Through Transfer

Where Transfer occurs, LCOIM should preserve:

pre-Transfer state, Transfer event, affected requirement, affected Subjects, post-Transfer state, and continuing Liability identity.

Transfer vs Historical Replacement

A new Subject association should not replace the historical Subject association.

Therefore:

Transfer ≠ Historical Subject Replacement.

Continuity Through Sharing

Where Liability is shared, the resulting Subject-specific Relationships should remain connected to the applicable common structure while preserving independent identity.

Continuity Through Recourse

Recourse should remain connected to the primary Liability structure from which its relevance arose.

The Recourse Relationship should nevertheless retain its own lifecycle.

Continuity Through Resolution

Resolution should remain connected to the active Liability requirement it resolved.

Therefore:

Resolved Liability ≠ Historically Disconnected Liability.

Continuity Through Satisfaction

Satisfaction records should remain associated with the exact Liability requirement they fulfill.

Continuity Through Settlement

Settlement should remain traceable to:

its affected Liability Relationship, Settlement Object, Subjects, Scope, terms, performance, and resulting Resolution effect.

Continuity Through Discharge

Discharge should remain connected to the Liability requirement that ceased requiring active performance.

Therefore:

Discharge ≠ Continuity Termination.

Continuity After Resolution

Resolution may end active performance without ending historical continuity.

Therefore:

Liability Resolution ≠ Liability Continuity Termination.

Continuity After Discharge

A discharged Liability requirement remains part of the governed lifecycle.

Therefore:

Liability Discharge ≠ Historical Disconnection.

Continuity Into Finality

Finality governance requires continuity sufficient to determine what exactly is proposed to reach Finality.

A Finality Object without lifecycle continuity may be incorrectly bounded.

Finality Object Association

LCOIM should support later association between the Continuity Object and the applicable Finality Object.

Continuity Beyond Finality

When Liability reaches Finality, historical continuity should remain.

Therefore:

Liability Finality ≠ Continuity Erasure.

Representation Continuity

A Liability structure may appear in different forms across its lifecycle.

Representations may include:

structured records, documents, system objects, case records, transaction records, audit records, Settlement records, Discharge records, or archival representations.

Representation Change

A representation may change without changing the governed Liability identity.

The canonical distinction is:

Representation Change ≠ Liability Identity Change by default.

Multiple Representations

One Liability structure may possess multiple representations simultaneously.

Therefore:

Multiple Representations ≠ Multiple Liability Structures by default.

Duplicate Records

Multiple records may represent the same Liability Relationship.

Therefore:

Multiple Liability Records ≠ Multiple Liability Relationships by default.

Fragmented Records

A single Liability lifecycle may be fragmented across multiple systems or datasets.

Fragmentation should not automatically be interpreted as separate Liability lifecycles.

Record Association

LCOIM should establish sufficient association between fragmented records to support continuity without relying on unsupported assumptions.

Record Similarity

Similarity alone is insufficient to establish identity.

Therefore:

Record Similarity ≠ Liability Identity.

Record Difference

Differences between records do not necessarily establish separate Liability identities.

Therefore:

Record Difference ≠ Distinct Liability by default.

System Migration

A Liability structure may migrate between technical systems.

Therefore:

System Migration ≠ New Liability Origin.

System Identifier Change

Technical identifiers may change during migration or restructuring.

Therefore:

System Identifier Change ≠ Liability Identity Change by default.

Cross-System Continuity

LCOIM should preserve the ability to associate the same Liability structure across multiple systems where sufficient governance Basis exists.

Organizational Change

A Liability lifecycle may continue through organizational restructuring.

The continuity assessment should distinguish changes in organizational representation from changes in the underlying Liability structure.

Succession

Where a successor Subject assumes a relevant position, LCOIM should preserve the relationship between predecessor and successor states.

Therefore:

Succession ≠ Historical Subject Replacement.

Identity Representation Change

Changes in names, identifiers, legal forms, system accounts, or other Subject representations should not automatically break Liability continuity.

Authority Change

Changes in governance authority should remain historically represented without erasing earlier valid governance states.

Framework Change

Where an applicable external framework changes, continuity should preserve the Liability history before and after the change.

Temporal Continuity

Liability continuity should preserve the chronological relationship between material lifecycle states.

Effective Time

Each material continuity transition should identify its effective time where available.

Recording Time

The time at which a change is recorded may differ from the time it became effective.

Therefore:

Recording Time ≠ Effective Time by default.

Retrospective Identification

A lifecycle transition may be identified retrospectively.

Later identification should not create a false later Origin.

Continuity Gap

A Continuity Gap exists where a material portion of the lifecycle cannot be reliably connected to preceding or subsequent states.

Gap Transparency

Continuity gaps should remain explicit.

Therefore:

Historical Gap ≠ Continuity by Assumption.

Unknown Continuity

Where identity continuity cannot be established, the state should remain Unknown rather than being forced.

Therefore:

Unknown Continuity ≠ Broken Continuity by assumption.

Uncertain Continuity

Where multiple materially plausible continuity structures exist, uncertainty should remain explicit.

Therefore:

Uncertain Continuity ≠ Authority to Select a Preferred Continuity Structure.

Disputed Continuity

Subjects or systems may dispute whether records belong to the same Liability lifecycle.

The dispute should remain represented until legitimately resolved.

Conflicting Continuity Evidence

Conflicting identity or continuity information should not be silently reconciled.

Therefore:

Conflicting Continuity Evidence ≠ Established Liability Identity.

Continuity Boundary

The Continuity Boundary defines what belongs to the same continuing Liability structure and what remains outside it.

Boundary Overreach

LCOIM should prevent unrelated Liability from being absorbed into an existing continuity structure merely because of shared Subjects, events, or records.

Boundary Fragmentation

LCOIM should also prevent one continuing Liability structure from being unnecessarily divided into artificial separate lifecycles.

Same Origin vs Same Lifecycle

Two Liability Relationships may share an Origin without being the same lifecycle object.

Therefore:

Shared Liability Origin ≠ Same Liability Relationship.

Same Subject vs Same Liability

A Subject may participate in multiple independent Liability structures.

Therefore:

Same Subject ≠ Same Liability.

Same Liability Object vs Same Relationship

Multiple Liability Relationships may concern the same Liability Object.

Therefore:

Same Liability Object ≠ Same Liability Relationship.

Same Payment vs Same Liability

One payment may affect multiple requirements or Relationships.

Therefore:

Same Payment ≠ Same Liability Relationship.

Same Settlement vs Same Liability Relationship

One Settlement may govern multiple Liability Relationships.

Therefore:

Shared Settlement ≠ Relationship Identity.

Continuity Dependency

LCOIM should identify dependencies necessary to preserve continuity between lifecycle states.

Dependency vs Identity

A dependency between two Liability structures does not make them the same structure.

Therefore:

Liability Dependency ≠ Liability Identity.

Continuity Association

An association should identify how two lifecycle representations are connected without necessarily declaring them identical.

Association vs Identity

Therefore:

Liability Association ≠ Liability Identity.

Continuity Confidence

Where implementation requires confidence representation, confidence should reflect the strength of the continuity association without replacing explicit uncertainty.

Continuity Correction

An incorrect continuity association may be corrected.

The prior association should remain historically reconstructable where materially relevant.

Therefore:

Continuity Correction ≠ Historical Erasure.

Continuity Reassessment

A previously valid continuity association may require reassessment when materially relevant information changes.

Therefore:

Continuity Reassessment ≠ Continuity Correction.

Continuity Supersession

A later continuity representation may supersede an earlier representation.

Therefore:

Continuity Supersession ≠ Historical Deletion.

Re-Entry Continuity

Where Liability re-enters active governance, LCOIM should preserve association with the earlier Resolution, Discharge, or Finality state.

Re-Entry vs New Origin

Therefore:

Re-entry ≠ New Origin by default.

Reopening Continuity

A reopened Liability should retain its historical identity where the same lifecycle continues.

Therefore:

Liability Reopening ≠ New Liability by default.

Re-Entry History

The continuity structure should preserve:

pre-Re-entry state, Re-entry Trigger, Re-entry Basis, Re-entry destination, and subsequent lifecycle state.

Re-Entry Must Not Rewrite History

The foundational historical rule applies:

Re-entry must not rewrite history.

Continuity and Finality Reassessment

If Finality is later reassessed, LCOIM should preserve continuity between the original Finality assessment and the later governance state.

Multiple Finality Cycles

One Liability Origin may contain more than one Finality assessment over time where legitimate Re-entry occurs.

These assessments remain part of one continuing history unless a genuinely new Liability Origin is established.

Continuity Provenance

LCOIM should preserve provenance sufficient to explain why lifecycle representations are associated.

This may include:

Origin references, Relationship identifiers, Subject associations, state-transition records, Transfer records, Resolution records, Discharge records, Re-entry records, and other relevant governance links.

Continuity Rationale

Where identity or association is not self-evident, the continuity structure should include a rationale explaining why records or states are treated as belonging to the same lifecycle.

Continuity State

LCOIM may represent Continuity States such as:

Established, Partially Established, Pending, Unknown, Uncertain, Disputed, Fragmented, Reconstructed, Corrected, Superseded, or another explicitly governed state.

These are methodological states, not universal legal classifications.

Automated Continuity Identification

AI or automated systems may assist with:

record linkage, identity matching, relationship mapping, duplicate detection, timeline reconstruction, system migration mapping, Re-entry association, and network identification.

Automation should not silently collapse uncertain Liability structures.

Therefore:

Automated Record Matching ≠ Governed Liability Identity.

Automated Duplicate Detection

Automated systems may identify potential duplicate records.

Potential duplication should be verified before records are treated as the same Liability structure.

Therefore:

Potential Duplicate ≠ Established Duplicate.

Automated Fragment Reconstruction

Automated systems may propose relationships between fragmented records.

The proposed structure should preserve uncertainty and provenance.

Continuity Failure

Continuity failure exists where the relevant Liability lifecycle cannot be sufficiently connected to support reliable downstream historical or Finality governance.

The failure should remain explicit rather than being hidden through assumed linkage.

Downstream Effect of Continuity Failure

Material continuity failure may prevent reliable:

historical reconstruction, Finality assessment, Re-entry routing, or Finality recording.

Upstream Re-Entry

Where continuity analysis reveals a material defect in an earlier LIGA™ governance space, governance should return to the earliest materially affected space.

The canonical rule is:

Re-enter the earliest materially affected governance space.

Targeted Reassessment

The canonical principle is:

Reassess what materially changed. Preserve what remains valid.

Governed Liability Continuity Identification Structure

The principal output of LCOIM is:

Governed Liability Continuity Identification Structure

It should identify, where applicable:

Continuity Object, Liability Origin, Liability identity, Liability Relationship identity, Liability Network association, primary and secondary Relationships, Subject continuity, Scope continuity, Allocation continuity, State continuity, Transfer continuity, Recourse continuity, Resolution continuity, Discharge continuity, Finality association, Re-entry association, record associations, cross-system associations, effective times, continuity boundaries, continuity dependencies, continuity gaps, uncertainty, disputes, conflicts, corrections, reassessments, provenance, and continuity rationale.

Meaning of the Output

A Governed Liability Continuity Identification Structure means that the Liability structures requiring continuous historical treatment have been sufficiently identified and bounded for subsequent LCFS governance.

It does not establish complete historical reconstruction.

It does not establish Finality.

It does not determine whether Re-entry is required.

Those functions belong to subsequent LCFS governance.

Minimum Implementation Framework

A conforming implementation of LCOIM must provide exactly the following eight capabilities:

Continuity Object, Origin & Liability Identity Identification

Capability to identify the Continuity Object, relevant Liability Origin, continuing Liability identity, and the distinction between current representation and underlying lifecycle identity.

Subject, Relationship & Liability Network Continuity Governance

Capability to preserve Subject associations, independent Liability Relationship identities, primary and secondary Relationships, Recourse structures, and Liability Network associations without collapsing distinct Relationships.

Scope, Allocation, State, Transfer & Recourse Continuity Governance

Capability to associate material changes in Scope, Allocation, State, Transfer, Sharing, succession, and Recourse with the continuing Liability structure while preserving earlier states.

Resolution, Satisfaction, Settlement, Discharge & Finality Continuity Governance

Capability to preserve continuity through Resolution-related lifecycle stages and maintain association between active Liability, Resolution, Discharge, and later Finality governance.

Record, Representation, System & Temporal Continuity Governance

Capability to associate multiple records, representations, systems, migrations, identifiers, effective times, and fragmented lifecycle data without assuming identity from similarity alone.

Continuity Boundary, Gap, Uncertainty, Conflict & Duplicate Governance

Capability to distinguish same from separate Liability structures, identify continuity boundaries and gaps, preserve Unknown, Uncertain, and Disputed states, and govern potential duplicate or conflicting records.

Provenance, Correction, Reassessment & Re-Entry Continuity Governance

Capability to preserve continuity rationale and provenance, govern corrections and reassessments, associate Re-entry and Reopening with prior lifecycle states, and prevent historical rewriting.

LCFS Historical Record Handoff Governance

Capability to produce a Governed Liability Continuity Identification Structure sufficiently explicit, bounded, traceable, and historically associated for Liability Historical Record governance.

Cross-Architecture Boundary

LCOIM may use identity, operational, evidence, provenance, and lifecycle inputs from adjacent OOF® architectures.

OBIDENITY® may provide Origin, identity, ownership, authority, provenance, succession, and continuity inputs.

ORA™ may establish operational events and state transitions.

AGA™ may provide Responsibility, Accountability, delegation, and authority structures.

ADIT® may provide evidence continuity and auditability.

VALIDOS™ may validate continuity-relevant inputs.

HAGA™ may preserve Harm lifecycle identity independently from Liability identity.

VFM® may provide Value Flow continuity relevant to payment, Transfer, or Recourse.

AIG® and ASGA™ may provide AI or autonomous-system identity and operational events relevant to Liability continuity.

These inputs may support Liability continuity identification.

They do not define Liability identity merely because an external identity or event remains continuous.

The canonical interoperability principle is:

External systems may establish identity, events, evidence, provenance, or continuity of their own governed objects; LCOIM determines how those inputs relate to the continuing Liability governance structure.

Canonical Boundary

LCOIM begins when a Liability structure must be identified across multiple lifecycle states, records, representations, Relationships, systems, or terminal governance conditions.

LCOIM governs:

Continuity Objects, Liability Origin associations, Liability identity, Subject continuity, Relationship continuity, Liability Network continuity, Scope continuity, Allocation continuity, State continuity, Transfer continuity, Recourse continuity, Resolution continuity, Discharge continuity, Finality associations, Re-entry associations, record and representation associations, cross-system continuity, temporal continuity, continuity boundaries, gaps, uncertainty, conflicts, duplicates, provenance, correction, reassessment, and continuity rationale.

LCOIM does not independently establish:

the complete Liability Historical Record, Liability Finality, or the requirement for Re-entry.

LCOIM ends when the applicable continuity structure is sufficiently represented as a:

Governed Liability Continuity Identification Structure

for historical-record governance.

Use Case 1 — Liability Continues Across System Migration

An organization migrates an active Liability matter from one case-management system to another.

The technical identifier changes, document locations change, and the Liability is represented in a new data structure.

LCOIM associates the old and new representations with the same Liability Origin and Liability Relationship while preserving the migration event and identifier change.

The migration therefore does not create a false new Liability lifecycle.

System Migration ≠ New Liability Origin.

Use Case 2 — Primary Liability Resolves While Recourse

Continues

A primary Liability Relationship reaches Resolution and Discharge.

A related Recourse Relationship remains active between different Subjects.

LCOIM preserves both Relationships as distinct lifecycle objects while maintaining their network association and shared historical connection.

The primary Liability may later proceed toward Relationship Finality without losing continuity with the active Recourse structure.

Primary Liability Relationship ≠ Recourse Liability Relationship.

Canonical Closing Statement

Liability continuity is not the preservation of a label.

It is the preservation of identity across change.

Subjects may change.

States may change.

Scope and Allocation may change.

Liability may be transferred, shared, resolved, discharged, reopened, and eventually reach Finality.

Systems and records may also change.

None of these changes should silently create a false new Liability Origin or erase the path that connects the current state to what came before it.

Representation Change ≠ Liability Identity Change by default.

Preserve the identity of the Liability lifecycle before reconstructing its history or deciding whether it may legitimately reach Finality.
