Canonical Definition

Autonomous Identity Lifecycle Module (AILM) defines the structural
conditions under which autonomous operational identity is created,
maintained, modified, suspended, transferred, retired, and archived
while preserving governance integrity throughout the complete
autonomous operational lifecycle.

AILM governs the autonomous identity lifecycle.

The module establishes the governance conditions required to ensure
that autonomous operational identity remains governed from its
creation to its retirement.

Identity has a beginning.

Identity has an operational life.

Identity has an end.

AILM governs that lifecycle.

Module Operational Role

AILM defines the identity lifecycle layer of AIS.

Its role is to govern every lifecycle stage of autonomous
operational identity.

Module Operational Space

AILM governs:

  • autonomous identity creation,
  • identity activation,
  • identity modification,
  • identity suspension,
  • identity transfer,
  • identity retirement,
  • identity archival,
  • lifecycle governance.


Module Function

The module applies wherever organizations must govern autonomous
operational identity across its complete lifecycle while preserving
governance integrity, operational continuity, and accountability.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Identity Lifecycle Object

Identify which autonomous operational identities require lifecycle
governance.

2. Define Lifecycle Conditions Establish governance conditions for
identity creation, activation, modification, suspension, transfer,
retirement, and archival.

3. Define Lifecycle Failure Logic

Identify conditions where lifecycle governance becomes incomplete,
inconsistent, unauthorized, or no longer governable.

4. Define Governance Response

Define governance actions for lifecycle approval, suspension,
retirement, restoration, or administrative review.

5. Preserve Lifecycle Traceability

Preserve lifecycle events, governance approvals, operational
records, and supporting evidence throughout the complete identity
lifecycle.

Use Case 1 — Autonomous Inspection Drone

Scenario

A fleet of autonomous inspection drones is deployed, upgraded, and
eventually retired after years of operation.

Application

AILM governs the complete lifecycle of each drone's operational
identity from initial activation through retirement.

Result

The organization preserves continuous governance, operational
history, and lifecycle accountability.

Use Case 2 — Enterprise AI Agent Platform

Scenario

An enterprise continuously creates, updates, suspends, and retires
autonomous AI agents performing operational tasks.

Application

AILM governs every lifecycle transition while preserving identity
continuity and governance integrity.

Result

The organization maintains controlled autonomous identity management
throughout the entire operational lifecycle.

Canonical Closing Statement

Governable autonomy requires governable identity from beginning to
end. Autonomous Identity Lifecycle ensures that every operational
identity remains governed throughout its complete existence,
preserving trust, accountability, and operational continuity.
