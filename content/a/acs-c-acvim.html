Canonical Definition

Accountability Chain Validity Integrity Module (ACVIM) defines the
structural conditions under which accountability- chain
relationships, accountability ownership structures, accountability
transitions, accountability delegations, accountability transfers,
accountability validations, accountability outcomes, and
accountability-governance activities remain legitimate, recognized,
governable, reconstructable, reviewable, enforceable, and
operationally valid throughout the accountability-chain lifecycle.

ACVIM governs accountability-chain validity.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine whether accountability-chain relationships remain
legitimate across governance layers, whether accountability
transfers remain valid, and whether accountability-chain structures
remain suitable for governance, investigation, consequence
attribution, and accountability enforcement.

Module Operational Role

ACVIM defines the chain-validity governance layer of ACS-C.

Its role is to ensure that accountability chains remain legitimate
and governance-valid throughout the accountability-chain lifecycle.

Module Operational Space

ACVIM governs:

  • accountability-chain validity
  • accountability-chain legitimacy
  • accountability-transfer validity
  • chain recognition
  • governance-valid accountability chains
  • accountability enforceability
  • chain lifecycle governance
  • multi-layer accountability validity


The module applies wherever governance requires confirmation that
accountability chains remain valid.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • legitimate accountability-chain relationships
  • recognized accountability structures
  • enforceable accountability obligations
  • reconstructable accountability history
  • governance-valid accountability-chain states
  • operationally reliable accountability governance


Its function is to ensure that governance can determine whether
accountability chains remain valid despite organizational,
contractual, technological, regulatory, or governance changes.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Chain Validity Object

The organization must define which accountability-chain environments
require validity governance.

This may include:

  • contractor networks
  • subcontractor structures
  • corporate groups
  • holding-company ecosystems
  • regulatory environments
  • AI governance systems
  • Human-AI operational environments
  • multi-layer governance ecosystems


2. Define Chain Validity Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which
accountability-chain validity remains valid.

This includes:

  • legitimacy requirements
  • recognition requirements
  • enforceability requirements
  • accountability requirements
  • governance requirements
  • chain-validity conditions


3. Define Validity Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how accountability-chain validity
failures are identified.

This may include:

  • invalid accountability transfers
  • illegitimate chain relationships
  • unenforceable accountability structures
  • governance-invalid chain states
  • accountability-chain corruption
  • accountability legitimacy failures


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for chain-validity failures.

Governance response may include:

  • chain review
  • legitimacy verification
  • governance intervention
  • chain reassessment
  • corrective actions
  • chain validation
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • accountability relationships
  • chain validations
  • governance reviews
  • validation activities
  • intervention procedures
  • resulting chain states


An accountability-chain environment must not remain validity-valid
if materially significant chain relationships cannot be
reconstructed, reviewed, validated, recognized, enforced,
or governed.

Scenario

A contractor network consisting of multiple companies participates
in the delivery of a regulated operational activity.

Application

ACVIM evaluates whether accountability-chain relationships remain
legitimate, enforceable, and governance-valid across the
entire contractor structure.

Result

Governance gains visibility into the validity
of accountability-chain governance.

Canonical Closing Statement

Accountability Chain Validity Integrity Module (ACVIM) defines the
structural conditions under which accountability- chain
relationships, accountability ownership structures, accountability
transitions, accountability delegations, accountability transfers,
accountability validations, accountability outcomes,
and accountability-governance activities

remain legitimate, recognized, governable, reconstructable,
reviewable, enforceable, and operationally valid throughout
the accountability-chain lifecycle.

An accountability chain may exist, but a chain that lacks legitimacy
or enforceability cannot be trusted. Accountability Chain Validity
Integrity therefore becomes a foundational condition of trustworthy
accountability- chain governance.
