Canonical Definition

Authority Accountability Integrity Module (AAIM) defines the
structural conditions under which authority remains attributable,
accountable, traceable, evidence-supported, governable,
reconstructable, and operationally valid throughout its lifecycle.

AAIM governs authority accountability.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine who exercised authority, who approved authority, who
delegated authority, who supervised authority, and who remains
accountable for decisions, actions, consequences, and outcomes
arising from authority.

Module Operational Role

AAIM defines the authority-accountability governance layer of AGS.

Its role is to ensure that authority remains connected to
accountable actors throughout governance analysis.

Module Operational Space

AAIM governs:

  • authority accountability
  • authority ownership
  • authority attribution
  • authority consequence accountability
  • authority decision accountability
  • authority supervision accountability
  • authority traceability
  • accountable authority governance


The module applies wherever governance must determine accountability
connected to authority.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • accountable authority structures
  • attributable authority decisions
  • reconstructable authority history
  • traceable authority usage
  • evidence-supported authority governance
  • governance-valid consequence attribution


Its function is to ensure that authority remains connected to
accountable governance actors.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Authority Accountability Object

The organization must define which authority environments
require accountability governance.

This may include:

  • management authority
  • supervisory authority
  • delegated authority
  • regulatory authority
  • contractor authority
  • Human-AI governance authority
  • AI orchestration authority
  • autonomous operational authority


2. Define Authority Accountability Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which authority
accountability remains valid.

This includes:

  • attribution requirements
  • accountability requirements
  • traceability requirements
  • evidence requirements
  • governance requirements
  • accountability-valid authority conditions


3. Define Accountability Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how authority-accountability failures
are identified.

This may include:

  • anonymous authority decisions
  • undocumented authority actions
  • accountability gaps
  • hidden authority structures
  • unverifiable authority usage
  • governance-blind authority exercise


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for
authority-accountability failures.

Governance response may include:

  • accountability review
  • attribution verification
  • governance intervention
  • escalation
  • corrective actions
  • authority restrictions
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • authority decisions
  • authority approvals
  • authority delegations
  • governance reviews
  • intervention procedures
  • resulting authority outcomes


An authority-governance environment must not remain
accountability-valid if materially significant authority activities
cannot be attributed, reconstructed, reviewed, validated,
or governed.

Use Case 1 — Executive Decision Environment

Scenario

An executive authorizes a major operational decision affecting
organizational performance, compliance, and resource allocation.

Application

AAIM determines who exercised authority, who approved the decision,
and who remains accountable for resulting consequences.

Result

Governance gains visibility into accountability connected
to authority-driven decisions.

Use Case 2 — Multi-Agent Governance

Environment

Scenario

A supervisory AI system authorizes actions executed by multiple
operational agents across a distributed environment.

Application

AAIM reconstructs authority accountability throughout the decision
and execution chain.

Result

Governance gains visibility into accountable authority across
intelligent operational systems.

Canonical Closing Statement

Authority Accountability Integrity Module (AAIM) defines the
structural conditions under which authority remains attributable,
accountable, traceable, evidence-supported, governable,
reconstructable, and operationally valid throughout its lifecycle.

Authority grants the right to act, but authority without
accountability creates governance risk. Authority accountability
integrity therefore becomes a foundational condition of
trustworthy authority governance.
