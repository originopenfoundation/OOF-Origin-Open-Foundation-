ArtData Registry™

Governance Architecture Space

ArtData Registry™ governs the architectural space responsible for
registering, classifying, indexing, organizing, preserving,
managing, and governing qualified ArtData® assets throughout
their lifecycle.

The space exists because qualified evidence loses value when it
cannot be located, understood, managed, categorized, compared,
or reused.

ArtData Registry™ exists to transform qualified evidence assets into
structured governance knowledge.

Canonical Definition

ArtData Registry™ is the governable architectural space responsible
for registering, classifying, indexing, organizing, preserving,
managing, governing, and maintaining qualified ArtData® assets
throughout their lifecycle.

The Core Problem

Organizations frequently accumulate large amounts of evidence
without maintaining a structured governance mechanism capable of
managing that evidence.

As a result:

  • evidence becomes difficult to locate,
  • knowledge becomes fragmented,
  • governance learning becomes limited,
  • operational experience becomes lost,
  • evidence value decreases over time.


ArtData Registry™ was created to address this challenge.

Why ArtData Registry™ Exists

ArtData Qualification™ determines whether evidence qualifies
as ArtData®.

However, qualified evidence still requires:

  • organization,
  • classification,
  • indexing,
  • stewardship,
  • governance.


Organizations therefore require a mechanism capable of answering a
critical question:

  • Where does qualified evidence belong?


ArtData Registry™ exists to answer this question.

The Registry Principle

A fundamental principle of ArtData Registry™ is:

Qualified evidence should remain discoverable, governable, and
reusable throughout its lifecycle.

The objective is not merely preserving evidence.

The objective is preserving usable governance knowledge.

Core Questions

ArtData Registry™ seeks to answer:

Where is the evidence located?

How is the evidence classified?

Which evidence belongs together?

Which evidence remains active?

Which evidence remains relevant?

Which evidence supports governance learning?

Which evidence supports future decision-making?

Can qualified evidence be efficiently discovered and reused?
