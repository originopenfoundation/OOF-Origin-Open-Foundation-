Canonical Definition

Accountability Attribution Integrity Module (AAIM) defines the
structural conditions under which accountability remains
attributable to identifiable, legitimate, traceable, governable,
reconstructable, enforceable, reviewable, and operationally valid
accountability holders throughout the accountability lifecycle.

AAIM governs accountability attribution.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine who ultimately remains answerable for outcomes, decisions,
actions, omissions, governance failures, operational results,
and consequence-bearing events.

Module Operational Role

AAIM defines the accountability-attribution governance layer
of AGS-A.

Its role is to ensure that accountability remains connected to
identifiable accountability holders.

Module Operational Space

AAIM governs:

  • accountability attribution
  • accountability ownership
  • answerability attribution
  • outcome ownership
  • governance ownership
  • accountability-holder identification
  • accountability allocation
  • consequence attribution preparation


The module applies wherever governance must determine who ultimately
answers for outcomes.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • identifiable accountability holders
  • attributable accountability relationships
  • reconstructable answerability chains
  • governance-valid accountability ownership
  • enforceable accountability attribution
  • operational accountability clarity


Its function is to ensure that governance can determine who remains
accountable when outcomes, failures, incidents, disputes, governance
reviews, or consequence-bearing events occur.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Accountability Attribution Object

The organization must define which accountability environments
require attribution governance.

This may include:

  • organizational structures
  • governance systems
  • contractor environments
  • certification systems
  • regulatory systems
  • AI governance systems
  • Human-AI operational environments
  • accountability frameworks


2. Define Accountability Attribution Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which accountability
attribution remains valid.

This includes:

  • attribution requirements
  • ownership requirements
  • answerability requirements
  • accountability-holder requirements
  • governance requirements
  • attribution-valid accountability conditions


3. Define Attribution Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how accountability-attribution failures
are identified.

This may include:

  • unidentified accountability holders
  • accountability ambiguity
  • accountability fragmentation
  • ownership conflicts
  • governance-blind attribution
  • unverifiable answerability structures


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic
for accountability-attribution failures.

Governance response may include:

  • attribution review
  • accountability verification
  • governance intervention
  • accountability reassignment
  • corrective actions
  • attribution validation
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • accountability holders
  • attribution decisions
  • governance reviews
  • validation activities
  • intervention procedures
  • resulting accountability states


An accountability-governance environment must not remain
attribution-valid if materially significant accountability
relationships cannot be attributed, reconstructed, reviewed,
validated, enforced, or governed.

Use Case 1 — Governance Failure Investigation

Scenario

A major operational failure occurs involving multiple organizations,
managers, contractors, supervisors, and governance actors.

Application

AAIM reconstructs governance relationships and determines who
ultimately remains accountable for the outcome.

Result

Governance gains visibility into final accountability ownership.

Use Case 2 — Human-AI Accountability

Environment

Scenario

An AI-enabled operational environment produces significant outcomes
requiring accountability assessment.

Application

AAIM reconstructs governance structures and identifies the
accountable human, organizational, or governance entities
responsible for answerability.

Result

Governance gains visibility into accountability ownership across
intelligent operational ecosystems.

Canonical Closing Statement

Accountability Attribution Integrity Module (AAIM) defines the
structural conditions under which accountability remains
attributable to identifiable, legitimate, traceable, governable,
reconstructable, enforceable, reviewable, and operationally valid
accountability holders throughout the accountability lifecycle.

Governance may identify authority, responsibility, control,
oversight, and evidence, but accountability attribution determines
who ultimately answers for outcomes. Accountability attribution
integrity therefore becomes a foundational condition of
trustworthy accountability governance.
