Canonical Definition

Authority Delegation Scope Integrity Module (ADSIM) defines the
structural conditions under which the extent, limits, permissions,
restrictions, applicability, reach, and operational boundaries of
delegated authority remain identifiable, traceable, governable,
accountable, and operationally valid throughout the
delegation lifecycle.

ADSIM governs delegation scope.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine exactly what authority was delegated, what authority was
not delegated, where delegated authority applies, and where
delegated authority ends.

Module Operational Role

ADSIM defines the delegation-scope governance layer of ADS.

Its role is to preserve visibility of delegated-authority limits and
ensure that delegated authority remains exercised only within
its legitimate scope.

Module Operational Space

ADSIM governs:

  • delegated authority scope
  • delegation boundaries
  • delegated permissions
  • delegated restrictions
  • delegation reach
  • delegation applicability
  • delegation limitations
  • scope accountability


The module applies wherever governance requires clear limits
on delegated authority.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • visible delegation boundaries
  • accountable delegated authority
  • reconstructable delegation scope
  • traceable authority permissions
  • governance-valid delegation limits
  • operational authority clarity


Its function is to ensure that governance can determine what
authority was delegated and what authority remained with the
original authority holder.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Delegation Scope Object

The organization must define which delegated-authority environments
require scope governance.

This may include:

  • management delegation
  • supervisory delegation
  • contractor governance
  • regulatory delegation
  • project governance structures
  • AI orchestration systems
  • autonomous operational systems
  • Human-AI governance environments


2. Define Delegation Scope Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which delegation
scope remains valid.

This includes:

  • permission requirements
  • restriction requirements
  • boundary requirements
  • governance requirements
  • accountability requirements
  • governance-valid delegation conditions


3. Define Scope Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how delegation-scope failures are identified.

This may include:

  • delegated-authority overreach
  • unauthorized actions
  • scope violations
  • undocumented authority expansion
  • governance-blind delegation
  • delegation-boundary ambiguity


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for
delegation-scope failures.

Governance response may include:

  • scope review
  • delegation verification
  • governance intervention
  • escalation
  • corrective actions
  • authority restriction
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • delegated permissions
  • delegated restrictions
  • delegation boundaries
  • governance reviews
  • intervention procedures
  • resulting delegation structures


A delegation-governance environment must not remain scope-valid if
materially significant delegation boundaries cannot be
reconstructed, reviewed, validated, or governed.

Use Case 1 — Organizational Delegation

Environment

Scenario

A director delegates authority to a department manager to approve
operational expenditures up to a defined limit.

Application

ADSIM defines what authority was delegated and what authority
remained outside the delegation scope.

Result

Governance gains visibility into the exact boundaries of
delegated authority.

Use Case 2 — Orchestrated Agent Environment

Scenario

An orchestration platform delegates authority to AI agents for task
execution while retaining authority over governance rules and
policy modification.

Application

ADSIM defines the operational limits of delegated agent authority.

Result

Governance gains visibility into the scope and boundaries of
delegated authority across intelligent operational systems.

Canonical Closing Statement
