Canonical Definition

Accountability Chain Origin Integrity Module (ACOIM) defines the
structural conditions under which accountability- chain
relationships remain attributable to identifiable, legitimate,
traceable, governable, reconstructable, reviewable, accountable, and
operationally valid accountability origins throughout
the accountability-chain lifecycle.

ACOIM governs accountability-chain origin.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine where an accountability chain begins, which accountability
relationship serves as the chain origin, and whether the
accountability-chain origin remains legitimate and governable.

Module Operational Role

ACOIM defines the chain-origin governance layer of ACS-C.

Its role is to preserve visibility into the starting point
of accountability chains.

Module Operational Space

ACOIM governs:

  • accountability-chain origin
  • accountability-source attribution
  • accountability-chain provenance
  • chain-origin governance
  • accountability-root relationships
  • accountability-source legitimacy
  • chain-origin traceability
  • accountability-chain ownership origin


The module applies wherever governance requires reconstruction
of accountability-chain origins.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • identifiable accountability origins
  • traceable chain sources
  • reconstructable accountability roots
  • accountable chain relationships
  • governance-valid chain attribution
  • operationally valid accountability provenance


Its function is to ensure that governance can determine where an
accountability chain begins and which accountability relationship
serves as the chain origin.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Chain Origin Object

The organization must define which accountability-chain environments
require origin governance.

This may include:

  • contractor networks
  • subcontractor chains
  • corporate groups
  • holding structures
  • regulatory environments
  • AI governance systems
  • Human-AI operational environments
  • multi-layer governance ecosystems


2. Define Chain Origin Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which chain
origin remains valid.

This includes:

  • source requirements
  • attribution requirements
  • legitimacy requirements
  • reconstruction requirements
  • governance requirements
  • chain-origin conditions


3. Define Origin Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how chain-origin failures are identified.

This may include:

  • unidentified accountability origins
  • undocumented chain creation
  • illegitimate accountability sources
  • governance-blind chain formation
  • chain-origin ambiguity
  • unverifiable accountability roots


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for chain-origin failures.

Governance response may include:

  • chain review
  • source verification
  • governance intervention
  • chain reconstruction
  • corrective actions
  • chain reassessment
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • accountability origins
  • chain relationships
  • governance reviews
  • validation activities
  • intervention procedures
  • resulting chain states


An accountability-chain environment must not remain origin-valid if
materially significant chain relationships cannot be attributed,
reconstructed, reviewed, validated, or governed.

Use Case 1 — Multi-Layer Contractor Chain

Scenario

An operational incident occurs within a contractor-subcontractor
structure involving multiple organizations.

Application

ACOIM reconstructs the original accountability relationship from
which the accountability chain emerged.

Result

Governance gains visibility into the origin of the
accountability chain.

Use Case 2 — Human-AI Accountability Chain

Scenario

An outcome emerges through interactions between humans, AI agents,
orchestration systems, and governance actors.

Application

ACOIM identifies the original accountability relationship and
reconstructs the root of the accountability chain.

Result

Governance gains visibility into accountability-chain origins across
intelligent operational ecosystems.

Canonical Closing Statement

Accountability Chain Origin Integrity Module (ACOIM) defines the
structural conditions under which accountability- chain
relationships remain attributable to identifiable, legitimate,
traceable, governable, reconstructable, reviewable, accountable, and
operationally valid accountability origins throughout
the accountability-chain lifecycle.

An accountability chain cannot be reconstructed if its origin cannot
be identified. Accountability Chain Origin Integrity therefore
becomes a foundational condition of trustworthy
accountability-chain governance.
