Canonical Definition

Accountability Scope Integrity Module (ASIM) defines the structural
conditions under which accountability boundaries, accountability
domains, answerability obligations, governed outcomes, governed
decisions, governed actions, governed omissions, and accountability
responsibilities remain identifiable, traceable, governable,
reconstructable, enforceable, and operationally valid throughout
the accountability lifecycle.

ASIM governs accountability scope.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine exactly what a subject is accountable for, what remains
outside accountability boundaries, where accountability begins, and
where accountability ends.

Module Operational Role

ASIM defines the accountability-scope governance layer of AGS-A.

Its role is to preserve visibility of accountability boundaries
and accountability coverage.

Module Operational Space

ASIM governs:

  • accountability scope
  • accountability boundaries
  • accountability domains
  • answerability obligations
  • governed outcomes
  • governed decisions
  • governed actions
  • accountability coverage


The module applies wherever governance requires clarification
of accountability boundaries.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • clearly defined accountability boundaries
  • visible accountability coverage
  • reconstructable accountability domains
  • accountable governance assignments
  • governance-valid accountability limits
  • operational accountability clarity


Its function is to ensure that governance can determine exactly what
a subject is accountable for and what remains
outside accountability.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Accountability Scope Object

The organization must define which accountability environments
require scope governance.

This may include:

  • organizational structures
  • governance systems
  • contractor networks
  • certification environments
  • regulatory systems
  • AI governance systems
  • Human-AI operational systems
  • accountability frameworks


2. Define Accountability Scope Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which accountability
scope remains valid.

This includes:

  • accountability-boundary requirements
  • coverage requirements
  • answerability requirements
  • governance requirements
  • attribution requirements
  • scope-valid accountability conditions


3. Define Scope Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how accountability-scope failures
are identified.

This may include:

  • undefined accountability boundaries
  • accountability gaps
  • overlapping accountability structures
  • accountability ambiguity
  • governance-blind accountability assignments
  • unclear answerability domains


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for
accountability-scope failures.

Governance response may include:

  • accountability review
  • boundary verification
  • governance intervention
  • accountability clarification
  • corrective actions
  • accountability reassessment
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • accountability assignments
  • accountability boundaries
  • governance reviews
  • validation activities
  • intervention procedures
  • resulting accountability states


An accountability-governance environment must not remain scope-valid
if materially significant accountability boundaries cannot be
reconstructed, reviewed, validated, or governed.

and accountability limitations.

Result
