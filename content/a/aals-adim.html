Canonical Definition

Authority Delegation Integrity Module (ADIM) defines the structural
conditions under which authority, execution rights, governance
permissions, operational control capability, intervention
legitimacy, escalation rights, and decision responsibility may be
delegated, inherited, transferred, synchronized, restricted,
revoked, or expired without breaking accountability continuity or
operational traceability.

ADIM establishes the authority-delegation governance layer of AALS.

The module recognizes that future operational systems increasingly
depend on delegated authority structures across:
  • humans
  • AI systems
  • orchestration layers
  • distributed infrastructures
  • runtime environments
  • organizational governance systems
  • autonomous execution architectures


Where authority becomes distributed across execution environments,
delegation integrity must remain structurally governable.

Module Function

ADIM governs environments where authority is:
  • delegated
  • inherited
  • synchronized
  • escalated
  • transferred
  • restricted
  • revoked
  • temporarily assigned
  • runtime-expanded
  • operationally distributed


The module applies to:
  • enterprise governance systems
  • AI orchestration environments
  • delegated execution infrastructures
  • robotic operational systems
  • governmental operational hierarchies
  • runtime execution systems
  • multi-agent architectures
  • platform governance systems
  • autonomous execution environments
  • distributed operational infrastructures


Its function is not to prohibit delegation.

Its function is to preserve accountable authority continuity during
delegation processes.

Minimum Implementation Framework

Step 1 — Define the Delegation Object

The organization must define what authority condition is
being delegated.

Minimum requirement:
  • the delegation object is explicit
  • delegated authority scope is identifiable
  • operational boundaries are structurally defined
  • undefined authority states are excluded from valid delegation interpretation


The delegation object may include:
  • execution permissions
  • intervention rights
  • operational control authority
  • escalation capability
  • runtime governance permissions
  • override legitimacy
  • emergency operational authority
  • AI execution delegation
  • orchestration permissions
  • supervisory governance rights


Step 2 — Define Delegation Integrity Conditions

The system must define what conditions preserve valid
authority delegation.

Minimum requirement:
  • delegation integrity conditions are explicit
  • authority transfer is structurally reviewable
  • accountability continuity remains preservable


Delegation integrity conditions may include:
  • identity-linked delegation
  • explicit permission scope
  • bounded authority duration
  • escalation traceability
  • revocation capability
  • intervention continuity
  • operational responsibility persistence
  • delegation visibility
  • authority-boundary preservation
  • execution legitimacy continuity


Under ADIM:
  • Delegated authority remains governance-valid only while accountability continuity remains operationally preservable.


Step 3 — Define Delegation Interpretation Logic

The system must define how delegated authority is interpreted
according to governance continuity conditions.

Minimum requirement:
  • interpretation logic is explicit
  • delegation chains remain reconstructable
  • invalid authority expansion remains structurally visible


Interpretation logic may examine:
  • undeclared authority inheritance
  • unauthorized permission expansion
  • hidden delegation layers
  • runtime authority escalation
  • fragmented accountability pathways
  • revocation impossibility
  • symbolic supervision structures
  • operational-control dependency
  • orchestration abstraction
  • invisible authority synchronization


Under ADIM:

Authority may distribute operational execution. It may not
distribute accountability into operational invisibility.

Step 4 — Define Delegation Governance Logic

The system must define how delegated authority environments
remain governable.

Minimum requirement:
  • delegation remains reviewable
  • escalation legitimacy remains detectable
  • operational authority continuity remains active


Governance logic may include:
  • delegation-chain auditing
  • runtime authority tracing
  • permission-boundary validation
  • escalation governance
  • revocation verification
  • override legitimacy validation
  • orchestration accountability review
  • autonomous delegation limitation
  • escalation where delegation weakens accountability continuity


If delegated authority obscures accountable operational control, the
environment becomes governance-relevant.

Step 5 — Preserve Traceability and Restrict Invalid
Delegation Architecture

The system must preserve traceability of authority transfer,
delegation continuity, escalation structures, and operational
control capability.

Minimum requirement:
  • delegation pathways remain reconstructable
  • authority inheritance remains reviewable
  • operational accountability remains visible
  • invalid delegation architecture remains identifiable


A system becomes ADIM-invalid if:
  • authority expands without validated governance conditions
  • delegation obscures accountable responsibility
  • runtime permissions self-escalate without traceable legitimacy
  • intervention authority becomes operationally unclear
  • revocation capability becomes structurally impossible
  • symbolic supervision replaces executable governance capability
  • operational control becomes detached from accountable authority continuity


Use Case 1 — AI Runtime Permission Delegation

Scenario

A distributed AI orchestration system delegates execution
permissions across multiple autonomous runtime agents operating
under layered orchestration conditions.

Application

ADIM is used to examine:
  • delegation traceability
  • execution legitimacy
  • authority inheritance
  • runtime escalation visibility
  • and whether accountable operational control remains reconstructable


Result

The environment becomes governable according to authority continuity
conditions rather than symbolic orchestration structure alone.

Use Case 2 — Organizational Delegation Fragmentation

Scenario

An organization distributes operational authority across
contractors, agencies, subsidiaries, orchestration layers, and
supervisory entities while accountability becomes operationally
fragmented and difficult to reconstruct.

Application

ADIM is used to examine:
  • delegation-chain visibility
  • operational-control capability
  • escalation ownership
  • accountability continuity
  • and whether delegated authority remains structurally valid


Result

The system becomes reviewable according to accountable authority
continuity rather than fragmented administrative structure alone.

Canonical Closing Statement

ADIM defines the structural conditions under which delegated
authority, operational control, execution legitimacy, and escalation
capability remain continuously connected to accountable governance
continuity before distributed execution transforms authority into
operational power without reconstructable responsibility.
