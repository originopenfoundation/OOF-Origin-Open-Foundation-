Canonical Definition

Authority Validation Criteria Integrity Module (AVCIM) defines the
structural conditions under which the criteria, requirements,
standards, qualifications, rules, thresholds, permissions, and
governance conditions used to validate authority remain
identifiable, traceable, governable, accountable, consistent, and
operationally valid throughout the validation lifecycle.

AVCIM governs validation criteria.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine why authority was validated and whether the criteria used
for validation remain legitimate and governable.

Module Operational Role

AVCIM defines the validation-criteria governance layer of AVS.

Its role is to preserve visibility of the conditions under which
authority becomes recognized as valid.

Module Operational Space

AVCIM governs:

  • validation criteria
  • qualification requirements
  • certification requirements
  • authority-recognition rules
  • validation standards
  • validation thresholds
  • governance conditions
  • criteria accountability


The module applies wherever governance requires justification
of authority validation.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • identifiable validation criteria
  • traceable validation requirements
  • reconstructable validation decisions
  • accountable validation standards
  • governance-valid qualification requirements
  • operationally valid authority recognition


Its function is to ensure that governance can determine why
authority was validated and on what basis the validation occurred.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Validation Criteria Object

The organization must define which authority-validation environments
require criteria governance.

This may include:

  • certification systems
  • qualification systems
  • licensing environments
  • governance appointments
  • contractor qualification programs
  • AI authority frameworks
  • agent-permission systems
  • Human-AI governance environments


2. Define Validation Criteria Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which validation
criteria remain valid.

This includes:

  • qualification requirements
  • competency requirements
  • certification requirements
  • accountability requirements
  • governance requirements
  • validation-valid criteria conditions


3. Define Criteria Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how validation-criteria failures
are identified.

This may include:

  • undefined criteria
  • inconsistent criteria
  • unverifiable requirements
  • obsolete standards
  • governance-blind validation rules
  • undocumented qualification thresholds


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for
validation-criteria failures.

Governance response may include:

  • criteria review
  • standards verification
  • governance intervention
  • escalation
  • corrective actions
  • criteria revision
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • validation criteria
  • qualification requirements
  • standards reviews
  • governance decisions
  • intervention procedures
  • resulting validation states


An authority-validation environment must not remain criteria-valid
if materially significant validation requirements cannot be
reconstructed, reviewed, validated, or governed.

Use Case 1 — Professional Qualification

Environment

Scenario

A certification authority validates whether a worker possesses the
qualifications required to perform specialized
operational activities.

Application

AVCIM governs the criteria used to determine whether the worker's
authority remains valid.

Result

Governance gains visibility into the requirements
supporting authority validation.

Use Case 2 — AI Agent Authorization

Environment

Scenario

An orchestration platform validates whether an AI agent may access
tools, permissions, workflows, or operational privileges.

Application

AVCIM governs the validation criteria used to grant authority.

Result

Governance gains visibility into why authority was granted and
under which conditions.

Canonical Closing Statement

Authority Validation Criteria Integrity Module (AVCIM) defines the
structural conditions under which the criteria, requirements,
standards, qualifications, rules, thresholds, permissions, and
governance conditions used to validate authority remain
identifiable, traceable, governable, accountable, consistent, and
operationally valid throughout the validation lifecycle.

Authority validation is only as trustworthy as the criteria used to
validate it. Authority validation criteria integrity therefore
becomes a foundational condition of trustworthy authority
validation governance.
