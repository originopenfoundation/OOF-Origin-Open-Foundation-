Canonical Definition

Authority Legitimacy Integrity Module (ALIM) defines the structural
conditions under which authority remains valid, recognized,
verifiable, governable, accountable, and operationally legitimate
throughout its lifecycle.

ALIM governs authority legitimacy.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine whether authority represents a valid governance right
rather than an assumed, fabricated, expired, unauthorized, or
illegitimate authority claim.

Module Operational Role

ALIM defines the authority-legitimacy governance layer of AGS.

Its role is to ensure that authority is not merely present but
legitimately exists within a recognized governance structure.

Module Operational Space

ALIM governs:

  • authority legitimacy
  • authority validation
  • authority recognition
  • authority authenticity
  • authority verification
  • governance legitimacy
  • authority accountability
  • legitimacy traceability


The module applies wherever governance requires validation
of authority claims.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • legitimate authority structures
  • verifiable authority claims
  • accountable authority recognition
  • reconstructable authority validation
  • governance-valid authority rights
  • operationally recognized authority


Its function is to ensure that governance can determine whether
authority is genuinely legitimate.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Authority Legitimacy Object

The organization must define which authority environments
require legitimacy governance.

This may include:

  • management authority
  • supervisory authority
  • regulatory authority
  • contractual authority
  • delegated authority
  • institutional authority
  • AI governance authority
  • autonomous operational authority


2. Define Authority Legitimacy Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which authority
legitimacy remains valid.

This includes:

  • validation requirements
  • recognition requirements
  • verification requirements
  • accountability requirements
  • governance requirements
  • legitimacy-valid authority conditions


3. Define Legitimacy Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how legitimacy failures are identified.

This may include:

  • unauthorized authority claims
  • expired authority
  • fabricated authority
  • unverifiable authority
  • governance-invalid authority
  • authority impersonation


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for
legitimacy-integrity failures.

Governance response may include:

  • authority validation
  • legitimacy review
  • governance intervention
  • escalation
  • authority suspension
  • corrective actions
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • authority claims
  • legitimacy reviews
  • validation activities
  • governance decisions
  • intervention procedures
  • resulting authority states


An authority-governance environment must not remain legitimacy-valid
if materially significant authority claims cannot be validated,
reconstructed, reviewed, or governed.

Use Case 1 — Organizational Decision

Environment

Scenario

An individual issues directives affecting operational activities
and personnel decisions.

Application

ALIM determines whether the individual possesses legitimate
authority to issue those directives.

Result

Governance gains visibility into the validity of authority claims
before operational decisions are accepted.

Use Case 2 — Autonomous Agent Governance

Environment

Scenario

A supervisory AI agent issues instructions affecting
multiple execution agents.

Application

ALIM validates whether the supervisory authority remains
legitimately assigned and operationally recognized.

Result

Governance gains visibility into authority legitimacy across
intelligent operational systems.

Canonical Closing Statement

Authority Legitimacy Integrity Module (ALIM) defines the structural
conditions under which authority remains valid, recognized,
verifiable, governable, accountable, and operationally legitimate
throughout its lifecycle.

Authority may exist, but not all authority is legitimate. Authority
legitimacy integrity therefore becomes a foundational condition of
trustworthy authority governance.
