Canonical Definition System

Canonical Definition

Accountability Verification Module (AVM) defines
the structural conditions under which
governance-accountability relationships remain verifiable,
attributable, traceable, reviewable, governable,
enforceable, auditable, and operationally valid
throughout the governance lifecycle.

AVM governs accountability verification.

The module establishes the foundational conditions
required to determine whether accountability ownership remains
legitimate, whether accountability relationships remain valid,
whether accountability attribution remains correct, and whether
accountability verification remains reconstructable
throughout operational reality.

Accountability may be assigned.

Accountability may be preserved.

Accountability must also be verified.

AVM governs that verification.

Module Operational Role

AVM defines the accountability-verification
governance layer of GAS-A.

Its role is to establish governance-valid
accountability verification throughout
governance environments.

Module Operational Space

AVM governs:

  • accountability verification
  • accountability legitimacy verification
  • accountability ownership validation
  • accountability attribution verification
  • accountability auditability
  • accountability traceability
  • accountability reviewability
  • accountability reconstruction


The module applies wherever accountability relationships
must be verified before governance conclusions are reached.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • verifiable accountability ownership
  • legitimate accountability attribution
  • traceable accountability verification
  • reconstructable accountability history
  • governance-valid accountability records
  • operational accountability confidence


Its function is to ensure that governance can verify
who remains accountable for governance outcomes.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Accountability Verification Object

The organization must define which governance
environments require accountability-verification governance.
This may include:

  • organizations
  • institutions
  • governments
  • governance committees
  • corporate governance structures
  • AI governance systems
  • autonomous-agent ecosystems
  • Human-AI governance environments


2. Define Accountability Verification Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which
accountability verification remains valid.
This includes:

  • verification requirements
  • attribution requirements
  • legitimacy requirements
  • traceability requirements
  • validation requirements
  • governance-valid verification conditions


3. Define Verification Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how accountability-verification
failures are identified.
This may include:

  • unverifiable accountability ownership
  • incorrect accountability attribution
  • hidden accountability relationships
  • accountability ambiguity
  • missing verification records
  • governance-invalid accountability activities


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for
accountability-verification failures.
Governance response may include:

  • verification review
  • accountability validation
  • governance intervention
  • corrective actions
  • accountability reconstruction
  • ownership verification
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • accountability verifications
  • accountability ownership records
  • governance reviews
  • intervention procedures
  • validation activities
  • resulting accountability states


A governance environment must not remain verification-valid
if materially significant accountability relationships cannot be
reconstructed, reviewed, verified, attributed,
preserved, or governed.
