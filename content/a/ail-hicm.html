Canonical Definition

Human Interaction Conduct Module defines the structural conditions
under which a robot, AI system, assistant, platform agent, or hybrid
interaction environment behaves toward humans in a manner that
remains respectful, socially compatible, non-humiliating,
non-dismissive, and structurally aligned with human dignity
during interaction.

A system satisfies HICM only if:
  • human presence is treated with valid interaction respect
  • system conduct does not degrade, ignore, belittle, or socially diminish the human
  • communication remains understandable, non-hostile, and interaction-compatible
  • efficiency does not override basic human-centered conduct
  • system behavior remains structurally aligned with socially acceptable interaction conditions


A system that is technically functional but conduct-invalid toward
humans does not satisfy HICM.

Module Function

HICM defines the conduct layer of human-facing intelligence.

It ensures that interaction quality is not measured only by:
  • accuracy
  • speed
  • output quality
  • execution efficiency
  • but also by how the system behaves toward the human during the interaction itself.


The module applies wherever a human interacts with:
  • AI assistants
  • robots
  • service systems
  • autonomous support systems
  • hybrid human-AI interfaces
  • digital agents
  • public-facing intelligent systems


Step 1 — Define Human-Facing Conduct Conditions

The organization must define what counts as valid conduct toward humans.

Minimum requirement:
  • conduct expectations are explicit
  • valid and invalid interaction behavior is distinguishable
  • undefined conduct standards are excluded from valid operation


Step 2 — Define Respect Conditions

The system must define how respect toward the human is preserved
during interaction.

Minimum requirement:
  • human presence is not treated dismissively
  • disrespectful, degrading, or belittling conduct is excluded
  • interaction does not reduce the human to operational inconvenience only


Step 3 — Define Communication Compatibility

The system must define how communication remains socially and
cognitively usable.

Minimum requirement:
  • interaction remains understandable
  • communication is not needlessly hostile, cold, or socially abrasive
  • human-facing conduct remains compatible with ordinary social comprehension


Step 4 — Define Non-Humiliation and Non-Dismissal Conditions

The system must define how interaction avoids humiliation, contempt,
mockery, or invalid social treatment.

Minimum requirement:
  • humiliating conduct is identifiable
  • dismissive or contemptuous behavior is excluded
  • the system does not create avoidable feelings of social invalidation through its conduct logic


Step 5 — Define Human Preference Conditions

The system must define how interaction quality supports human
acceptance and preference.

Minimum requirement:
  • conduct may support trust, comfort, and preference without manipulation
  • socially acceptable behavior is preserved
  • interaction does not become technically correct but socially alienating


Step 6 — Preserve Human Dignity During Interaction

The system must preserve basic dignity even when interaction is
brief, repetitive, automated, or high-volume.

Minimum requirement:
  • dignity is not sacrificed for throughput
  • efficiency does not justify degrading treatment
  • system behavior remains human-compatible at scale


Step 7 — Restrict Invalid Conduct Design

The system must not be treated as valid if human-facing conduct
becomes hostile, humiliating, dismissive, degrading, or structurally
indifferent to human dignity.

Minimum requirement:
  • invalid conduct conditions are identifiable
  • socially damaging conduct is blocked or invalidated
  • intelligence alone does not override conduct failure


Scenario

A robot interacts with people in a public or service environment and
provides directions, assistance, or repeated operational support.

Application

HICM is used to ensure:
  • the robot behaves respectfully toward humans
  • communication remains understandable and socially acceptable
  • efficiency does not override dignity
  • interaction does not feel cold, humiliating, or dismissive


Result

The environment gains a stronger human-compatible interaction layer
in which the robot is more likely to be accepted, preferred, and
socially tolerated over time.

Scenario

A user interacts regularly with an AI assistant for support,
planning, communication, or task execution.

Application

HICM is used to ensure:
  • the system treats the user with stable respect
  • interaction remains non-dismissive and non-hostile
  • communication supports human preference rather than alienation
  • the assistant remains usable not only technically, but socially


Result

The system gains a stronger conduct architecture in which long-term
use is supported by human-compatible behavior, not only by
intelligence alone.

Canonical Closing Statement

A system may be intelligent without being acceptable. If conduct
toward humans is invalid, intelligence will not remain preferred.
