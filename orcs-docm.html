Canonical Definition

Delegated Operational Continuity Module (DOCM) defines the
structural conditions under which operational responsibility remains
materially continuous after execution, control, operational tasks,
runtime authority, or decision capability are delegated across
synchronized operational environments.

DOCM establishes the delegated-execution layer of ORCS.

The module recognizes that delegation may redistribute execution
activity without automatically terminating operational
responsibility continuity.

Where operational continuity, dependency, benefit, control
influence, or synchronized consequence remain active, responsibility
continuity must remain structurally reconstructable.

Module Function

DOCM governs environments where:

  • operational execution is delegated
  • runtime authority is transferred partially
  • operational tasks are distributed across entities
  • synchronized execution remains active
  • operational dependency persists
  • and operational consequence propagates across delegated structures


The module applies to:

  • subcontracting systems
  • delegated management environments
  • temp-agency execution structures
  • platform-mediated labor
  • AI-agent orchestration systems
  • distributed execution chains
  • outsourced operational systems
  • delegated compliance environments
  • federated operational architectures
  • cross-border execution systems


Its function is not to prohibit delegation.

Its function is to preserve responsibility continuity visibility
once operational execution becomes distributed.

Minimum Implementation Framework

Step 1 — Define the Delegated Operational Object

The organization must define what operational execution, authority,
or runtime activity has been delegated.

Minimum requirement:

  • the delegated operational object is explicit
  • the delegating entity is identifiable
  • the executing entity is identifiable
  • the operational scope is structurally bounded
  • undefined delegation conditions are excluded from valid continuity
    interpretation


The delegated object may include:

  • labor execution
  • management authority
  • production execution
  • operational supervision
  • AI-agent runtime activity
  • platform operations
  • certification-dependent tasks
  • compliance execution
  • infrastructure management
  • operational decision systems


Step 2 — Define Delegation Continuity Conditions

The system must define when operational responsibility continuity
remains active despite delegation.

Minimum requirement:

  • continuity conditions are explicit
  • delegation is not treated as automatic responsibility transfer
  • synchronized operational dependency remains reviewable


Delegation continuity conditions may include:

  • retained operational influence
  • continuing economic benefit
  • runtime visibility
  • dependency on execution outcome
  • coordinated operational consequence
  • ability to intervene or stop execution
  • synchronized infrastructure reliance
  • operational-control persistence
  • governance influence continuity


Under DOCM:

Delegated execution does not automatically eliminate responsibility
continuity.

Step 3 — Define Delegated Value Flow and Control Interpretation
Logic

The system must define how delegated execution interacts with value
flow, operational control, and synchronized dependency.

Minimum requirement:

  • delegated value flow remains traceable
  • operational-control continuity remains reviewable
  • synchronized benefit is interpreted together with delegated
    execution continuity


Interpretation logic may examine:

  • who benefits economically from delegated execution
  • who retains operational influence
  • who controls runtime conditions
  • who depends on the operational outcome
  • who externalizes operational risk
  • whether responsibility transfer was explicit
  • whether delegated execution remained materially synchronized with
    the benefiting structure


Under DOCM and Value Flow Mechanism (VFM):

Delegated execution without reviewable responsibility continuity
creates operational governance instability.

Step 4 — Define Delegated Responsibility Governance Logic

The system must define how responsibility continuity is governed
across delegated operational structures.

Minimum requirement:

  • responsibility continuity remains reconstructable
  • delegation pathways remain reviewable
  • unclear responsibility transfer does not automatically terminate
    continuity interpretation


Governance logic may include:

  • delegated execution review
  • operational-control continuity assessment
  • synchronized benefit analysis
  • runtime authority reconstruction
  • intervention-capability review
  • dependency-chain interpretation
  • escalation to delegating or benefiting structures where continuity
    remains materially active


If operational responsibility transfer is not explicit, synchronized
operational continuity must remain reviewable across the delegated
structure.

Step 5 — Preserve Traceability and Restrict Invalid Delegation
Shielding

The system must preserve traceability of delegated execution,
operational continuity, synchronized dependency, and responsibility
transfer.

Minimum requirement:

  • delegated operational structures remain reconstructable
  • responsibility transfer remains explicit
  • synchronized operational dependency remains reviewable
  • invalid delegation shielding remains identifiable


A system becomes DOCM-invalid if:

  • delegated execution conceals continuing operational influence


synchronized operational benefit remains active while responsibility
visibility disappears delegation chains become structurally
non-reviewable runtime authority continuity remains operationally
hidden fragmented delegation obscures real operational dependency
operational consequence propagates while no entity remains
reviewably responsible responsibility transfer is implied but not
structurally defined

Use Case 1 — Delegated Workplace Execution

Scenario

A company delegates operational labor through external staffing or
subcontracting structures while retaining control over the
workplace, operational environment, execution requirements, and
runtime conditions.

The external structure performs execution, but the assigning
structure continues benefiting from the operational outcome.

Application

DOCM is used to examine:

  • whether operational influence remained active
  • whether execution continuity remained synchronized
  • who controlled runtime conditions
  • whether responsibility transfer was explicit
  • whether delegation became a responsibility-obscuring mechanism


Result

The environment becomes reviewable through operational continuity
interpretation rather than formal delegation alone.

Use Case 2 — AI-Agent Delegated Execution Environment

Scenario

A distributed AI system delegates execution tasks across multiple
agents, orchestration layers, or external infrastructures while
retaining centralized operational objectives and synchronized value
extraction.

When harmful or invalid execution occurs, responsibility becomes
fragmented across the delegated chain.

Application

DOCM is used to examine:

  • operational-control continuity
  • delegated execution dependency
  • synchronized value flow
  • intervention capability
  • runtime authority persistence
  • and responsibility continuity across the delegated environment


Result

The AI execution ecosystem becomes reviewable as a synchronized
delegated operational structure rather than as isolated fragmented
execution nodes.

Canonical Closing Statement

DOCM defines the structural conditions under which delegated
operational execution cannot be used to terminate responsibility
continuity where synchronized dependency, operational influence,
value flow, or coordinated operational consequence remain materially
active.
