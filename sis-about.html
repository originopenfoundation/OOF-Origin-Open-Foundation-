About the Surface Integrity Standard (SIS)

Canonical Definition

Surface Integrity defines the condition under which any surface remains intact,
unaltered, and free from unauthorized modification.

A surface is considered integrity-valid only if its structure, appearance, and material
state remain unaffected.

Any unauthorized alteration constitutes a violation.

What This Standard Is

The Surface Integrity Standard (SIS) establishes a universal rule:

A surface must not be altered without authorization.

It defines surfaces as:
  • protected structures
  • controlled interaction layers
  • integrity-bound physical assets
SIS applies across all environments — from products to cities — creating a single, consistent
definition of what it means for a surface to remain intact.

What This Standard Is Not

SIS is not:
  • a design guideline
  • a maintenance recommendation
  • a cosmetic standard
  • a cultural restriction
It does not define aesthetics.
It defines integrity conditions.

The Core Problem

In most systems today, surfaces are treated as passive objects.

This leads to:
  • uncontrolled marking
  • repeated damage
  • normalization of degradation
  • unclear responsibility
Surfaces become:
  • communication channels without permission
  • carriers of unwanted content
  • continuously degraded assets
The system absorbs cost.
Responsibility disappears.

What SIS Changes

SIS introduces a structural shift:

A surface is not an open medium. It is a controlled asset.

This means:
  • every interaction must be authorized
  • every modification must be reversible
  • every action must be attributable

Why This Matters

Without surface integrity:
  • products lose value
  • cities degrade visually and structurally
  • maintenance costs increase
  • ownership loses meaning
With SIS:
  • surfaces remain controlled
  • responsibility is enforced
  • degradation is no longer normalized
  • systems become accountable

Use Case 1 — Product Damage Through Labeling

Scenario
A product is labeled using adhesive material that:
  • cannot be removed cleanly
  • leaves residue

 Without SIS
  • damage is accepted

  • product value decreases
 With SIS
product is classified as: Integrity Compromised Product
  • customer has right to return
  • responsibility is enforced
 Result
Product integrity becomes:
  • measurable
  • enforceable
  • protected

A surface belongs to its owner or to the system that governs it.

If it is used without authorization:

it is not interaction.
