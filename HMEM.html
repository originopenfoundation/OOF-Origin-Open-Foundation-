HMEM — Harm Mitigation Effect Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HMEM — Harm Mitigation Effect Module governs how the material effect
of a Governed Harm Mitigation Action on existing Harm or its consequences
is identified, measured, characterized, attributed sufficiently for
Mitigation governance, monitored, and distinguished from the action that
produced it and from the broader Mitigation Outcome that follows.

HMEM determines whether the Mitigation Action produced a material change
in the targeted Harm condition, the direction and magnitude of that
change, where and for whom the effect occurred, whether the effect is
immediate or delayed, temporary or persistent, uniform or uneven, direct
or indirect, and whether the intervention simultaneously produced adverse
or unintended Harm effects.

It establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Mitigation Action + Mitigation Baseline → Effect Observation
→ Effect Identification → Effect Characterization → Effect Magnitude &
Distribution → Effect Persistence → Governed Harm Mitigation Effect

Its central question is:

What material change in existing Harm or its consequences actually
occurred following the Governed Harm Mitigation Action?

  • Operational Role


HMNM establishes:

what Harm should materially improve.

HMAM establishes:

what intervention is used to attempt that improvement.

HMEM establishes:

what material change actually occurred in the Harm condition.

Its operational role is:

to move Mitigation governance from intervention activity to observable
Harm consequence.

Therefore:

Action Executed ≠ Mitigation Effect Established

  • Position Within HMS


The HMS governance flow is:

Mitigation Need → Mitigation Action → Mitigation Effect → Mitigation
Outcome → Mitigation Verification

HMEM governs the third stage:

Mitigation Effect

It receives:

Governed Harm Mitigation Action

together with relevant:

Mitigation Target

Mitigation Baseline

Mitigation Objective

current Harm State

and:

material Harm observations

It produces:

Governed Harm Mitigation Effect

for the Harm Mitigation Outcome Module.

  • Governed Object


The primary governed object is:

Material Change in Existing Harm Following Mitigation Action

HMEM does not primarily govern whether an intervention occurred correctly.

It governs what happened to the Harm after that intervention.

The effect may concern:

magnitude

severity

duration

persistence

affected scope

functional impairment

material consequence

or:

another governed Harm characteristic

  • Foundational Effect Principle


The foundational rule is:

Mitigation Effect is established by change in Harm, not by activity
performed against Harm.

Therefore:

Action Completion ≠ Harm Reduction

Operational Effort ≠ Mitigation Effect

Intervention Cost ≠ Mitigation Effect

and:

Intent to Reduce Harm ≠ Actual Harm Reduction

  • Action-to-Effect Separation


HMEM maintains a strict boundary:

Mitigation Action → Mitigation Effect

The action describes:

what was done.

The effect describes:

what changed in Harm.

These must never be collapsed into one governance object.

A perfectly executed intervention may produce:

strong effect

weak effect

no material effect

or:

adverse effect

  • Effect Baseline


Effect determination requires comparison against a sufficient Mitigation
Baseline.

Conceptually:

Baseline Harm Condition → Mitigation Action → Post-Action Harm Condition

The relevant change is:

Post-Action Harm Condition − Baseline Harm Condition

interpreted according to the applicable Harm methodology.

Without a sufficient baseline, effect magnitude may remain uncertain.

  • Baseline Change


The baseline may itself become outdated if Harm changes materially before
or during Mitigation.

HMEM must therefore determine whether the baseline remains appropriate.

Canonical distinction:

Original Baseline ≠ Valid Current Comparison Basis by Default

Where necessary, the comparison basis must be reassessed without rewriting
historical Harm reality.

  • Effect Identification


HMEM determines whether a materially relevant change occurred after
Mitigation Action.

Possible findings include:

Material Harm Reduction

Limited Harm Reduction

No Material Change

Mixed Effect

Adverse Effect

or:

Effect Uncertain

The existence of any observable difference does not automatically
establish meaningful Mitigation.

  • Direction of Effect


The Mitigation Effect may be:

Beneficial

Neutral

Adverse

or:

Mixed

A Mitigation Action intended to reduce Harm may therefore produce an
adverse effect.

Governance must represent the actual direction rather than the intended
direction.

Canonical principle:

Intended Benefit ≠ Beneficial Effect

  • Effect Magnitude


Where appropriate, HMEM determines how much the targeted Harm condition
changed.

Effect magnitude may be represented as:

Minimal

Limited

Moderate

Substantial

Near-Complete

or through another methodology appropriate to the Harm domain.

Magnitude must represent Harm change.

Therefore:

Large Intervention ≠ Large Mitigation Effect

  • Materiality


Not every measurable change is materially meaningful.

HMEM should distinguish:

detectable change

from:

material Harm reduction

A minor numerical change that does not meaningfully alter the governed
Harm condition should not automatically establish significant Mitigation
Effect.

Therefore:

Measurable Change ≠ Material Mitigation Effect by Default

  • Effect Scope


HMEM determines where the effect occurred.

Scope may include:

specific subject

multiple subjects

defined group

object

system

organization

infrastructure

environment

or:

society

The effect scope must remain connected to the governed Harm scope.

  • Effect Distribution


Mitigation effects may be uneven.

For example:

Subject A → substantial improvement

Subject B → limited improvement

Subject C → no change

Subject D → adverse change

HMEM must preserve these differences where materially relevant.

Canonical principle:

Average Improvement ≠ Uniform Harm Reduction

  • Component Effects


Composite Harm may contain several components.

An intervention may produce:

Component₁ ↓

Component₂ unchanged

Component₃ ↑

HMEM must preserve the component-level effects before the overall
Mitigation Outcome is determined.

Therefore:

Component Improvement ≠ Overall Mitigation Effect by Default

  • Collective Effects


Where Harm affects a collective, selected individual improvements cannot
automatically represent the collective effect.

HMEM must evaluate the appropriate governed level.

Therefore:

Individual Improvement ≠ Collective Harm Reduction by Default

A collective effect may differ materially from the sum of selected
individual observations.

  • Cross-Domain Effects


A Mitigation Action may produce different effects across Harm domains.

For example:

Physical Harm ↓

while:

Financial Harm unchanged

and:

Organizational Harm ↑

HMEM must preserve materially relevant cross-domain effects.

Canonical distinction:

Improvement in One Harm Domain ≠ Complete Harm Reduction

  • Direct and Indirect Effects


Mitigation Effect may be:

Direct

or:

Indirect

A direct effect acts on the targeted Harm condition.

An indirect effect changes a materially relevant condition that
subsequently reduces Harm.

Indirect effect does not mean irrelevant effect.

However, the relationship must remain sufficiently supported for
Mitigation governance.

  • Immediate and Delayed Effects


Some Mitigation effects appear immediately.

Others emerge only after a materially relevant delay.

HMEM must support:

Immediate Effect

Delayed Effect

and:

Effect Not Yet Determinable

Therefore:

No Immediate Improvement ≠ No Mitigation Effect by Default

where the applicable Harm mechanism reasonably involves latency.

  • Temporary and Persistent Effects


An intervention may initially reduce Harm before the Harm returns.

HMEM must distinguish:

Temporary Effect

from:

Persistent Effect

Conceptually:

Harm A → Reduction → Return toward Harm A

is not equivalent to:

Harm A → Reduction → Sustained Lower Harm Condition

Canonical principle:

Temporary Harm Reduction ≠ Persistent Mitigation Effect

  • Effect Decay


A Mitigation Effect may weaken gradually.

HMEM should support:

Effect Decay

where the magnitude of Harm reduction decreases over time.

This may trigger:

Mitigation reassessment

additional action

or:

replacement action

Effect decay must not remain hidden behind the original successful
intervention.

  • Effect Reversal


A previously beneficial effect may reverse.

Conceptually:

Harm ↓ → Harm returns → Harm ↑

Where this occurs, the historical beneficial effect remains valid as
historical information, but the current Harm condition must reflect the
reversal.

Therefore:

Historical Effect ≠ Current Effect

  • Harm Reduction vs Harm Displacement


An intervention may appear to reduce Harm in one location while shifting
it elsewhere.

For example:

Harm in Scope A ↓

while:

Harm in Scope B ↑

HMEM must distinguish:

reduction

from:

displacement

Canonical principle:

Moving Harm ≠ Reducing Harm

  • Harm Reduction vs Harm Substitution


An intervention may reduce one Harm while producing another.

Conceptually:

Harm₁ ↓ + Harm₂ ↑

HMEM must preserve both effects.

It must not collapse them into a single positive claim before Outcome
governance.

Therefore:

Reduction of Harm₁ ≠ Absence of Harm₂

  • Intervention-Induced Harm


Where the Mitigation Action produces new Harm, HMEM must identify the
adverse effect sufficiently for HAGA™ governance.

The new Harm may require:

Recognition

Boundary

Determination

and subsequent lifecycle governance depending on its state.

Canonical principle:

Mitigation benefit does not make Mitigation-induced Harm methodologically
invisible.

  • Net Effect


Where beneficial and adverse effects coexist, a net effect may be useful.

Conceptually:

Beneficial Harm Reduction − Additional Harm Effect → Net Effect

However, net representation must not erase materially distinct Harm.

Therefore:

Positive Net Effect ≠ No Adverse Harm

and:

Net Improvement ≠ Complete Mitigation

  • Continuing Harm Production


Existing Harm may be reduced while new Harm continues to emerge.

For example:

Mitigation Effect: −10

while:

Continuing Harm Production: +6

may result in net improvement while an active Harm pathway remains.

HMEM must preserve both dynamics.

Therefore:

Observed Harm Reduction ≠ Harm Production Stopped

HCOS remains responsible for Containment.

  • Effect Attribution Boundary


HMEM must establish sufficient association between Mitigation Action and
observed Harm change to support Mitigation governance.

However, it does not become a general causation or forensic architecture.

The relevant question is:

Is there sufficient basis to treat the observed Harm change as a
Mitigation Effect for this governed Mitigation process?

Not:

What is the complete causal history of every condition involved?

Therefore:

Mitigation Effect Association ≠ General Causation Determination

  • Confounding Conditions


Harm may change because of factors other than the Mitigation Action.

These may include:

natural recovery

external intervention

environmental change

behavioral change

system change

independent operational events

or:

other simultaneous Mitigation actions

HMEM must preserve material confounding conditions where they affect
effect interpretation.

  • Multiple Concurrent Actions


Where several Mitigation Actions operate simultaneously, the observed
effect may not be attributable to one action individually.

HMEM may therefore represent:

Combined Mitigation Effect

or:

Effect Attribution Uncertain

rather than inventing false precision.

Canonical principle:

Multiple Actions ≠ Separately Measurable Effects by Default

  • Distributed Effects


Mitigation may be executed across distributed systems or organizations
while effects emerge elsewhere.

HMEM must follow the Harm consequence rather than the organizational
location of the action.

Canonical principle:

Mitigation Effect belongs where Harm changes, not merely where
intervention occurs.

  • Effect Uncertainty


Effect uncertainty may arise from:

incomplete baseline

insufficient observation

latency

multiple simultaneous interventions

confounding conditions

distributed Harm

measurement limitations

or:

uncertain persistence

HMEM must preserve uncertainty explicitly.

Therefore:

Uncertain Effect ≠ No Effect

and:

Uncertain Effect ≠ Verified Reduction

  • Effect Monitoring


Material Mitigation Effects may require monitoring across time to
determine:

direction

magnitude

distribution

persistence

decay

reversal

and:

adverse consequences

Monitoring requirements should reflect the Harm dynamic rather than one
universal observation period.

  • Predicted and Simulated Effects


Predictions and simulations may inform expected Mitigation Effect.

But:

Predicted Effect ≠ Observed Effect

and:

Simulated Effect ≠ Operational Mitigation Effect

PGA™ and SIMULOS® remain adjacent governance architectures.

HMEM governs the effect observed in operational Harm reality.

  • Effect-to-Outcome Handoff


The principal HMEM output is:

Governed Harm Mitigation Effect

This transfers to:

HMOM — Harm Mitigation Outcome Module

The handoff asks:

Given the material effects that occurred, what is the resulting governed
Mitigation condition?

Therefore:

Mitigation Effect ≠ Mitigation Outcome

One or more effects must be interpreted within the complete governed Harm
context before an Outcome is established.

  • Minimum Implementation Framework


A minimum HMEM implementation requires eight capabilities.

  • Action-to-Effect Traceability


Maintain explicit linkage between the Governed Harm Mitigation Action,
Mitigation Target, Baseline, and observed Harm change.

  • Effect Identification & Materiality Assessment


Determine whether a materially relevant Harm change occurred and
distinguish meaningful Harm reduction from insignificant variation.

  • Direction & Magnitude Governance


Represent whether the effect is beneficial, neutral, adverse, or mixed and
determine its materially relevant magnitude.

  • Scope, Distribution & Composite Effect Governance


Preserve effects across subjects, Harm components, collectives, domains,
systems, locations, and distributed environments.

  • Temporal & Persistence Governance


Distinguish immediate, delayed, temporary, persistent, decaying, and
reversed Mitigation Effects.

  • Adverse, Displaced & Substituted Harm Governance


Identify intervention-induced Harm, Harm displacement, Harm substitution,
and materially relevant adverse effects without hiding them inside net
improvement.

  • Attribution, Uncertainty & Monitoring


Maintain sufficient Action-to-Effect association, preserve confounding
conditions and uncertainty, and monitor materially relevant effect
development.

  • Governed Output


Produce a traceable Governed Harm Mitigation Effect suitable for
Mitigation Outcome governance. 38. Use Case 1 — Strong Initial Improvement
That Does Not Persist

A Mitigation Action produces a substantial immediate reduction in an
existing Harm condition.

The initial intervention appears successful.

After a materially relevant period, however, the effect begins to decay
and the Harm condition moves toward its earlier level.

HMEM records:

Initial Beneficial Effect → Effect Decay → Reduced Current Effect

rather than preserving the initial reduction as the permanent Mitigation
Effect.

The governing principle is:

A Harm reduction that occurred historically must not be represented as a
Harm reduction that still exists.

  • Use Case 2 — Harm Decreases for One Group but Increases for
    Another


A Mitigation Action materially reduces Harm for one affected group.

The same intervention creates a new adverse condition for another group.

HMEM preserves:

Beneficial Effect — Group A

and:

Adverse Effect — Group B

without collapsing them into a generic claim of success.

The complete meaning of these effects is transferred to Mitigation Outcome
governance.

The governing principle is:

Improvement for one affected subject does not make worsening Harm
elsewhere invisible.

  • Governance Logic


HMEM applies the following logic:

Does a Governed Harm Mitigation Action exist?

If no:

→ no Action-derived Mitigation Effect should be established through HMEM.

If yes:

→ identify the Mitigation Target and relevant Baseline.

Then ask:

What materially changed in the Harm condition after the action?

If nothing material changed:

→ establish No Material Effect where sufficiently supported.

If change occurred:

→ determine its direction.

Beneficial?

Neutral?

Adverse?

Mixed?

Then determine:

What is the magnitude?

Where did the effect occur?

Who or what experienced it?

Is it component-specific or complete?

Individual or collective?

Single-domain or cross-domain?

Then determine:

Was the effect immediate or delayed?

Temporary or persistent?

Is it decaying or reversing?

Did Harm move elsewhere?

Did new Harm emerge?

Is continuing Harm still being produced?

Then assess:

Is there sufficient basis to associate the change with the Mitigation
Action?

If uncertain:

→ preserve Effect Uncertainty.

Finally:

Governed Harm Mitigation Effect → Harm Mitigation Outcome Governance

  • Canonical Boundary


The HMEM — Harm Mitigation Effect Module governs the material change in an
existing governed Harm or its consequences following a Governed Harm
Mitigation Action. It governs Effect identification, direction, magnitude,
materiality, scope, distribution, component and collective effects,
cross-domain effects, direct and indirect effects, latency, persistence,
decay, reversal, displacement, substitution, intervention-induced Harm,
net effects, sufficient Action-to-Effect association, confounding
conditions, uncertainty, and monitoring. HMEM does not establish the
original Mitigation Need, select or execute Mitigation Action, determine
the complete Mitigation Outcome, perform final Mitigation Verification,
independently classify Harm State, perform Containment, govern Recovery or
Residual Harm, establish Resolution or Finality, determine general
causation, or assign accountability, responsibility, or liability.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Mitigation activity becomes meaningful only when something material
changes in Harm.

An action may be large while its effect is small, an initial improvement
may later disappear, and Harm may decrease in one place while increasing
somewhere else.

HMEM keeps those effects visible before they are compressed into an
overall Mitigation Outcome.

The action records what was done. The effect records what Harm actually
experienced.
