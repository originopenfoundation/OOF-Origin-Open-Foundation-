  • Canonical Definition


The Harm Recovery & Residual Harm Standard (HRRHS) governs how recovery
from governed Harm is initiated, observed, evaluated, and distinguished
from Harm reduction, and how Harm that continues to remain after recovery
is identified and represented as Residual Harm.

HRRHS establishes the methodological structure for determining what has
recovered, what has not recovered, how far recovery has progressed,
whether recovery is stable, whether the post-Harm condition differs
materially from the pre-Harm condition, what continuing Harm remains after
achievable recovery, and what Residual Harm State can legitimately be
established.

It governs the transition:

Governed Harm Mitigation State + Current Harm State → Recovery Initiation
→ Recovery Progress → Recovery Evaluation → Residual Harm Identification →
Residual Harm State → Governed Harm Recovery & Residual Harm State

Its foundational distinction is:

Recovery does not necessarily restore the pre-Harm state, and recovery
does not necessarily eliminate Residual Harm.

  • Operational Role


The operational role of HRRHS is:

to determine what has actually recovered from Harm and what Harm continues
to remain after recovery, without confusing Mitigation, improvement,
stabilization, adaptation, compensation, or absence of immediate symptoms
with Recovery.

HMS asks:

How can existing Harm or its consequences be materially reduced?

HRRHS asks:

What has recovered from Harm, to what extent, and what Harm continues to
remain?

HRRHS therefore governs the transition:

Harm Reduction → Recovery → Post-Recovery Harm Reality

It creates the methodological boundary between:

what improved

what recovered

and:

what remains harmed

This position establishes an essential architecture boundary:

Mitigation determines what Harm has been reduced.

Recovery determines what has regained function, condition, capability,

Residual Harm determines what Harm continues to remain.

Likewise, Recovery should not be declared complete merely because active

  • Recovery as a Distinct Governance Object


Recovery is not merely another name for Harm reduction.

A Mitigation action may materially reduce Harm without restoring the
affected subject's relevant function, condition, capability, stability,
integrity, or operational state.

Conceptually:

Harm ↓ ≠ Recovery ↑ by default

For example:

pain may decrease while function remains impaired

system corruption may stop while operational capability remains degraded

financial Harm may be reduced while organizational capability remains
damaged

environmental Harm may stabilize while ecosystem function remains altered

Therefore:

Mitigation ≠ Recovery

  • Recovery Baseline


Recovery requires a meaningful reference condition.

Where appropriate, HRRHS may distinguish:

Pre-Harm Baseline

Post-Harm Baseline

Post-Mitigation Baseline

and:

Current Recovery State

The pre-Harm condition may provide an important reference.

However, it must not automatically become the required Recovery
destination.

Canonical principle:

Pre-Harm State ≠ Mandatory Recovery Endpoint

Some Harm permanently changes what recovery can realistically mean.

  • Recovery Target


A Recovery Target defines the condition toward which recovery is being
evaluated.

It may concern:

function

capability

stability

integrity

independence

performance

operational continuity

social functioning

or another materially relevant condition.

The target must remain grounded in the affected Harm object.

It must not be defined merely by completion of a recovery program.

Therefore:

Recovery Activity ≠ Recovery Target

  • Recovery Initiation


Recovery begins when the affected Harm condition enters a materially
meaningful process of regaining a relevant state, function, capability,
stability, or other recoverable condition.

Recovery may begin:

during Mitigation

after Mitigation

before Mitigation is complete

or:

while some Harm remains active

where operational reality supports it.

Therefore:

Mitigation Completion ≠ Mandatory Recovery Start

HAGA™ must permit overlapping lifecycle processes without collapsing their
governance boundaries.

  • Recovery Progress


Recovery Progress represents material movement toward the governed
Recovery Target.

Progress may be:

Continuous

Intermittent

Uneven

Accelerating

Decelerating

Plateaued

Regressing

or:

Uncertain

Recovery must therefore be treated as a trajectory rather than a binary
event.

Canonical principle:

Recovery Started ≠ Recovery Achieved

  • Functional Recovery


A subject may regain materially relevant function without returning to its
original structural condition.

Conversely, structural repair may occur without restoring function.

Therefore:

Structural Restoration ≠ Functional Recovery by Default

and:

Functional Recovery ≠ Full Restoration

HRRHS preserves the actual dimension in which Recovery occurs.

  • Recovery Without Restoration


Recovery may legitimately produce a new stable condition different from
the pre-Harm condition.

Conceptually:

Pre-Harm State A → Harm → Post-Harm State B → Recovery → Stable State C

where:

C ≠ A

may still constitute legitimate Recovery.

This creates a foundational HAGA™ principle:

Recovery is not defined by returning to the past. It is defined by the
governed condition that has actually been regained.

  • Adaptation ≠ Recovery


An affected subject or system may adapt to continuing Harm without the
underlying Harm having recovered.

For example:

a person may compensate behaviorally

an organization may build workarounds

a system may reroute around damaged capability

infrastructure may operate under degraded conditions

These may restore some function.

But:

Adaptation ≠ Elimination of Harm

and:

Operational Compensation ≠ Complete Recovery

HRRHS must preserve the distinction between:

recovered capability

and:

capability maintained despite continuing Harm

  • Recovery Plateau


Recovery may reach a point where further material improvement no longer
occurs.

This may represent:

temporary plateau

uncertain plateau

or:

maximum currently achievable recovery

A plateau does not automatically establish:

Recovery Complete

or:

Residual Harm Final

It requires evaluation.

Canonical distinction:

No Further Observed Improvement ≠ Recovery Finality

  • Recovery Regression


A previously recovered condition may deteriorate.

Conceptually:

Recovery Progress → Improved State → Regression

Historical Recovery remains valid as historical lifecycle information.

But:

Historical Recovery ≠ Current Recovery State

Material regression requires reassessment of:

Recovery State

Harm State

Residual Harm

and where applicable:

Mitigation Need

  • Recovery Stability


Recovery must be sufficiently stable for the strength of the Recovery
claim being made.

Immediate improvement may support:

Early Recovery

without supporting:

Stable Recovery

Therefore:

Observed Recovery ≠ Stable Recovery by Default

The required period and basis depend on the governed Harm.

  • Partial Recovery


Recovery need not be complete to be legitimate.

A subject may regain:

some function

some capability

some stability

or:

some affected dimensions

while other Harm remains.

Therefore:

Partial Recovery ≠ Recovery Failure

Partial Recovery must be representable without converting it into complete
Recovery.

  • Composite Recovery


Where Harm affects multiple components, Recovery must remain
component-aware.

For example:

Component A → Recovered

Component B → Partially Recovered

Component C → Permanently Impaired

The overall Recovery State must preserve all materially relevant
components.

Therefore:

Component Recovery ≠ Composite Recovery

  • Collective Recovery


Where Harm affects a group, organization, population, environment, or
society, selected individual recovery cannot automatically establish
collective Recovery.

Therefore:

Individual Recovery ≠ Collective Recovery by Default

Collective Recovery must be evaluated against the governed collective Harm
object.

Canonical principle:

  • Residual Harm


Residual Harm is Harm that remains materially present after the relevant
Recovery process has been sufficiently evaluated and cannot legitimately
be represented as recovered, eliminated, or otherwise absent.

Residual Harm may be:

Residual Harm is not a methodological failure.

It is a governed Harm condition.

  • Residual Harm Identification


Residual Harm Identification asks:

What Harm continues to remain after the achieved Recovery condition is
accounted for?

Conceptually:

Post-Harm Condition → Mitigation → Recovery → Remaining Harm → Residual

Harm Determination

However:

Remaining Harm During Recovery ≠ Residual Harm by Default

The distinction depends on whether the Harm is still within active
Recovery progression or has become a continuing post-Recovery condition.

  • Residual Harm Boundary


Residual Harm must remain bounded to the actual Harm that continues.

Its boundary may differ from the original Harm Boundary.

Some original Harm may have:

been mitigated

recovered

or:

changed form

while other Harm remains.

Therefore:

Original Harm Boundary ≠ Residual Harm Boundary by Default

Residual Harm must reflect current Harm reality.

  • Residual Harm Is Not Recovery Failure


A legitimate Recovery process may reach its maximum achievable condition
while Harm remains.

Therefore:

Residual Harm ≠ Failed Recovery by Default

The architecture must be able to represent:

Successful Recovery + Residual Harm

simultaneously.

This distinction is critical for physical, medical, environmental,
infrastructural, organizational, digital, and societal Harm.

Residual Harm may persist:

indefinitely

permanently

Residual Harm ≠ Immutable Harm

  • Residual Harm and Adaptation


Adaptation may reduce the operational consequences of Residual Harm
without eliminating the Residual Harm itself.

For example:

Residual Harm remains

Therefore:

Adapted Function ≠ Residual Harm Eliminated

  • Residual Harm and Compensation


Compensation may address consequences associated with Harm.

It does not automatically alter the Harm itself.

Therefore:

Compensation ≠ Recovery

and:

Compensation ≠ Residual Harm Elimination

Claims, compensation, liability, and legal resolution remain outside HRRHS
unless they materially change the actual Harm condition.

  • Residual Harm and Accountability


Residual Harm may exist regardless of whether accountability has been
established.

Therefore:

No Accountability Determination ≠ No Residual Harm

Likewise:

Accountability Established ≠ Residual Harm Resolved

AGA™ governs accountability.

HAGA™ continues to govern Harm.

  • Residual Harm and Liability


Liability status does not determine whether Residual Harm exists.

Therefore:

Liability ≠ Residual Harm

and:

No Liability ≠ No Residual Harm

HRRHS remains consequence-centered.

Residual Harm ≠ Value Loss

  • Canonical Boundary


The Harm Recovery & Residual Harm Standard (HRRHS) governs how Recovery
from governed Harm is initiated, progressed, evaluated, stabilized,
reassessed, and distinguished from Mitigation, adaptation, compensation,
restoration activity, and other post-Harm conditions, and how Harm that
continues to remain after relevant Recovery is identified, bounded,
represented, monitored, and governed as Residual Harm. It governs Recovery
Baseline, Recovery Target, Recovery trajectory, progress, stability,
plateau, regression, partial, composite, collective, distributed and
cross-domain Recovery, Recovery uncertainty, Residual Harm identification,
boundary, persistence, distribution, change, uncertainty, state, renewed
Mitigation triggers, and Resolution readiness. HRRHS does not determine
whether Harm originally exists, perform Harm Recognition or Determination,
independently establish the original Harm Boundary, govern Harm Change &
Propagation, perform Harm Containment, determine or verify Harm
Mitigation, establish accountability, responsibility, or liability,
determine compensation or claims, replace operational reality, evidence,
validation, integrity, prediction, or simulation governance, or establish
Harm Resolution or Finality.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Recovery is not the disappearance of every trace of Harm, and Residual
Harm is not proof that Recovery failed.

A person, system, organization, infrastructure, environment, or society
may recover substantially without returning to its pre-Harm condition, may
regain function through a new stable condition, and may continue to carry
Harm that remains real after Recovery has reached its legitimate limit.

HRRHS preserves all three realities: what has been regained, what has not
been regained, and what Harm continues to remain.

Recovery governs what returns. Residual Harm governs what remains.
