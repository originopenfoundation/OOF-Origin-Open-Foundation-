HSBM — Harm Subject Boundary Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HSBM — Harm Subject Boundary Module governs how the subjects
materially belonging to a recognized Harm object are identified,
distinguished, included, excluded, grouped, separated, expanded,
contracted, disputed, reassessed, and preserved as part of the governed
Harm Boundary.

HSBM establishes the transition:

Governed Boundary Identification State → Subject Candidates → Subject
Relationship Assessment → Subject Inclusion / Exclusion → Governed Harm
Subject Boundary

Its central question is:

Who or what legitimately belongs within the subject boundary of the
governed Harm?

  • Operational Role


HBIM identifies subjects that may be relevant to the Harm Boundary.

HSBM determines which of those candidates legitimately belong to the
governed Harm as affected subjects.

Its operational role is:

to establish the affected-subject extent of Harm without limiting Harm to
the first visible, reporting, directly affected, individually
identifiable, or administratively recorded subject.

The resulting Subject Boundary becomes one component of the complete:

Governed Harm Boundary

  • Position Within HBS


The HBS internal flow is:

Boundary Identification → Subject Boundary → Temporal Boundary → Effect
Boundary → Downstream Boundary

The Core Module sequence is:

HBIM → HSBM → HTBM → HEBM → HDBM

HSBM receives:

Governed Boundary Identification State

and produces:

Governed Harm Subject Boundary

for integration with subsequent boundary dimensions.

  • Foundational Subject Principle


The foundational distinction is:

Affected Subject ≠ Reporting Subject

The entity that reports Harm is not necessarily:

the only harmed subject

or even:

the harmed subject at all.

Harm governance must therefore identify subjects from the Harm itself
rather than from reporting structure alone.

  • Subject Inclusion


A subject may be included where a materially supportable relationship
exists between that subject and the governed Harm.

The relationship may be:

direct

indirect

collective

distributed

persistent

delayed

or:

downstream

depending on the Harm structure.

Inclusion means:

the subject belongs to the Harm object being bounded.

It does not establish causation, accountability, liability, compensation,
or claim entitlement.

  • Subject Exclusion


A candidate subject may be excluded where available governance establishes
that the subject:

was not materially affected

belongs to another Harm object

was incorrectly associated

was duplicated

or:

does not satisfy the applicable subject-boundary relationship

Exclusion must remain reasoned and traceable.

Canonical distinction:

Excluded From This Harm ≠ Not Harmed by Anything

  • Direct and Indirect Subjects


A Direct Subject experiences Harm through a materially immediate
relationship.

An Indirect Subject experiences a materially associated consequence
through an intermediate relationship.

Indirectness alone is not sufficient for exclusion.

Therefore:

Indirect Subject ≠ Irrelevant Subject

The relevant question is whether the subject remains materially connected
to the governed Harm.

  • Primary and Secondary Subjects


Where useful, HSBM may distinguish:

Primary Subject

from:

Secondary Subject

This classification describes relationship structure.

It must not create an automatic hierarchy of Harm significance.

Therefore:

Primary Subject ≠ More Severe Harm by Default

and:

Secondary Subject ≠ Lesser Harm by Default

  • Multiple Subjects Within One Harm


One Harm object may affect multiple subjects.

Conceptually:

Harm → Subject A

Harm → Subject B

Harm → Subject C

The existence of multiple subjects does not require separate Harm objects.

Canonical principle:

Multiple Subjects ≠ Multiple Harms by Default

  • One Subject Across Multiple Harms


The opposite is equally important.

One subject may belong to several distinct Harm lifecycles.

Conceptually:

Harm A → Subject

Harm B → Subject

Therefore:

Same Subject ≠ Same Harm Object

Subject identity alone must never be used to merge distinct Harms.

  • Collective Harm Subjects


Harm may exist at a collective level.

A governed subject may therefore be:

a defined group

an organization

an infrastructure system

an environment

a population

or:

society

without requiring the Harm to be represented exclusively as the sum of
individually identified subjects.

Foundational principle:

The absence of a single harmed subject does not preclude governed Harm.

  • Group Boundary


Where a group is treated as a Harm subject, HSBM must establish what makes
the group sufficiently coherent for inclusion.

Relevant characteristics may include:

shared exposure

shared Harm effect

shared environment

shared dependency

shared operational relationship

shared affected condition

A statistical or administrative grouping alone does not automatically

Therefore:

Collective Inclusion ≠ Identical Individual Harm

HSBM must preserve individual distinctions where they materially matter.

  • Physical and Digital Object Subjects


Harm may concern a:

physical object

or:

digital object

impairment, destruction, loss of function, or another Harm state.

Object ownership is not required to define the Harm itself.

Therefore:

Harmed Object ≠ Harmed Owner by Default

  • System and Infrastructure Subjects


Operational systems and infrastructure may themselves be Harm subjects

integrity

capacity

continuity

operational condition

The individual components of a system do not necessarily constitute
separate Harm subjects.

Conversely, Harm to one component does not automatically establish Harm to
the complete system.

  • Environmental Subject


An environment may constitute a governed Harm subject where materially
harmful change affects the environmental system itself.

HAGA™ must not require the environmental Harm to be represented only
through downstream effects on humans or organizations.

Therefore:

Environmental Harm Does Not Require a Human Proxy

Some Harm may operate at population or societal scale.

Such Harm may involve:

distributed effects

collective degradation

systemic impairment

population-level consequences

or:

societal disruption

The inability to enumerate every affected individual must not
automatically prevent the population or society from being represented as
a governed Harm subject.

A subject may be known to exist without its identity being known.

  • Minimum Implementation Framework


A minimum HSBM implementation requires eight capabilities.

  • Subject Classification


Represent individuals, multiple individuals, groups, organizations,
physical objects, digital objects, systems, infrastructure, environments,
populations, and society as possible Harm subjects.

  • Subject Inclusion & Exclusion


Apply traceable criteria for Included, Provisionally Included, Excluded,
Provisionally Excluded, Uncertain, and Disputed subject states.

  • Individual & Collective Governance


Support individual and collective Harm without requiring collective Harm
to be reduced entirely to individually enumerated subjects.

  • Subject Relationship Governance


Represent direct, indirect, dependent, distributed, cross-domain, and
multi-subject relationships without converting them into causation,
accountability, or liability conclusions.

  • Identity & Duplication Control


Distinguish unidentified subjects, duplicate representations, multiple
identifiers, and actual distinct subjects.

  • Boundary Bias Controls


Prevent reporting ability, visibility, location, organization, system,
claim status, or administrative structure from automatically determining
subject inclusion.

  • Reassessment & Historical Continuity


Support subject-boundary expansion, contraction, reclassification,
dispute, reopening, and correction while preserving previous states.

  • Governed Output


Produce a traceable Governed Harm Subject Boundary suitable for
integration into the complete Harm Boundary.

A system that recognizes only the first reporting, individually
identifiable, directly affected, or administratively recorded subject does
not fully implement HSBM.

  • Use Case 1 — Harm Initially Recognized Through One Person Affects


Multiple Subjects

A harmful condition is first recognized because one individual reports a
clear effect.

Subsequent governance identifies:

two additional affected individuals

one indirectly affected dependent subject

and:

a broader group with a shared Harm pattern

HSBM does not automatically treat all exposed persons as harmed.

It determines which subjects have a materially supportable relationship to
the governed Harm and establishes:

individual subject inclusion

collective subject structure

uncertain subjects

and:

excluded exposure-only subjects

The governing principle is:

The first person through whom Harm becomes visible does not define the
complete population to which the Harm belongs.

  • Use Case 2 — Distributed AI Operation Affects Users Across
    Multiple


Systems

A composite AI operation spans several agents, tools, sessions, and
platforms.

Harm is initially recognized through one affected user on one interface.

Further analysis identifies materially related effects affecting users
through other execution paths.

HSBM follows the Harm relationship across the technical structure rather
than limiting the Subject Boundary to the original interface or session.

It establishes the supported affected-subject set while keeping merely
exposed or unrelated users outside the Harm Boundary.

The governing principle is:

Technical distribution may divide execution, but it does not automatically
divide the population affected by the resulting Harm.

  • Governance Logic


HSBM applies the following logic:

Has HBIM identified Subject Candidates?

If no:

→ maintain the Subject Boundary as incomplete.

If yes:

→ classify candidate subjects.

Is the candidate materially affected by the recognized Harm?

If sufficiently supported:

→ include the subject.

If potentially supported but unresolved:

→ establish a Provisional or Uncertain state.

If unsupported:

→ exclude the subject from this Harm Boundary.

Is the subject merely exposed?

→ do not convert Exposure into Harm inclusion automatically.

Is the subject indirect?

→ assess the material relationship rather than excluding by default.

Do several records represent the same subject?

→ consolidate duplicate representations.

Is a subject known to exist but identity unresolved?

→ preserve an Unidentified Affected Subject state.

Does Harm exist at group, system, environmental, population, or societal
level?

→ assess the collective subject directly rather than requiring artificial
individualization.

Does the subject appear through another system, organization, session,
agent, location, or domain?

→ do not fragment the Subject Boundary solely because the technical or
administrative boundary changed.

Has new information changed the affected-subject set?

→ expand, contract, reclassify, dispute, or reopen while preserving
history.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HSBM — Harm Subject Boundary Module governs which individuals,
multiple individuals, defined groups, organizations, physical objects,
digital objects, operational systems, infrastructure, environments,
populations, and societal entities legitimately belong within the subject
boundary of a recognized Harm object. It governs subject classification,
inclusion, exclusion, collective subject structures, direct and indirect
subject relationships, unidentified subjects, duplication, uncertainty,
dispute, expansion, contraction, reclassification, reopening, and
historical Subject Boundary continuity. It does not establish the Harm
Temporal Boundary, Effect Boundary, or Downstream Boundary; formally
determine Harm; establish general causation; assign accountability or
responsibility; determine liability or claim entitlement; or replace
identity, operational reality, evidence, validation, integrity, AI
behavior, autonomous execution, simulation, Value, or tax-event
governance.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm is not bounded by whoever reports it first, whoever is easiest to
identify, or whoever fits most conveniently into an existing case record.

A Harm may belong to one individual, many individuals, an organization, an
object, a system, an environment, or a population — and those subjects may
become visible at different times and through different systems.

HSBM ensures that the subject boundary follows the materially affected
reality while excluding entities that do not legitimately belong to that
Harm.

The subjects of Harm are determined by the Harm relationship, not by the
administrative structure through which they become visible.
