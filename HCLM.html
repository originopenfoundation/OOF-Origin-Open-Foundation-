HCLM — Harm Closure Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HCLM — Harm Closure Module governs how a governed Harm lifecycle
transitions from established Resolution into formal Closure after
applicable Resolution Criteria have been sufficiently satisfied.

HCLM determines whether the resolved Harm is ready for Closure, what
materially relevant conditions must remain represented at Closure, whether
Residual Harm, dependencies, monitoring requirements, unresolved but
Resolution-compatible conditions, uncertainty, and future Reopening
triggers have been sufficiently preserved, and whether active Harm
lifecycle governance can legitimately cease under the current Resolution
basis without destroying future governability.

It establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Resolution State + Governed Harm Resolution Criteria State →
Closure Readiness → Closure Conditions → Closure Determination → Lifecycle
Preservation → Governed Harm Closure State

Its central question is:

When can active governance of a resolved Harm lifecycle legitimately be
closed without erasing continuing governance-relevant conditions or
preventing future Reopening?

  • Operational Role


HRSM-F establishes:

the Resolution State.

HRCM-F establishes:

whether applicable Resolution Criteria are sufficiently satisfied.

HCLM determines:

whether the resolved Harm lifecycle can now be closed.

Its operational role is:

to terminate active Harm lifecycle handling under the current Resolution
basis while preserving everything required to understand, monitor,
reassess, reopen, and eventually determine Finality.

The foundational principle is:

Closure must reduce active governance without destroying future
governability.

  • Position Within HRFS


The HRFS governance flow is:

Resolution State → Resolution Criteria → Closure → Reopening → Finality

HCLM governs the third stage:

Closure

It receives principally:

Governed Harm Resolution State

and:

Governed Harm Resolution Criteria State

It produces:

Governed Harm Closure State

for:

HROM — Harm Reopening Module

and ultimately:

HFFM — Harm Finality Formation Module

  • Governed Object


The primary governed object is:

Closure of the Active Governed Harm Lifecycle

Closure does not mean that:

the Harm never existed

all Harm disappeared

Residual Harm is absent

all external processes ended

or:

Finality has been reached

Therefore:

Harm Closure ≠ Harm Erasure

  • Foundational Closure Principle


The foundational rule is:

A Harm lifecycle may close only when active governance can legitimately
cease under the current Resolution basis while sufficient continuity
remains for future reassessment and Reopening.

Closure therefore has two simultaneous requirements:

Active Governance Can Cease

and:

Future Governability Must Remain

Neither may replace the other.

  • Resolution Before Closure


Closure ordinarily requires legitimate Resolution.

Conceptually:

Resolution → Closure

Therefore:

Unresolved Harm ≠ Closure-Ready Harm

and:

Resolution Criteria Satisfied ≠ Closure Automatically Completed

Closure is its own governance determination.

  • Closure Readiness


Closure Readiness exists where:

Resolution is sufficiently established

applicable Resolution Criteria are sufficiently satisfied

material post-Resolution conditions are represented

and:

active Harm lifecycle governance may potentially cease

Closure Readiness does not itself close the lifecycle.

Therefore:

Closure Ready ≠ Closed

  • Closure Conditions


Before Closure, HCLM must determine what conditions must remain governed
after active lifecycle handling ceases.

These may include:

Residual Harm

conditional Resolution requirements

material dependencies

monitoring requirements

latent Harm conditions

uncertainty

reopening triggers

preservation requirements

or:

other materially relevant post-Closure conditions

Closure must not erase them.

  • Residual Harm at Closure


Residual Harm may remain when Closure occurs.

Therefore:

Residual Harm Exists ≠ Closure Impossible

Where Residual Harm remains, Closure must preserve its materially
relevant:

state

boundary

scope

distribution

dependencies

and:

conditions relevant to future reassessment

  • Conditional Resolution at Closure


A conditionally resolved Harm may potentially close where the conditions
supporting Resolution remain sufficiently governed.

The Closure State must preserve those conditions.

Therefore:

Conditional Resolution + Closure ≠ Unconditional Closure

The continuing conditions remain part of the Closure basis.

  • Dependency-Supported Closure


Closure may depend on continuing external conditions.

Examples may include:

ongoing support

protective infrastructure

monitoring systems

treatment

technical safeguards

or:

environmental controls

Such dependencies must remain visible.

Therefore:

Dependency-Supported Closure ≠ Dependency-Free Closure

  • Monitoring After Closure


Closure may legitimately coexist with continued monitoring.

Monitoring does not necessarily mean the Harm lifecycle remains actively
open.

Therefore:

Monitoring Continues ≠ Harm Lifecycle Open

Monitoring may instead preserve the ability to detect:

Resolution degradation

Residual Harm change

dependency failure

or:

Reopening triggers

  • Closure Under Uncertainty


Perfect certainty is not required for Closure.

But materially relevant uncertainty must be sufficiently:

identified

bounded

represented

and where necessary:

linked to Reopening conditions

Canonical principle:

Closure may coexist with bounded uncertainty, but not with hidden
uncertainty that materially undermines the Resolution basis.

  • Closure Scope


Every Closure determination must have explicit scope.

Closure may apply to:

the complete governed Harm

a defined Harm component

a specific subject set

a location

a domain

or:

another explicitly bounded Harm object

Therefore:

Closed Component ≠ Closed Complete Harm Lifecycle

  • Composite Closure


Composite Harm may contain different Closure conditions.

For example:

Component A → Closed

Component B → Closure Pending

Component C → Not Closure-Ready

HCLM must preserve these differences.

Therefore:

Closure of Components ≠ Composite Closure by Default

  • Collective Closure


Where Harm affects a group or population, Closure must correspond to the
governed collective Harm object.

Closure for most affected subjects does not automatically establish
collective Closure.

Therefore:

Majority Closure ≠ Collective Harm Closure

  • Distributed Closure


Distributed Harm may close differently across:

locations

systems

organizations

infrastructure

or:

environments

Therefore:

Local Closure ≠ Distributed Harm Closure

  • Cross-Domain Closure


Where Harm spans multiple domains, Closure may differ across those
domains.

For example:

Physical Harm → Closed

Operational Harm → Closed

Financial Harm → Closure Pending

Environmental Harm → Not Closure-Ready

The complete Closure determination must preserve materially relevant
differences.

  • Closure and Continuing Harm


Closure must not conceal actively changing Harm.

Where Harm remains materially:

emerging

accumulating

compounding

propagating

or:

cascading

Closure requires strong scrutiny and may be inappropriate.

Canonical principle:

Active Harm progression cannot be administratively transformed into Harm
Closure.

  • Closure and Stable Residual Harm


Stable Residual Harm may be compatible with Closure.

This is methodologically important.

The lifecycle does not necessarily need to remain actively open merely
because a stable, sufficiently governed Harm consequence continues to
exist.

Therefore:

Stable Residual Harm ≠ Permanently Open Harm Lifecycle

  • Closure and Permanent Harm


Permanent Harm may remain after Closure.

For example, a permanent impairment may remain materially real while
active Harm lifecycle progression is no longer required.

Therefore:

Permanent Harm ≠ Permanent Active Harm Governance

Closure does not deny the continuing Harm.

It changes the lifecycle governance condition.

  • Closure and Historical Harm


Closure must preserve the distinction between:

current Harm

and:

historical Harm

A closed lifecycle may continue to contain a complete historical record of
Harm that is no longer currently active.

Therefore:

Historical Harm Record ≠ Current Active Harm State

  • Administrative Closure Boundary


An organization may close an:

incident

ticket

case

investigation

complaint

or:

internal process

That does not establish HAGA™ Closure.

Therefore:

Administrative Closure ≠ Harm Lifecycle Closure

  • Legal Closure Boundary


Legal proceedings may end independently from the Harm lifecycle.

Therefore:

Legal Closure ≠ Harm Closure

and:

Legal Finality ≠ Harm Finality

HCLM does not substitute legal procedural status for Harm governance.

  • Claim Closure Boundary


An insurance or compensation claim may close while Harm continues.

Likewise, Harm may close while a claim remains active.

Therefore:

Claim Closure ≠ Harm Closure

  • Financial Settlement Boundary


Financial settlement does not establish Harm Closure.

Therefore:

Settlement Completed ≠ Harm Lifecycle Closed

A settlement may be materially relevant but remains a separate governance
object.

  • Accountability Boundary


Accountability may remain unresolved after Harm Closure.

Likewise, accountability may be determined before Closure.

Therefore:

Accountability Closure ≠ Harm Closure

  • Value Boundary


Value consequences may remain after Harm Closure.

HCLM does not determine whether value has been restored.

Therefore:

Harm Closure ≠ Value Restoration

VFM® governs the Value lifecycle.

Closure must remain traceable through the Harm lifecycle.

Governed Harm → Containment → Mitigation → Recovery → Residual Harm →

Canonical principle:

  • Closure Does Not Erase Evidence


Closure must not destroy evidence required for legitimate future
governance.

ADIT® governs evidence and auditability.

HCLM requires that Closure remain compatible with necessary evidence
continuity.

Therefore:

Closure ≠ Evidence Destruction

  • Closure Does Not Freeze Reality


Closure represents the Harm condition at the time the Closure basis is
established.

It does not guarantee that reality will never change.

Therefore:

Closed Harm ≠ Immutable Harm Reality

This is why Reopening exists as the next HRFS governance stage.

  • Reopening Conditions at Closure


Where materially appropriate, Closure should establish the conditions that
may require Reopening.

These may include:

Residual Harm increase

Recovery regression

latent Harm manifestation

dependency failure

new materially relevant effects

Harm Boundary expansion

continuing Harm resumption

or:

material invalidation of the Resolution basis

  • Reopening Trigger Visibility


Reopening conditions must be sufficiently visible to the mechanisms or
actors responsible for detecting them.

A trigger that cannot realistically be observed cannot reliably protect
Closure.

Canonical principle:

A Reopening condition must be governable enough to become actionable when
the Harm reality changes.

  • Closure Monitoring Link


Where monitoring is required, HCLM must establish what monitoring is

This may include monitoring of:

Residual Harm

latent conditions

or:

specific Reopening triggers

Monitoring must remain proportionate.

  • Closure Persistence


Closure persists while the materially relevant Closure basis remains
valid.

Therefore:

Closed Once ≠ Closed Forever

Before Finality, Closure remains capable of being reopened.

  • Closure Degradation


A Closure basis may weaken without immediately requiring full Reopening.

For example:

dependency reliability decreases

or:

Residual Harm uncertainty increases

This may create:

Closure Under Reassessment

where appropriate.

The governance state should reflect uncertainty before reality justifies
stronger action.

  • Closure Invalidity


Where the basis supporting Closure materially fails, the lifecycle cannot
remain closed merely because Closure was previously legitimate.

Therefore:

Historical Closure ≠ Current Closure Validity

A Reopening determination may become necessary.

  • Closure History


The lifecycle must preserve material Closure history.

A Harm may experience:

Resolution → Closure → Reopening → Resolution → Closure

Each state remains part of the governed lifecycle.

Therefore:

Reopening ≠ Erasure of Historical Closure

  • Canonical Boundary


The HCLM — Harm Closure Module governs how legitimately resolved Harm
transitions from active lifecycle governance into Closure while preserving
materially relevant Residual Harm, dependencies, monitoring, uncertainty,
scope, lifecycle traceability, Reopening conditions, and future
governability. It governs Closure Readiness, Closure Conditions,
Conditional Closure, Closure scope, preservation, monitoring linkage,
Closure reassessment, Closure history, and formation of the Governed Harm
Closure State. HCLM does not determine the original Harm, perform
Containment, Mitigation or Recovery, identify Residual Harm, independently
define Resolution Criteria, govern Reopening as its primary object,
establish Finality, determine general causation, accountability,
responsibility, liability, claims, compensation or value consequences, or
substitute administrative, legal, insurance, financial, or organizational
closure for Harm lifecycle Closure.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Closure is not the disappearance of Harm and not the destruction of the
Harm lifecycle record.

It is the governed point at which active lifecycle handling may
legitimately cease while the conditions required to understand, monitor,
reassess, and reopen that Harm remain intact.

A closed Harm lifecycle must therefore preserve both what justified
Closure and what could invalidate it.

Closure ends active handling. It must never end governability.
