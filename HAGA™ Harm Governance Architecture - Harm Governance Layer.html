Compatible OOF® Architectures

HAGA™ interoperates with multiple OOF® architectures governing
complementary spaces surrounding Harm.

GOA™ — Governance Architecture provides the constitutional governance
layer defining how governance itself is structured, bounded, delegated,
validated, and maintained.

ORA™ — Operational Reality Architecture governs what is actually occurring
in Operational Reality, while HAGA™ governs Harm that exists, emerges,
changes, propagates, persists, or remains within that reality.

AGA™ — Accountability Governance Architecture governs accountability
relationships surrounding harmful events, decisions, actions, omissions,
systems, and outcomes, while HAGA™ governs Harm itself independently from
whether accountability has been established.

AIG® — AI Governance Architecture governs AI behavioral integrity and
behavioral governance, while HAGA™ governs Harm that may result from AI
behavior without becoming an architecture for AI behavior itself.

ASGA™ — Autonomous Systems Governance Architecture governs
autonomous-system identity, authority, execution, constraints,
coordination, intervention, escalation, and operational governability,
while HAGA™ governs Harm resulting from autonomous operation independently
from the mechanism through which the Harm was produced.

ADIT® — Continuous Audit & Evidence Governance Architecture governs
evidential observation, preservation, correlation, verification,
examination, and auditability, while HAGA™ determines what sufficiently
supported Harm information means within the Harm lifecycle.

VALIDOS™ — Validation Governance Architecture governs whether a validation
basis sufficiently supports a Validation Determination, while HAGA™
governs Harm lifecycle determinations and states supported by relevant
validated information.

INTEGROS® — Integrity Governance Architecture governs integrity across
objects, states, evidence, and processes, while HAGA™ governs Harm as a
distinct lifecycle object.

VFM® — Value Flow Mechanism governs what happens to Value as a consequence
of events and conditions, while HAGA™ governs Harm itself.

SIMULOS® — Simulation Governance Architecture governs simulation purpose,
structure, execution, outputs, and lifecycle, while HAGA™ governs actual
Harm and does not treat simulated Harm as operational Harm by default.

HAGA™ serves as the common Harm Governance architecture supporting the
broader OOF® ecosystem by ensuring that Harm becomes a governed lifecycle
object rather than an undefined consequence attached to an incident,
action, failure, or liability process.

Architectural Position

HAGA™ governs the complete Harm Lifecycle from Harm-relevant context and
exposure to legitimate Resolution and Finality.

The architecture progresses through:

Harm Context → Harm Exposure → Harm Emergence → Harm Recognition → Harm
Boundary → Harm Determination → Harm State → Harm Change & Propagation →
Harm Containment → Harm Mitigation → Harm Recovery & Residual Harm → Harm
Resolution & Finality

Together these standards govern the context in which Harm can exist, how
exposure arises, when Harm begins to exist, when it becomes recognized,
what belongs within its boundary, whether Harm can legitimately be
established, what its current state is, how it changes or propagates, how
further Harm is contained, how existing Harm is reduced, what recovers and
what remains, and when the Harm lifecycle can legitimately reach
Resolution and Finality.

HAGA™ remains distinct from the mechanisms that produce Harm.

Human Action / AI Behavior / Autonomous Action / Process Failure /
Physical Interaction / Environmental Condition / Composite Operation →
Governed Harm Lifecycle under HAGA™

Physical contact is an operational mechanism. Harm is a governed
consequence.

HAGA™ governs Harm — not the mechanism that produced it. Core Governed

Each can fail independently.

Simulated Harm Reduction ≠ Actual Harm Reduction. Architectural Origin

HAGA™ evolved from the foundational methodological problem that Harm is
frequently treated only as an outcome attached to another governance
process.

Traditional systems commonly move from:

Event → Investigation → Accountability / Liability → Resolution

while the Harm itself may remain insufficiently governed as an independent
lifecycle object.

This becomes increasingly inadequate where Harm:

emerges gradually

exists before recognition

accumulates across repeated interactions

results from multiple individually insufficient actions

propagates beyond its original boundary

continues after its producing mechanism stops

changes after Mitigation

remains after Recovery

or:

exists without liability having been determined

HAGA™ therefore establishes Harm as an independent governed object.

Its foundational lifecycle is:

Context → Exposure → Emergence → Recognition → Boundary → Determination →
State → Change & Propagation → Containment → Mitigation → Recovery &
Residual Harm → Resolution & Finality Architectural Structure

The architecture deliberately separates governable spaces that can
independently fail.

Harm may:

  • exist in an incorrectly understood context
  • arise through exposure that was never identified
  • emerge before it becomes recognized
  • remain unrecognized despite already existing
  • be incorrectly bounded
  • be claimed without sufficient Determination
  • be assigned an inaccurate current State
  • accumulate, compound, propagate, or cascade without being tracked
  • continue expanding despite intervention
  • be contained without being mitigated
  • be mitigated without meaningful Recovery
  • remain as Residual Harm after legitimate Recovery
  • appear resolved while material Harm conditions remain unresolved
  • be closed without preserving Reopening conditions
  • or remain indefinitely active because Finality was never


methodologically defined.

HAGA™ treats these not as one generic harm management problem, but as
distinct Harm Governance spaces requiring distinct methodological
controls. Recursive Lifecycle Principle

HAGA™ does not assume that Harm moves through its lifecycle only once.

A governed Harm may change after Determination.

During its lifecycle:

Harm State may change.

Harm may accumulate.

Harm may compound.

Harm may propagate or cascade.

Containment may fail.

Mitigation may require renewed action.

Recovery may regress.

Residual Harm may increase, decrease, or become newly recoverable.

Resolved Harm may degrade.

Closed Harm may require Reopening.

The lifecycle may therefore proceed:

Governed Harm → State Change → Containment → Mitigation → Recovery →
Residual Harm → Resolution → Closure

and where material reality changes:

Closure → Reopening → Appropriate HAGA™ Re-Entry Point → Renewed
Governance → Resolution → Closure

Only when sufficient terminal conditions exist does the lifecycle proceed
to:

Finality

Reopening does not erase legitimate historical Resolution or Closure.

Finality does not erase Harm history. Foundational Architecture Principles

HAGA™ governs Harm — not the mechanism that produced it.

Exposure ≠ Harm.

Harm-Relevant Occurrence ≠ Established Harm.

Unrecognized Harm may still be existing Harm.

Harm does not require a linguistic decision, explicit reasoning trace,
discrete incident, or single identifiable action in order to exist.

The absence of a single harmed subject does not preclude governed Harm.

The absence of a single Harm-producing action does not preclude governed
Harm.

Harm may emerge from a composite operation even where no individual
action, output, session, agent, or component independently constitutes the
complete Harm-producing condition.

Physical contact is an operational mechanism. Harm is a governed
consequence.

Termination of the originating mechanism does not necessarily terminate
the Harm lifecycle.

Containment ≠ Mitigation.

Containment prevents additional Harm. Mitigation reduces Harm that already
exists or reduces its existing consequences.

Mitigation ≠ Recovery.

Recovery does not necessarily restore the pre-Harm state.

Recovery does not necessarily eliminate Residual Harm.

Recovery governs what returns. Residual Harm governs what remains.

Residual does not mean inactive.

Residual does not mean permanent.

Recovery ≠ Resolution.

Resolution ≠ Closure.

Closure ≠ Finality.

Residual Harm Exists ≠ Resolution Impossible.

Residual Harm Exists ≠ Finality Impossible.

Closure must reduce active governance without destroying future
governability.

Reopening does not erase legitimate historical Resolution.

Finality closes active Harm governance. It does not erase Harm history.

Harm Finality ≠ Harm Erasure. Canonical Principle

HAGA™ does not govern the operational mechanism that produced Harm,
perform general incident investigation, determine general causation,
allocate accountability or liability, administer claims or compensation,
determine Value consequences, perform evidence governance, make validation
determinations, or substitute for legal, medical, safety, regulatory, or
domain-specific decision systems. HAGA™ governs the methodological
conditions under which Harm becomes contextually identifiable,
exposure-aware, emergent, recognizable, bounded, determinable,
state-governed, traceable through change and propagation, containable,
mitigable, recoverable where possible, distinguishable from Residual Harm,
legitimately resolvable, closable, reopenable when reality changes, and
capable of reaching governed Finality throughout its lifecycle.

Harm may begin before it is recognized, continue after its cause has
stopped, propagate beyond its original boundary, remain after Recovery,
and exist without liability having been determined. HAGA™ provides the
governance architecture for that entire lifecycle.

HAGA™ governs Harm from exposure to finality.
