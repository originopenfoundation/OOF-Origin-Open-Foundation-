Canonical Definition

Change Impact Assessment Module (CIAM) is the Core Module within
Software Change Management Standard (SCMS) that governs how the
potential and observed consequences of proposed software changes are
identified, analyzed, classified, evidenced, and translated into
requirements for subsequent software lifecycle governance.

CIAM establishes change impact as a multidimensional relationship between

an identifiable proposed modification, the software objects it
directly affects, the dependencies and conditions through which its
consequences may propagate, and the resulting implications for
software functionality, quality, security, configuration, operational
continuity, and lifecycle integrity.

The module distinguishes the technical size of a modification from its

governance significance, direct impact from indirect impact,
anticipated consequences from verified consequences, and impact
assessment from implementation authorization.

It requires material effects, dependencies, assumptions, uncertainties,

reversibility limitations, and verification needs to remain
identifiable rather than being concealed within simplified change
classifications or aggregate risk scores.

CIAM applies to human-proposed changes, automated modifications,

AI-generated code, dependency updates, configuration revisions,
security remediation, infrastructure-related software changes, and
autonomous software systems.

Its principal governance output is the Software Change Impact Profile.

Operational Role

CIAM establishes the substantive assessment layer between change request

formation and coordinated lifecycle processing.

It governs:

  • Change impact assessment identity
  • Proposed change interpretation
  • Assessment scope
  • Current-state and intended-state references
  • Directly affected software objects
  • Indirect impact propagation
  • Functional consequences
  • Dependency consequences
  • Configuration and build consequences
  • Quality and security consequences
  • Data and interface compatibility
  • Operational consequences
  • Reversibility and recovery implications
  • Combined and concurrent change effects
  • Assessment evidence
  • Assumptions and uncertainty
  • Impact significance
  • Feasibility considerations
  • Required verification
  • Lifecycle governance implications
  • Material reassessment triggers
  • Impact assessment conclusions

CIAM does not authorize implementation, perform technical changes,
replace specialized lifecycle assessments, or establish final
operational acceptance.

Operational Question

How can the direct, indirect, combined, and potentially evolving
consequences of a proposed software change be identified, evaluated,
and supported by evidence so that its governance significance,
affected lifecycle mechanisms, uncertainty, and required controls
remain visible before the change proceeds?

It receives identifiable change requests and supporting evidence from

Its conclusions may be revised when implementation evidence,

  • Governed Space

Software Change Impact Governance

This governed space concerns the identification, evaluation,

classification, and evidence-supported interpretation of software
change consequences.

It begins when an identifiable software change requires substantive
assessment of its potential effects.

It includes direct modifications, indirect dependency effects, interactions

with other changes, and consequences extending beyond the initially
proposed scope.

It continues through assessment, clarification, impact classification,
uncertainty treatment, reassessment, and the establishment of
applicable lifecycle governance implications.

The governed space does not end merely because an initial impact
report has been produced.

Material new evidence may require the impact assessment to be
reopened.

Foundational Principle

The governance significance of a software change is determined by the
consequences it may introduce, not by the apparent size, simplicity,
or speed of the modification.

A one-line code change may alter authorization behavior.

A dependency update may affect multiple services.

A configuration modification may create data integrity consequences.

A small firmware change may alter physical system behavior.

An automated correction may resolve one defect while introducing
another.

Modification Size ≠ Impact Significance.

Local Correctness ≠ System-Level Compatibility.

Known Direct Impact ≠ Complete Change Impact.

Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum CIAM implementation must establish eight governance layers.
Layer 1 — Assessment Identity

Identify the change request, applicable request version, software product,

assessment scope, and responsible assessment function. Layer 2 — State
Comparison

Establish the relevant current software state, intended state, and proposed

modification. Layer 3 — Direct Impact

Identify the software objects, functions, artifacts, configurations, and

conditions directly affected. Layer 4 — Indirect Impact

Evaluate dependency propagation, interfaces, data relationships, external

systems, and other material indirect consequences. Layer 5 —
Consequence Assessment

Evaluate quality, security, operational, resource, reversibility, recovery,

and combined-change implications. Layer 6 — Evidence and Uncertainty

Preserve supporting evidence, assumptions, assessment coverage,

limitations, and unresolved material questions. Layer 7 — Governance
Implications

Establish impact significance, verification requirements, coordination

implications, and affected lifecycle mechanisms. Layer 8 — Governed
Output and Continuity

Establish the Software Change Impact Profile, preserve reassessment

triggers, and provide structured outputs to CHCM and LRECM.

The implementation may be manual, tool-assisted, automated, or
AI-supported.

The methodological requirements remain independent of the assessment

technology.

Cross-Module Boundary

CRMM → CIAM

CRMM establishes the change request identity, origin, purpose, preliminary

scope, and supporting evidence.

CIAM evaluates the substantive consequences of the proposed
modification.

CIAM → CHCM

CIAM identifies affected objects, dependencies, interactions, risks, and

coordination implications.

CHCM governs the coordination of actors, decisions, activities, and related

changes.

CIAM → LRECM

CIAM identifies affected lifecycle mechanisms and required verification

implications.

LRECM determines and controls the applicable lifecycle re-entry
pathway.

CIAM → CCLM

CIAM establishes expected consequences and verification implications.

CCLM uses relevant assessment references when evaluating the resulting
change outcome and unresolved obligations.

Impact Assessed ≠ Change Authorized.

Cross-Architecture Boundary

CIAM identifies change-related consequences across SLGA™ but does not
replace specialized lifecycle governance.

Use Case 1 — A One-Line Permission Change Creates System-Wide Security

Exposure

A developer proposes a one-line modification to an authorization
function.

The modification is intended to resolve an access problem affecting a
limited user group.

Local tests confirm that the affected users can access the required
function.

However, the modified condition also grants access to other users who

previously lacked permission.

CIAM evaluates:

Change Request → Direct Code Modification → Authorization Logic → Affected

User Groups → Security Impact → Verification Requirements → Lifecycle
Implications

The assessment identifies:

  • The source code modification is small
  • The intended functional correction is achieved in local testing
  • The modification changes the effective permission boundary
  • Additional users may receive unintended access
  • Security assessment and broader authorization testing are
    required

The appropriate classification is:

Material Security Impact Identified — Additional Verification
Required.

One-Line Modification ≠ Limited Governance Impact.

Critical Distinctions

Modification Size ≠ Impact Significance.

Local Correctness ≠ System-Level Compatibility.

Known Direct Impact ≠ Complete Change Impact.

Proposed State ≠ Verified Resulting State.

Directly Modified Object ≠ Only Affected Object.

Limited Change Scope ≠ Limited Operational Consequences.

Dependency Identified ≠ Dependency Impact Fully Understood.

No Known Impact ≠ Verified Absence of Impact.

Existing Test Coverage ≠ Sufficient Change Verification.

Canonical Boundary

CIAM governs the identification, assessment, classification, and

evidence-supported interpretation of the direct, indirect, combined,
and evolving consequences of proposed software changes.

It establishes the relationship between a change request, current and

intended software states, affected objects, impact propagation,
material consequences, assumptions, uncertainty, verification
requirements, and lifecycle governance implications.

CIAM does not independently authorize implementation, execute software
modifications, replace specialized quality or security verification,
or establish final operational acceptance.

Its principal governance output is the:

Software Change Impact Profile

This output provides the evidence-supported basis for change coordination,

lifecycle re-entry, and subsequent change outcome assessment.

Canonical Closing Statement

A software change cannot be assessed reliably by examining only the

component being modified. Its significance depends on the
relationships, conditions, and operational consequences that the
modification may alter.

A one-line permission change may create system-wide security exposure.

A dependency update may affect services that the requester does not
directly control.

A configuration adjustment may invalidate earlier quality evidence.

A database migration may prevent safe rollback.

An AI-generated patch may pass local tests while introducing new
operational risks.

And a firmware modification may alter physical behavior through
dependencies that remain invisible at the source code level.

CIAM establishes the governance structure required to identify and assess

these consequences.

It requires change impact to be evaluated across relevant software objects,

dependencies, interfaces, quality conditions, security requirements,
operational environments, and recovery constraints.

It preserves direct and indirect effects, evidence limitations, material

assumptions, and unresolved uncertainty.

It recognizes that individually acceptable changes may produce
unacceptable combined consequences.

It identifies the lifecycle governance mechanisms that must be
revisited without replacing their specialized responsibilities.

And it ensures that impact conclusions remain traceable and subject to
reassessment when new evidence or changing conditions invalidate
earlier assumptions.

The purpose of software change impact assessment is not merely to
predict whether a modification will work. It is to establish what the
modification may affect, how its consequences may propagate, which
conditions require verification, and which governance obligations must
be satisfied before the resulting software state can be considered
acceptable.

Change Impact Assessment Module (CIAM) transforms software change analysis

from a local technical estimate into an evidence-supported governance
assessment of system-wide consequences, uncertainty, and lifecycle
obligations.
