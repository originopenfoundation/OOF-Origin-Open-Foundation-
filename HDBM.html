Canonical Definition

The HDBM — Harm Downstream Boundary Module governs how far the boundary of
a recognized Harm legitimately extends through downstream effects,
consequences, affected subjects, domains, systems, dependencies,
locations, and temporal stages by establishing whether later consequences
remain materially continuous with the original governed Harm, constitute
propagation of that Harm, or have crossed a sufficient boundary break to
require separate governance.

HDBM establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Subject Boundary + Governed Harm Temporal Boundary +
Governed Harm Effect Boundary → Downstream Consequence Candidates →
Continuity / Break Assessment → Downstream Inclusion / Exclusion →
Governed Harm Downstream Boundary

Its central question is:

How far downstream does the governed Harm legitimately extend before a
consequence falls outside its boundary or becomes a separate Harm object?

  • Operational Role


Harm may continue beyond:

the originating event

the original subject

the first effect

the original system

the original organization

the original location

or:

the termination of the Harm-producing mechanism

But Harm cannot be extended indefinitely merely because later consequences
can be connected through some chain of events.

HDBM governs this boundary.

Its operational role is:

to preserve legitimate Harm continuity across downstream consequences
while establishing where that continuity materially ends.

  • Foundational Downstream Principle


The foundational distinction is:

Downstream Consequence ≠ Same Harm by Default

But equally:

Downstream Consequence ≠ Separate Harm by Default

HDBM exists because neither automatic inclusion nor automatic separation

  • Downstream Consequence


A Downstream Consequence is an effect, subject impact, system consequence,
domain transition, dependency consequence, or other materially related
development occurring beyond the immediate Harm effect structure.

Conceptually:

Governed Harm → Effect → Downstream Consequence

The consequence becomes a candidate for boundary assessment.

  • Immediate vs Downstream Effect


HEBM governs the effects belonging to Harm.

HDBM focuses on the point at which consequences extend beyond the
immediate effect structure.

The distinction may not always be physically or temporally obvious.

The governing question is:

Does sufficient Harm continuity remain?

  • Harm Continuity


Downstream inclusion requires a materially supportable continuity
relationship with the governed Harm.

Continuity may be supported through combinations of:

subject continuity

effect continuity

temporal continuity

dependency continuity

domain continuity

system relationship

operational relationship

propagation structure

composite relationship

No single factor is universally sufficient.

  • Boundary Continuity ≠ General Causation


HDBM may require sufficient association to determine whether a consequence
remains inside the Harm Boundary.

It does not establish general causation.

Therefore:

Downstream Boundary Continuity ≠ Final Causal Attribution

  • Direct Downstream Consequence


A downstream consequence may remain closely connected to the original

  • Multi-Step Downstream Chain


Harm may develop through several stages:

Harm → Effect A → Consequence B → Consequence C

The existence of multiple steps does not automatically break Harm

But each additional step may require stronger boundary analysis.

Canonical principle:

Chain Length Alone Does Not Determine Harm Continuity

  • Propagation Governance vs Boundary Governance


This distinction is critical:

Therefore:

Downstream Boundary ≠ Harm Propagation Governance

  • Use Case 1 — Harm Continues Beyond the Original Subject and Event


A physical Harm affects Subject A.

The originating mechanism stops.

The resulting impairment prevents Subject A from performing a critical
dependent function, producing a materially connected effect on Subject B.

HDBM evaluates whether Subject B's consequence remains part of the
original Harm lifecycle or whether a materially independent Harm has
emerged.

It does not assume:

New Subject = New Harm

nor:

Related Consequence = Same Harm

The governing principle is:

Downstream continuity must be established, not presumed.

  • Governance Logic


HDBM applies the following logic:

Has HEBM established the Harm Effect Boundary?

If no:

→ do not finalize the Downstream Boundary.

If yes:

→ identify downstream consequence candidates.

Is the consequence materially continuous with the governed Harm?

If strongly supported:

→ include it within the Downstream Boundary.

If plausible but unresolved:

→ maintain Provisional or Uncertain state.

If continuity is insufficient:

→ exclude it from this Harm Boundary.

Has an independent intervening condition appeared?

→ assess potential Boundary Break.

Has a materially independent Harm emerged?

→ establish Separate Harm Candidate while preserving the relationship to
the original Harm.

Does the consequence occur later, elsewhere, in another domain, system,
organization, jurisdiction, or subject?

→ do not treat that transition as a Boundary Break by itself.

Does the consequence arise from several upstream effects collectively?

→ assess Composite Downstream Continuity.

Has the originating mechanism stopped?

→ do not terminate Harm automatically.

Is inclusion based only on correlation or remote association?

→ prevent Boundary Inflation.

Is exclusion based only on administrative, technical, organizational,
temporal, or claim boundaries?

→ prevent Boundary Suppression.

Has new information changed the downstream structure?

→ expand, contract, reclassify, reopen, or correct while preserving
history.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HDBM — Harm Downstream Boundary Module governs how far a recognized
Harm legitimately extends through downstream effects, subjects, domains,
systems, dependencies, locations, temporal stages, propagation
relationships, composite structures, and distributed consequences. It
governs downstream continuity, inclusion, exclusion, Boundary Breaks,
intervening conditions, separate Harm candidacy, Harm chains, composite
and distributed downstream structures, uncertainty, dispute, expansion,
contraction, reclassification, reopening, and historical Downstream
Boundary continuity. It does not govern Harm propagation itself, formally
determine Harm, establish general causation, assign accountability or
responsibility, determine liability, govern claims or compensation, or
replace operational reality, evidence, validation, integrity, identity, AI
behavior, autonomous execution, simulation, Value, or tax-event
governance.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm must not stop merely because an incident, system, organization,
session, jurisdiction, or producing mechanism has ended. But neither may
Harm expand indefinitely through every consequence that can somehow be
connected to it.

HDBM governs the line between those two failures.

It preserves Harm where material continuity remains and establishes a
Boundary Break where a genuinely separate Harm lifecycle begins.

The downstream boundary of Harm ends where the Harm itself ends — not
where the systems surrounding it find it convenient to stop following the
consequences.
