HDEM — Harm Determination Evaluation Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HDEM — Harm Determination Evaluation Module governs how the Governed
Harm Determination Input Set is evaluated against the Governed Harm
Determination Criteria Set to establish what the complete governed basis
supports, does not support, contradicts, leaves uncertain, or cannot yet
establish about the recognized and bounded Harm.

HDEM governs criterion application, supporting and contradictory basis
evaluation, composite and distributed evaluation, uncertainty,
sufficiency, confidence, alternative interpretations, evaluation
limitations, reassessment, and evaluation traceability without itself
assigning the final Harm Determination State.

HDEM establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Determination Input Set + Governed Harm Determination
Criteria Set → Criterion Application → Basis Evaluation → Sufficiency
Assessment → Evaluation Conclusion → Governed Harm Determination
Evaluation

Its central question is:

What does the complete governed basis establish when the qualified Harm
Determination inputs are evaluated against the applicable Determination
Criteria?

  • Operational Role


HDIM establishes:

what information may legitimately enter Determination.

HDCM-D establishes:

what that information must satisfy.

HDEM establishes:

what the governed evaluation actually shows.

Its operational role is:

to apply the Determination methodology consistently to the complete
governed basis without changing the inputs, criteria, threshold, or Harm
Boundary merely to produce a preferred conclusion.

  • Position Within HDS


The HDS internal flow is:

Qualified Inputs → Determination Criteria → Evaluation → Determination
State → Governed Harm Record

The Core Module sequence is:

HDIM → HDCM-D → HDEM → HDSM → HDRM

Governed Harm Determination Input Set

and:

Governed Harm Determination Criteria Set

It produces:

Governed Harm Determination Evaluation

for HDSM.

  • Foundational Evaluation Principle


The foundational rule is:

Evaluation must follow the governed basis and criteria wherever they lead.

Therefore:

Preferred Outcome ≠ Evaluation Method

and:

Evaluation Result ≠ Predetermined Conclusion

  • Evaluation Readiness


HDEM should begin substantive evaluation only where:

the Harm object is sufficiently identified

the Governed Harm Boundary is available

the Determination Input Set is ready or appropriately qualified

and:

the applicable Determination Criteria Set is ready

Where a material prerequisite is absent, HDEM should preserve:

Not Ready

rather than manufacture an evaluation.

Each applicable criterion must be evaluated against the inputs relevant to
that criterion.

A criterion may be:

Satisfied

Partially Satisfied

Not Satisfied

Unable to Evaluate

Not Applicable

or:

Disputed

The basis for the state must remain traceable.

  • Criterion Satisfaction ≠ Final Determination


A criterion-level result does not automatically establish the complete
Harm Determination.

Therefore:

Criterion Satisfaction ≠ Harm Determination State

HDEM evaluates the complete criteria structure before handing the result
to HDSM.

  • Complete-Basis Evaluation


HDEM must evaluate the complete materially relevant basis rather than only
the most convenient inputs.

This includes:

supporting information

contradictory information

limitations

missing information

uncertainty

source dependencies

composite relationships

and:

material alternative interpretations

Canonical principle:

A legitimate evaluation must be capable of seeing both what supports the
conclusion and what challenges it.

  • Supporting Basis


Supporting inputs should be mapped to the criteria they support.

HDEM must distinguish:

direct support

indirect support

corroborative support

composite support

and:

conditional support

Supporting information does not become stronger merely through repetition.

  • Contradictory Basis


Contradictory inputs must be evaluated with the same methodological
discipline as supporting inputs.

HDEM must establish:

what is contradicted

how materially

with what qualification

with what scope

and:

what effect the contradiction has on the evaluation

Canonical principle:

Contradiction must be evaluated, not edited away.

  • Evidence Absence and Evidence of Absence


HDEM must preserve the distinction between:

Absence of Evidence

and:

Evidence of Absence

The first may weaken support or create Insufficient Basis.

The second may materially support non-establishment.

Therefore:

No Supporting Evidence ≠ Evidence That Harm Did Not Exist

  • Evaluation of Missing Information


Missing information should affect evaluation according to the criteria
established by HDCM-D.

HDEM should ask:

Was the missing information required?

Could another governed input satisfy the same criterion?

Does the absence materially prevent evaluation?

Does it affect only one Harm dimension?

Missing information must not automatically invalidate the complete
evaluation.

  • Uncertainty Evaluation


HDEM evaluates uncertainty rather than attempting to remove it.

Uncertainty may concern:

subject scope

time

effects

downstream relationships

measurement

source reliability

interpretation

or:

method

Different uncertainty dimensions must remain distinguishable where
materially relevant.

  • Local Uncertainty ≠ Global Uncertainty


Uncertainty concerning one characteristic must not automatically spread to
the complete Harm object.

For example:

exact onset uncertain

may coexist with:

Harm existence strongly supported

Therefore:

Uncertainty in One Dimension ≠ Uncertainty in the Whole Harm

  • Evaluation Confidence


HDEM may assign confidence to:

individual criterion evaluations

specific Harm propositions

or:

the overall evaluation

Confidence must preserve its object.

Canonical distinction:

Evaluation Confidence ≠ Harm Severity

  • Composite Evaluation


HDEM must support situations where no individual input or action
independently satisfies the Determination Criteria.

Conceptually:

Input A + Input B + Input C → Composite Support

The evaluation must consider whether the combined structure becomes
sufficient.

Canonical principle:

Individual Insufficiency ≠ Composite Insufficiency

  • Composite Harm Evaluation


The same principle applies where Harm itself emerges through multiple
individually insufficient conditions.

Conceptually:

Condition A + Condition B + Condition C → Harm

HDEM must evaluate Harm at the level at which it materially exists.

It must not require one isolated Harm-producing action where the governed
Harm is composite.

One agent, system, report, subject, or operational component may contain
insufficient support.

The complete governed structure may nevertheless satisfy the criteria.

Therefore:

Local Insufficiency ≠ Global Insufficiency

HDEM may therefore need to evaluate:

Evidence strength may differ across domains.

  • Alternative Interpretations


Where the same governed basis supports materially different

For each material interpretation, it may assess:

assumptions

limitations

and:

Canonical principle:

A competing explanation must be evaluated where it could materially change
the Harm Determination.

  • Alternative Explanation ≠ Automatic Refutation


The existence of another plausible explanation does not automatically
invalidate the Harm proposition.

Likewise, the existence of a Harm proposition does not automatically
invalidate alternatives.

HDEM evaluates their relative support under the applicable criteria.

  • Assumption Governance


Where an evaluation requires assumptions, they must remain explicit.

An assumption should not silently become:

evidence

or:

Therefore:

Assumption ≠ Established Input

  • Evaluation of Derived Inputs


Derived or aggregated inputs must be evaluated with awareness of:

limitations

Canonical distinction:

Derived Conclusion Quality Cannot Exceed Its Materially Relevant Basis
Without Additional Support.

  • Corroboration Evaluation


HDEM must distinguish genuine independent corroboration from repeated
representations of the same source.

Therefore:

Signal Replication ≠ Independent Corroboration

Several records derived from one underlying source must not automatically
receive the weight of several independent observations.

  • Source Authority


Institutional status may be relevant to qualification but cannot
substitute for evaluation.

Therefore:

Authority Status ≠ Evaluation Outcome

A materially strong input should not be rejected merely because it
conflicts with a more authoritative source.

  • Subject Reports


Subject reports must be evaluated according to the applicable criteria and
qualification.

HDEM must avoid both:

automatic acceptance

automatic dismissal

Canonical distinction:

Subject Report ≠ Determination Conclusion

Supporting and contradictory propositions should be evaluated under

Canonical principle:

The methodology should not demand extraordinary support from one

time

or:

downstream scope

→ HBS should be reopened.

  • Criteria Integrity


HDEM must not modify Determination Criteria because the existing criteria

Where the methodology itself is defective:

→ HDCM-D should be reopened.

Canonical principle:

Evaluation applies criteria; it does not rewrite them after seeing the

  • Sufficient Basis ≠ Complete Knowledge


A determination basis may be sufficient even where some information

Therefore:

Sufficient Basis ≠ Complete Knowledge

An inconclusive evaluation is a governed result, not a methodological

  • Evaluation Conflict


Where materially credible inputs support incompatible conclusions and the

  • Minimum Implementation Framework


A minimum HDEM implementation requires eight capabilities.

  • Criterion Application


Apply the Governed Harm Determination Criteria Set to the relevant
qualified inputs and preserve criterion-level evaluation states.

  • Complete-Basis Evaluation


Evaluate supporting, contradictory, missing, uncertain, derived, and
materially alternative information rather than only evidence favoring one
conclusion.

  • Composite & Distributed Evaluation


Evaluate accumulated, continuous, collective, composite, cross-domain, and
distributed Harm at the level at which the Harm materially exists.

  • Sufficiency & Uncertainty Assessment


Determine whether the governed basis is Sufficient, Partially Sufficient,
Insufficient, Inconclusive, Conflicted, or Unable to Evaluate while
preserving uncertainty by dimension.

  • Alternative & Contradictory Interpretation Governance


Identify and evaluate materially credible competing explanations and
counterevidence.

  • Evaluation Independence & Bias Controls


Prevent preferred outcomes, authority, severity, visibility, financial
exposure, liability, organizational pressure, automation, or downstream
consequences from changing the evaluation methodology.

  • Reassessment & Historical Continuity


Support evaluation correction, reassessment, new inputs, legitimate
criteria changes, Boundary changes, and preservation of prior evaluation
states.

  • Governed Output


Produce a traceable Governed Harm Determination Evaluation suitable for
formal Determination State assignment by HDSM.

A system that merely counts supporting evidence, ignores contradictory
information, or changes its methodology after seeing the likely outcome
does not fully implement HDEM.

  • Use Case 1 — Strong Harm Basis with Uncertain Exact Onset


A recognized and bounded Harm is supported by:

subject information

operational records

measured effects

and:

independent corroboration

The exact Harm onset cannot be established to a single date.

HDEM evaluates Harm existence and temporal precision separately.

The evaluation may conclude:

Harm existence — Criteria Satisfied

Subject scope — Criteria Satisfied

Effect scope — Criteria Satisfied

Exact onset — Unable to Establish Precisely

The complete evaluation can therefore remain sufficient for Harm
Determination without manufacturing temporal precision.

The governing principle is:

Uncertainty about exactly when Harm began does not automatically create
uncertainty about whether Harm existed.

  • Use Case 2 — Composite AI Harm Across Multiple Systems


An AI-mediated operation spans:

Model A

Agent B

Tool C

External System D

and:

physical execution E

No local component independently satisfies the complete Harm Determination
Criteria.

The governed inputs, however, establish a coherent operational sequence
and a materially supported composite Harm effect.

HDEM evaluates the complete structure rather than asking whether one
isolated output alone caused the entire Harm.

The evaluation may therefore establish sufficient composite support even
though each local component remains individually insufficient.

The governing principle is:

If Harm exists at the level of the whole operation, Determination
evaluation must be capable of evaluating the whole operation.

  • Governance Requirements


HDEM requires that:

  • evaluation begin only from governed inputs and governed criteria
  • criterion-level results remain distinct from final Determination
    State
  • supporting and contradictory basis both remain visible
  • absence of evidence remain distinct from evidence of absence
  • missing information affect evaluation according to methodological


significance; - uncertainty remain dimension-specific where material; -
confidence remain distinct from Harm severity; - composite and distributed
support remain evaluable; - local insufficiency not automatically
establish global insufficiency; - collective, accumulated, continuous,
cross-domain, and non-linguistic Harm remain evaluable; - material
alternative interpretations remain visible; - assumptions not silently
become facts; - source repetition remain distinct from independent
corroboration; - subject and machine conclusions not automatically become
Harm conclusions; - outcome, authority, visibility, severity, automation,
and organizational bias remain controlled; - HBS Boundary, HDIM input
qualification, and HDCM-D criteria not be silently rewritten during
evaluation; - sufficiency remain distinct from complete knowledge; -
inconclusive and conflicted evaluations remain legitimate states; -
reassessment and correction preserve historical integrity; - evaluation
remain explainable through its inputs, criteria, uncertainty, and
reasoning structure; - the final output remain separate from formal
Determination State assignment.

  • Governance Logic


HDEM applies the following logic:

Are the Governed Harm Determination Input Set and Governed Harm
Determination Criteria Set ready?

If no:

→ establish Not Ready.

If yes:

→ apply each applicable criterion.

What supports the criterion?

→ identify supporting basis.

What contradicts it?

→ identify contradictory basis.

What is missing or uncertain?

→ preserve the relevant limitation.

Are multiple inputs dependent on the same underlying source?

→ prevent false corroboration.

Are individually insufficient elements collectively material?

→ evaluate composite sufficiency.

Is the Harm distributed across systems, agents, subjects, domains, or
time?

→ evaluate at the materially coherent level.

Could an alternative interpretation materially change the conclusion?

→ evaluate it.

Does uncertainty affect only one characteristic?

→ do not automatically propagate it to the entire Harm object.

Does the complete governed basis satisfy the criteria?

→ establish the appropriate evaluation sufficiency state.

Has evaluation exposed a problem with the Harm Boundary?

→ return the relevant issue to HBS.

Has it exposed an input qualification problem?

→ return it to HDIM.

Has it exposed a defective or incomplete criterion?

→ return it to HDCM-D.

Otherwise:

→ produce the Governed Harm Determination Evaluation for HDSM.

At every stage:

Evaluation ≠ Boundary Rewriting

Evaluation ≠ Input Requalification

Evaluation ≠ Criteria Modification

Sufficiency ≠ Complete Knowledge

Evaluation Conclusion ≠ Final Determination State

  • Canonical Boundary


The HDEM — Harm Determination Evaluation Module governs the application of
the Governed Harm Determination Criteria Set to the Governed Harm
Determination Input Set, including criterion evaluation, supporting and
contradictory basis assessment, missing-information impact, uncertainty,
confidence, composite and distributed evaluation, alternative
interpretations, assumptions, corroboration, sufficiency, conflict,
limitations, reassessment, correction, and historical evaluation
continuity. It produces the Governed Harm Determination Evaluation but
does not qualify the original Determination inputs, establish or rewrite
Determination Criteria, silently modify the Governed Harm Boundary, assign
the formal Harm Determination State, create the final Governed Harm
Record, establish general causation, assign accountability or liability,
or replace evidence, validation, integrity, operational reality, identity,
AI behavior, autonomous execution, simulation, prediction, Value, claim,
or jurisdiction-specific governance.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Evaluation is where a Harm Determination methodology proves whether it is
genuinely governed or merely designed to justify a conclusion.

Every material supporting fact, contradiction, uncertainty, limitation,
and alternative explanation must be allowed to affect the evaluation
according to the same methodology.

HDEM does not ask which conclusion is easiest, safest, most convenient, or
most desirable. It asks what the complete governed basis actually
supports.

A legitimate Harm evaluation follows the evidence and criteria wherever
they lead.
