HAHM — Harm Appearance Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HAHM — Harm Appearance Module governs how a materially developing Harm
formation process becomes manifested, detectable, observable, inferable,
experienced, functionally expressed, or otherwise apparent within governed
reality before the final Harm Transition is established.

HAHM governs the first materially supportable appearance of Harm-relevant
effect without equating appearance, detection, visibility, reporting, or
observation with formal Harm Emergence by default.

HAHM establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Emergence Condition Set → Harm-Relevant Manifestation →
Appearance Qualification → Appearance State → Harm Transition Readiness

Its central question is:

How does a materially developing Harm formation process become apparent in
governed reality, and what does that appearance legitimately establish?

  • Operational Role


HEMM maps the formation pathway.

HDHM governs how that pathway develops.

HCHM governs the conditions under which Harm may become materially
sufficient.

HAHM governs how that developing harmful reality first becomes apparent.

Appearance may occur through:

  • observable effect
  • experienced effect
  • functional impairment
  • physical manifestation
  • digital manifestation
  • behavioral consequence
  • system degradation
  • environmental effect
  • organizational impairment
  • collective effect
  • inferred manifestation
  • delayed discovery
  • reconstructed historical manifestation


Its operational role is:

to distinguish the appearance of a Harm-relevant consequence from the
later governance determination that Harm has legitimately transitioned
into an existing Harm state.

The foundational distinction is:

Harm Appearance ≠ Harm Recognition

A harmful effect may become real before anybody formally recognizes it.

It may also become apparent to one subject while remaining unrecognized by
an organization, institution, system, or decision-maker.

Harm Emergence.

However:

where additional governance criteria remain unresolved.

  • Appearance Trigger


HAHM begins where the governed formation pathway produces a materially

inferred, reconstructed, or otherwise represented as an appearance of

Conceptually:

  • Direct Appearance


Direct Appearance occurs where the Harm-relevant effect becomes apparent
without material interpretive intermediaries.

Conceptually:

Formation → Observable Effect

Examples may include:

visible physical damage

immediate functional loss

directly measurable system failure

immediate environmental change

Directness does not eliminate the need for governance.

  • Indirect Appearance


Indirect Appearance occurs where the Harm-relevant effect becomes apparent
through an intermediate consequence.

Conceptually:

Formation → Intermediate Effect → Apparent Harm-Relevant Consequence

The intermediate relationship must remain explicit.

  • Experienced Appearance


A subject may experience a Harm-relevant effect before objective
measurement is available.

Examples may include:

pain

loss of access

functional restriction

cognitive effect

service deprivation

perceived impairment

Subject experience is governed evidence of appearance where relevant.

But:

Experience ≠ Formal Harm Determination

  • Observed Appearance


Observed Appearance exists where a materially relevant effect is directly
observed by a person, system, sensor, process, or governed mechanism.

Observation must preserve:

who or what observed;

what was observed;

when;

under what conditions;

with what uncertainty.

  • Detected Appearance


Some Harm-relevant effects are not directly visible but are detectable
through instrumentation, monitoring, diagnostics, telemetry, analytics, or
other technical means.

Therefore:

Invisible ≠ Non-Existing

  • Inferred Appearance


A Harm-relevant effect may be inferred from sufficiently governed
indicators even where direct observation is unavailable.

Conceptually:

Observed Indicators → Supported Inference → Harm-Relevant Appearance

Inference must remain distinguishable from direct observation.

Inferred Appearance ≠ Observed Appearance

  • Reconstructed Appearance


A Harm-relevant manifestation may be discovered retrospectively.

Conceptually:

Past Development → Later Evidence → Historical Reconstruction → Earlier
Harm Appearance

The fact that appearance is reconstructed later does not mean it first
existed at the moment of discovery.

  • Delayed Appearance

Conceptually:

  • Post-Exposure Appearance

A Harm-relevant effect may appear gradually.

Minor Manifestation → Increasing Manifestation → Material Appearance

HAHM must not force a single appearance moment where reality supports an
appearance interval.

  • Sudden Appearance


Some harmful effects become apparent abruptly.

Conceptually:

No Apparent Effect → Immediate Material Appearance

Sudden appearance may coincide closely with Harm Transition but must
remain methodologically distinct until HTHM completes the transition.

  • Continuous Appearance


An effect may remain continuously apparent over a period.

Conceptually:

Appearance Start → Continuous Appearance → Appearance Continuation

Continuity may be materially important to downstream Harm Transition.

  • Intermittent Appearance


Some effects appear and disappear.

Conceptually:

underlying formation process.

Intermittent Appearance ≠ Intermittent Harm by Default

A Harm-relevant manifestation may recur.

The governance model must distinguish these possibilities.

Reduced Appearance ≠ Reduced Harm Development by Default

Reduced Appearance ≠ Harm Mitigation

  • Canonical Boundary


The HAHM — Harm Appearance Module governs how a materially developing Harm
formation pathway becomes manifested, observable, detectable, experienced,
inferable, reconstructable, functionally expressed, or otherwise apparent
within governed reality. It governs direct, indirect, latent, delayed,
progressive, sudden, continuous, intermittent, recurring, hidden,
suppressed, compensated, collective, multi-subject, composite,
distributed, cross-domain, and historically reconstructed Harm Appearance,
including its timing, scope, visibility, uncertainty, conflict,
reassessment, and historical continuity. It does not itself establish the
final Harm Transition, govern Harm Recognition, complete Harm Boundary
formation, formal Harm Determination, general causation, accountability,
responsibility, liability, Harm State, Harm propagation, mitigation,
recovery, resolution, operational execution, evidence governance,
validation, integrity, identity, simulation, Value lifecycle, or tax-event
determination.

HEMM asks:

Through what pathway may Harm be forming?

HDHM asks:

How is that formation pathway developing?

HCHM asks:

Under what conditions can that development become sufficient for Harm
Emergence?

HAHM asks:

How has the Harm-relevant consequence become apparent in governed reality?

HTHM next asks:

Has the governed reality now legitimately crossed into Existing Harm?

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm can become real before it becomes visible, visible before it becomes
recognized, recognized before it becomes formally determined, and formally
determined long before accountability or liability is resolved.

HAHM governs the appearance layer so the moment a harmful consequence
becomes materially apparent is neither confused with the moment somebody
notices it nor prematurely converted into a final Harm determination.

What becomes visible is not necessarily when Harm began — but once Harm
becomes apparent, governance must be able to represent exactly what
appeared, where, to whom, when, and with what support.
