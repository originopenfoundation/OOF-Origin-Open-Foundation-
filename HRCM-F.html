Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HRCM-F — Harm Resolution Criteria Module governs how the criteria
required for legitimate Harm Resolution are defined, qualified, applied,
satisfied, maintained, and reassessed against the current governed Harm
condition.

HRCM-F determines what must be materially true before a Harm Resolution
State can legitimately support Resolution, which criteria are mandatory or
conditional for the governed Harm, whether those criteria have been
sufficiently satisfied, what unresolved conditions remain, how uncertainty
affects the determination, and whether the established Resolution basis is
sufficiently complete to support Harm lifecycle Closure.

It establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Resolution State → Resolution Criteria Formation → Criteria
Applicability → Criteria Evaluation → Criteria Satisfaction → Resolution
Determination → Governed Harm Resolution Criteria State

Its central question is:

What must be sufficiently true for the governed Harm to be legitimately
resolved?

  • Operational Role


HRSM-F establishes:

the current Resolution State of the Harm.

HRCM-F establishes:

the conditions that must be satisfied for that Harm to legitimately
qualify as resolved.

Its operational role is:

to convert Resolution from a general status into a criteria-based
governance determination grounded in the actual Harm lifecycle.

The foundational principle is:

Resolution is criteria-based, not disappearance-based.

  • Position Within HRFS


The HRFS governance flow is:

Resolution State → Resolution Criteria → Closure → Reopening → Finality

Resolution Criteria

  • Governed Object


The primary governed object is:

Criteria for Legitimate Harm Resolution

HRCM-F does not ask merely:

Has Harm disappeared?

It asks:

What conditions must be satisfied for this governed Harm lifecycle to
legitimately enter Resolution?

This allows Resolution to remain applicable across very different Harm
conditions without imposing a universal requirement of complete
restoration or zero Residual Harm.

Some criteria may be mandatory for a particular governed Harm.

Failure of a mandatory criterion prevents legitimate Resolution.

Mandatory Criterion Unsatisfied → Resolution Not Established

Some criteria apply only where a specified condition exists.

Conditional Criterion ≠ Universal Criterion

decreasing

Where Harm remains actively:

emerging

accumulating

compounding

propagating

or:

cascading

Resolution ordinarily lacks sufficient basis unless the governed

Canonical principle:

  • Containment Criterion


Where continuing expansion or propagation was materially relevant,
Resolution Criteria may require sufficient Containment.

However:

Containment Achieved ≠ Resolution Achieved

Containment may be one condition supporting Resolution.

It does not replace the Resolution determination.

  • Mitigation Criterion


Where existing Harm required material reduction, the Resolution basis may
require a sufficient Mitigation condition.

But:

Mitigation Verified ≠ Resolution Established

HRCM-F evaluates whether the Mitigation condition is sufficient for
Resolution in combination with the other applicable criteria.

  • Recovery Criterion


Recovery may be a materially relevant Resolution Criterion.

The criterion must correspond to the legitimate Recovery condition
required for the Harm.

It must not automatically require:

return to the pre-Harm state

or:

complete restoration

Therefore:

Recovery Criterion Satisfied ≠ Pre-Harm State Restored

  • Partial Recovery and Resolution Criteria


Partial Recovery may satisfy the applicable Recovery Criterion where the
legitimate Resolution model does not require greater Recovery.

Therefore:

Partial Recovery ≠ Resolution Criterion Failure by Default

The criterion must be determined by the Harm governance requirement rather
than by an assumption that Recovery must always be complete.

  • Residual Harm Criterion


Where Residual Harm exists, HRCM-F determines what must be true about that
Harm before Resolution can be legitimate.

The criterion may require that Residual Harm be sufficiently:

identified

bounded

represented

stable or otherwise understood

monitored where necessary

and:

compatible with Resolution

Therefore:

Residual Harm Exists ≠ Resolution Criterion Failed

  • Residual Harm Absence Is Not Universal


HRCM-F explicitly rejects:

Residual Harm = 0

as a universal Resolution requirement.

Some Harm may legitimately resolve while Residual Harm remains.

Therefore:

No Residual Harm ≠ Universal Resolution Criterion

  • Continuing Harm Criterion


Where Harm continues materially, the Resolution basis must determine
whether that continuing Harm is:

compatible with Resolution

incompatible with Resolution

or:

insufficiently understood

A Resolution claim cannot simply ignore continuing Harm.

  • Stability Criterion


Resolution may require sufficient stability of the post-Harm condition.

The applicable stability period or basis must be proportionate to the
Harm.

Therefore:

Momentary Stability ≠ Sufficient Stability by Default

and:

Long Stability ≠ Resolution by Itself

available

sufficient

maintainable

monitored

or:

Therefore:

Hidden Dependency ≠ Legitimate Unconditional Resolution

Where Resolution depends on continuing conditions, HRCM-F may establish

Conditional Resolution

Conceptually:

Canonical principle:

A conditional Resolution must expose the conditions on which its

Some Harm may require continued observation after Resolution.

Monitoring may be appropriate where:

latent Harm remains materially relevant

Recovery stability remains important

  • Uncertainty Criterion


Perfect certainty is not required for Resolution.

But materially relevant uncertainty must be sufficiently bounded.

HRCM-F must distinguish:

acceptable bounded uncertainty

from:

uncertainty that prevents legitimate Resolution

Canonical principle:

Resolution requires sufficient governance, not impossible certainty.

  • Evidence Sufficiency


Resolution Criteria may require sufficient supporting evidence.

But HRCM-F does not itself become an evidence architecture.

ADIT® governs evidence.

Resolution Criteria evaluation.

Therefore:

Evidence Available ≠ Criterion Satisfied

Therefore:

Validated Basis ≠ Resolution Criterion Satisfaction by Default

Resolution Criteria must correspond to actual post-Harm reality.

Therefore:

Declared Condition ≠ Operational Condition

  • External Criteria Boundary


Legal, contractual, insurance, organizational, or administrative systems
may establish their own closure or settlement criteria.

Those criteria may be relevant.

But they do not automatically become HAGA™ Harm Resolution Criteria.

Therefore:

External Closure Criterion ≠ Harm Resolution Criterion by Default

  • Accountability Boundary


Resolution Criteria must not require accountability determination unless
that condition is genuinely necessary to the Harm Resolution object.

Ordinarily:

Accountability ≠ Harm Resolution

Therefore:

Accountability Criterion Satisfied ≠ Harm Resolution Criterion Satisfied

  • Liability Boundary


Liability determination is not a universal Harm Resolution Criterion.

Therefore:

Liability Determined ≠ Harm Resolved

and:

Liability Unresolved ≠ Harm Unresolved by Default

  • Compensation Boundary


Compensation may be materially relevant to some Harm consequences but does

Compensation Paid ≠ Resolution Criterion Satisfied by Default

  • Value Boundary


Value restoration is governed separately from Harm Resolution.

Therefore:

Value Restored ≠ Harm Resolved

Resolution Criteria Satisfied ≠ Closure Completed

  • Canonical Boundary


The HRCM-F — Harm Resolution Criteria Module governs how Harm-specific
Resolution Criteria are formed, classified, scoped, applied, evaluated,
satisfied, integrated, maintained, and reassessed to determine whether a
governed Harm has sufficient basis for legitimate Resolution and
subsequent Closure governance. It governs mandatory and conditional
criteria, Harm condition criteria, Mitigation, Recovery and Residual Harm
criteria, stability, dependencies, monitoring, uncertainty, composite and
distributed criteria, satisfaction states, Resolution sufficiency, and
Closure Readiness. HRCM-F does not itself perform Harm Closure, Reopening
or Finality; independently govern Containment, Mitigation, Recovery or
Residual Harm; determine general causation, accountability,
responsibility, liability, claims, compensation or value consequences; or
substitute legal, administrative, contractual, insurance, audit,
validation, or external closure criteria for governed Harm Resolution
Criteria.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Resolution becomes legitimate only when the conditions required by the
Harm itself are sufficiently satisfied.

Those conditions cannot be replaced by elapsed time, process completion,
administrative closure, favorable averages, compensation, or the simple
absence of an immediately observable harmful effect.

HRCM-F makes the basis of Resolution explicit, scoped, testable, and
reassessable before the Harm lifecycle is allowed to close.

Harm Resolution is not declared because nothing more is being done. It is
established because the conditions required for Resolution are
sufficiently true.
