Canonical Definition

Authority Escalation Validity Integrity Module (AEVIM) defines the
structural conditions under which authority escalation remains
legitimate, justified, recognized, accepted, governable,
accountable, reconstructable, and operationally valid throughout
the escalation lifecycle.

AEVIM governs escalation validity.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine whether an escalation was necessary, appropriate, properly
executed, accepted by the receiving authority, and
operationally valid.

Module Operational Role

AEVIM defines the escalation-validity governance layer of AES.

Its role is to ensure that escalation outcomes remain legitimate and
governance-valid after escalation occurs.

Module Operational Space

AEVIM governs:

  • escalation validity
  • escalation legitimacy
  • escalation justification
  • escalation acceptance
  • escalation recognition
  • escalation enforceability
  • escalation accountability
  • escalation lifecycle governance


The module applies wherever governance requires confirmation that
escalation remained valid.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • legitimate escalation decisions
  • recognized escalation outcomes
  • reconstructable escalation history
  • accountable escalation processes
  • governance-valid escalation pathways
  • operationally reliable escalation outcomes


Its function is to ensure that governance can determine whether
escalation was correctly performed and accepted.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Escalation Validity Object

The organization must define which escalation environments
require validity governance.

This may include:

  • management escalation systems
  • compliance escalation structures
  • safety escalation frameworks
  • contractor governance systems
  • regulatory escalation environments
  • AI governance systems
  • autonomous-agent environments
  • Human-AI governance structures


2. Define Escalation Validity Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which escalation
remains valid.

This includes:

  • legitimacy requirements
  • acceptance requirements
  • justification requirements
  • accountability requirements
  • governance requirements
  • escalation-valid conditions


3. Define Validity Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how escalation-validity failures
are identified.

This may include:

  • unjustified escalation
  • invalid escalation
  • rejected escalation
  • escalation to incorrect authority
  • governance-invalid escalation
  • unrecognized escalation outcomes


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for
escalation-validity failures.

Governance response may include:

  • escalation review
  • escalation verification
  • governance intervention
  • escalation correction
  • reassignment
  • corrective actions
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • escalation decisions
  • escalation justifications
  • escalation acceptance events
  • governance reviews
  • intervention procedures
  • resulting escalation outcomes


An authority-escalation environment must not remain validity-valid
if materially significant escalation activities cannot be
reconstructed, reviewed, validated, accepted, or governed.

Use Case 1 — Organizational Governance

Environment

Scenario

A manager escalates a high-risk operational issue to executive
leadership for resolution.

Application

AEVIM determines whether escalation was justified, correctly
executed, and accepted by the appropriate authority.

Result

Governance gains visibility into the validity of the
escalation process.

Use Case 2 — Autonomous Agent Governance

Environment

Scenario

An AI agent escalates a decision beyond its authority scope to a
supervisory AI system or human operator.

Application

AEVIM validates whether escalation occurred under legitimate
conditions and reached the correct authority level.

Result

Governance gains visibility into the validity of escalation across
intelligent operational systems.

Canonical Closing Statement

Authority Escalation Validity Integrity Module (AEVIM) defines the
structural conditions under which authority escalation remains
legitimate, justified, recognized, accepted, governable,
accountable, reconstructable, and operationally valid throughout
the escalation lifecycle.

Escalation may occur, but not every escalation is valid. Authority
escalation validity integrity therefore becomes a foundational
condition of trustworthy escalation governance.
