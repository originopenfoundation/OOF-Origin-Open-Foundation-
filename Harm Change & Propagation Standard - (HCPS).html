  • Canonical Definition


The Harm Change & Propagation Standard (HCPS) governs how existing Harm
changes after it has become a formally governed Harm object, including how
Harm increases, decreases, accumulates, compounds, propagates across
subjects, objects, systems, domains, locations, and boundaries, and
produces cascading or downstream Harm.

HCPS establishes the methodological structure required to distinguish
ordinary State variation from material Harm change and to determine
whether existing Harm is remaining local, accumulating within its existing
scope, becoming more severe through compounding conditions, propagating
into additional affected spaces, or generating consequential Harm beyond
its original manifestation.

HCPS governs the dynamics of Harm change.

It does not merely ask:

What State is the Harm in?

It asks:

What is happening to the Harm, through what change pattern, across what
scope, and with what downstream consequences?

  • Operational Role


HSS governs the current State of Harm.

HCPS governs the dynamics that may cause that State, scope, structure, or
downstream consequence to change.

Its operational role is:

to identify, distinguish, govern, and preserve the material pathways
through which existing Harm changes, accumulates, compounds, propagates,
or cascades after formal Harm Determination.

HCPS creates the transition:

Governed Harm State → Harm Change Identification → Harm Accumulation →
Harm Compounding → Harm Propagation → Harm Cascading → Governed Harm
Change & Propagation State

  • Core Question


How does existing Harm change, accumulate, compound, spread, cascade, or
create downstream Harm?

Harm Change is a material alteration in the governed Harm or one of its
materially relevant dimensions.

Change may involve:

intensity

duration

frequency

scope

effect

manifestation

affected subjects

affected objects

domain

location

or:

functional consequence

Not every variation constitutes material Harm Change.

Therefore:

Variation ≠ Material Harm Change

Increasing Harm

Decreasing Harm

Stable Harm Dynamics

Directionally Uncertain Harm

For example:

Active Harm + Increasing Dynamic

may later support:

Escalating State

Material Harm Change may differ substantially in magnitude.

HCPS should support proportional representation of:

minor change

material change

major change

Magnitude must not be confused with overall Harm severity.

Therefore:

Change Magnitude ≠ Harm Severity

Harm may change:

non-linearly

  • Accumulation


Harm Accumulation occurs where Harm increases through repeated,
continuing, or additive contributions over time.

Conceptually:

Harm₁ + Contribution₁ + Contribution₂ + Contribution₃ → Accumulated Harm₁

Examples may include repeated:

exposure

financial loss

physical stress

digital disruption

environmental contamination

or:

cognitive impact

Canonical principle:

Individually Limited Contributions May Produce Material Accumulated Harm.

  • Accumulation Without a Single Dominant Event


Accumulated Harm may exist without any single contribution being
sufficient to explain the complete Harm.

Therefore:

No Single Dominant Harm-Producing Event ≠ No Accumulated Harm

This is particularly important for:

continuous

repeated

distributed

and:

long-duration

Harm.

  • Compounding


Harm Compounding occurs where existing Harm interacts with additional
conditions or Harm effects in a manner that produces a materially greater
consequence than simple addition alone would represent.

Conceptually:

Harm A + Condition B → Harm Effect > Independent Contribution of A + B

Compounding may occur across:

subjects

effects

domains

systems

or:

time

  • Accumulation ≠ Compounding


These dynamics must remain distinct.

Accumulation concerns additive or continuing growth.

Compounding concerns interaction that materially changes or amplifies the
consequence.

Therefore:

Repeated Harm ≠ Compounded Harm by Default

and:

Multiple Harm Factors ≠ Compounding by Default

The interaction must matter.

  • Propagation


Harm Propagation occurs where existing Harm extends beyond its prior
affected scope through a materially traceable Harm pathway.

Propagation may occur across:

additional subjects

objects

systems

locations

domains

organizations

infrastructure

environments

or:

populations

Conceptually:

Harm₁ / Scope A → Propagation Pathway → Scope B

  • Propagation Without Continuing Origin Activity


Harm may continue propagating after the mechanism that originally produced
it has stopped.

Examples may include:

persistent contamination

distributed digital effects

secondary financial consequences

information replication

infrastructure dependency effects

or:

downstream organizational disruption

Canonical principle:

Termination of the originating mechanism does not necessarily terminate
Harm propagation.

  • Propagation Pathway


HCPS should identify the materially relevant pathway through which Harm
propagates.

A pathway may involve:

physical transfer

digital transmission

dependency

resource relationship

behavioral interaction

organizational process

environmental movement

economic relationship

or another Harm-relevant connection.

HCPS governs the Harm pathway sufficiently to govern propagation.

It does not become a general forensic or causation architecture.

  • Propagation Boundary Interaction


Propagation may cause existing Harm to extend beyond its previously
governed Boundary.

Where this occurs:

Propagation Detection → Boundary Reassessment

may be required.

HCPS must not silently expand the Governed Harm Boundary without
appropriate HAGA™ Boundary governance.

Therefore:

Observed Propagation ≠ Automatic Boundary Redefinition

  • Cascading Harm


Harm Cascading occurs where one Harm consequence creates conditions that
generate additional downstream Harm consequences through sequential
dependency or consequence relationships.

Conceptually:

Harm A → Consequence B → Harm C → Consequence D → Harm E

A cascade may cross:

systems

domains

subjects

organizations

infrastructure

or:

societal layers

  • Propagation ≠ Cascading


Propagation extends Harm through a pathway.

Cascading Harm creates sequential downstream Harm relationships.

Therefore:

Spread ≠ Cascade by Default

A Harm may propagate without producing a cascade.

A cascade may involve several distinct but related Harm objects.

  • Downstream Harm


HCPS must distinguish:

expanded manifestation of the same Harm

from:

new downstream Harm produced by the original Harm

For example:

Harm₁ → Downstream Condition → Harm₂

Harm₂ may require its own:

Emergence

Recognition

Boundary

Determination

and:

State

governance.

Canonical principle:

Related Harm ≠ Same Harm by Default

  • Harm Identity Across Change


Material change does not automatically create a new Harm identity.

Conceptually:

Harm₁ / Condition A → Change → Harm₁ / Condition B

But where the downstream consequence becomes methodologically distinct:

Harm₁ → Harm₂

may be required.

HCPS must preserve this distinction.

  • Composite Harm Dynamics


Composite Harm may change through interactions among multiple components.

A component may:

increase

decrease

stabilize

or:

propagate

while the composite Harm behaves differently.

Therefore:

Component Change ≠ Composite Harm Change by Default

HCPS must govern both levels where materially necessary.

  • Collective Harm Dynamics


Collective Harm may change even where individual subject outcomes differ.

For example:

some subjects recover

while:

collective Harm continues expanding

Therefore:

Individual Improvement ≠ Collective Harm Reduction by Default

  • Distributed Harm Dynamics


Harm may develop across distributed:

agents

systems

organizations

locations

platforms

or:

infrastructure

No individual component may represent the complete Harm dynamic.

Canonical principle:

Distributed Harm must remain governable as a coherent Harm reality even
when its change is fragmented across multiple operational environments.

  • Composite Operations and Harm


Harm may emerge or change through the combined effect of multiple
individually insufficient actions.

Therefore:

Safe or Limited Individual Action₁ + Action₂ + Action₃ ≠ Safe Composite
Outcome by Default

Within HAGA™:

Absence of a Single Harm-Producing Action ≠ Absence of Harm Change

HCPS governs the consequence dynamics, not the behavioral permissibility
of the individual actions.

  • Cross-Domain Propagation


Harm may propagate between domains.

For example:

Digital Harm → Financial Harm

Physical Harm → Organizational Harm

Environmental Harm → Health Harm

Cognitive Harm → Societal Harm

Cross-domain transition does not automatically mean the same Harm object
continues unchanged.

HCPS must preserve relationships while invoking new Harm governance where
a distinct downstream Harm forms.

  • Temporal Propagation


Propagation may be:

gradual

intermittent

or:

Therefore:

No Immediate Downstream Effect ≠ No Propagation

later.

  • Propagation and Visibility


The observable portion of Harm may differ from its actual propagation.

Therefore:

Observed Propagation ≠ Complete Propagation by Default

HCPS must preserve uncertainty and known observation limitations.

  • Propagation and Prediction


Prediction may identify potential future propagation.

But:

Predicted Propagation ≠ Actual Propagation

PGA™ may govern the Prediction.

HCPS governs propagation that has become part of the actual governed Harm
reality.

Predictions may inform monitoring and Containment without being silently
converted into actual Harm.

  • Propagation and Simulation


SIMULOS® may simulate:

cascade pathways

or:

downstream effects

But:

Simulated Propagation ≠ Actual Propagation

Simulation may inform governance without replacing actual Harm
determination.

  • Operational Reality Boundary


ORA™ may govern the operational mechanisms and conditions through which

Therefore:

Operational Change ≠ Harm Change by Default

Operational Propagation ≠ Harm Propagation by Default

  • HSS Boundary


HSS and HCPS are directly adjacent but distinct.

HSS governs the State produced by Harm dynamics.

For example:

Canonical distinction:

Harm Change ≠ Harm State Transition

The two may be related without being identical.

  • Governance Requirements


HCPS requires that:

  • existing Harm remain distinguishable from the mechanisms changing
    it
  • material Harm Change remain distinguishable from ordinary
    variation
  • direction, magnitude, velocity, scope, and persistence of change


remain governable; - accumulation remain distinguishable from compounding;
- propagation remain distinguishable from cascading; - downstream Harm
remain distinguishable from expanded manifestation of the same Harm; -
Harm identity remain continuous where the same Harm changes; - new
downstream Harm receive separate lifecycle governance where materially
distinct; - propagation beyond an existing Boundary trigger appropriate
Boundary reassessment; - distributed, composite, collective, and
cross-domain Harm remain governable; - termination of the originating
mechanism not automatically terminate Harm dynamics; - prediction and
simulation remain separate from actual Harm propagation; - Harm Change
remain separate from Harm State Transition; - causation, accountability,
responsibility, and liability remain outside the HCPS determination; -
uncertainty and observation limitations remain explicit; - material Harm
change history remain traceable; - change and propagation outputs support
timely Containment governance.

  • Governance Logic


HCPS applies the following logic:

Does a Governed Harm State exist?

If no:

→ HCPS cannot formally govern Harm change.

If yes:

→ establish the current Harm baseline.

Has the Harm materially changed?

If no:

→ preserve current Harm dynamics subject to monitoring.

If yes:

→ identify the change.

Is the Harm increasing through repeated or continuing contributions?

→ govern Accumulation.

Are interacting conditions materially amplifying the Harm beyond simple
addition?

→ govern Compounding.

Is existing Harm extending into additional materially affected scope?

→ govern Propagation.

Is one Harm consequence generating sequential downstream Harm
consequences?

→ govern Cascading.

Then determine:

Does the change remain within the existing Harm Boundary?

If no:

→ trigger Boundary reassessment.

Does the downstream condition remain part of the same Harm object?

If uncertain:

→ preserve the relationship and invoke appropriate HAGA™ lifecycle
governance.

Has a distinct downstream Harm formed?

If yes:

→ initiate appropriate Emergence, Recognition, Boundary, and Determination
governance for that Harm.

Does the change create an immediate need to prevent further expansion?

If yes:

→ produce a Containment Need for HCOS.

At every stage:

Variation ≠ Material Harm Change

Accumulation ≠ Compounding

Propagation ≠ Cascading

Related Harm ≠ Same Harm

Prediction ≠ Actual Propagation

Simulation ≠ Actual Propagation

Harm Change ≠ Harm State Transition

Cause Stopped ≠ Harm Dynamics Stopped

  • Canonical Boundary


The Harm Change & Propagation Standard (HCPS) governs the material
dynamics through which an already governed Harm changes, increases,
decreases, accumulates, compounds, propagates, crosses materially relevant
scope, produces downstream consequences, or creates cascading Harm
relationships. It governs the Harm consequence dynamics sufficiently to
preserve identity, scope, trajectory, relationships, uncertainty, and
Containment readiness. HCPS does not determine whether Harm originally
exists, perform Recognition or Determination, independently redefine the
Governed Harm Boundary, classify the current Harm State, replace State
Transition governance, perform Containment or Mitigation, govern Recovery
or Residual Harm determination, establish Resolution or Finality,
determine general causation, assign accountability, responsibility, or
liability, govern claims or compensation, or replace operational reality,
evidence, validation, integrity, identity, AI behavior, autonomous
execution, prediction, simulation, Value, tax-event, or
jurisdiction-specific governance.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm does not remain confined to the form, place, subject, system, or
moment in which it first became visible.

It may accumulate through repeated contributions, compound through
interacting conditions, propagate beyond its original scope, and generate
downstream Harm after the originating mechanism has already stopped.

HCPS governs those dynamics without confusing change with State,
propagation with causation, or downstream consequence with automatic
identity continuity.

Harm governance must follow consequence wherever consequence materially
changes, spreads, compounds, or continues.
