About the Harm Determination Standard (HDS)

Standard: Harm Determination Standard (HDS) Architecture: HAGA™ — Harm
Governance Architecture Governed Space: Harm Determination

What It Governs

The Harm Determination Standard (HDS) governs whether recognized and
bounded Harm can legitimately be formally established as a governed Harm
object and what Harm has been established.

It governs the transition from:

Recognized and Bounded Harm → Qualified Inputs → Determination Criteria →
Evaluation → Determination State → Governed Harm Record

HDS provides the methodological point at which Harm moves from being
recognized for governance to being formally determined. Why It Exists

Harm may exist before it can be formally established.

Recognition may be sufficient to require governance while the available
basis remains insufficient for Determination. Conversely, incomplete
information or unresolved uncertainty must not automatically be
interpreted as proof that no Harm exists.

HDS exists to prevent two opposite failures:

determining Harm without sufficient governed basis

and:

treating the inability to determine Harm as proof that Harm does not
exist.

Its foundational distinction is:

Recognition ≠ Determination

And equally:

Insufficient Basis ≠ No Harm How It Works

HDS receives the:

Governed Harm Recognition State

from HRS and the:

Governed Harm Boundary

from HBS.

It then governs:

Qualified Inputs → Determination Criteria → Evaluation → Determination
State → Governed Harm Record

Qualified inputs are evaluated against explicit Determination Criteria
rather than against an outcome selected in advance.

The resulting Determination State may be:

Established

Partially Established

Pending

Insufficient Basis

or:

Not Established

Where Harm is established, HDS produces a traceable:

Governed Harm Record

That record becomes the governed Harm object used by the next HAGA™ layer.
What It Prevents

HDS prevents shortcuts such as:

Recognition = Determined Harm

Report = Determined Harm

Machine Detection = Determined Harm

Institutional Acknowledgement = Determined Harm

Institutional Non-Acknowledgement = No Harm

Insufficient Evidence = No Harm

Uncertainty = No Harm

No Single Harm-Producing Action = No Harm

Local Insufficiency = Global Insufficiency

Operational Failure = Harm

Unsafe AI Behavior = Harm

Value Loss = Harm

Determined Harm = Accountability

Determined Harm = Liability

It also prevents the opposite error of lowering Determination requirements
simply because Harm is severe, visible, politically significant,
financially consequential, or institutionally important. Relationship to
HAGA™

HDS occupies the transition between Harm Boundary and Harm State:

Harm Emergence → Harm Recognition → Harm Boundary → Harm Determination →

Harm State

HEMS asks:

When and how did Harm begin to exist?

HRS asks:

When and on what basis did that Harm become recognized for governance?

HBS asks:

What belongs to that governed Harm and what remains outside it?

HDS asks:

Can that Harm legitimately be formally determined to exist, and what
governed Harm has been established?

HSS then asks:

What is the current governed state of that Harm?

This separation prevents Recognition, Boundary, Determination, and State
from collapsing into one decision. Use Case 1 — Harm Is Recognized but the
Determination Basis Is Incomplete

Multiple credible signals establish sufficient basis to recognize possible
Harm and initiate governance.

HRS therefore produces a Governed Harm Recognition State, and HBS
establishes the currently supportable Harm Boundary.

However, material information required for formal Determination remains
unresolved.

HDS does not force a binary conclusion.

Instead, it may establish:

Pending

or:

Insufficient Basis

while preserving the recognized Harm object for continued governance.

The governing principle is:

Failure to reach Harm Determination does not prove that Harm does not
exist. Use Case 2 — Distributed Harm Is Established Through a Composite
Basis

An AI-mediated operation is distributed across multiple agents, sessions,
tools, systems, and interactions.

No individual action or evidence item independently establishes the
complete Harm.

When the governed inputs are evaluated together, however, they establish a
materially coherent Harm affecting the bounded subjects, period, and
effects.

HDS evaluates the complete basis rather than requiring one independently
sufficient Harm-producing action.

Where the applicable criteria are satisfied, it establishes the Harm and
creates the Governed Harm Record.

The governing principle is:

Individual insufficiency does not establish composite insufficiency.

What It Does Not Govern

HDS does not determine:

when Harm originally emerged

when Harm became recognized

what belongs within the Harm Boundary

the current Harm State

how Harm propagates

how Harm should be contained or mitigated

how recovery occurs

whether Residual Harm remains

when Harm reaches finality

It also does not establish general causation beyond what is necessary for
Harm Determination, assign accountability or responsibility, determine
legal liability, establish compensation or claim entitlement, or replace
evidence, validation, integrity, operational reality, identity, AI
behavior, autonomous execution, simulation, prediction, Value, tax-event,
or jurisdiction-specific governance.

Why It Matters

Without a dedicated Determination layer, governance tends to collapse into
one of two extremes.

Either Harm is treated as established as soon as it is reported or
recognized, or Harm is treated as nonexistent whenever complete proof is
unavailable.

Both approaches distort reality.

HDS provides the methodological layer between them.

It allows uncertainty, contradiction, incomplete information, distributed
evidence, and composite Harm to remain governable while still requiring an
explicit basis before Harm becomes formally established.

Most importantly, it preserves the distinction between:

what may exist in reality

and:

what governance can currently establish.

Closing Statement

Harm should not become formally established merely because it has been
alleged, reported, recognized, or feared. Nor should Harm disappear merely
because governance has not yet accumulated enough basis to establish it.

HDS governs that critical boundary.

Recognition brings Harm into explicit governance. Determination
establishes what Harm can legitimately be said to have been established.
