Canonical Definition

Responsibility Traceability Validity Integrity Module (RTVIM)
defines the structural conditions under which responsibility-history
relationships, traceability chains, responsibility records,
reconstruction pathways, traceability decisions, traceability
validations, and responsibility-governance activities remain
legitimate, recognized, reviewable, reconstructable, auditable,
governable, enforceable, and operationally valid throughout
the responsibility lifecycle.

RTVIM governs responsibility-traceability validity.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine whether responsibility traceability remains legitimate,
whether reconstructed responsibility chains remain trustworthy, and
whether responsibility-history records remain suitable for
governance, audit, investigation, accountability, and
evidentiary use.

Module Operational Role

RTVIM defines the traceability-validity governance layer of RTS.

Its role is to ensure that responsibility reconstruction remains
legitimate and governance-valid throughout the
responsibility lifecycle.

Module Operational Space

RTVIM governs:

  • traceability validity
  • reconstruction legitimacy
  • responsibility-history validity
  • traceability-chain legitimacy
  • reconstruction reliability
  • governance-valid traceability
  • traceability auditability
  • traceability lifecycle governance


The module applies wherever governance requires confirmation that
responsibility traceability remains valid.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • legitimate traceability chains
  • recognized reconstruction pathways
  • reliable responsibility-history records
  • governance-valid traceability states
  • auditable traceability structures
  • operationally trustworthy reconstruction


Its function is to ensure that governance can determine whether
reconstructed responsibility history remains legitimate, reliable,
and suitable for governance use.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Traceability Validity Object

The organization must define which traceability environments
require validity governance.

This may include:

  • organizational structures
  • contractor networks
  • governance frameworks
  • compliance systems
  • audit environments
  • investigation environments
  • AI governance systems
  • Human-AI operational environments


2. Define Traceability Validity Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which responsibility
traceability remains valid.

This includes:

  • legitimacy requirements
  • reconstruction requirements
  • auditability requirements
  • reliability requirements
  • governance requirements
  • traceability-valid conditions


3. Define Validity Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how traceability-validity failures
are identified.

This may include:

  • unreliable reconstruction pathways
  • invalid responsibility-history records
  • illegitimate traceability relationships
  • governance-invalid traceability structures
  • traceability-chain corruption
  • responsibility-history inconsistencies


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for
traceability-validity failures.

Governance response may include:

  • traceability review
  • reconstruction verification
  • governance intervention
  • traceability reassessment
  • corrective actions
  • traceability validation
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • responsibility-history records
  • traceability validations
  • governance reviews
  • audit activities
  • intervention procedures
  • resulting traceability states


A responsibility-traceability environment must not remain
validity-valid if materially significant traceability relationships
cannot be reconstructed, reviewed, validated, audited, enforced,
recognized, or governed.
