About the Harm Resolution & Finality Standard (HRFS)

Standard: Harm Resolution & Finality Standard (HRFS) Architecture: HAGA™ —
Harm Governance Architecture Governed Space: Harm Resolution & Finality
Governance

What It Governs

The Harm Resolution & Finality Standard (HRFS) governs the final stage of
the governed Harm lifecycle: determining when Harm can legitimately be
considered resolved, when the lifecycle can be closed, when that Closure
must be reopened, and when the Harm lifecycle has reached Finality.

HRFS governs the transition:

Governed Harm Recovery & Residual Harm State → Resolution State →
Resolution Criteria → Closure → Reopening → Finality

Its central distinction is:

Resolution ≠ Closure ≠ Finality

A Harm lifecycle does not end simply because Harm has decreased, Recovery
has occurred, an intervention has ended, or an external process has been
closed. Why It Exists

Harm can remain methodologically unresolved even after the event that
produced it has ended.

A system may be repaired while consequences remain. A person may
substantially recover while Residual Harm persists. An organization may
close an incident while affected subjects remain harmed. Compensation may
be paid while Recovery continues. A legal proceeding may end without
determining whether the Harm itself has reached the end of its lifecycle.

Without a distinct Resolution and Finality layer, these different
conditions can be incorrectly treated as equivalent.

HRFS exists to establish:

when Harm is resolved

when active Harm governance can be closed

what can reopen that Closure

and:

when the lifecycle has legitimately reached its endpoint How It Works

HRFS begins with the:

Governed Harm Recovery & Residual Harm State

produced by the preceding HAGA™ lifecycle stage.

It first determines whether the Harm is ready for Resolution evaluation.

Where it is, applicable Resolution Criteria are evaluated against the
current Harm condition, including materially relevant Recovery, Residual
Harm, continuing Harm, dependencies, stability, and uncertainty.

Where Resolution is sufficiently established, HRFS determines whether the
active Harm lifecycle can be closed.

Closure remains governable through defined Reopening conditions.

If materially relevant Harm reality later changes, the lifecycle can
return to active governance rather than preserving an obsolete Resolution
state.

Only when the applicable lifecycle conditions support completion can HRFS
establish:

Governed Harm Finality State What It Prevents

HRFS prevents premature Harm lifecycle termination based on conditions
such as:

Incident Closed → Harm Resolved

Mitigation Completed → Harm Resolved

Recovery Achieved → Harm Resolved

Compensation Paid → Harm Resolved

Claim Closed → Harm Resolved

Legal Process Ended → Harm Finality

No Immediate Harm Observed → Harm Finality

It also prevents the opposite error: assuming that any remaining Residual
Harm makes Resolution or Finality permanently impossible.

The governing principle is:

Residual Harm may remain without requiring the Harm lifecycle to remain
permanently active. Relationship to HAGA™

HRFS is the twelfth and final Parent Standard of the HAGA™ lifecycle:

Harm Context → Harm Exposure → Harm Emergence → Harm Recognition → Harm
Boundary → Harm Determination → Harm State → Harm Change & Propagation →
Harm Containment → Harm Mitigation → Harm Recovery & Residual Harm → Harm
Resolution & Finality

The preceding standards establish what Harm exists, how it changes, how it
is contained and reduced, what recovers, and what Harm remains.

HRFS answers the final governance question:

When can governed Harm legitimately be considered resolved, and when has
its lifecycle reached finality?

Its principal output is:

Governed Harm Finality State Use Case 1 — Resolution With Residual Harm

A person, system, organization, or infrastructure experiences Harm and
subsequently undergoes Mitigation and Recovery.

Substantial Recovery is achieved, but a stable and well-understood
Residual Harm condition remains.

HRFS does not automatically require:

Residual Harm = Zero

before Resolution can be considered.

Instead, it evaluates whether the remaining Harm is sufficiently governed,
whether applicable Resolution Criteria have been satisfied, whether
relevant dependencies and uncertainties are understood, and whether active
Harm lifecycle progression is still required.

Where those conditions are satisfied, the Harm may legitimately reach
Resolution even though Residual Harm remains.

Residual Harm Exists ≠ Resolution Impossible Use Case 2 — Closed Harm
Lifecycle Must Be Reopened

A Harm lifecycle reaches Resolution and is subsequently closed after
Recovery and Residual Harm conditions appear stable.

Later, previously latent Harm becomes materially observable, Recovery
regresses, or the Residual Harm Boundary expands.

HRFS does not allow the historical Closure to override current Harm
reality.

The lifecycle can be reopened and returned to the appropriate HAGA™
governance stage.

The original Resolution remains part of the lifecycle history, but it no
longer determines the current state.

Closure must remain reversible when the Harm reality supporting it
materially changes.

What It Does Not Govern

HRFS does not determine whether Harm originally exists.

It does not perform Harm Recognition, Harm Determination, Harm Boundary
establishment, Harm Change governance, Containment, Mitigation, Recovery,
or Residual Harm Identification as their primary governance functions.

It does not determine:

general causation

accountability

responsibility

legal liability

claims

compensation

financial settlement

value restoration

or:

jurisdiction-specific legal finality

These may interact with the Harm lifecycle but remain separate governance
objects.

Why It Matters

Without governed Finality, a Harm architecture has no rigorous endpoint.

Ending governance too early can hide continuing, latent, or Residual Harm.

Never allowing governance to end creates the opposite problem: every
historical Harm would remain permanently active simply because some
consequence, uncertainty, or future risk could theoretically remain.

HRFS establishes the methodological boundary between those extremes.

It allows Harm governance to end when the lifecycle has legitimately
reached completion while preserving:

Harm history

Residual Harm

reopening conditions

and:

the distinction between current reality and historical reality

Closing Statement

A Harm lifecycle does not end because an event ended, a case closed,
compensation was paid, Recovery occurred, or Harm became less visible.

It ends when Resolution is legitimate, Closure remains valid against
materially relevant Reopening conditions, and the governed lifecycle has
sufficient basis to reach Finality.

Finality closes active Harm governance. It does not erase Harm history.

HAGA™ governs Harm from exposure to finality.
