Canonical Definition

The HFFM — Harm Finality Formation Module governs how a resolved and
appropriately closed Harm lifecycle reaches legitimate Finality.

HFFM determines whether the complete governed Harm lifecycle has reached a
condition in which no further active HAGA™ lifecycle progression is
presently required on the established basis, whether materially relevant
Resolution, Closure, Residual Harm, dependency, uncertainty, monitoring,
and Reopening conditions have been sufficiently addressed, and whether the
Harm lifecycle can legitimately transition from a closed but still
reopenable governance condition into its terminal governed state.

It establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Closure State + Governed Harm Reopening State → Finality
Readiness → Finality Conditions → Finality Determination → Finality
Formation → Governed Harm Finality State

Its central question is:

When has the governed Harm lifecycle legitimately reached its endpoint?

  • Operational Role


HCLM establishes:

when active Harm lifecycle governance can close.

HROM establishes:

whether that Closure must be reversed through Reopening.

HFFM establishes:

whether the lifecycle itself has now reached legitimate completion.

Its operational role is:

to convert sufficiently durable Harm Resolution and Closure into a
terminal governed lifecycle state without confusing Finality with Harm
disappearance, administrative completion, elapsed time, legal finality, or
historical erasure.

The foundational principle is:

A Harm lifecycle ends through governed Finality, not merely through
disappearance of an immediately observable harmful effect.

The HRFS governance flow is:

Resolution State → Resolution Criteria → Closure → Reopening → Finality

HFFM governs the fifth and final stage:

Finality

Resolution & Finality

Its principal output is:

Governed Harm Finality State

Harm Finality is the terminal governance condition in which the governed
Harm lifecycle has sufficient basis to conclude that no further active
HAGA™ lifecycle progression is presently required.

Finality does not mean:

all Harm disappeared

or:

Therefore:

Harm Finality ≠ Harm Erasure

  • Canonical Boundary


The HFFM — Harm Finality Formation Module governs when a resolved and
legitimately closed Harm lifecycle has sufficient basis to reach its
terminal governed state. It governs Finality Readiness, Finality
Conditions, Residual Harm compatibility with Finality, dependencies,
monitoring, uncertainty, latent Harm, Finality scope, composite and
distributed Finality, Finality Formation, durability, post-Finality Harm
relationships, exceptional reassessment, lifecycle history, Harm identity
preservation, and formation of the Governed Harm Finality State. HFFM does
not erase Harm, require universal zero Harm or complete restoration,
independently perform Recognition, Determination, Containment, Mitigation,
Recovery, Residual Harm Identification, Resolution, Closure, or Reopening,
determine general causation, accountability, responsibility, liability,
claims, compensation or value consequences, or substitute legal,
administrative, insurance, audit, validation, prediction, simulation, or
other external finality for Harm lifecycle Finality.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Finality is not the moment Harm disappears. It is the moment the governed
Harm lifecycle has legitimately completed everything that still requires
active Harm governance.

Residual Harm may remain. Permanent consequences may remain. Historical
evidence may remain. Future risk may remain. Accountability, liability,
claims, compensation, and value consequences may remain governed
elsewhere.

What ends is the active progression of that Harm through HAGA™.

Finality therefore does not erase Harm. It establishes that the Harm
lifecycle itself has reached its legitimate endpoint.

HAGA™ governs Harm from exposure to finality.
