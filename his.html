A. Standard Abstract

The Honey Integrity Standard (HIS) defines the structural
conditions under which honey preserves its natural composition,
biological activity, and origin authenticity throughout its
lifecycle.

The objective is to ensure that:
  • honey remains 100% natural
  • no substances are added or removed
  • biological integrity is preserved
  • processing does not degrade quality
  • origin remains transparent and traceable
 This standard does not redefine honey.
It defines integrity conditions of honey.

B. Canonical Definition

Honey Integrity (HIS) is the condition in which honey remains:
  • compositionally pure
  • biologically active
  • non-degraded by processing
  • traceable to its origin
 Honey must remain in its natural state as produced by bees,
without alteration, dilution, or structural degradation.

C. Structural Base

This standard is based on:
  • Natural Composition Principle
  • Non-Intervention Principle
  • Biological Preservation Principle
  • Origin Transparency Principle
 Honey is not a processed product.
It is a natural biological substance.

D. Scope

This standard applies to:
  • raw honey
  • filtered honey (minimal processing only)
  • direct-to-consumer honey
  • premium and natural honey products
 This standard does not apply to:
  • honey-based products
  • flavored honey
  • honey mixtures or blends


E. Structural Requirements

1. Composition Integrity
Honey MUST:
  • consist of 100% honey
  • contain no additives
  • contain no external sugars or syrups
 2. Non-Dilution Requirement
Honey MUST NOT:
  • be diluted
  • be mixed with water
  • be mixed with syrups or external substances
 3. Non-Blending Requirement (Critical)
Honey MUST NOT:
  • be blended across unknown or mixed origins
  • be labeled ambiguously (e.g. "EU / non-EU blend")
Definition
If honey is blended without full origin transparency:

→ it is not considered integrity-preserved honey

4. Thermal Integrity Requirement
Honey MUST NOT be exposed to temperatures that degrade its
natural biological structure.

This includes:
  • enzyme degradation
  • structural alteration
  • loss of natural activity
Principle
Honey must remain in its natural biological state.

5. Processing Integrity
Allowed processing:
  • natural settling
  • minimal filtration
  • mechanical separation
Not allowed:
  • excessive heating
  • industrial processing altering structure
  • chemical treatment
 6. Origin Transparency Requirement
Honey MUST:
  • be traceable to a defined origin
  • clearly state source (region / beekeeper / batch)
Not allowed:
  • anonymous origin
  • mixed-source without disclosure
 7. Biological Integrity Condition
Honey must preserve:
  • natural enzymes
  • biological activity
  • structural composition
Critical Principle
Processing must not convert honey into a biologically inactive
substance.

F. Prohibited Practices

The following are prohibited:
  • addition of sugar syrups
  • artificial feeding affecting final product
  • high-temperature processing
  • industrial homogenization degrading structure
  • misleading labeling of origin


G. Classification

HIS-Compliant Honey
  • 100% pure
  • non-degraded
  • traceable
  • biologically active
 Non-Compliant Honey
  • blended without transparency
  • thermally degraded
  • diluted or altered


H. Threshold Logic

Honey condition is:

VALID → meets all integrity conditions
INVALID → any violation present

No partial compliance is permitted.

Structural Principle

Honey is defined by nature.

Canonical Closing Statement

Honey is already defined.

Honey Integrity Standard (HIS) defines whether that definition is
preserved or violated.

If honey is altered, degraded, or obscured,
it may remain honey by name —
but not by integrity.
