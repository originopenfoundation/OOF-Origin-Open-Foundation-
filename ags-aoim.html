Canonical Definition

Authority Origin Integrity Module (AOIM) defines the structural
conditions under which authority remains attributable to an
identifiable, legitimate, traceable, governable, and operationally
valid source throughout its lifecycle.

AOIM governs authority origin.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine where authority originated before authority may be
exercised, delegated, escalated, validated, or used
within governance environments.

Module Operational Role

AOIM defines the authority-origin governance layer of AGS.

Its role is to establish the source from which authority originates
and to preserve traceability of authority creation
throughout governance analysis.

Module Operational Space

AOIM governs:

  • authority origin
  • authority source identification
  • authority attribution
  • authority creation
  • authority traceability
  • authority legitimacy source
  • governance-source validation
  • authority provenance


The module applies wherever governance requires identification
of authority origin.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • identifiable authority sources
  • traceable authority creation
  • reconstructable authority history
  • accountable authority assignment
  • governance-valid authority attribution
  • operationally valid authority origin


Its function is to ensure that governance can determine where
authority came from.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Authority Origin Object

The organization must define which authority environments
require origin governance.

This may include:

  • organizational hierarchies
  • management structures
  • regulatory authorities
  • contractual authorities
  • delegated authorities
  • AI orchestration systems
  • autonomous operational environments
  • Human-AI governance structures


2. Define Authority Origin Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which authority
origin remains valid.

This includes:

  • source-identification requirements
  • attribution requirements
  • traceability requirements
  • accountability requirements
  • validation requirements
  • governance-valid origin conditions


3. Define Origin Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how authority-origin failures are identified.

This may include:

  • unidentified authority sources
  • illegitimate authority creation
  • undocumented authority assignment
  • governance-blind authority structures
  • unverifiable authority claims
  • hidden authority origins


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for
authority-origin failures.

Governance response may include:

  • source verification
  • authority review
  • governance intervention
  • escalation
  • authority reconstruction
  • corrective actions
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • authority sources
  • authority assignments
  • validation activities
  • governance reviews
  • intervention procedures
  • resulting authority structures


An authority-governance environment must not remain origin-valid if
materially significant authority sources cannot be identified,
reconstructed, reviewed, validated, or governed.

Use Case 1 — Organizational Authority Structure

Scenario

A manager issues operational instructions affecting employees,
contractors, and external service providers.

Application

AOIM determines how the manager obtained authority and whether the
authority originated from a legitimate governance source.

Result

Governance gains visibility into the origin and legitimacy
of authority.

Use Case 2 — AI Orchestration Environment

Scenario

A supervisory AI agent directs multiple execution agents operating
within an orchestration system.

Application

AOIM determines where supervisory authority originated and whether
it remains attributable to a legitimate governance source.

Result

Governance gains visibility into the origin of authority across
intelligent operational systems.

Canonical Closing Statement

Authority Origin Integrity Module (AOIM) defines the structural
conditions under which authority remains attributable to an
identifiable, legitimate, traceable, governable, and operationally
valid source throughout its lifecycle.

Authority without identifiable origin creates governance
uncertainty. Authority origin integrity therefore becomes a
foundational condition of trustworthy authority governance.
