About Authority

OOF® — Methodological
Reference Authority

Canonical Position

OOF® — OriginOpen® Foundation operates as a private, non-executive
methodological reference authority.

It does not exercise public power.
It does not enforce law.
It does not issue binding regulatory decisions.

OOF® defines structure, meaning, and methodological conditions.
Public institutions retain full sovereignty and decision-making authority.

Nature of the Authority

Within OOF®, authority is methodological and semantic.

It does not imply:
  • sovereign status
  • regulatory mandate
  • certification under public law
  • enforcement jurisdiction
OOF® governs meaning, not institutions.
OOF® defines structure, not sovereignty.

Meta-Layer Position

OOF® operates as a meta-layer framework.

A meta-layer:
  • does not replace systems
  • does not compete with systems
  • does not execute system functions
It defines the conditions under which systems remain interoperable,
consistent, and valid.

Authority at this level is structural, not operational.

Compatibility within OOF® is not an approval process.
It is a state of structural alignment with defined conditions.

Authority vs. Implementation

OOF® does not provide services.

  • consultants apply frameworks
  • vendors implement solutions
  • institutions execute decisions
OOF® defines:
  • the methodological system
  • canonical definitions
  • structural conditions
  • compatibility states
Authority defines the structure. Systems operate within it.

How Authority Is Established

OOF® authority does not emerge from:
  • market share
  • funding
  • popularity
  • consensus
Authority exists when:
  • a previously undefined space is precisely defined
  • meanings are fixed and stable
  • structural logic remains coherent
  • systems operate independently of individual actors
OOF® authority exists because its system remains internally consistent,
structurally defined, and semantically stable.

Canonical Definitions & Semantic Stability

A core function of OOF® is the establishment of canonical definitions.

Canonical means:
  • fixed reference meaning
  • anchor against semantic drift
  • deterministic interpretation point
Without stable meaning:
  • compatibility collapses
  • validation fails
  • systems diverge
  • governance becomes inconsistent
Without stable meaning, no system remains valid.

Universal Canonical Language (UCL)

All OOF® standards, methodologies, and definitions are maintained in:

Universal Canonical Language (UCL™) — English

English serves as the canonical carrier language.

Meaning is not derived from language.
Meaning is defined through canonical definitions within UCL™.

“English = representation. UCL™ = meaning.”

Translations may exist for accessibility.
They do not redefine meaning.

If meaning changes, the translation is invalid.

In case of ambiguity:

The meaning defined in UCL™ remains the authoritative reference.

Non-Executive Character

OOF® does not:
  • manage implementations
  • control technologies
  • certify by default
  • adjudicate disputes
  • compel compliance
Adoption of OOF® frameworks is:
  • voluntary
  • reference-based
  • structurally defined

System Principle

OOF® defines the conditions under which systems are considered valid.
It does not enforce their adoption.

Final Statement

Authority within OOF® is not exercised.
It is defined through structure.
