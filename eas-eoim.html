Canonical Definition

Evidence Origin Integrity Module (EOIM) defines the structural
conditions under which evidence remains attributable to
identifiable, legitimate, traceable, governable, accountable,
reconstructable, verifiable, and operationally valid evidence
sources throughout the evidence lifecycle.

EOIM governs evidence origin.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine where evidence originated, who produced the evidence, how
the evidence was generated, under what conditions the evidence was
collected, and whether the source of evidence remains legitimate
and trustworthy.

Module Operational Role

EOIM defines the evidence-origin governance layer of EAS.

Its role is to preserve visibility of where evidence enters
a governance environment.

Module Operational Space

EOIM governs:

  • evidence origin
  • evidence-source traceability
  • evidence attribution
  • evidence provenance
  • evidence-source legitimacy
  • evidence-generation integrity
  • evidence accountability
  • evidence-source governance


The module applies wherever governance requires verification
of evidence origins.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • identifiable evidence sources
  • traceable evidence origins
  • reconstructable evidence history
  • accountable evidence creation
  • governance-valid evidence attribution
  • operationally valid evidence provenance


Its function is to ensure that governance can determine where
evidence originated and whether the source remains trustworthy.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Evidence Origin Object

The organization must define which evidence environments
require origin governance.

This may include:

  • audit records
  • compliance records
  • certification records
  • investigation materials
  • operational logs
  • AI-generated evidence
  • Human-AI environments
  • governance records


2. Define Evidence Origin Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which evidence
origin remains valid.

This includes:

  • source-identification requirements
  • attribution requirements
  • provenance requirements
  • accountability requirements
  • legitimacy requirements
  • governance-valid origin conditions


3. Define Origin Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how evidence-origin failures are identified.

This may include:

  • unidentified evidence sources
  • undocumented evidence creation
  • manipulated evidence origins
  • unverifiable evidence provenance
  • governance-blind evidence collection
  • illegitimate evidence generation


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for
evidence-origin failures.

Governance response may include:

  • evidence review
  • source verification
  • governance intervention
  • evidence reconstruction
  • corrective actions
  • evidence reassessment
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • evidence sources
  • evidence creation events
  • governance reviews
  • validation activities
  • intervention procedures
  • resulting evidence states


An evidence-governance environment must not remain origin-valid if
materially significant evidence cannot be attributed, reconstructed,
reviewed, validated, or governed.

Use Case 1 — Workplace Investigation

Environment

Scenario

An investigation relies on documents, photographs, emails,
production records, witness statements, and operational logs.

Application

EOIM reconstructs where each evidence item originated and verifies
the legitimacy of evidence sources.

Result

Governance gains visibility into the provenance and
trustworthiness of evidence.

Use Case 2 — Human-AI Evidence Environment

Scenario

An AI system generates operational reports, recommendations, event
records, and analytical outputs used in governance decisions.

Application

EOIM reconstructs the origin of AI-generated evidence and identifies
the systems, models, data sources, and governance
conditions involved.

Result

Governance gains visibility into evidence origins across
intelligent operational ecosystems.

Canonical Closing Statement

Evidence Origin Integrity Module (EOIM) defines the structural
conditions under which evidence remains attributable to
identifiable, legitimate, traceable, governable, accountable,
reconstructable, verifiable, and operationally valid evidence
sources throughout the evidence lifecycle.

Evidence cannot be trusted if its origin cannot be trusted. Evidence
origin integrity therefore becomes a foundational condition of
trustworthy evidence governance.
