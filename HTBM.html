Canonical Definition

The HTBM — Harm Temporal Boundary Module governs how the temporal extent
of a recognized Harm object is established, including when the governed
Harm begins, continues, changes in temporal form, becomes intermittent,
recurs, remains open, ends, or requires retrospective correction, without
substituting Exposure, mechanism operation, manifestation, reporting,
Recognition, investigation, or administrative timestamps for the actual
temporal boundary of Harm.

HTBM establishes the transition:

Governed Boundary Identification State + Governed Harm Subject Boundary →
Harm-Relevant Temporal Information → Temporal Boundary Assessment → Harm
Start / Continuity / End → Governed Harm Temporal Boundary

Its central question is:

What period of time legitimately belongs to the governed Harm?

  • Operational Role


A Harm Boundary cannot be legitimate without distinguishing the Harm's own
temporal lifecycle from the timestamps of the systems surrounding it.

A harmful mechanism may begin before Harm.

Harm may exist before Recognition.

Exposure may end while Harm continues.

A visible effect may disappear while Harm remains.

A mechanism may stop while downstream Harm persists.

HTBM therefore governs:

when the Harm itself begins and how long it remains within the governed
Harm object.

→ maintain Open Temporal Boundary.

→ establish supported Harm End.

→ expand, contract, correct, or reopen while preserving prior states.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HTBM — Harm Temporal Boundary Module governs the temporal extent of a
recognized Harm object by establishing and maintaining the materially
supportable Harm start, continuity, interruption, recurrence, progression,
open duration, Harm end, uncertainty, confidence, dispute, retrospective
reconstruction, expansion, contraction, correction, reopening, and
historical temporal continuity. It preserves the distinction between Harm
time and Exposure, mechanism, manifestation, reporting, Recognition,
investigation, administrative, legal, accountability, liability, and
evidence timelines. It does not determine Harm Emergence independently of
HEMS, govern Recognition Latency owned by HLRM, establish the Effect or
Downstream Boundary, formally determine Harm, assign accountability or
liability, or replace operational reality, evidence, validation,
integrity, identity, AI behavior, autonomous execution, simulation, Value,
or tax-event governance.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm has its own timeline.

It may begin after Exposure starts, exist before anyone recognizes it,
continue after the mechanism stops, remain after visible effects
disappear, and be reconstructed years after the original event.

HTBM prevents administrative timestamps, technical execution periods,
reporting dates, and governance awareness from replacing that temporal
reality.

The temporal boundary of Harm begins when Harm becomes real and ends when
the Harm lifecycle legitimately ends — not when the surrounding system
starts or stops looking at it.
