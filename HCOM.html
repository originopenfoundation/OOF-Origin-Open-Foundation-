HCOM — Harm Compounding Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HCOM — Harm Compounding Module governs how existing Harm becomes
materially amplified through interaction between multiple Harm conditions,
effects, vulnerabilities, dependencies, or other materially relevant
factors such that the resulting Harm cannot be adequately represented as
simple additive accumulation.

HCOM establishes whether a materially amplifying interaction exists, which
conditions participate in it, how the interaction changes the governed
Harm, whether amplification remains within the existing Harm object or
contributes to distinct downstream Harm, and what resulting Compounding
State must be carried into Propagation, Cascading, State, and Containment
governance.

It establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Accumulation State + Interacting Harm Conditions →
Interaction Identification → Compounding Evaluation → Amplification
Determination → Compounded Harm Condition → Governed Harm Compounding
State

Its central question is:

Are interacting Harm conditions materially amplifying existing Harm beyond
what their independent contributions would adequately represent?

  • Operational Role


HACM governs:

how Harm builds through repeated, continuing, or additive contributions.

HCOM governs:

how interacting conditions materially amplify Harm.

Its operational role is:

to identify and govern non-additive Harm amplification without treating
every combination of harmful conditions as Compounding.

The conceptual distinction is:

Accumulation: A + A + A → Greater A

Compounding: A × B → Materially Amplified Harm Condition

The multiplication symbol is conceptual, not a mandatory mathematical
formula.

  • Position Within HCPS


The HCPS governance flow is:

Change Identification → Accumulation → Compounding → Propagation →
Cascading

HCOM receives materially relevant outputs from:

Harm Change Identification

and, where applicable:

Harm Accumulation

It produces:

Governed Harm Compounding State

for subsequent HCPS and HAGA™ governance.

  • Governed Object


The primary governed object is:

Material Harm Amplification Through Interaction

The presence of multiple harmful conditions is not sufficient.

HCOM requires a materially relevant interaction.

Therefore:

Multiple Harm Conditions ≠ Harm Compounding by Default

  • Foundational Compounding Principle


The foundational rule is:

Harm becomes compounded when interacting conditions materially amplify
consequence beyond what their independent effects adequately explain.

This means governance must evaluate:

interaction

not merely:

coexistence

Canonical distinction:

Coexistence ≠ Compounding

A compounding relationship may involve interaction between:

multiple Harm effects

multiple domains

or:

other materially interacting conditions

The specific interaction must remain governable.

HCOM must establish:

The question is not simply:

Are these conditions present together?

It is:

Does their interaction materially change the Harm consequence?

  • Independent Effects Baseline


Compounding requires a meaningful comparison with what the conditions
would represent independently.

Conceptually:

Independent Effect A

Independent Effect B

versus:

Interactive Effect A+B

Where the interactive condition materially amplifies the resulting Harm
beyond the independent representation, Compounding may be established.

Canonical principle:

Compounding Requires Interaction, Not Mere Arithmetic Aggregation.

other materially relevant Harm dimensions

The methodology must not imply mathematical precision where none exists.

Compounding Magnitude ≠ Mathematical Multiplication Requirement

  • Temporal Compounding


Compounding conditions need not emerge simultaneously.

For example:

Existing Harm → Later Condition → Amplified Harm

A later condition may interact with Harm that already exists.

Therefore:

Different Emergence Times ≠ No Compounding

The relevant question is whether the conditions materially interact.

  • Sequential Compounding


Compounding may develop through several interaction stages:

Harm A + Condition B → Compounded Condition AB

then:

AB + Condition C → Further Compounded Condition ABC

HCOM must preserve the interaction sequence where it materially affects
governance.

This remains distinct from Harm Cascading unless new downstream Harm
objects or sequential consequence relationships emerge.

  • Accumulated Harm as a Compounding Condition


Accumulated Harm may become one side of a later compounding relationship.

Conceptually:

Accumulated Harm₁ + New Condition → Compounded Harm₁

This creates a direct relationship between HACM and HCOM.

Canonical principle:

Accumulation May Create the Conditions for Compounding Without Becoming
Compounding Itself.

  • Compounding Across Harm Dimensions


Different Harm dimensions may interact.

For example:

Physical Harm + Cognitive Harm

Digital Harm + Financial Harm

Environmental Harm + Health Harm

or:

Organizational Harm + Infrastructure Harm

Where interaction materially amplifies consequence, HCOM may govern the
Compounding relationship.

  • Cross-Domain Compounding


Compounding may cross Harm domains without automatically creating a new
Harm object.

However, where interaction produces a materially distinct downstream Harm:

Harm₁ + Condition → Harm₂

appropriate HAGA™ lifecycle governance may be required for Harm₂.

Therefore:

Cross-Domain Interaction ≠ Same Harm by Default

  • Subject Vulnerability and Compounding


An existing Harm may interact with a materially relevant subject condition
such that the resulting consequence becomes amplified.

HCOM may govern the interaction where necessary to represent the Harm
accurately.

But:

Vulnerability ≠ Harm

and:

Vulnerability Alone ≠ Compounding

The governed issue is the materially amplified Harm consequence.

  • Dependency Compounding


Harm may be amplified through dependencies.

For example:

System Harm + Critical Dependency → Amplified Operational Consequence

or:

Infrastructure Harm + Dependency Failure → Amplified Harm

The dependency itself is not automatically Harm.

Canonical distinction:

Dependency ≠ Harm

but:

Dependency Interaction May Amplify Harm

  • Multi-Subject Compounding


Harm affecting multiple subjects may create interaction effects that are
not captured by treating each subject independently.

Where appropriate, HCOM must support:

individual Harm conditions

and:

collective interaction effects

without assuming that every multi-subject Harm is compounded.

Therefore:

Multiple Affected Subjects ≠ Collective Compounding by Default

  • Composite Compounding


Composite Harm may contain several interacting components.

A component that appears limited independently may materially amplify
another component.

Therefore:

Limited Component Effect ≠ Limited Composite Consequence

HCOM must evaluate the relationship at the appropriate Harm-object level.

  • Distributed Compounding


Compounding conditions may exist across different:

agents

systems

organizations

locations

platforms

or:

operational environments

No single component may observe the full interaction.

Canonical principle:

Distribution of the interacting conditions must not make compounded Harm
methodologically invisible.

  • Compounding Without a Single Harm-Producing Action


Compounded Harm may result from several individually insufficient actions
or conditions.

Therefore:

No Single Harm-Producing Action ≠ No Compounded Harm

The governance object is the resulting Harm interaction, not the
requirement to identify one dominant act.

  • Compounding vs Accumulation


The distinction must remain explicit:

Accumulation: repeated or additive contributions increase Harm.

Compounding: interaction among conditions amplifies Harm.

A Harm may experience both:

Accumulation → Compounding

or:

Compounding → Further Accumulation

Therefore:

Accumulation and Compounding May Coexist Without Becoming the Same
Dynamic.

  • Compounding vs Propagation


Compounded Harm does not necessarily spread.

It may become substantially worse while remaining within the same:

subject

object

system

location

or:

Boundary

Therefore:

Compounding ≠ Propagation

If amplified Harm extends into additional affected scope, Propagation
governance becomes relevant.

  • Compounding vs Cascading


Compounding concerns interacting conditions.

Cascading concerns sequential downstream consequence relationships.

Conceptually:

Compounding: A + B → amplified AB

Cascading: A → B → C → D

Therefore:

Interaction ≠ Cascade

The two dynamics may coexist, but must remain distinguishable.

  • Compounding and Harm State


Compounding may contribute to a Harm State transition.

For example:

Active + Material Compounding → Escalating

But:

Compounding ≠ Escalating State by Default

HCOM governs the dynamic.

HSS governs the resulting State.

  • Compounding After Mechanism Termination


The original Harm-producing mechanism may stop while existing Harm
continues interacting with other conditions.

Therefore:

Mechanism Termination ≠ Compounding Termination

The interaction may persist independently of the original mechanism.

  • Intervention and Compounding


An intervention may reduce one Harm component while leaving another
interaction pathway active.

Therefore:

Component Mitigation ≠ Compounding Termination

Similarly:

Intervention Completion ≠ Compounded Harm Resolved

The actual interaction and Harm consequence must be evaluated.

  • Compounding Detection and Visibility


Compounding may become visible only after Harm has materially intensified.

Delayed recognition must not be interpreted as delayed Compounding.

Therefore:

Compounding Detection Time ≠ Compounding Start Time by Default

Where supportable, HCOM should preserve:

interaction start

amplification development

detection time

and:

recording time

separately.

  • Compounding Uncertainty


HCOM must preserve uncertainty where:

interaction is suspected but incomplete

independent effects cannot be fully separated

multiple possible amplification pathways exist

data are incomplete

timing is uncertain

Possible governed states may include:

Compounding Partially Established

Canonical principle:

Uncertain Interaction ≠ No Interaction by Default

  • Potential Downstream Harm


Compounding may create conditions from which a materially distinct
downstream Harm emerges.

Where this occurs:

Compounded Harm₁ → Downstream Condition → Potential Harm₂

HCOM should preserve the relationship while invoking appropriate HAGA™
lifecycle governance for Harm₂.

It must not silently absorb all downstream consequences into Harm₁.

  • Containment Relevance


Compounded Harm may materially increase the urgency or complexity of
Containment.

HCOM should therefore be capable of generating:

Compounding Containment Need

where continued interaction is likely to amplify Harm further.

A useful governance question becomes:

Which interaction must be interrupted to prevent further Harm
amplification?

HCOS owns the actual Containment governance.

  • Compounding Cessation


Compounding ceases when the materially amplifying interaction no longer
operates.

But the Harm produced through Compounding may remain.

Therefore:

Compounding Ended ≠ Compounded Harm Ended

Conceptually:

Interaction Stopped → Existing Compounded Harm Remains

The remaining Harm continues through applicable HAGA™ governance.

  • Governed Output


Produce a traceable Governed Harm Compounding State for subsequent HCPS
and HAGA™ governance. 35. Use Case 1 — Existing Harm Interacts With a New
Condition

A subject already has a governed Harm.

A new Harm-relevant condition emerges that would independently create only
limited additional effect.

When combined with the existing Harm, however, the interaction materially
amplifies the overall consequence.

HCOM distinguishes:

Existing Harm + Additional Condition

from:

Existing Harm × Material Interaction → Compounded Harm

It therefore governs the amplification rather than treating the result as
simple accumulation.

The governing principle is:

The significance of a condition may lie not in what it does alone, but in
what it does when interacting with Harm that already exists.

  • Use Case 2 — Distributed Systems Create an Amplifying Interaction


Several connected systems each create limited operational effects.

Independently, none represents the complete Harm amplification.

Their outputs interact through a shared dependency and materially worsen
an already governed Harm.

HCOM preserves:

System A Condition + System B Condition + Dependency Interaction →
Compounded Harm

without requiring one system to contain the complete interaction.

The governing principle is:

A Harm-amplifying interaction remains governable even when no single
operational component contains the complete compounding mechanism.

  • Governance Logic


HCOM applies the following logic:

Does an existing governed Harm exist?

If no:

→ HCOM cannot formally govern Compounding.

If yes:

→ identify materially relevant additional conditions.

Are multiple conditions merely present together?

If yes, but no material interaction is established:

→ Compounding is not established.

Do the conditions interact?

If no:

→ evaluate whether the Harm dynamic is better represented as Accumulation
or another HCPS process.

If yes:

→ determine whether the interaction materially amplifies Harm.

Does the interactive consequence exceed what independent or additive
representation adequately explains?

If yes:

→ establish Harm Compounding.

Then determine:

which conditions interact

which Harm dimensions are amplified

when the interaction began

how materially it amplifies Harm

whether it persists

and:

with what uncertainty

Next:

Is the Harm also accumulating?

→ preserve both dynamics.

Is the compounded Harm spreading into additional scope?

→ continue into Propagation governance.

Is the interaction generating sequential downstream Harm?

→ continue into Cascading governance.

Does the Compounding support Harm State reassessment?

→ provide the relevant input to HSS.

Does continued interaction require Containment?

→ produce the appropriate Containment Need.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HCOM — Harm Compounding Module governs how materially interacting
conditions amplify an already governed Harm beyond what their independent
or merely additive effects adequately represent. It governs participating
conditions, interaction relationships, independent-effects baselines,
amplification, magnitude, timing, duration, complex and distributed
Compounding, uncertainty, cessation, historical continuity, and relevant
downstream governance triggers. It does not determine whether Harm
originally exists, perform Recognition or Determination, independently
redefine the Governed Harm Boundary, classify or transition Harm State,
govern additive build-up as Accumulation, govern spread as Propagation,
govern sequential downstream consequence chains as Cascading, perform
Containment or Mitigation, govern Recovery, Residual Harm, Resolution or
Finality, determine general causation, assign accountability or liability,
or replace operational reality, evidence, validation, integrity,
prediction, simulation, AI behavior, autonomous execution, identity,
Value, tax-event, or jurisdiction-specific governance.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm is not always the sum of its parts.

Existing Harm may interact with additional conditions, dependencies,
vulnerabilities, or effects in ways that materially amplify consequence
beyond what any component adequately explains alone.

HCOM governs that interaction without confusing coexistence with
Compounding, Compounding with Propagation, or amplification with a new
Harm by default.

When Harm becomes greater because its conditions interact, that
interaction itself must remain governable.
