Canonical Definition

The HCHM — Harm Conditions Module governs the conditions under which a
mapped and developing Harm formation pathway becomes materially capable of
crossing from pre-emergence development into existing Harm by identifying,
qualifying, structuring, combining, constraining, and reassessing the
conditions that enable, support, prevent, delay, accelerate, suppress, or
otherwise shape Harm Emergence without treating the presence of such
conditions as automatic proof that Harm has emerged.

HCHM establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Development State → Emergence-Relevant Conditions →
Condition Qualification → Condition Interaction → Emergence Sufficiency
Structure

Its central question is:

Under what governed conditions can the developing Harm formation pathway
become sufficient for Harm to begin to exist?

  • Operational Role


HEMM maps how Harm may form.

HDHM governs how that formation pathway develops.

HCHM determines under what conditions that development can become
sufficient for Harm Emergence.

Those conditions may involve:

  • subject state
  • exposure state
  • environmental conditions
  • system state
  • operational constraints
  • duration
  • repetition
  • accumulation
  • dependency
  • capability
  • vulnerability
  • barrier effectiveness
  • concurrent developments
  • downstream interaction
  • contextual thresholds


Its role is:

to govern the conditions that make Harm Emergence possible, prevented,
delayed, accelerated, constrained, or materially sufficient without
confusing emergence conditions with Harm itself.

  • Condition Identification


HCHM must identify which conditions materially affect whether Harm can
emerge from the governed development pathway.

A condition should not enter the governed set merely because it exists in
the surrounding context.

The test is:

Does this condition materially affect whether, when, how, or under what
circumstances the developing pathway can cross into Harm?

If no:

→ it remains contextual but not an Emergence Condition.

  • Emergence-Relevant Condition


An Emergence-Relevant Condition is any state, circumstance, dependency,
property, constraint, capability, barrier, interaction, or other factor
that materially shapes the possibility or sufficiency of Harm Emergence.

Its existence does not establish Harm.

Emergence-Relevant Condition ≠ Harm Emergence

  • Required Condition


A Required Condition is a condition that must be present for the governed
emergence pathway to proceed under the applicable model.

Conceptually:

Required Condition absent → Pathway cannot reach Emergence under current
model

This requirement must remain evidence-based.

Required Condition ≠ Sufficient Condition

  • Canonical Boundary


The HCHM — Harm Conditions Module governs the identification,
qualification, interaction, persistence, effectiveness, uncertainty, and
historical continuity of the conditions under which a mapped and
developing Harm formation pathway may become materially capable of
crossing into Harm. It governs required, contributing, sufficient,
enabling, constraining, preventive, protective, compensating,
accelerating, delaying, triggering, threshold-relevant, subject,
environmental, operational, system, temporal, accumulation, composite,
distributed, barrier, and constraint conditions. It does not determine
that Harm has emerged, govern Harm Appearance or the final Harm
Transition, establish general causation, accountability, responsibility,
liability, operational execution, evidence governance, validation,
integrity, identity, simulation, Value lifecycle, or tax-event
determination.

HEMM asks:

Through what pathway may Harm be forming?

HDHM asks:

How is that pathway developing?

HCHM asks:

Under what conditions can that development become sufficient for Harm
Emergence?

HAHM next asks:

How does Harm become materially apparent within the governed emergence
process?

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm does not emerge from development alone. It emerges under conditions
that enable, constrain, accelerate, delay, combine, suppress, or permit
that development to cross into a harmful state.

HCHM makes those conditions governable without turning their presence into
premature proof of Harm.

If the emergence of Harm depends on the conditions of the whole,
governance must understand the whole condition structure before it
declares the transition complete.
