Canonical Definition

Authority Continuity Module (ACM) defines the structural conditions under which runtime
authority continuity, delegated authority persistence, distributed authority
coordination, and consequence-bearing authority flow remain materially stable,
traceable, and operationally governable across runtime operational environments.

A system satisfies ACM only if:
  • runtime authority continuity remains materially stable
  • delegated authority persistence preserves operational legitimacy
  • distributed authority coordination remains coherent
  • consequence-bearing authority flow remains traceable
  • authority fragmentation does not destabilize operational continuity


A system that preserves runtime execution while authority continuity materially fragments
does not satisfy ACM.

Module Operational Role

ACM defines the authority continuity layer of OAIS by preserving stable runtime
authority continuity across operational environments.

Module Operational Space

ACM governs the authority continuity space of OAIS by preserving delegated authority
persistence, distributed authority coherence, escalation continuity, and
consequence-bearing authority stability across runtime systems.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:
  • runtime authority continuity
  • delegated authority persistence
  • distributed authority coordination
  • escalation authority continuity
  • consequence-bearing authority flow
  • governance-valid operational legitimacy


Its function is to ensure that runtime operational authority remains materially continuous
strongly enough to preserve governance-valid operational legitimacy across execution
environments.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Authority Continuity Object

The organization must define which operational authority structures require continuity
governance.

This may include:
  • delegated runtime authority
  • orchestration authority routing
  • escalation authority pathways
  • distributed operational hierarchies
  • autonomous runtime delegation
  • consequence-bearing authority flows
  • operational-critical authority chains


2. Define Authority Continuity Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which operational authority continuity remains
materially stable and operationally aligned.

This includes:
  • delegated authority persistence
  • distributed authority coherence
  • escalation continuity
  • operational legitimacy continuity
  • governance-valid authority traceability


3. Define Authority Fragmentation Detection Logic

The system must define how materially unstable authority continuity or authority
fragmentation is identified.

This may include:
  • delegation discontinuity
  • escalation instability
  • distributed authority divergence
  • authority-traceability degradation
  • consequence-bearing authority fragmentation


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for materially unstable authority continuity
conditions.

Governance response may include:
  • authority synchronization
  • delegation stabilization
  • escalation review activation
  • authority reconstruction
  • distributed coordination correction
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of authority continuity and
authority-fragmentation states. A system must not remain authority-valid if runtime
operational authority materially fragments while systems continue assuming governance-valid
legitimacy remains preserved.

Application
ACM preserves governance-valid authority continuity through distributed delegation
governance and escalation continuity stabilization.

Result

fragmentation across distributed AI systems.

Canonical Closing Statement

If runtime operational authority cannot remain materially continuous across operational
environments, systems may preserve execution continuity while governance-valid
legitimacy progressively fragments across runtime coordination layers.
