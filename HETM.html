HETM — Harm Exposure Transition Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HETM — Harm Exposure Transition Module governs how Harm Exposure
changes from one governed Exposure state to another, including activation,
escalation, reduction, interruption, resumption, expansion, contraction,
stabilization, termination, reopening, and transition toward possible Harm
Emergence, without treating Exposure change as automatic evidence that
Harm has emerged.

HETM establishes the transition:

Governed Exposure State → Material Exposure Change → Transition
Qualification → Updated Exposure State → Exposure Continuation /
Termination / Harm Emergence Handoff

Its central question is:

How does the governed Exposure state change, when does that change become
materially significant, and when should Exposure governance hand off to
Harm Emergence governance?

  • Operational Role


Exposure is rarely static.

A subject may move into or out of a Harm-capable condition.

A protective barrier may fail.

An AI output may go from private to widely distributed.

An autonomous system may expand its action space.

A digital pathway may become inaccessible.

An environmental condition may spread, weaken, or disappear.

Repeated Exposure may accumulate.

A previously ended Exposure may reappear.

HETM therefore governs change in the Exposure relationship itself.

Its role is:

to establish legitimate Exposure state transitions without confusing
change in Exposure with change in Harm.

  • Position Within Harm Exposure Standard (HES)


The HES internal flow is:

Exposure Identification → Exposure Conditions → Exposure Scope → Exposure
Duration → Exposure Transition

HETM is the final module of the Harm Exposure Standard.

It receives:

  • Exposure Identification State
  • Governed Exposure Condition Set
  • Governed Harm Exposure Scope
  • Governed Exposure Temporal State


It determines how the Exposure state changes and whether Exposure remains
within HES or becomes ready for handoff to Harm Emergence Standard (HEMS).

  • Governed Object


The governed object is the Exposure Transition State.

This may represent movement between states such as:

These are Exposure states.

They are not Harm states.

  • Transition Formation


An Exposure Transition occurs when a material change affects the governed
Exposure relationship.

Conceptually:

Exposure State₁ + Material Change → Exposure State₂

The change must be materially relevant to Exposure governance.

Not every operational change creates a new Exposure state.

  • Materiality of Transition


A change becomes governance-relevant where it materially affects one or
more of:

Exposure Existence

Exposure Conditions

Exposure Scope

Exposure Duration

Exposure Pathway

Exposure Magnitude

Exposure Continuity

Exposure Uncertainty

Exposure Termination

The test is:

Would ignoring this change materially distort the governed Exposure state?

  • Exposure Activation


Exposure Activation occurs when a previously non-active or candidate
relationship becomes active.

Conceptually:

No Active Exposure → Exposure Conditions Satisfied → Active Exposure

Activation does not establish Harm.

Exposure Activation ≠ Harm Emergence

  • Exposure Escalation


Exposure Escalation occurs when Exposure becomes materially broader,
stronger, more persistent, more frequent, or otherwise more significant

This may involve:

Expanded Scope

Higher Frequency

Longer Duration

Reduced Protection

Wider Distribution

Increased Access

Greater Pathway Availability

HETM governs the transition.

It does not determine that Harm has escalated.

Exposure Escalation ≠ Harm Escalation

  • Exposure Reduction


Exposure Reduction occurs where Exposure becomes materially narrower,
shorter, less frequent, less accessible, or more constrained.

Reduction may result from:

  • protection
  • system change
  • reduced access
  • environmental improvement
  • barrier restoration
  • subject movement
  • process change


Reduced Exposure ≠ Harm Mitigation

Harm Mitigation belongs later in HAGA™.

Exposure Interruption occurs where an active Exposure temporarily stops.

Conceptually:

The system must preserve the possibility of resumption.

Interruption ≠ Termination

  • Exposure Resumption


Exposure Resumption occurs where Exposure becomes active again after

Conceptually:

Interrupted Exposure → Active Exposure

Resumed Exposure

New Exposure

Exposure Termination ≠ Harm Resolution

  • Exposure Closure


Exposure Closure is the governed confirmation that no active Exposure
relationship remains under the current Exposure object.

Closure should require sufficient basis.

Where future recurrence remains plausible but unsupported, the Exposure
may still be closed with historical continuity preserved.

  • Exposure Reopening


A closed Exposure may be reopened where:

  • new information appears
  • a pathway reactivates
  • exposure resumes
  • earlier termination was incorrect
  • a subject is newly discovered
  • residual condition persists
  • system state changes


Conceptually:

Closed Exposure → Reopening Trigger → Reassessed Exposure State

Exposure Closure ≠ Permanent Immutability

Current Scope Reduction ≠ Historical Non-Exposure

Exposure may shift from one set of subjects or objects to another without

Exposure Expansion ≠ Harm Propagation

Receive the current Exposure Identification State, Governed Exposure

  • Transition Detection


Identify materially relevant changes in Exposure existence, conditions,

  • Transition Qualification


Classify activation, escalation, reduction, stabilization, interruption,

  • State Continuity


Preserve the relationship between prior and new Exposure states without

  • Uncertainty & Conflict Governance


Represent probable, possible, disputed, unknown, and conflicting

  • Handoff Boundary Control


Distinguish Exposure change from Harm Emergence and identify when the

  • Reassessment & Historical Continuity


Permit transition revision without erasing prior governed states.

  • Governed Output


Produce a traceable Exposure Transition State and, where appropriate, an

  • Governance Logic


HETM applies the following logic:

Does a governed Exposure state exist?

If no:

→ no Exposure transition should be manufactured.

If yes:

→ identify material change signals.

Does the change materially affect Exposure existence, conditions, Scope,
duration, pathway, magnitude, continuity, or uncertainty?

If no:

→ retain the current Exposure state.

If yes:

→ classify the transition.

Has Exposure activated?

→ establish Active Exposure.

Has Exposure expanded or escalated?

→ update Exposure state without inferring Harm.

Has Exposure reduced or contracted?

→ update Exposure state without inferring Harm Mitigation.

Has Exposure temporarily stopped?

→ establish Interruption where appropriate.

Has Exposure resumed?

→ determine whether Resumed or New Exposure.

Has Exposure terminated?

→ establish Exposure Termination without inferring Harm Resolution.

Has a closed Exposure become active again?

→ reopen the Exposure object where justified.

Is the transition disputed or uncertain?

→ preserve qualified transition state.

Is the resulting Exposure state sufficiently governed for Harm Emergence
assessment?

If no:

→ remain within HES.

If yes:

→ produce an Emergence-Ready Exposure State for HEMS.

At every stage:

Exposure Transition ≠ Harm Transition

Exposure Escalation ≠ Harm Escalation

Exposure Termination ≠ Harm Resolution

Emergence Readiness ≠ Harm Emergence

  • Canonical Boundary


The HETM — Harm Exposure Transition Module governs material changes in
Harm Exposure state, including activation, escalation, reduction,
stabilization, interruption, resumption, expansion, contraction,
redistribution, pathway change, condition change, persistence,
termination, closure, reopening, uncertainty, reassessment, and historical
transition continuity. It determines how Exposure moves from one governed
state to another and when that state becomes ready for Harm Emergence
assessment, without itself determining that Harm has emerged, escalated,
propagated, been mitigated, recovered from, resolved, or reached finality.

HEIM determines:

Which relationship requires Exposure governance?

HECM determines:

Under what conditions does Exposure operate?

HESM determines:

Who or what belongs within Exposure Scope?

HEDM determines:

For how long and in what temporal pattern does Exposure exist?

HETM determines:

How does that Exposure change, end, reopen, or become ready for Harm
Emergence governance?

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Exposure governance is incomplete if it records only whether Exposure
exists and ignores how that Exposure changes.

Exposure may activate, expand, contract, recur, stabilize, terminate,
reopen, or persist after its originating mechanism has stopped — and none
of those transitions alone proves that Harm has emerged.

HETM closes the Harm Exposure layer by governing exactly when Exposure
changes state and when HAGA™ may legitimately move from governing the
possibility of Harm to asking whether Harm has actually begun to exist.
