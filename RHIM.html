RHIM — Residual Harm Identification Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The RHIM — Residual Harm Identification Module governs how Harm that
continues to remain after materially relevant Recovery has been evaluated
is identified as a candidate for Residual Harm and distinguished from
temporary unrecovered conditions, continuing active Harm, newly emerging
Harm, unresolved Recovery uncertainty, historical Harm, and consequences
that no longer constitute current Harm.

RHIM determines whether materially relevant Harm remains after Recovery,
what remaining Harm belongs to the original governed Harm lifecycle, which
subjects, objects, components, domains, locations, or effects it continues
to affect, how its boundary may differ from the original Harm Boundary,
and whether the remaining condition has a sufficient governed basis to be
identified as Residual Harm.

It establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Recovery Evaluation + Remaining Harm Candidate Set →
Remaining Harm Examination → Residual Qualification → Residual Boundary →
Residual Harm Identification → Governed Residual Harm Identification

Its central question is:

What Harm continues to remain after Recovery, and which remaining
conditions can legitimately be identified as Residual Harm?

  • Operational Role


HREM determines:

what has recovered and what remains unrecovered.

RHIM determines:

which remaining Harm has become Residual Harm.

Its operational role is:

to convert materially supported remaining Harm into a governed Residual
Harm identification without treating every unrecovered condition as
residual.

This distinction is fundamental:

Unrecovered Condition ≠ Residual Harm by Default

  • Position Within HRRHS


The HRRHS governance flow is:

Recovery Initiation → Recovery Progress → Recovery Evaluation → Residual
Harm Identification → Residual Harm State

RHIM governs the fourth stage:

Residual Harm Identification

It receives principally:

Governed Harm Recovery Evaluation

and:

Remaining Harm Candidate Set

It produces:

Governed Residual Harm Identification

for:

RHSM — Residual Harm State Module

  • Governed Object


The primary governed object is:

Remaining Harm potentially qualifying as Residual Harm

RHIM does not govern all remaining differences from the pre-Harm
condition.

A changed condition is not necessarily Harm.

Likewise, an unrecovered condition is not necessarily Residual Harm.

Therefore:

Post-Harm Difference ≠ Residual Harm

Unrestored Condition ≠ Residual Harm

Remaining Harm Candidate ≠ Residual Harm Established

  • Foundational Residual Harm Principle


The foundational rule is:

Residual Harm is Harm that remains materially present after relevant
Recovery has been sufficiently evaluated and cannot legitimately be
represented as recovered, eliminated, absent, or merely historical.

Residual Harm is therefore a current governed Harm condition.

It is not simply:

evidence that Harm once occurred.

  • Residual Harm Qualification


A remaining condition may qualify for Residual Harm Identification where
there is sufficient basis that:

Harm remains materially present

the remaining Harm belongs to the relevant governed Harm lifecycle

Recovery has been sufficiently evaluated for the relevant condition

the condition is not merely a temporary unresolved Recovery observation

and:

the remaining condition still constitutes Harm

Qualification must remain appropriate to the Harm being governed.

  • Recovery Evaluation Sufficiency


Residual Harm should not be established prematurely where Recovery remains
too uncertain or insufficiently evaluated.

Therefore:

Recovery Incomplete ≠ Residual Harm Established by Default

A condition may remain:

Remaining Harm Candidate

until sufficient Recovery evaluation exists.

This prevents ordinary Recovery progression from being prematurely
classified as Residual Harm.

  • Residual Harm and Ongoing Recovery


Residual Harm does not universally require all Recovery activity to have
ended.

Some Harm may become sufficiently established as residual while other
dimensions continue recovering.

Therefore:

Recovery Continuing ≠ Residual Harm Impossible

The relevant question is whether the particular remaining Harm has a
sufficient basis for Residual identification.

  • Residual Harm and Recovery Limits


Recovery may reach a materially supported limit.

Conceptually:

Recovery → Achievable Recovery Condition → Harm Still Remains

The remaining Harm may then qualify as Residual Harm.

However:

Current Recovery Limit ≠ Permanent Recovery Limit by Default

Future conditions, technologies, interventions, resources, or knowledge
may alter what is recoverable.

  • Residual Harm and Partial Recovery


Partial Recovery may naturally coexist with Residual Harm.

Conceptually:

Original Harm → Partial Recovery → Residual Harm

This is not a contradiction.

Canonical principle:

Recovery and Residual Harm describe different parts of the post-Harm
reality.

  • Residual Harm and Successful Recovery


Successful Recovery does not require that every Harm consequence
disappear.

A legitimate Recovery Target may be achieved while Harm remains.

Therefore:

Recovery Target Achieved + Residual Harm = Valid Governed Condition

and:

Residual Harm ≠ Recovery Failure

  • Residual Harm and Pre-Harm Difference


A post-Recovery condition may differ permanently from the pre-Harm state
without constituting Residual Harm.

For example, a system may operate through a different but non-harmful
configuration.

Therefore:

Difference from Pre-Harm State ≠ Residual Harm

RHIM identifies Harm, not historical difference.

  • Residual Harm and Adaptation


Adaptation may conceal continuing Harm.

A subject may regain function by working around a permanent impairment.

Therefore:

Adapted Function ≠ Residual Harm Eliminated

RHIM must examine whether Harm remains beneath or alongside the adapted
condition.

  • Residual Harm and Compensation


Compensation may address consequences associated with Harm without
eliminating the Harm itself.

Therefore:

Compensation ≠ Residual Harm Elimination

A compensated Harm may remain Residual Harm.

Conversely, compensation may continue after Harm has ceased.

The two objects remain separate.

  • Residual Harm and Dependency


A subject may regain function only because a continuing dependency
compensates for remaining Harm.

RHIM must preserve whether the underlying Harm remains.

Therefore:

Dependency-Supported Recovery ≠ Residual Harm Absence

External support may manage consequences without removing the Harm
condition.

  • Residual Harm vs Active Continuing Harm


Not every continuing Harm is Residual Harm.

Some Harm may still be:

actively developing

propagating

accumulating

compounding

or:

being newly produced

Such Harm remains governed through the appropriate earlier HAGA™ lifecycle
spaces.

Canonical distinction:

Continuing Harm ≠ Residual Harm by Default

Residual identification must not hide active Harm production.

  • Residual Harm vs New Harm


New Harm emerging during Recovery must not automatically be absorbed into
the residual condition of the original Harm.

Therefore:

New Harm ≠ Residual Harm of Original Harm by Default

Where appropriate, the new Harm enters its own governed HAGA™ lifecycle.

  • Residual Harm vs Historical Harm


Historical Harm may have existed but no longer remain materially present.

Therefore:

Historical Harm ≠ Residual Harm

Residual Harm concerns what continues to exist now.

A record of past Harm remains evidence of history, not proof of current
Residual Harm.

  • Residual Harm vs Latent Harm


A Harm may remain materially present without producing continuously
observable effects.

RHIM must therefore allow Residual Harm that is:

latent

intermittent

conditional

or:

periodically expressed

Canonical principle:

Absence of continuous manifestation does not establish absence of Residual
Harm.

  • Residual Harm vs Risk


Residual Harm is existing Harm.

Risk concerns possible future Harm.

Therefore:

Residual Harm ≠ Risk

Residual Harm may create future risk, but the objects remain distinct.

  • Residual Harm Boundary


The Residual Harm Boundary identifies what belongs to the remaining
governed Harm after Recovery.

It may include materially relevant:

subjects

objects

effects

domains

locations

time conditions

and:

downstream consequences

The Residual Harm Boundary must correspond to current post-Recovery
reality.

  • Original Harm Boundary vs Residual Harm Boundary


The Residual Harm Boundary may differ substantially from the original Harm
Boundary.

Conceptually:

Original Harm Boundary → Recovery → Residual Harm Boundary

Some originally affected components may recover fully.

Others may remain harmed.

Therefore:

Original Harm Boundary ≠ Residual Harm Boundary by Default

  • Boundary Contraction


Recovery may reduce the Harm Boundary.

For example:

10 affected components → 3 components remain harmed

The Residual Harm Boundary should reflect the remaining three rather than
preserve the historical ten as currently harmed.

Canonical principle:

Historical Harm Scope must not inflate current Residual Harm Scope.

  • Boundary Redistribution


Residual Harm may remain in a different distribution from the original
Harm.

For example, direct Harm may recover while downstream Harm persists
elsewhere.

Therefore:

Residual Harm Location ≠ Original Harm Location by Default

RHIM must follow the Harm that remains.

  • Residual Harm Transformation


Remaining Harm may differ in form from the original Harm.

A temporary operational Harm may leave a persistent capability impairment.

A physical Harm may produce a lasting functional consequence.

Therefore:

Residual Harm Form ≠ Original Harm Form by Default

The connection must remain governed without requiring identical
manifestation.

  • Residual Harm Across Domains


Original Harm may span several domains while Residual Harm remains only in
selected domains.

For example:

Physical Harm → Recovered

Operational Harm → Recovered

Financial Harm → Residual

or:

Environmental Harm → Residual

RHIM must preserve domain-specific Residual Harm.

  • Composite Residual Harm


Composite Harm may leave residual conditions in selected components.

For example:

Component A → No Residual Harm

Component B → Residual Harm Identified

Component C → Identification Uncertain

Therefore:

Residual Harm in One Component ≠ Residual Harm Across Entire Composite by
Default

  • Collective Residual Harm


Where Harm affects a group or population, Residual Harm may remain
concentrated in a subset.

A strong collective Recovery average must not erase remaining Harm
affecting a materially significant subgroup.

Therefore:

Collective Recovery ≠ Universal Residual Harm Absence

  • Distributed Residual Harm


Distributed systems, infrastructure, organizations, environments, or
societies may retain Residual Harm in particular locations or nodes.

Therefore:

Recovery in Most Locations ≠ No Distributed Residual Harm

RHIM must preserve materially relevant residual concentration.

  • Residual Harm Persistence


Residual Harm may persist across time.

Persistence may be:

continuous

intermittent

conditional

recurrent

or:

uncertain

  • Residual Harm Severity


Residual Harm may be:

substantial

or otherwise domain-appropriately characterized.

However:

Reduced Severity ≠ Harm Absence

Harm may become less severe through Recovery while remaining legitimate

  • Residual Harm Accumulation


Residual Harm may continue to accumulate even after Recovery of other
conditions.

Where accumulation represents active Harm Change or Propagation, HCPS

RHIM must not use “residual” to remove actively changing Harm from earlier

Therefore:

Residual Classification ≠ Exemption from Harm Change Governance

  • Residual Harm Reversibility


Residual Harm may later become recoverable.

Therefore:

Residual Harm ≠ Irreversible Harm

A Harm condition can legitimately be residual today and recoverable later.

New interventions, knowledge, resources, environmental changes, or
technologies may alter the Recovery possibility.

  • Residual Harm Permanence


Permanence should not be assumed merely because Harm has been classified
as residual.

Therefore:

Residual Harm ≠ Permanent Harm by Default

Where permanence is materially supported, it may be represented.

Where it is uncertain, uncertainty must remain explicit.

Therefore:

Residual Harm Association ≠ General Causation Determination

A minimum RHIM implementation requires exactly eight capabilities.

  • Remaining Harm Examination

  • Residual Qualification

  • Residual Harm Boundary Establishment


Identify the current subjects, objects, effects, domains, locations,

irreversible, and uncertain Residual Harm without assuming that residual

Maintain sufficient traceability to the governed Harm lifecycle while
preserving boundaries with new Harm, active Harm Change, general
causation, accountability, liability, and value governance.

  • Uncertainty & Reassessment Governance


Preserve uncertainty and require reassessment when Recovery, Harm,
boundary, persistence, or the identification basis materially changes.

  • Canonical Boundary


The RHIM — Residual Harm Identification Module governs how materially
relevant Harm remaining after Recovery Evaluation is examined, qualified,
bounded, differentiated, and identified as Residual Harm. It governs
remaining Harm qualification, Residual Harm Boundary formation, boundary
contraction and redistribution, transformed Harm, composite, collective,
distributed and cross-domain Residual Harm, persistence characteristics,
latent Harm, reversibility, permanence uncertainty, lifecycle
traceability, identification uncertainty, and reassessment. RHIM does not
govern Recovery Progress or Recovery Evaluation as its primary object,
determine the subsequent Residual Harm State, convert active continuing or
newly emerging Harm into residual merely for convenience, perform general
causation or forensic investigation, determine accountability,
responsibility, liability, claims or compensation, govern value
consequences, or establish Harm Resolution or Finality.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Residual Harm is not everything that failed to return to the past. It is
the Harm that remains in the present.

It may persist after substantial Recovery, exist beneath adapted or
dependency-supported function, contract to only part of the original Harm
Boundary, change form, remain latent, or become recoverable again as
conditions change.

RHIM ensures that Recovery does not erase continuing Harm—and that
historical difference is not mislabeled as Harm merely because the
pre-Harm condition was not restored.

Residual Harm begins where legitimate Recovery evaluation ends and Harm
still remains.
