Canonical Definition

End-of-Support Management Module (ESMM) is the Core Module within
Software Retirement Standard (SRTS) that governs how changes to the
availability, scope, responsibility, and termination of software
support and maintenance are identified, evaluated, communicated,
controlled, and preserved throughout the software retirement
lifecycle.

ESMM establishes end-of-support as an identifiable governance condition

affecting the relationship between a software object, its support
provider, supported capabilities, maintenance obligations, security
update availability, operational users, dependent systems, and
continuing responsibilities.

The module distinguishes support termination from maintenance termination,

support availability from support effectiveness, vendor support status
from actual operational use, and the end of a support obligation from
the elimination of operational risk.

It requires support transitions to remain connected to identifiable

software versions, support arrangements, applicable time or event
conditions, affected stakeholders, available assistance, known
limitations, continued operational dependencies, and required
retirement or replacement pathways.

ESMM recognizes that software may remain in production after formal
support ends, that different support capabilities may terminate at
different times, and that third-party, internal, community, or
extended support may provide different levels of continuing
assistance.

It also recognizes that unsupported software may remain subject to
operational, security, contractual, evidentiary, and continuity
obligations even when ordinary maintenance is no longer available.

ESMM governs the classification and controlled treatment of these

conditions without treating the cessation of support as proof that
software has been retired.

It applies to enterprise applications, commercial software, open-source

components, cloud services, operating systems, firmware, AI systems,
autonomous software agents, and embedded or industrial software.

Its principal governance output is the Software End-of-Support
Profile.

Operational Question

How can software support and maintenance transitions remain
identifiable, evidence-supported, and governable when support
capabilities, providers, contractual conditions, security update
availability, operational dependencies, and continuing
responsibilities may change or terminate while the software itself
remains in use?

Its governance remains applicable when software support ends before

  • Governed Space

Software End-of-Support Governance

This governed space concerns the identification, modification, reduction,

transfer, and termination of software support and maintenance
capabilities.

It begins when a software support condition becomes relevant to an
identifiable software object.

It includes support scope, provider responsibility, service availability,

maintenance capability, security updates, transition conditions,
affected stakeholders, operational restrictions, and continuing
obligations.

It remains active where software continues operating after support
ends or where support-related responsibilities remain unresolved.

It concludes for a defined support relationship only when the
applicable support transition has been established and remaining
obligations have been appropriately resolved, transferred, or
preserved under governance.

Foundational Principle

The termination of software support changes the conditions under which

software can be maintained, corrected, secured, and relied upon; it
does not automatically terminate the software's operational presence
or the responsibilities associated with its continued use.

Support status must be established according to the actual
capabilities and obligations available for the identified software
object.

Support Terminated ≠ Software Withdrawn.

Support Available ≠ Support Sufficient.

Vendor Support Ended ≠ All Support Capability Eliminated.

Support and Maintenance Distinction

ESMM must preserve the difference between support and maintenance.

Support may involve assistance, diagnosis, guidance, recovery, and
communication.

Maintenance may involve software modification, correction, updating,
and continued technical adaptation.

A software product may retain technical assistance while no longer
receiving ordinary maintenance updates.

Conversely, a software component may continue receiving community
maintenance without a formal support service.

Support Capability ≠ Maintenance Capability.

Support and Operational Reality

ESMM must distinguish declared support status from actual operational
conditions.

A software product may be officially unsupported but remain
operationally critical.

A product may be listed as supported while essential correction
capabilities are unavailable.

A vendor may announce extended support that applies only to specific
versions or configurations.

An internally maintained component may remain technically serviceable
without a formal external support contract.

Support governance must therefore evaluate the actual support
relationship and available capabilities.

Declared Support Status ≠ Verified Support Capability.

Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum ESMM implementation must establish eight governance layers.
Layer 1 — Software and Support Identity

Identify the software object, applicable version, support provider, and

support arrangement. Layer 2 — Support Capability Definition

Establish which support and maintenance capabilities are available,
restricted, or unavailable. Layer 3 — Support Transition Recognition

Identify the origin, effective condition, scope, and timing of material

support changes. Layer 4 — Operational Consequence Assessment

Identify continuing software use, dependencies, affected stakeholders,

security exposure, and operational restrictions. Layer 5 —
Responsibility and Authority

Establish support responsibility, applicable authority, provider transfer

conditions, and continuing obligations. Layer 6 — Transition
Communication

Preserve material notices, affected recipients, required actions, and

communication evidence. Layer 7 — Support Status Verification

Verify the resulting support condition and preserve uncertainty,

exceptions, and reassessment requirements. Layer 8 — Support
Continuity and Handoff

Cross-Module Boundary

RPM → ESMM

RPM identifies support and maintenance conditions relevant to retirement

planning.

ESMM governs the detailed recognition, assessment, transition, and

continuing consequences of those support conditions.

ESMM → SMM

ESMM identifies support capabilities, limitations, and transition

conditions affecting migration.

SMM governs the actual migration of software functions, data, users, and

operational dependencies.

ESMM → RDMM

ESMM identifies unsupported software relationships and dependencies

affected by support termination.

RDMM governs residual dependencies that remain associated with
software retirement.

ESMM → RCCLM

ESMM establishes the resulting support status and continuing support

obligations.

RCCLM evaluates whether these obligations have been appropriately addressed

within the retirement closure determination.

End-of-support governance establishes the support condition. It does
not establish that the software has ceased operating or that
retirement is complete.

Practical Use Case 1 — Unsupported Industrial Software

An industrial facility operates equipment controlled by software that
is no longer supported by its original vendor.

The equipment continues performing an essential production function.

A software inventory lists the product as obsolete, but the organization

has not established whether security updates, technical assistance, or
corrective maintenance remain available.

ESMM establishes:

Software Identity → Support Provider → Support Scope → Support

Capabilities → End-of-Support Condition → Operational Dependencies →
Continuing Responsibilities

The assessment identifies:

  • Ordinary vendor maintenance has ended
  • Security updates are no longer provided for the installed
    version
  • The equipment remains operationally necessary
  • Immediate software replacement is not technically feasible
  • A third-party provider can supply limited diagnostic assistance
    but
  • Continued operation requires explicit restrictions and a
    governed

cannot provide comprehensive security corrections. replacement
pathway.

The resulting support classification is:

Unsupported — Restricted Operation; Alternative Diagnostic Support

Available.

The Software End-of-Support Profile preserves the actual support

capabilities, operational restrictions, responsible function, and
continuing retirement requirements.

Alternative Technical Assistance ≠ Full Software Support.

Practical Use Case 2 — AI Service Support Termination

An organization relies on an external AI inference service integrated into

several internal applications.

The service provider announces that support for the current model
version will end.

A successor model is available, but it uses a modified interface and
produces different output characteristics.

ESMM establishes:

Model Service Identity → Supported Version → Provider Notice → Support End

Condition → Affected Consumers → Security and Maintenance Implications
→ Migration Requirements

The assessment identifies:

  • The support announcement applies only to the older model version
  • Existing applications continue calling the old endpoint
  • Technical assistance will remain available temporarily, but
  • Several applications require compatibility assessment before
    migration
  • The provider's announced support termination does not establish
    that

model-specific corrections will stop. all dependent applications have
transitioned.

The resulting support classification is:

Support Transition Pending — Migration and Compatibility Requirements

Outstanding.

ESMM preserves the transition conditions and directs the applicable

migration and dependency requirements to subsequent governance.

Successor Model Available ≠ Existing Integration Ready for Retirement.

Canonical Boundary

End-of-Support Management Module (ESMM) governs the identification,

classification, transition, verification, and continuing consequences
of software support and maintenance conditions throughout the
retirement lifecycle.

It establishes the governance relationship between identifiable software

objects, support providers, available capabilities, effective support
conditions, affected operational dependencies, and continuing
responsibilities.

ESMM does not determine that software is retired merely because
support has ended.

It does not independently execute software migration, resolve residual
dependencies, withdraw software from operation, or establish
retirement closure.

It does not replace contractual interpretation, legal determination,
security remediation, or specialized lifecycle governance.

Its principal governance output is the:

Software End-of-Support Profile

This output establishes the evidence-supported support condition of the

software and the obligations that remain relevant to its continued
operation, migration, or retirement.

Canonical Closing Statement

The end of software support is not the end of the software's
consequences.

An unsupported application may continue processing essential
information.

A discontinued library may remain embedded within active systems.

An industrial controller may depend on firmware that no longer
receives security updates.

An AI service may remain integrated into operational applications
after its provider announces the end of support.

And an organization may continue relying on software whose available
maintenance capabilities are substantially narrower than its declared
support status suggests.

ESMM establishes the governance structure required to make these conditions

explicit.

It identifies the actual support capabilities available for each software

object and applicable configuration.

It distinguishes maintenance termination from broader support
termination.

It preserves the consequences of continued unsupported operation.

It governs transitions between support providers, extended support

arrangements, and restricted operational conditions.

It connects support changes to migration, dependency management, and

retirement requirements.

And it ensures that the disappearance of a support service does not
erase the operational responsibilities associated with software that
remains in use.

Support governance must follow the capabilities that actually exist,
the software that actually operates, and the obligations that actually
remain.

End-of-Support Management Module (ESMM) transforms support termination

from an administrative lifecycle announcement into a verifiable
governance condition that preserves operational awareness,
responsibility continuity, and the basis for controlled software
retirement.
