Canonical Definition System

Canonical Definition

Behavioral Compatibility Module (BCOM) defines the structural conditions under
which AI behavior remains behaviorally compatible with humans, AI systems,
autonomous systems, organizations, and governed operational environments while
preserving governance integrity, accountability, and predictable interaction.

BCOM governs behavioral compatibility.

The module establishes the governance conditions required to ensure that
independently operating participants can interact without creating behavioral
conflict, governance inconsistency, or operational incompatibility.

Systems may communicate successfully.

Their behavior must also remain compatible.

BCOM governs that compatibility.

Module Operational Role

BCOM defines the behavioral-compatibility layer of BIS.

Its role is to preserve behavioral compatibility across interacting governance
participants.

Module Operational Space

BCOM governs:

  • behavioral compatibility
  • compatible AI behavior
  • Human-AI compatibility
  • AI-to-AI compatibility
  • governance compatibility
  • collaborative behavior
  • operational compatibility
  • compatibility governance


The module applies wherever organizations require multiple participants to
cooperate through compatible behavior.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • compatible behavior,
  • governance consistency,
  • predictable interaction,
  • accountable cooperation,
  • operational alignment,
  • trustworthy collaboration.


Its function is to ensure that interaction remains behaviorally compatible
before coordination begins.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Behavioral Compatibility Object

The organization must define which behavioral interactions require
compatibility governance.

2. Define Compatibility Conditions

The system must define governance conditions including:

  • behavioral alignment,
  • governance consistency,
  • interaction compatibility,
  • accountability,
  • operational predictability,
  • collaboration requirements.


3. Define Compatibility Failure Detection Logic

The system must identify:

  • behavioral incompatibility,
  • governance conflicts,
  • inconsistent interaction,
  • coordination failures,
  • operational misalignment,
  • governance-invalid compatibility.


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

Governance response may include:

  • compatibility assessment,
  • governance review,
  • behavioral alignment,
  • operational adjustment,
  • coordination improvement,
  • escalation.


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • compatibility evaluations,
  • governance reviews,
  • behavioral interactions,
  • alignment decisions,
  • supporting evidence,
  • resulting governance outcomes.


An AI behavioral environment must not be considered behaviorally interoperable
if materially significant behavioral compatibility cannot be independently
demonstrated, reviewed, validated, preserved, and governed.

Use Case 1 — Multi-Agent Healthcare Environment

Scenario

Several AI systems collaborate with clinicians to coordinate diagnostics,
treatment planning, and patient monitoring.

Application

BCOM ensures that behavioral interactions remain compatible across all
participating AI systems and healthcare professionals.

Result

The healthcare organization strengthens coordinated decision-making, reduces
behavioral conflicts, and preserves governance consistency across clinical
operations.

Use Case 2 — Smart Manufacturing Ecosystem

Scenario

Multiple autonomous robots and AI controllers coordinate production, quality
assurance, and logistics within the same factory.

Application

BCOM governs behavioral compatibility to ensure that independently operating
systems cooperate without conflicting operational behaviors.

Result

The manufacturer improves coordination, operational reliability, and
trustworthy collaboration across the autonomous production environment.

Canonical Closing Statement

Interoperability begins with compatibility. When behavior remains compatible
across independent participants, cooperation becomes predictable, governance
remains coherent, and complex AI ecosystems can operate with confidence.
