HRDM — Harm Recognition Delay Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HRDM — Harm Recognition Delay Module governs how and why Harm
Recognition fails to occur immediately after materially relevant
Recognition Signals become available, including delays caused by detection
gaps, information fragmentation, transmission failure, classification
failure, uncertainty, suppression, organizational processes, escalation
failure, human or machine limitations, distributed execution, evidence
gaps, or other materially relevant barriers.

HRDM establishes the transition:

Governed Recognition Signal State → Recognition Availability → Delay
Identification → Delay Qualification → Delay Continuation / Removal →
Governed Recognition Delay State

Its central question is:

Why has materially recognizable Harm not yet become recognized for
governance, and what is sustaining that delay?

  • Operational Role


HRIM identifies information relevant to Harm Recognition.

HRSM structures that information into governed Recognition Signals.

HRDM governs the next problem:

Why does Recognition still not occur even though materially relevant
signals exist?

A governance system may possess enough information to begin Recognition
but still fail to recognize Harm because of:

poor monitoring

reporting barriers

technical routing failures

uncertainty

human error

automation failure

conflicting signals

institutional incentives

suppression

HRDM makes those delays explicit and governable.

  • Foundational Delay Principle


The foundational distinction is:

Recognition Delay ≠ Harm Absence

A delayed Recognition process changes neither whether Harm exists nor when
Harm began.

Canonical principle:

Delayed Recognition does not create delayed Harm.

  • Delay Trigger


HRDM begins where:

Recognition Signals Exist + Recognition Has Not Materially Progressed

Conceptually:

Governed Recognition Signal → Expected Recognition Progression → No
Progression → Delay Trigger

A delay trigger does not itself establish governance failure.

The reason for the delay must be assessed.

  • Delay Requires a Reference Point


Recognition Delay must be measured against a meaningful reference point.

Possible reference points include:

Without a reference point, delay cannot be meaningfully governed.

  • Delay Start


A Recognition Delay may begin when the information necessary for
legitimate Recognition becomes materially available but Recognition does
not proceed.

Therefore:

Delay Start ≠ Harm Emergence Time

and:

Delay Start ≠ Signal Origin Time by Default

  • Delay End


Recognition Delay ends when the specific barrier sustaining the delay is
removed or Recognition legitimately progresses.

Possible endings include:

Recognition begins

Recognition is confirmed

signal is legitimately rejected

misclassification is corrected

authority is obtained

information becomes sufficient

suppression is removed

The end of delay does not define Harm emergence.

  • Delay ≠ Latency


HRDM must remain distinct from HLRM.

Recognition Delay asks:

What prevented or slowed Recognition?

How much temporal separation exists between Harm and Recognition?

Therefore:

Delay Cause ≠ Latency Duration

  • No Delay ≠ Immediate Recognition

Therefore:

No Recognition Delay ≠ Zero Recognition Latency

This distinction is critical for HLRM.

A minimum HRDM implementation requires eight capabilities.

  • Delay Reference Point

  • Delay Cause Classification


Distinguish detection, observation, reporting, transmission, processing,

mechanisms.

  • Composite Delay Governance


Support sequential, parallel, multi-cause, distributed, cross-domain,

  • Governance Logic


HRDM applies the following logic:

Do governed Recognition Signals exist?

If no:

→ HRDM has no Recognition Delay to govern.

If yes:

→ determine whether Recognition has materially progressed.

Has appropriate Recognition occurred?

If yes:

→ no active Recognition Delay, subject to historical recording.

If no:

→ identify a Delay reference point.

When did sufficient Recognition-relevant information become available?

→ establish the earliest materially supportable delay baseline.

What is preventing Recognition progression?

Assess:

detection

reporting

transmission

processing

classification

interpretation

correlation

validation

identity resolution

authority

escalation

organizational structure

human review

machine processing

suppression

other barriers

Are several barriers present?

→ govern the composite delay structure.

Is the delay legitimate and proportionate?

If yes:

→ preserve justified pending state and reassess.

If no:

→ classify disproportionate or unresolved delay as appropriate.

Is suppression or concealment suspected?

→ preserve that state without prematurely assigning accountability.

Has the delaying condition been removed?

If yes:

→ update or close the Delay State.

Does Recognition now have sufficient conditions to proceed?

If yes:

→ hand forward the Governed Recognition Delay State for latency and
Confirmation governance.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HRDM — Harm Recognition Delay Module governs how and why Harm
Recognition fails to occur immediately after materially relevant
Recognition Signals become available, including detection, observation,
reporting, transmission, processing, classification, interpretation,
correlation, validation, integrity, identity, authority, escalation,
organizational, human, machine, distributed, cross-domain,
cross-organizational, suppression, concealment, incentive-driven, and
other materially relevant Recognition delays. It governs Delay Start,
Delay End, duration, persistence, justification, proportionality,
multi-cause delay, sequential and parallel delay chains, uncertainty,
conflict, reassessment, and historical continuity. It does not determine
Harm Emergence, measure the full Recognition Latency lifecycle owned by
HLRM, confirm Harm Recognition, establish the Harm Boundary, formally
determine Harm, establish general causation, assign accountability or
responsibility, determine liability, or replace evidence, validation,
