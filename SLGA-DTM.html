Canonical Definition

The Development Traceability Module (DTM) governs how material

relationships between software creation objectives, applicable
requirements, creation actors, development methods, authority
conditions, execution activities, constituent actions, tools, software
artifacts, transformations, composition states, and supporting
evidence are identified, established, maintained, evaluated, and
reconstructed throughout software creation.

DTM establishes the methodological conditions under which a software

creation history may be recognized as sufficiently traceable to
support the reconstruction of how, why, by whom or by what, under
which authority, and through which material actions a software
artifact came into existence.

It governs the continuity and evidentiary support of development

relationships across human-directed programming, AI-assisted
development, autonomous coding agents, multi-agent systems, automated
software generation, software transformation, and hybrid creation
processes.

DTM distinguishes the existence of development logs from governed

traceability, recorded relationships from evidence-supported
relationships, and reconstruction of a creation history from proof
that every underlying action was legitimate or that the resulting
software satisfies its governing requirements.

Its principal output is:

Governed Development Traceability State

Its foundational distinction is:

Recorded Development Activity ≠ Governed Development Traceability.

Operational Role

The operational role of DTM is:

To ensure that material software creation decisions, actors, authority
references, execution activities, constituent actions, artifact
transformations, and resulting software outputs remain connected
through identifiable, evidence-supported, temporally meaningful, and
reconstructable relationships across the software creation process.

DTM establishes the development traceability layer of SCS.

It connects the relevant governance states produced by the preceding SCS

Core Modules without replacing their respective responsibilities.

DTM does not independently determine that an action was authorized,
that an artifact possesses sufficient integrity, or that a software
product is functionally correct.

It preserves the relationships and evidence required for competent

governance processes to make those determinations.

Traceability relationships may be established continuously rather than

Central Operational Question

How can the complete material history of software creation remain
reconstructable across requirements, actors, authority, methods,
tools, actions, transformations, and resulting artifacts without
confusing recorded technical activity with evidence-supported
governance traceability?

Development Traceability Governance Object

Every DTM assessment shall identify the Development Traceability Governance

Object.

The object represents a defined software creation activity, artifact

history, or material relationship set whose traceability is being
governed.

It should identify:

Traceability Identity: The identifiable traceability assessment or
record.

Creation Reference: The relevant software creation activity.

Creation Objective: The intended software outcome.

Requirements Reference: The applicable governed requirements basis.

Actor References: Material human, organizational, AI, or autonomous

participants.

Authority References: Relevant authorization and delegation
conditions.

Method References: Applicable development methods.

Execution References: Material development activities and actions.

Artifact References: Resulting and contributing software artifacts.

Transformation References: Material artifact modification events.

Composition References: Relevant constituent and composite artifact

relationships.

Evidence References: Records supporting the claimed relationships.

Traceability State: The current governance condition of the relationship

set.

The traceability object shall remain distinguishable from any individual

record or artifact within it.

Traceability Object ≠ Individual Development Log.

Development Traceability Scope

DTM shall establish the scope of the software creation history being

assessed.

Scope may concern:

  • A complete software creation activity
  • A particular feature
  • A software component
  • A development task
  • An autonomous agent execution
  • A multi-agent development workflow
  • An artifact transformation
  • A composite software artifact
  • A defined development period
  • A specific creation-governance question

The required traceability depth shall reflect the materiality of the

governed activity.

DTM does not require every insignificant technical event to be
preserved indefinitely.

It requires material relationships to remain sufficiently
reconstructable.

Traceability Scope ≠ Unlimited Event Collection.

Development Traceability Identity

DTM shall establish identifiable references for material traceability

relationships.

Identity may be supported by:

  • Traceability object identifiers
  • Creation activity references
  • Requirements identifiers
  • Actor identifiers
  • Execution-event identifiers
  • Artifact identifiers
  • Transformation identifiers
  • Composition identifiers
  • Evidence references

A relationship identifier shall not be treated as sufficient proof
that the relationship is accurate.

Traceability Identifier ≠ Verified Traceability Relationship.

Traceability Relationship Classification

DTM shall classify material development relationships according to their

governance meaning.

Relevant relationship classes may include:

Origin Relationship: Connects a creation object or artifact to its

supported origin.

Purpose Relationship: Connects an activity to its intended objective.

Requirements Relationship: Connects creation activities or artifacts to

applicable requirements.

Authority Relationship: Connects material actions to their authority

references.

Actor Relationship: Connects activities to the actors or mechanisms

performing them.

Method Relationship: Connects execution to the recognized development

method.

Action Relationship: Connects constituent actions to the broader creation

activity.

Tool-Path Relationship: Connects actions to the tools and paths used.

Transformation Relationship: Connects source and resulting artifact

states.

Composition Relationship: Connects constituent artifacts to composite

outputs.

Evidence Relationship: Connects claims to supporting evidence.

Continuity Relationship: Connects historical creation states across

change.

These classes shall remain distinguishable where their evidentiary
meaning differs.

Recorded Association ≠ Governed Relationship.

Creation Objective Traceability

DTM shall preserve the relationship between material development activities

and the intended software creation objective.

Relevant questions include:

  • Which objective initiated the activity?
  • Which material actions were undertaken to achieve it?
  • Which artifacts resulted?
  • Did the objective change?
  • Were new objectives introduced?
  • Which evidence supports the relationships?

An activity may be technically related to a project without being
connected to the authorized creation objective.

Project Association ≠ Creation Objective Traceability.

Requirements-to-Creation Traceability

DTM shall preserve material relationships between governed requirements and

software creation activities.

Relevant references may include:

  • Requirements baseline
  • Applicable requirement identity
  • Creation task
  • Implementation activity
  • Resulting artifact
  • Relevant verification reference
  • Requirements change history

DTM shall distinguish a recorded requirements link from evidence that
the artifact actually satisfies the requirement.

Requirement-to-Code Link ≠ Requirement Satisfaction.

Detailed requirements traceability governance remains within:

RTM — Requirements Traceability Module

under Software Requirements Standard (SRS).

DTM governs the creation-side reconstruction of those relationships.

Creation Authority Traceability

DTM shall preserve material relationships between software creation actions

and their applicable authority references.

These may include:

  • Creation authorization
  • Delegated execution authority
  • Permitted action categories
  • Tool permissions
  • Artifact modification authority
  • Agent delegation
  • Relevant approval events

The existence of an authority reference does not independently
establish that the reference was valid or applicable.

Authority Reference ≠ Established Action Authorization.

DTM preserves the relationship for competent authority assessment.

Actor Traceability

DTM shall identify material actors involved in software creation.

Actors may include:

  • Human developers
  • Development organizations
  • External suppliers
  • AI coding assistants
  • Autonomous agents
  • Multi-agent systems
  • Automated development tools
  • Build and generation mechanisms

Traceability shall distinguish actor identity from the actor's role and

authority.

A recorded username may not establish the identity of the actual human
or autonomous mechanism responsible for an action.

Recorded User Identity ≠ Established Execution Actor.

Human Development Traceability

DTM applies to human-directed software creation.

Relevant relationships may include:

  • Assigned development tasks
  • Implementation decisions
  • Source-code modifications
  • Review activities
  • Artifact creation
  • Relevant approvals
  • Material exceptions

Human participation does not eliminate the need for traceability.

A developer's recollection may support reconstruction but shall not
automatically substitute for sufficient contemporaneous evidence.

Human Testimony ≠ Complete Development History by default.

AI-Assisted Development Traceability

DTM applies where AI systems contribute to software creation under
human direction.

Relevant traceability relationships may include:

  • Human creation objective
  • AI-assisted task
  • Material input context
  • Generation mechanism
  • Generated output
  • Human modification
  • Accepted artifact state
  • Supporting evidence

DTM shall distinguish AI-generated content from human-authored content

where the distinction is material and sufficiently supported.

AI Assistance Record ≠ Complete AI Contribution Traceability.

Autonomous Development Traceability

DTM applies to autonomous software creation.

Relevant relationships may include:

  • Delegated objective
  • Autonomous actor identity
  • Execution plan
  • Constituent actions
  • Tool invocations
  • Data access
  • Artifact modifications
  • Exceptions
  • Interventions
  • Resulting artifacts

An autonomous system may complete a task while its material execution
path remains insufficiently reconstructable.

Autonomous Task Completion ≠ Autonomous Execution Traceability.

Detailed autonomous execution control remains within ASCCM.

Multi-Agent Development Traceability

DTM shall preserve material relationships across multiple agents

contributing to software creation.

These may include:

  • Agent identities
  • Agent roles
  • Task delegation
  • Handoff events
  • Shared context
  • Tool actions
  • Artifact contributions
  • Integration decisions
  • Resulting outputs

Traceability shall preserve material authority and execution continuity

across agent boundaries.

Agent Handoff Record ≠ Complete Delegation Traceability.

Development Method Traceability

DTM shall preserve the relationship between creation activities and the

applicable development method.

Its principal reference is:

Governed Development Method Selection State

from DMSM.

Relevant information may include:

  • Selected method
  • Method applicability
  • Material method conditions
  • Actual execution approach
  • Method deviations
  • Method changes
  • Reassessment references

A method may be documented while the actual creation process
materially differs from it.

Documented Method ≠ Executed Method.

Development Decision Traceability

DTM shall preserve material development decisions where they affect
the creation outcome or its governance interpretation.

Relevant decisions may include:

  • Implementation approach
  • Component selection
  • Tool selection
  • Architecture-related implementation choices
  • Artifact transformation
  • Scope adaptation
  • Integration strategy
  • Exception handling

Decision traceability should identify:

Decision Context → Decision Actor → Decision Basis → Resulting Action or

Artifact

A recorded decision shall not automatically be treated as justified or

authorized.

Recorded Decision ≠ Governed Decision.

Constituent Action Traceability

DTM shall preserve material relationships between a software creation

objective and the actions performed to achieve it.

A relevant chain may be:

Creation Objective → Delegated Authority → Constituent Action → Tool

Execution → Resulting Artifact State

Constituent actions may include:

  • File access
  • Code generation
  • Repository modification
  • Package installation
  • Command execution
  • Data retrieval
  • Test execution
  • Artifact transformation
  • Agent delegation

DTM shall preserve relevant action identity, actor, context, and
evidence.

Task Traceability ≠ Constituent Action Traceability.

Tool-Path Traceability

DTM shall preserve material relationships between development actions and

the tool paths used.

A tool path may include:

Agent → Tool Interface → External Service → Data Source → Execution Result

Relevant evidence may concern:

  • Tool identity
  • Invocation context
  • Actor
  • Permission reference
  • Material input
  • Resulting output
  • Downstream action
  • Execution outcome

A single successful tool invocation does not establish the legitimacy
of the complete path.

Tool Invocation Success ≠ Governed Tool-Path Validity.

Data and Context Traceability

Software creation may depend on material information inputs.

These may include:

  • Requirements documents
  • Source-code context
  • Retrieved documentation
  • External data
  • Internal databases
  • Agent memory
  • Shared development context
  • Generated intermediate information

DTM shall preserve material relationships between such inputs and the

resulting creation activities where necessary for reconstruction.

It does not independently authorize data access or determine the
legitimacy of every information source.

Data Usage Record ≠ Authorized Data Use.

Software Artifact Traceability

DTM shall preserve material relationships between creation activities and

resulting software artifacts.

Relevant references may include:

  • Artifact identity
  • Artifact version
  • Creation event
  • Contributing actor
  • Development method
  • Material inputs
  • Transformation history
  • Composition references
  • Supporting evidence

DTM consumes relevant integrity information from CRIM.

It does not independently establish complete artifact integrity.

Artifact Link ≠ Artifact Integrity Proof.

Artifact Transformation Traceability

DTM shall preserve material relationships across software artifact

transformations.

The traceability chain is:

Source Artifact State → Transformation Event → Transformation Actor or

Mechanism → Resulting Artifact State

Transformations may include:

  • Editing
  • Refactoring
  • Regeneration
  • Compilation
  • Code translation
  • Optimization
  • Automated rewriting
  • Packaging

A transformation record shall remain connected to the relevant source and

resulting artifact identities.

Transformation Log ≠ Verified Transformation History.

Artifact Composition Traceability

DTM shall preserve material relationships between constituent artifacts and

resulting composite artifacts.

Its principal reference is:

Governed Software Artifact Composition State

from SACM.

Relevant relationships may include:

  • Constituent artifact identity
  • Constituent version
  • Composition role
  • Integration mechanism
  • Composition transformation
  • Resulting composite identity
  • Supporting evidence

A composition manifest may contribute to traceability but does not
independently establish the complete composition history.

Composition Manifest ≠ Complete Composition Traceability.

Generated Artifact Lineage

DTM shall preserve material lineage relationships for generated software

artifacts.

A relevant chain may be:

Generation Objective → Material Inputs → Generation Mechanism → Generated

Artifact → Subsequent Transformation

The required level of detail depends on materiality and available
evidence.

DTM shall distinguish recorded generation facts from inferred
explanations.

Generated Artifact Explanation ≠ Verified Artifact Lineage.

Development Event Identity

DTM shall identify material development events sufficiently to distinguish

them from other events.

Relevant event information may include:

  • Event identifier
  • Event type
  • Actor reference
  • Associated objective
  • Relevant tool
  • Artifact reference
  • Temporal context
  • Supporting evidence

Event identity shall not be confused with event validity.

Recorded Event ≠ Governed Event.

Temporal Traceability

DTM shall preserve relevant temporal relationships between material

development events.

These may include:

  • Event occurrence time
  • Event ordering
  • Relevant version state
  • Authority validity period
  • Requirements applicability period
  • Transformation sequence
  • Agent handoff sequence
  • Evidence creation time

Temporal ordering may be uncertain in distributed development systems.

DTM shall preserve that uncertainty rather than invent a definitive
sequence.

Timestamp Availability ≠ Verified Event Order.

Distributed Development Traceability

Software creation may occur across multiple repositories, systems,
organizations, and execution environments.

DTM shall preserve material cross-environment relationships.

Relevant conditions may include:

  • Actor identity continuity
  • Event correlation
  • Artifact identity continuity
  • Tool-path references
  • Distributed timestamps
  • Evidence source differences
  • Repository transitions
  • Organizational handoffs

A common project identifier does not independently establish complete
cross-system traceability.

Shared Project Identifier ≠ Distributed Traceability Continuity.

Traceability Evidence

DTM shall preserve evidence supporting material development
relationships.

Evidence may include:

  • Requirements records
  • Development decisions
  • Authority references
  • Repository history
  • Tool execution records
  • Agent activity records
  • Artifact identifiers
  • Transformation records
  • Composition manifests
  • Review records
  • Build records
  • Relevant system logs

Evidence requirements shall reflect the materiality and intended governance

use of the traceability relationship.

Log Availability ≠ Traceability Evidence Sufficiency.

Traceability Evidence Competence

DTM shall assess whether evidence sources are competent to support the
specific relationships claimed.

For example:

A repository record may support a recorded modification event.

A tool log may support an invocation claim.

An artifact hash may support content correspondence.

A requirements record may support the existence of a requirement
reference.

An agent-generated explanation may support a proposed reconstruction
hypothesis.

These sources do not establish identical facts.

Evidence Source Availability ≠ Evidence Source Competence.

Traceability Evidence Integrity

DTM shall preserve the integrity of evidence supporting material

traceability claims.

Relevant considerations may include:

  • Evidence identity
  • Source
  • Content correspondence
  • Temporal relevance
  • Preservation history
  • Transformation
  • Access restrictions
  • Material contradictions
  • Missing records

Evidence integrity does not independently establish the correctness of
the claim that the evidence is used to support.

Evidence Integrity ≠ Claim Validity.

Recorded and Inferred Relationships

DTM shall distinguish directly supported relationships from inferred

relationships.

For example:

A build record may directly identify a source version used during
compilation.

An analytical system may infer that a particular generated function
originated from a prior agent instruction.

Both may be useful.

They do not possess the same evidentiary status.

Inferred Relationship ≠ Established Relationship.

Where inference is used, DTM shall preserve the inference basis,
uncertainty, and relevant limitations.

Traceability Completeness

DTM shall assess whether material development relationships are
sufficiently represented for the intended governance purpose.

Completeness may concern:

  • Objective coverage
  • Requirements references
  • Actor identification
  • Authority relationships
  • Material actions
  • Tool paths
  • Artifact transformations
  • Composition relationships
  • Evidence support
  • Temporal continuity

Completeness shall be assessed relative to defined scope and
materiality.

Numerous Traceability Links ≠ Complete Traceability.

Traceability Consistency

DTM shall assess whether material traceability relationships are
mutually consistent.

Possible inconsistencies include:

  • Contradictory actor attribution
  • Conflicting artifact versions
  • Incompatible event ordering
  • Requirements references outside their applicable period
  • Tool actions inconsistent with the recorded method
  • Composition records inconsistent with resulting artifacts

Consistency does not independently establish completeness or truth.

Traceability Consistency ≠ Traceability Completeness.

Traceability Consistency ≠ Historical Accuracy.

Traceability Coverage

DTM shall identify which material portions of the software creation process

are supported by traceability evidence.

Coverage may concern:

  • Creation objectives
  • Requirements
  • Actors
  • Actions
  • Tools
  • Artifacts
  • Transformations
  • Composition
  • Evidence
  • Continuity

Coverage may be represented qualitatively or quantitatively where
appropriate.

A numerical coverage percentage shall not automatically be interpreted as

governance sufficiency.

Traceability Coverage Percentage ≠ Traceability Governance Adequacy.

Traceability Gap

A Development Traceability Gap exists where a material creation
relationship cannot be sufficiently established.

Examples include:

  • An artifact with no supported creation actor
  • A code modification without a reconstructable transformation
    event
  • An autonomous tool action without a relevant authority reference
  • A generated component without sufficient input lineage
  • A composite artifact with missing constituent references
  • A requirements implementation claim without supporting creation

evidence.

DTM shall identify the gap and its material significance.

Missing Traceability ≠ Proven Misconduct.

Critical Traceability Gap

A Critical Development Traceability Gap exists where missing or
contradictory evidence prevents reconstruction of a materially
consequential software creation relationship.

Examples may include:

  • A critical software component with unknown modification history
  • An autonomous agent execution involving untraceable privileged
    tool
  • A production-related artifact with no reliable source-to-output
  • A safety-relevant software transformation whose actor and method

calls. linkage. cannot be established.

DTM shall preserve the critical gap and support competent escalation.

It does not independently determine legal responsibility or
operational suspension.

Critical Traceability Gap Identification ≠ Liability Determination.

Traceability Conflict

Traceability conflicts arise when material records support
incompatible accounts of a development relationship.

Examples include:

  • Different actors attributed to the same event
  • Contradictory artifact versions
  • Inconsistent event timestamps
  • Conflicting authority references
  • Incompatible composition records
  • Disagreement between tool logs and repository history

DTM shall preserve the conflicting evidence.

It shall not silently resolve contradictions by selecting the most

convenient record.

Conflicting Traceability Evidence ≠ Permission to Invent Continuity.

Traceability Uncertainty

Development traceability may remain uncertain due to:

  • Missing historical records
  • Incomplete tool logs
  • Distributed system timing
  • Unavailable AI generation context
  • Ambiguous actor attribution
  • Incomplete transformation evidence
  • Reconstructed rather than original records
  • Conflicting source information

DTM shall preserve material uncertainty.

Uncertainty may affect specific relationships without invalidating the
entire traceability object.

Partial Traceability Uncertainty ≠ Universal Traceability Failure.

Traceability Drift

Traceability Drift occurs when recorded relationships no longer
adequately represent the actual software creation state.

Possible causes include:

  • Unrecorded code changes
  • Agent-generated modifications
  • Requirements changes
  • Method changes
  • Tool-path changes
  • Artifact replacement
  • Composition changes
  • Repository migration
  • Loss of evidence continuity

DTM shall identify material drift and support reassessment.

Previously Established Traceability ≠ Permanently Established

Traceability.

Traceability Change Governance

DTM shall preserve material changes to traceability relationships.

These may include:

  • Added relationships
  • Removed relationships
  • Corrected attribution
  • Updated artifact references
  • Superseded requirements links
  • New evidence
  • Revised reconstruction
  • Resolved conflicts

Changes shall preserve the distinction between historical facts and later

corrections.

A later correction shall not silently erase the original recorded state

where that history remains material.

Traceability Correction ≠ Historical Record Erasure.

Traceability Reconstruction

DTM shall support reconstruction of material software creation
histories.

Reconstruction may involve combining:

  • Repository records
  • Requirements history
  • Agent execution evidence
  • Tool logs
  • Artifact provenance
  • Composition manifests
  • Development decisions
  • Human testimony
  • Other competent evidence

Reconstruction shall distinguish:

Directly Evidenced History

from:

Inferred Historical Relationships

and:

Unresolved Historical Gaps

A coherent reconstructed narrative shall not automatically be treated as a

verified history.

Plausible Reconstruction ≠ Verified Reconstruction.

Traceability Governance States

DTM shall support identifiable traceability governance states.

Possible states include:

Identified: The traceability object has been established.

Under Assessment: Material relationships are being evaluated.

Traceability-Supported: Relevant relationships are sufficiently
evidenced.

Conditionally Supported: Recognition depends on explicit conditions.

Partially Supported: Some material relationships remain insufficiently

established.

Evidence-Limited: Available evidence is insufficient.

Origin-Unresolved: Material origin relationships remain unsupported.

Actor-Unresolved: Material actor attribution remains uncertain.

Authority-Unresolved: Relevant authority relationships cannot be

sufficiently established.

Transformation-Unresolved: Material artifact transformation history

remains incomplete.

Conflicted: Material traceability evidence contradicts itself.

Traceability-Failed: A material traceability condition has been

determined not to hold.

Superseded: A later governed traceability state has replaced the earlier

state.

Unresolved: Available information does not support a sufficiently
determined state.

Traceability states shall not automatically be interpreted as

authorization, quality, or compliance determinations.

Traceability-Supported ≠ Creation Legitimacy Established.

Traceability Recognition

DTM shall determine whether material development relationships are
sufficiently supported for their intended governance use.

Recognition should consider:

  • Traceability scope
  • Relationship identity
  • Evidence competence
  • Evidence integrity
  • Materiality
  • Coverage
  • Completeness
  • Consistency
  • Temporal relevance
  • Uncertainty
  • Conflicts
  • Continuity

Recognition shall remain limited to the relationships actually
assessed.

Recognized Traceability ≠ Universal Creation Acceptance.

Traceability and Accountability

DTM may preserve evidence relevant to identifying actors, decisions,
actions, and outcomes.

Such evidence may support competent accountability assessments.

However, traceability alone does not establish:

  • Legal liability
  • Organizational responsibility
  • Fault
  • Negligence
  • Authorized conduct
  • Contractual responsibility
  • Causal responsibility

These determinations require the applicable competent governance and legal

processes.

Action Attribution ≠ Accountability Determination.

Traceability Evidence ≠ Liability Proof.

Traceability and Software Quality

DTM may connect software artifacts to requirements, implementation
activities, and verification evidence.

However, the existence of these relationships does not establish
functional correctness.

A software artifact may be completely traceable and still contain
defects.

A requirement may be linked to a test that fails.

A passing test may cover only part of the relevant requirement.

Traceable Software ≠ Correct Software.

Test Link ≠ Verified Requirement Satisfaction.

Detailed software quality governance remains within Software Quality
Standard (SQS).

Traceability and Software Security

DTM may preserve evidence relevant to security assessment, including:

  • Source-code changes
  • Dependency introductions
  • Tool actions
  • Artifact transformations
  • Relevant actor attribution
  • Security review references

However, traceability does not independently establish security.

A fully traceable software component may still contain
vulnerabilities.

Complete Creation History ≠ Secure Software.

Detailed security governance remains within Software Security Standard
(SSS).

Traceability and Operational Reality

DTM shall distinguish recorded development activity from the actual

operational conditions under which the activity occurred.

Technical records may omit:

  • Unrecorded human intervention
  • External system behavior
  • Undocumented environmental changes
  • Tool failures
  • Missing data access
  • Unobserved execution conditions

Where operational reality cannot be sufficiently reconstructed, the
limitation shall remain explicit.

Recorded Development Reality ≠ Complete Operational Reality.

Competent operational reality governance remains within ORA™.

Traceability Continuity

DTM shall preserve material creation relationships across changes in:

  • Human developers
  • AI agents
  • Development tools
  • Repositories
  • Software versions
  • Requirements baselines
  • Organizations
  • Development environments
  • Generation mechanisms
  • Artifact composition

Continuity shall be supported by identifiable relationships and sufficient

evidence.

It shall not be inferred merely from the persistence of a project name or

repository.

Project Continuity ≠ Development Traceability Continuity.

Traceability Preservation

DTM shall preserve material traceability evidence for the periods and

purposes established by applicable governance conditions.

Preservation may require:

  • Stable identifiers
  • Historical version references
  • Evidence integrity
  • Relationship history
  • Relevant temporal context
  • Controlled corrections
  • Supersession records
  • Accessible reconstruction references

DTM does not establish universal legal retention periods.

Evidence preservation shall remain compatible with competent privacy,

security, retention, and deletion obligations.

Evidence Preservation ≠ Unlimited Data Retention Authority.

Traceability Reassessment

DTM shall support reassessment when material creation conditions
change.

Triggers may include:

  • New evidence
  • Requirements changes
  • Artifact modifications
  • Autonomous execution changes
  • New actor attribution
  • Tool-path changes
  • Composition changes
  • Integrity conflicts
  • Repository migration
  • Evidence loss
  • Traceability drift

Reassessment shall preserve historical traceability states where
necessary for reconstruction.

Traceability Governance Object: The creation history or relationship set

under governance.

Traceability Identity: The relevant traceability reference.

Creation Objective: The intended software outcome.

Requirements References: The applicable governed requirements.

Creation Actors: Relevant human, organizational, AI, and autonomous

participants.

Actor Roles: The material roles performed.

Authority References: Applicable creation and execution authority

relationships.

Development Method References: The methods associated with creation.

Development Decisions: Material implementation and transformation

decisions.

Execution Activities: Relevant development events.

Constituent Actions: Material actions performed to achieve the
objective.

Tool Paths: Relevant tool-selection and execution relationships.

Data and Context References: Material information inputs.

Artifact References: Relevant software artifacts and versions.

Transformation Relationships: Source-to-result artifact relationships.

Composition Relationships: Constituent-to-composite artifact

relationships.

Minimum Implementation Framework

A conforming implementation of DTM must provide the following six
functional capabilities.

  • Development Traceability Object and Relationship Governance

Capability to identify traceability objects, define material relationship

types, establish creation scope, and preserve stable references across
objectives, requirements, actors, methods, actions, tools, and
artifacts.

The implementation must distinguish relationship identifiers from
evidence-supported relationship claims.

  • Actor, Authority, Method, and Constituent Action Traceability
    Governance

Capability to preserve material relationships connecting creation actors,

authority references, development methods, execution decisions,
constituent actions, and tool paths.

The implementation must not infer authorization solely from recorded
action execution.

  • Artifact, Transformation, Composition, and Temporal Traceability

Governance

Capability to reconstruct material relationships between source artifacts,

transformations, generated outputs, constituent artifacts, composite
software, and relevant event ordering.

The implementation must preserve uncertainty where temporal or
historical relationships cannot be sufficiently established.

  • Traceability Evidence, Coverage, and Sufficiency Governance

Capability to associate material relationship claims with competent

evidence, evaluate evidence integrity, assess coverage, and
distinguish completeness from consistency.

The implementation must not treat high log volume or numerous links as
proof of traceability adequacy.

  • Traceability Gap, Conflict, Drift, and Reconstruction Governance

Capability to identify missing relationships, critical gaps, contradictory

evidence, material drift, and historical reconstruction limitations.

The implementation must distinguish directly evidenced history from
inferred reconstruction and unresolved gaps.

  • Governed Development Traceability State and Lifecycle Handoff
    Governance

Capability to produce a Governed Development Traceability State with

sufficient relationship, evidence, uncertainty, continuity, and
governance-state information for competent SCS creation-state
recognition and downstream SLGA™ lifecycle governance.

The implementation must preserve material unresolved conditions during
handoff.

Use Case 1 — An AI Coding Agent Produces Working Software Without a

Reconstructable Tool Path

An organization authorizes an AI coding agent to create a new internal

software feature.

The agent generates code, executes commands, retrieves external

information, modifies repository files, and runs tests.

The resulting feature works.

The development platform records the final code and a summary of the

agent's activities.

However, the organization cannot sufficiently reconstruct:

  • Which tools were invoked
  • Which external data sources were accessed
  • Which actions modified the software
  • Whether all constituent actions remained within permitted
    boundaries
  • Which material generation inputs contributed to the final
    artifact
  • Which execution evidence supports the agent's summary

DTM evaluates:

Creation Objective → Authority → Agent → Constituent Actions → Tool Paths

→ Artifact Transformations → Resulting Software

The assessment identifies material gaps in the execution and artifact

lineage.

The final artifact may be technically functional.

Its development traceability state may nevertheless be:

Evidence-Limited

or:

Partially Supported

until sufficient relationships can be established.

Autonomous Task Completion ≠ Autonomous Execution Traceability.

Tool Invocation Success ≠ Governed Tool-Path Validity.

AI-Generated Explanation ≠ Verified Development History.

DTM prevents successful software generation from concealing an

insufficiently reconstructable creation process.

  • Which actors created material software components
  • Which development methods were used
  • Which source versions produced the executable package
  • Which material transformations occurred
  • Whether autonomous agents participated
  • Which authority conditions applied to material creation actions

DTM evaluates:

Requirements → Creation Objective → Actors → Methods → Actions → Artifacts

→ Composition → Evidence

The assessment identifies that artifact integrity evidence and
technical functionality do not independently establish a
reconstructable creation history.

The development traceability state may remain:

Evidence-Limited

or:

Origin-Unresolved

depending on the affected relationships.

Artifact Integrity ≠ Complete Development Traceability.

Composition Manifest ≠ Complete Composition Traceability.

Traceable Software ≠ Correct Software.

Technical Delivery ≠ Governed Creation History.

for evidence-supported reconstruction of material development

Canonical Closing Statement

Software creation increasingly occurs across distributed teams, automated

development environments, AI coding assistants, autonomous agents, and
multi-agent systems.

A single software artifact may result from numerous decisions, tool
invocations, generated outputs, transformations, integrations, and
delegated actions.

The final code may be functional.

Its artifact identity may be preserved.

Its constituent components may be identifiable.

Yet the relationships explaining how the software came into existence
may remain incomplete.

Technical logs may record actions without establishing their
authority.

Repository histories may preserve modifications without explaining the
decisions behind them.

AI-generated summaries may describe plausible development sequences
without proving that those sequences occurred.

And a complete-looking traceability graph may contain unsupported or
inferred relationships.

Recorded Development Activity ≠ Governed Development Traceability.

Task Traceability ≠ Constituent Action Traceability.

Recorded Association ≠ Governed Relationship.

Inferred Relationship ≠ Established Relationship.

Traceability-Supported ≠ Creation Legitimacy Established.

Traceable Software ≠ Correct Software.

DTM establishes the methodological conditions under which software creation

remains reconstructable across actors, methods, actions, tools,
artifacts, and evidence.

It preserves the difference between a technical record and a supported

governance relationship.

It does not require every development process to use the same tools or
generate identical logs.

It requires material creation relationships to remain identifiable,

evidentially supported, and continuous enough for competent governance
assessment.

DTM transforms development records from disconnected technical

observations into governed creation traceability — ensuring that
software can be created by humans, AI agents, and autonomous systems
without losing the evidence-supported relationships necessary to
reconstruct how, why, by whom or by what, and under which material
conditions the software came into existence.
