HCIM — Harm Change Identification Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HCIM — Harm Change Identification Module governs how material change
in an already governed Harm is identified, distinguished from ordinary
variation, associated with the correct Harm object, temporally and
spatially anchored, qualified by direction and materiality, and prepared
for subsequent Accumulation, Compounding, Propagation, or Cascading
governance.

HCIM establishes whether the Harm itself has materially changed before
governance determines what change dynamic produced that change.

It establishes the transition:

Governed Harm State + Current Harm-Relevant Information → Change Signal →
Change Comparison → Material Change Identification → Change Qualification
→ Governed Harm Change Identification

Its central question is:

Has the governed Harm materially changed, and if so, what changed, where,
when, and in what direction?

  • Operational Role


HCIM is the entry point into HCPS.

Its operational role is:

to establish whether a materially relevant change has occurred in an
existing Harm without prematurely classifying that change as accumulation,
compounding, propagation, or cascading.

HCIM therefore separates:

Change Identification

from:

Change Mechanism Classification

The first task is to establish that the Harm changed.

The next HCPS stages determine how.

  • Position Within HCPS


The HCPS governance flow is:

Change Identification → Accumulation → Compounding → Propagation →
Cascading

HCIM receives:

Governed Harm State

together with current Harm-relevant information.

It produces:

Governed Harm Change Identification

for subsequent HCPS governance.

  • Governed Object


The primary governed object is:

Material Change in an Existing Governed Harm

HCIM does not identify a new Harm merely because a new observation or
condition appears.

The first identity question is:

Does this change belong to the existing governed Harm?

Therefore:

New Harm-Relevant Information ≠ New Harm Object

and:

Changed Harm ≠ New Harm by Default

  • Foundational Change Identification Principle


The foundational rule is:

Harm Change must be established from a material change in the Harm itself,
not merely from a change in what surrounds, observes, records, or responds
to it.

Therefore:

System Change ≠ Harm Change

Observation Change ≠ Harm Change

Record Change ≠ Harm Change

Intervention Change ≠ Harm Change

unless the governed Harm condition itself materially changes.

  • Change Baseline


Material change requires a governed point of comparison.

HCIM therefore establishes the relevant:

Prior Harm Condition → Current Harm Condition

The baseline may include materially relevant prior information concerning:

intensity

duration

frequency

affected scope

manifestation

effect

subject condition

object condition

functional consequence

or:

other governed Harm dimensions

A change cannot be meaningfully identified without knowing what it changed
from.

  • Baseline Integrity


The comparison baseline must represent the same materially relevant Harm
object, scope, dimension, and temporal context.

Comparing incompatible conditions may manufacture false change.

Therefore:

Different Observation Basis ≠ Harm Change by Default

For example, an apparent increase may result from:

better measurement

broader monitoring coverage

new reporting channels

or:

improved detection

rather than actual Harm growth.

  • Change Signal


A Change Signal is information suggesting that the governed Harm may have
changed.

Signals may arise from:

monitoring

subject reports

operational observations

measurements

new manifestations

downstream effects

environmental changes

intervention outcomes

or:

other Harm-relevant information

But:

Change Signal ≠ Material Harm Change

A signal triggers evaluation.

It does not establish the conclusion.

  • Materiality


HCIM must distinguish ordinary variation from change that materially
affects Harm governance.

A change may be material because it alters:

the Harm condition

affected scope

persistence

functional consequence

trajectory

governance urgency

or:

the need for downstream HAGA™ action

Canonical distinction:

Variation ≠ Material Harm Change

Not every fluctuation should create a new Harm governance event.

  • Change Direction


Once material change is identified, HCIM should qualify its direction
where possible.

The Harm may be:

Increasing

Decreasing

Variable

Intermittently Changing

or:

Directionally Uncertain

Direction must describe the Harm, not the surrounding process.

For example:

Mitigation Increased ≠ Harm Decreased

The Harm itself must support the conclusion.

  • Change Magnitude


HCIM may qualify how materially the Harm changed.

The representation should be appropriate to the governed Harm and may be:

quantitative

qualitative

or:

hybrid

Where useful, change may be represented as:

Limited

Material

Substantial

or another governed scale.

But:

Change Magnitude ≠ Harm Severity

A severe Harm may change only slightly.

A lower-severity Harm may change rapidly.

  • Change Velocity


HCIM may identify how quickly material change occurred.

Possible patterns include:

Gradual

Rapid

Sudden

Episodic

Variable

or:

Unknown

Change velocity may materially affect:

monitoring cadence

Containment urgency

or:

escalation requirements

without becoming a Harm State itself.

  • Temporal Identification


HCIM should establish, where supportable:

when material change began

when it became observable

when it was detected

and:

when it was formally recorded

These times may differ.

Therefore:

Change Time ≠ Detection Time

and:

Detection Time ≠ Recording Time

Delayed detection must not rewrite the Harm trajectory.

  • Scope Identification


HCIM must identify where the material change occurred.

This may concern:

one subject

multiple subjects

one Harm dimension

multiple dimensions

one system

multiple systems

one location

or:

a broader affected scope

Scope change may later indicate Propagation, but HCIM does not assume this
automatically.

Therefore:

Scope Change ≠ Propagation by Default

  • Multi-Dimensional Change


A Harm may change differently across its dimensions.

For example:

Physical Harm — Decreasing

Financial Harm — Increasing

Organizational Harm — Stable

HCIM must preserve these differences rather than collapse them into a
single unsupported direction.

Canonical principle:

One Dimension's Change ≠ Whole-Harm Change by Default

  • Subject-Level Change


Different affected subjects may experience different Harm trajectories.

For example:

Subject A — Improving

Subject B — Stable

Subject C — Worsening

HCIM must preserve materially relevant subject differences.

Therefore:

Individual Improvement ≠ Collective Harm Improvement by Default

  • Composite and Distributed Change


Composite or distributed Harm may change through multiple individually
limited changes.

No single component may independently demonstrate the complete Harm
change.

HCIM must support:

Component Changes → Composite Change Evaluation

without assuming either:

every component change changes the whole

or:

no whole-Harm change exists because no individual component is sufficient

Canonical principle:

Absence of a Single Dominant Change ≠ Absence of Material Composite Harm
Change.

  • Intermittent Change


Harm may change episodically rather than continuously.

HCIM must distinguish:

intermittent manifestation of an unchanged Harm

from:

intermittent material change in the Harm

Therefore:

Manifestation Variability ≠ Harm Change by Default

The underlying governed Harm condition must be evaluated.

  • Change After Mechanism Termination


The mechanism that produced Harm may stop while Harm continues changing.

For example:

System — Stopped

Harm — Continuing to Worsen

or:

System — Removed

Harm — Gradually Decreasing

Canonical principle:

Mechanism State Does Not Determine Harm Change.

  • Change After Intervention


Containment, Mitigation, treatment, compensation, repair, or Recovery
activity may occur without material Harm change.

Conversely, Harm may change before an intervention is formally completed.

Therefore:

Intervention Progress ≠ Harm Change

and:

Intervention Completion ≠ Harm Improvement

HCIM evaluates consequence, not process completion.

  • Observation and Visibility


Apparent Harm change may result from a change in visibility.

For example:

more sensors

broader reporting

better detection

new diagnostic capability

or:

new data integration

may reveal previously existing Harm.

Therefore:

More Observed Harm ≠ More Harm by Default

Similarly:

Less Observed Harm ≠ Less Harm by Default

  • Newly Discovered Historical Harm


New information may reveal that the Harm was previously greater, broader,
or different than understood.

Where the Harm itself did not recently change, HCIM must distinguish:

new knowledge about historical Harm

from:

new Harm Change

Canonical distinction:

Knowledge Change ≠ Harm Change

This protects the Harm timeline from being rewritten by discovery timing.

  • Harm State Relationship


HCIM may identify a Harm dynamic that later supports HSS State Transition.

For example:

HCIM → Material Increase Identified

may support:

HSS → Active → Escalating

But:

Harm Change ≠ Harm State Transition

HCIM governs the material change.

HSPM governs the resulting State-to-State transition.

  • Accumulation, Compounding, Propagation and Cascading Boundary


HCIM does not prematurely decide why or through which HCPS dynamic the
Harm changed.

After material change is established, subsequent governance determines
whether the change represents:

Accumulation

Compounding

Propagation

Cascading

or another relevant Harm dynamic.

Therefore:

Change Identification Precedes Change-Dynamic Classification.

  • Potential New Harm


A newly observed condition may be materially related to existing Harm
while constituting a potentially distinct Harm object.

HCIM should identify:

Potential New Harm Relationship

where appropriate.

It must not silently absorb every downstream consequence into the original
Harm.

Canonical principle:

Related Change ≠ Same Harm by Default

Where necessary, appropriate HAGA™ Emergence, Recognition, Boundary, and
Determination governance should be invoked.

  • Uncertainty


HCIM must preserve uncertainty concerning:

whether material change occurred

direction

magnitude

velocity

timing

scope

or:

relationship to the existing Harm

Possible outputs may include:

Material Change Established

Material Change Partially Established

Potential Material Change

Insufficient Basis

or:

No Material Change Established

Uncertainty must remain explicit.

  • Minimum Implementation Framework


A minimum HCIM implementation requires eight capabilities.

  • Harm Baseline Control


Maintain a valid prior Harm condition against which current Harm-relevant
information can be compared.

  • Change Signal Evaluation


Receive and evaluate Harm Change Signals without treating signals
themselves as established material change.

  • Materiality Determination


Distinguish ordinary variation, measurement differences, and visibility
changes from material Harm change.

  • Change Qualification


Represent direction, magnitude, velocity, timing, scope, and relevant Harm
dimensions where supportable.

  • Complex Harm Change Representation


Support subject-level, multi-dimensional, collective, composite,
distributed, intermittent, and latent Harm conditions.

  • Identity & Boundary Preservation


Associate change with the correct governed Harm while identifying
potential new Harm or Boundary reassessment needs where appropriate.

  • Uncertainty & Historical Integrity


Preserve uncertainty and distinguish actual Harm Change from delayed
detection, historical discovery, classification correction, or improved
observability.

  • Governed Output


Produce a traceable Governed Harm Change Identification for subsequent
HCPS governance and relevant HSS reassessment. 28. Use Case 1 — More Harm
Becomes Visible Without the Harm Increasing

Monitoring of an existing governed Harm is expanded.

The new monitoring system detects substantially more affected elements
than the previous system.

The initial appearance is:

Observed Harm Increased

HCIM compares the new information with the previous observation basis and
establishes that the additional Harm was already present but previously
unobserved.

It therefore records:

Knowledge / Visibility Change

rather than falsely establishing:

Harm Increase

The governing principle is:

More visible Harm is not necessarily more Harm.

  • Use Case 2 — Harm Worsens After the Producing System Is Stopped


An autonomous system produces a governed Harm and is removed from
operation.

Several days later, the existing consequences continue deteriorating.

HCIM establishes:

Producing Mechanism — Terminated

while:

Governed Harm — Materially Increasing

The change remains associated with the existing Harm and proceeds into
HCPS governance to determine whether accumulation, compounding,
propagation, or cascading dynamics are present.

The governing principle is:

The state of the producing mechanism does not determine the trajectory of
the Harm it has already produced.

HCIM requires that:

  • material change remain associated with the correct governed Harm


identity; - a valid comparison baseline exist; - Change Signals remain
separate from established Harm Change; - ordinary variation remain
separate from material change; - changes in observation capability remain
separate from changes in Harm; - direction, magnitude, velocity, timing,

classification; - potential new Harm remain distinguishable from change in
the existing Harm; - uncertainty and observation limitations remain
explicit; - the Harm change trajectory remain historically traceable.

  • Governance Logic


HCIM applies the following logic:

Does an existing governed Harm object exist?

If no:

→ route to the appropriate earlier HAGA™ lifecycle stage.

If yes:

→ establish the relevant prior Harm baseline.

Has new Harm-relevant information appeared?

If no:

→ no new Change Identification is required.

If yes:

→ evaluate whether it represents a Change Signal.

Is the apparent difference caused primarily by changed observation,
measurement, reporting, or visibility?

If yes:

→ govern the information change without automatically establishing Harm
Change.

Does the current Harm materially differ from the relevant baseline?

If no:

→ preserve the existing Harm trajectory.

If yes:

→ establish Material Harm Change.

Then determine, where supportable:

what changed

in what direction

by how much

how quickly

when

where

and:

for whom or for what

Next:

Does the change remain associated with the same governed Harm?

If uncertain:

→ trigger appropriate identity or Boundary reassessment.

Does the change indicate possible State Transition?

→ provide the relevant input to HSS.

Does the change require HCPS dynamic classification?

→ continue into Accumulation, Compounding, Propagation, and Cascading
governance as applicable.

At every stage:

Change Signal ≠ Harm Change

Variation ≠ Material Harm Change

Visibility Change ≠ Harm Change

Knowledge Change ≠ Harm Change

Mechanism Change ≠ Harm Change

Intervention Change ≠ Harm Change

Harm Change ≠ Harm State Transition

  • Canonical Boundary


The HCIM — Harm Change Identification Module governs whether, when, where,
and in what materially relevant way an already governed Harm has changed
relative to its prior governed condition. It governs the baseline, Change
Signals, materiality, direction, magnitude, velocity, timing, scope,
complex Harm representation, uncertainty, identity continuity, and
historical integrity necessary to establish a Governed Harm Change
Identification. It does not determine whether Harm originally exists,
perform Recognition or Determination, independently redefine the Governed
Harm Boundary, classify or transition the Harm State, determine whether
established change constitutes Accumulation, Compounding, Propagation, or
Cascading, perform Containment or Mitigation, govern Recovery, Residual
Harm, Resolution or Finality, determine general causation, assign
accountability or liability, or replace operational reality, evidence,
validation, integrity, prediction, simulation, AI behavior, autonomous
execution, identity, Value, tax-event, or jurisdiction-specific
governance.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm governance must distinguish a changing Harm from a changing view of
Harm.

A new measurement may reveal what was already there, a new report may
describe an old condition, and a stopped system may leave behind Harm that
continues to change.

HCIM establishes the difference before accumulation, compounding,
propagation, or cascading is inferred.

Before governance asks how Harm is changing, it must first establish that
the Harm itself has actually changed.
