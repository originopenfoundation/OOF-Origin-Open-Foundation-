Canonical Definition

Model Alignment Integrity Module (MAIM) defines the structural conditions
under which internal reality models remain sufficiently aligned with observable reality,
operational evidence, environmental conditions, and evolving reality signals in a manner
that remains traceable, measurable, governable, and operationally valid throughout
cognition and reality-modeling processes.

MAIM governs model alignment.

The module ensures that cognition remains capable of maintaining reality-connected models
rather than operating on increasingly detached, outdated,
or inaccurate representations of reality.

A system satisfies MAIM only if:

  • model alignment remains assessable
  • reality comparisons remain possible
  • divergence remains detectable
  • alignment conditions remain measurable
  • correction pathways remain available
  • reality-connected modeling remains operationally valid

A cognition environment that cannot determine whether its model reflects reality
does not satisfy MAIM.

Module Operational Role

MAIM defines the model-alignment governance layer of CRMS.

Its role is to preserve alignment between internal models and operational reality.

Module Operational Space

MAIM governs:

  • model-reality alignment
  • reality comparison
  • alignment measurement
  • model validation
  • reality anchoring
  • alignment governance
  • reality-connected modeling
  • divergence visibility

The module applies wherever cognition relies on internal models to understand reality.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • reality-connected models
  • measurable alignment
  • model validation
  • governance-valid adaptation
  • operational reliability
  • accountable reality representations

Its function is to ensure that cognition continuously evaluates
whether its models remain aligned with reality.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Model Alignment Object
The organization must define which reality models require alignment governance.

This may include:

  • world models
  • environment models
  • operational models
  • strategic models
  • simulation models
  • forecasting models
  • digital twins
  • autonomous-agent models

2. Define Model Alignment Conditions
The system must define the conditions under which model alignment remains valid.

This includes:

  • alignment thresholds
  • reality-comparison requirements
  • validation requirements
  • correction requirements
  • traceability requirements
  • governance-valid alignment conditions

3. Define Alignment Degradation Detection Logic
The system must define how alignment degradation is identified.

This may include:

  • reality divergence
  • model inaccuracies
  • outdated representations
  • validation failures
  • operational mismatches
  • evidence-model inconsistencies

4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic
The system must define governance logic for alignment failures.

Governance response may include:

  • model review
  • alignment validation
  • model correction
  • governance intervention
  • escalation
  • model restriction
  • operational invalidation where required

5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions
The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • alignment measurements
  • validation activities
  • divergence events
  • correction actions
  • governance reviews
  • resulting model states

A cognition environment must not remain alignment-valid if materially significant divergence
from reality cannot be identified, measured, or governed.
