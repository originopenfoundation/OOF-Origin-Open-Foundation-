HMNM — Harm Mitigation Need Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HMNM — Harm Mitigation Need Module governs how the need to materially
reduce an existing governed Harm or its consequences is identified,
qualified, prioritized, maintained, reassessed, and translated into an
explicit Mitigation Objective.

HMNM determines what existing Harm remains materially relevant for
reduction, which Harm condition or consequence requires intervention, what
reduction is sought, how urgent or important that reduction is, and
whether the Mitigation Need changes as the governed Harm evolves.

It establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Containment State + Current Harm State → Mitigation Need
Identification → Mitigation Need Qualification → Mitigation Priority →
Mitigation Objective → Governed Harm Mitigation Need

Its central question is:

What existing Harm or consequence requires material reduction, and what
governed improvement must Mitigation seek to achieve?

  • Operational Role


The operational role of HMNM is:

to determine whether Mitigation is required and define what existing Harm
condition must materially improve before any Mitigation action is
selected.

HMNM governs:

the need for Harm reduction

not:

the intervention used to produce it.

This distinction prevents available actions from defining the problem they
are supposed to solve.

  • Position Within HMS


The HMS governance flow is:

Mitigation Need → Mitigation Action → Mitigation Effect → Mitigation
Outcome → Mitigation Verification

HMNM governs the first stage:

Mitigation Need

It may receive:

Governed Harm Containment State

Governed Harm State

Governed Harm Boundary

Governed Harm Change & Propagation State

and other materially relevant HAGA™ lifecycle information.

It produces:

Governed Harm Mitigation Need

for the Harm Mitigation Action Module.

  • Governed Object


The primary governed object is:

Need for Material Reduction of Existing Harm or Its Consequences

The existence of Harm does not automatically define what Mitigation should
target.

A governed Harm may contain:

multiple consequences

different affected subjects

different Harm components

different persistence conditions

or:

different levels of reducibility

HMNM identifies which of these require governed reduction.

  • Foundational Mitigation Need Principle


The foundational rule is:

Mitigation Need must be defined by the Harm condition requiring reduction,
not by the intervention that happens to be available.

Therefore:

Available Intervention ≠ Established Mitigation Need

and:

Existing Harm ≠ Fully Defined Mitigation Need

The Harm must first be translated into a governable reduction requirement.

  • Mitigation Need Identification


Mitigation Need may arise where existing governed Harm or its consequences
remain materially present and reduction is:

necessary

possible

required

beneficial within the governed Harm lifecycle

or:

necessary to support later Recovery

Identification should determine:

what remains harmed

what consequence remains

where the Harm remains

who or what remains affected

and:

what materially requires improvement

  • Containment State and Mitigation Need


Containment is an important input but not a universal prerequisite for
Mitigation.

A Harm may be:

Contained

Partially Contained

Containment Uncertain

or even:

still undergoing Containment

while Mitigation becomes necessary.

Therefore:

Incomplete Containment ≠ No Mitigation Need

Containment and Mitigation may operate in parallel where operational
reality requires it.

  • Containment Need ≠ Mitigation Need


The two needs answer different questions.

Containment Need:

What additional Harm must be prevented?

Mitigation Need:

What existing Harm must be reduced?

A single Harm may generate both simultaneously.

Therefore:

Containment Need ≠ Mitigation Need

and:

Containment Need Ended ≠ Mitigation Need Ended

  • Harm State and Mitigation Need


Current Harm State informs Mitigation Need but does not automatically
determine it.

A Harm classified as:

Active

Stabilized

Reduced

or:

Residual

may each produce different Mitigation requirements.

Likewise:

High Harm Severity ≠ Automatically High Mitigation Priority

unless the broader governed conditions support that determination.

HMNM must evaluate the actual reduction requirement.

  • Mitigation Need Qualification


Once identified, a Mitigation Need must be qualified sufficiently for
action governance.

Qualification may include:

affected Harm component

affected subject or object

current Harm condition

material consequence

reducibility

urgency

priority

scope

timing

dependencies

uncertainty

and:

expected direction of improvement

Qualification must remain proportionate to the Harm context.

  • Harm Reducibility


HMNM must distinguish between Harm that is:

fully reducible

partially reducible

conditionally reducible

currently non-reducible

or:

reducibility uncertain

A Harm does not cease to exist merely because complete reduction is
impossible.

Therefore:

Not Fully Reversible ≠ No Mitigation Need

Mitigation may legitimately seek partial reduction.

  • Irreversible Harm


Some Harm cannot be reversed.

HMNM must not create a false objective of restoring a condition that can
no longer exist.

Instead, Mitigation Need may target reducible consequences such as:

continuing impairment

secondary consequences

ongoing burden

severity

persistence

or:

preventable deterioration of the existing Harm condition

Canonical principle:

Irreversible Harm may still contain reducible consequences.

  • Mitigation Target Formation


HMNM identifies what aspect of existing Harm requires reduction.

Potential targets include:

Harm magnitude

severity

duration

persistence

affected scope

functional impairment

material consequence

specific manifestation

or:

another materially relevant Harm condition

The target must describe the Harm condition rather than the operational
intervention.

  • Mitigation Objective Formation


The Mitigation Objective defines the governed improvement sought.

A weak objective is:

Perform Intervention X.

A governed Mitigation Objective is:

Reduce Harm Condition A toward materially improved Condition B.

Therefore:

Action Description ≠ Mitigation Objective

The objective must remain outcome-oriented.

  • Baseline Harm Condition


Meaningful Mitigation requires sufficient understanding of the condition
from which reduction is being sought.

HMNM should establish an appropriate:

Mitigation Baseline

The baseline may describe:

current magnitude

current severity

affected scope

current functional condition

current persistence

or:

another relevant Harm characteristic

Without a sufficient baseline, later claims of Harm reduction may become
unreliable.

  • Baseline Uncertainty


The Harm baseline may itself contain uncertainty.

HMNM must preserve that uncertainty rather than create false precision.

Therefore:

Uncertain Baseline ≠ No Mitigation Need

but also:

Uncertain Baseline ≠ Precise Reduction Target by Default

The objective should reflect the level of certainty actually available.

  • Mitigation Priority


Multiple Mitigation Needs may exist simultaneously.

HMNM must support prioritization based on materially relevant factors such
as:

Harm severity

affected scope

persistence

reducibility

time sensitivity

risk of deterioration

dependency relationships

vulnerability of affected subjects

or:

relationship to Recovery

Priority must remain grounded in the governed Harm context.

  • Urgency and Priority


Urgency and priority are related but distinct.

A Harm consequence may require rapid intervention because delay materially
reduces Mitigation potential.

Another consequence may be more significant overall but less
time-sensitive.

Therefore:

Urgency ≠ Priority

HMNM should preserve both where the distinction matters.

  • Mitigation Window


Some Harm can be reduced only, or substantially more effectively, within a
particular period.

HMNM may therefore establish:

Mitigation Window

representing the materially relevant period within which Mitigation action
should occur.

A closing Mitigation Window may increase urgency even where Harm severity
has not changed.

  • Multiple Mitigation Needs


A single Harm object may generate several Mitigation Needs.

For example:

Need₁ — reduce physical consequence

Need₂ — reduce functional impairment

Need₃ — reduce persistent downstream effect

These needs may require:

different priorities

different timing

different objectives

and later:

different actions

HMNM must not collapse materially distinct reduction needs into one
generic intervention requirement.

  • Composite Harm


Where Harm is composite, Mitigation Need must be assessed at both
component and complete Harm levels.

A major reduction opportunity in one component may coexist with severe
non-reducible Harm elsewhere.

Therefore:

Component Mitigation Need ≠ Complete Harm Mitigation Need

The complete Harm object must remain governable.

  • Collective Harm


Where the Harm affects:

multiple individuals

a defined group

an organization

an environment

a population

or:

society

Mitigation Need must not be inferred only from selected individual
outcomes.

Canonical principle:

Individual Mitigation Need ≠ Collective Mitigation Need by Default

Collective Harm may require a collective reduction objective.

  • Distributed Harm


Mitigation Need may span several:

systems

locations

organizations

providers

agents

infrastructure components

or:

domains

Fragmented operational ownership must not fragment the governed Harm
object.

HMNM must preserve the complete Mitigation Need across distributed
environments.

  • Cross-Domain Mitigation Need


One Harm may produce reducible consequences in several domains.

For example:

Physical → Organizational → Financial

or:

Digital → Cognitive → Societal

Reduction in one domain may not address the others.

Therefore:

One Domain Mitigation Need ≠ Complete Harm Mitigation Need

Cross-domain needs should remain distinguishable but related.

  • Mitigation Need Under Continuing Harm


Existing Harm may require Mitigation while new Harm is still being
produced.

HMNM must preserve both realities:

Existing Harm → Mitigation Need

and:

Continuing Harm Development → Containment Need

Neither should erase the other.

Canonical principle:

Continuing Harm production does not make existing Harm unworthy of
reduction.

  • Mitigation Need and Recovery


Mitigation Need should support, but not replace, Recovery governance.

A reduction objective may be necessary to make Recovery possible.

However:

Mitigation Objective ≠ Recovery Objective by Default

HMS governs reduction of Harm.

HRRHS governs what has recovered and what Residual Harm remains.

  • Mitigation Need and Residual Harm


The possibility that some Harm will remain does not eliminate Mitigation
Need.

A realistic Mitigation Objective may seek:

maximum materially achievable reduction

without claiming complete elimination.

Therefore:

Expected Residual Harm ≠ No Mitigation Need

  • Mitigation Need Under Uncertainty


Uncertainty may concern:

current Harm magnitude

reducibility

affected scope

future persistence

available reduction pathways

or:

expected outcome

HMNM must distinguish:

Mitigation Need Established

from:

Mitigation Outcome Predictable

A need may be clear even where the eventual reduction remains uncertain.

  • Predicted Benefit


Forecasts may inform Mitigation prioritization.

They do not establish actual Mitigation.

Therefore:

Predicted Mitigation Benefit ≠ Actual Harm Reduction

PGA™ may govern prediction where applicable.

HMNM uses predictions only as supporting inputs where methodologically
appropriate.

  • Simulated Mitigation


SIMULOS® may support testing of possible interventions or expected Harm
reduction.

Simulation may inform:

reducibility

priority

timing

or:

objective feasibility

But:

Simulated Mitigation Success ≠ Actual Harm Mitigation

HMNM governs the Need, not simulated proof of outcome.

  • Accountability and Liability Independence


Mitigation Need may exist before responsibility or liability is
determined.

Harm should not remain unnecessarily unreduced merely because those
questions remain unresolved.

Therefore:

Unresolved Accountability ≠ No Mitigation Need

Unresolved Liability ≠ No Mitigation Need

HMS does not determine who is legally required to perform or fund
Mitigation.

  • Mitigation Need States


HMNM may represent Mitigation Need through states such as:

Mitigation Need Established

Mitigation Need Partially Established

Potential Mitigation Need

Mitigation Need Uncertain

Mitigation Need Deferred

No Current Mitigation Need Established

or:

Mitigation Need Closed

The state should reflect current governed Harm reality.

  • Mitigation Need Persistence


Mitigation Need may continue after:

Containment succeeds

initial Mitigation begins

some Harm is reduced

or:

Recovery starts

The Need remains active while materially relevant reducible Harm or
consequences continue to require reduction.

Therefore:

Mitigation Started ≠ Mitigation Need Ended

  • Mitigation Need Reassessment


HMNM must support reassessment where:

Harm State changes

Containment changes

Harm scope changes

reducibility changes

new consequences emerge

Mitigation effects appear

Recovery begins

or:

new information materially changes the Harm understanding

Reassessment may:

increase

decrease

reprioritize

divide

combine

defer

or:

close

a Mitigation Need.

  • Mitigation Need Closure


A Mitigation Need may be closed where:

the targeted Harm has been sufficiently reduced

the targeted consequence no longer exists

further material reduction is not possible

the Need has legitimately transitioned into Recovery / Residual Harm
governance

or:

the Need is superseded by a more accurate governed formulation

Closure must have an explicit basis.

Therefore:

No Further Mitigation Action Planned ≠ Mitigation Need Closed

  • Minimum Implementation Framework


A minimum HMNM implementation requires eight capabilities.

  • Existing Harm Identification


Identify the governed Harm or consequence that remains materially relevant
for reduction.

  • Mitigation Need Qualification


Determine the Harm condition, affected scope, reducibility, dependencies,
uncertainty, and other factors necessary to define the reduction
requirement.

  • Mitigation Baseline Establishment


Establish a sufficient current Harm baseline against which later Harm
reduction can be evaluated.

  • Mitigation Target & Objective Formation


Translate the Harm condition into an explicit outcome-oriented Mitigation
Target and Mitigation Objective without prescribing the action itself.

  • Priority, Urgency & Timing Governance


Determine Mitigation priority, urgency, and any materially relevant
Mitigation Window.

  • Composite, Collective & Distributed Need Governance


Preserve Mitigation Need across multiple Harm components, affected
subjects, domains, systems, organizations, and locations.

  • Reassessment & Closure Governance


Maintain, reprioritize, adapt, divide, combine, defer, or close Mitigation
Need as the Harm condition materially changes.

  • Governed Output


Produce a traceable Governed Harm Mitigation Need suitable for Mitigation
Action governance. 37. Use Case 1 — Harm Is Contained but Existing
Consequences Remain

A digital Harm has been successfully contained.

No additional systems are being affected.

However, already affected systems remain materially impaired.

HMNM identifies:

Containment Need → substantially ended

while:

Mitigation Need → remains active

It establishes the existing impairment as the Mitigation Target and
defines the required material improvement without yet selecting the
intervention.

The governing principle is:

Stopping additional Harm does not eliminate the need to reduce Harm
already produced.

  • Use Case 2 — Irreversible Harm With Reducible Consequences


A Harm has produced a permanent change that cannot be reversed.

Complete restoration is impossible.

However, several continuing consequences can still be materially reduced.

HMNM does not create an impossible objective of restoring the pre-Harm
condition.

Instead, it establishes Mitigation Needs for the consequences that remain
reducible and preserves the irreversible condition for later Recovery and
Residual Harm governance.

The governing principle is:

Irreversible Harm may still contain reducible consequences.

  • Governance Requirements


HMNM requires that:

  • Mitigation Need be grounded in existing governed Harm or its


consequences; - the Need remain distinct from Containment Need; -
available interventions not define the Mitigation Need; - Harm
reducibility be explicitly considered; - irreversible Harm not
automatically eliminate Mitigation Need; - Mitigation Targets describe
Harm conditions rather than interventions; - Mitigation Objectives remain
outcome-oriented; - a sufficient Mitigation Baseline be established where
necessary; - baseline uncertainty remain explicit; - urgency and priority
remain distinguishable; - materially relevant Mitigation Windows remain
governable; - multiple Mitigation Needs remain separately identifiable
where necessary; - component, composite, individual, collective,
distributed, and cross-domain Harm remain appropriately distinguished; -
continuing Harm production not erase existing Mitigation Need; - expected
Residual Harm not eliminate legitimate Mitigation Need; - prediction and
simulation not be treated as actual Harm reduction; - unresolved
accountability or liability not automatically prevent Mitigation Need
determination; - Mitigation Need remain reassessable; - Need closure
require an explicit governed basis.

  • Governance Logic


HMNM applies the following logic:

Does an existing governed Harm or Harm consequence remain?

If no:

→ no current Mitigation Need may exist.

If yes:

→ identify what Harm condition remains.

Then ask:

Is the Harm or consequence materially reducible?

If yes:

→ establish the relevant Mitigation Need.

If partially:

→ establish a bounded or partial Mitigation Need.

If uncertain:

→ preserve reducibility uncertainty while determining whether action
planning remains justified.

If irreversible:

→ identify whether materially reducible consequences remain.

Then determine:

What Harm condition should improve?

→ establish the Mitigation Target.

From what current condition?

→ establish the Mitigation Baseline.

Toward what materially improved condition?

→ establish the Mitigation Objective.

Then determine:

How urgent is the Need?

What priority does it carry?

Does a Mitigation Window exist?

Does the Need span multiple subjects, components, domains, systems, or
locations?

→ preserve the complete governed structure.

Finally:

Governed Harm Mitigation Need → Harm Mitigation Action

  • Canonical Boundary


The HMNM — Harm Mitigation Need Module governs how the need to materially
reduce an existing governed Harm or its consequences is identified,
qualified, prioritized, bounded, maintained, reassessed, and translated
into a governed Mitigation Target and Mitigation Objective. It governs
Harm reducibility, Mitigation baseline, priority, urgency, timing,
multiple and distributed Mitigation Needs, uncertainty, persistence,
reassessment, and closure. HMNM does not determine whether Harm originally
exists, perform Harm Recognition or Determination, independently define
the Governed Harm Boundary, perform Harm Containment, select or execute
Mitigation actions, determine Mitigation Effect or Outcome, perform final
Mitigation Verification, govern Recovery or Residual Harm, establish
Resolution or Finality, determine general causation, or assign
accountability, responsibility, or liability.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Mitigation should begin with the Harm that needs to change—not with the
intervention already available.

Some Harm can be reduced, some can only be partially reduced, and
irreversible Harm may still leave consequences that can materially
improve.

HMNM turns those realities into explicit, prioritized, and governable
reduction objectives before action begins.

A Mitigation Need exists wherever governed Harm still contains something
that can and should materially improve.
