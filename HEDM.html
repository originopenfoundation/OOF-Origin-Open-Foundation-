HEDM — Harm Exposure Duration Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HEDM — Harm Exposure Duration Module governs how the temporal extent,
continuity, recurrence, interruption, repetition, accumulation,
persistence, latency relevance, and uncertainty of Harm Exposure are
identified, structured, qualified, updated, and preserved after Exposure
Identification, Conditions, and Scope have been established, without
treating longer, repeated, or accumulated Exposure as automatic proof that
Harm exists or that Harm severity has increased.

HEDM establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Exposure Scope → Exposure Start → Exposure Duration →
Exposure Pattern → Interruption / Recurrence → Accumulation → Exposure
Temporal State

Its central question is:

For how long, how often, and in what temporal pattern does the governed
Exposure exist?

  • Operational Role


Exposure is not adequately governed by asking only whether it occurred.

A single Exposure may last milliseconds or years.

Several short Exposures may collectively matter more than one longer
Exposure.

An Exposure may pause and later resume.

A Harm-capable condition may stop while Exposure persists.

Exposure may end before Harm becomes recognizable.

HEDM therefore governs the temporal structure of Exposure itself.

Its role is:

to establish when Exposure begins, how long it persists, whether it recurs
or accumulates, when it is interrupted, and when it ends, without
converting temporal persistence into Harm Determination.

  • Position Within Harm Exposure Standard (HES)


The HES internal flow is:

Exposure Identification → Exposure Conditions → Exposure Scope → Exposure
Duration → Exposure Transition

HESM establishes who or what belongs within Exposure Scope.

HEDM establishes the temporal structure of that Exposure.

Its output becomes the basis for determining how Exposure changes,
transitions, ends, or moves toward possible Harm Emergence governance.

  • Exposure Start


Exposure Start is the earliest sufficiently supported time at which the
governed subject or object enters the materially relevant Exposure
relationship.

The start point must follow the Exposure relationship, not merely the
existence of the Harm-capable condition.

Condition Start ≠ Exposure Start

  • Exposure Start Uncertainty


The exact Exposure Start may be unknown.

Possible representations may include:

Exact Start

Estimated Start

Earliest Possible Start

Latest Possible Start

Start Interval

Unknown Start

Temporal precision must never exceed available support.

Temporal Precision Must Not Exceed Temporal Evidence

  • Exposure End


Exposure End is the point at which the materially relevant Exposure
relationship ceases.

The condition itself may continue after the subject leaves the pathway.

Or the condition may stop while the subject remains exposed through
residual circumstances.

Therefore:

Condition End ≠ Exposure End

  • Exposure End Uncertainty


Exposure termination may also be uncertain.

Possible states include:

Confirmed End

Probable End

Estimated End

Open Exposure

Unable to Determine

HEDM must preserve open-ended Exposure where closure cannot be
legitimately established.

  • Exposure Duration


Exposure Duration is the governed temporal interval between Exposure Start
and Exposure End, subject to interruption and recurrence logic.

Conceptually:

Exposure Duration = Governed Time Within the Exposure Relationship

Duration must remain distinguishable from:

Condition Duration

Harm Duration

Recovery Duration

  • Duration Precision


Duration may be represented as:

Exact

Approximate

Bounded Range

Minimum Known Duration

Maximum Possible Duration

Unknown

False temporal precision is methodologically unacceptable.

  • Instantaneous Exposure


Some Exposure may occur in a single discrete moment.

Examples may include:

Physical impact

Single unsafe command

One-time data release

One toxic contact

Instantaneous Exposure may still be materially important.

Short Exposure ≠ Low Harm Potential

  • Short-Term Exposure


Short-Term Exposure exists over a limited but continuous interval.

The duration threshold must be domain-appropriate.

HEDM does not impose one universal definition of “short-term.”

  • Long-Term Exposure


Long-Term Exposure persists across an extended interval.

However:

Long-Term Exposure ≠ Severe Harm by Default

The temporal dimension must not be converted directly into Harm magnitude.

  • Continuous Exposure


Continuous Exposure exists without a materially relevant interruption
during the governed interval.

Conceptually:

Exposure Active → Exposure Active → Exposure Active

Continuity may matter even where intensity changes.

  • Intermittent Exposure


Intermittent Exposure alternates between active and inactive states.

Conceptually:

Exposure → Pause → Exposure → Pause → Exposure

HEDM must preserve the pattern rather than collapsing it into one
continuous interval.

  • Recurrent Exposure


Recurrent Exposure consists of repeated Exposure episodes separated by
materially distinct intervals.

Conceptually:

E₁ → Gap → E₂ → Gap → E₃

Recurrence may be regular or irregular.

  • Repeated Exposure


Repeated Exposure means more than one Exposure occurrence involving the
same or materially related subject-condition relationship.

Repeated Exposure does not automatically establish accumulation.

Repeated Exposure ≠ Accumulated Exposure by Default

  • Accumulated Exposure


Accumulated Exposure exists where multiple Exposure episodes or durations
must be governed collectively because their combined temporal structure is
materially relevant.

Conceptually:

E₁ + E₂ + E₃ + … + Eₙ → Accumulated Exposure State

HEDM governs the temporal accumulation structure.

HEMS determines whether Harm emerges.

  • Exposure Frequency


Frequency represents how often Exposure occurs within a governed interval.

Possible representations may include:

Once

Rare

Periodic

Frequent

Continuous

Variable

Unknown

Frequency should be based on governed data, not impression.

  • Exposure Interval


An Exposure Interval is a bounded period during which the Exposure
relationship is active.

Multiple intervals may exist within one larger Exposure lifecycle.

This allows:

Interval₁ + Interval₂ + Interval₃

without falsely declaring one uninterrupted Exposure.

  • Exposure Gap


An Exposure Gap is a period during which the Exposure relationship is
inactive.

A gap may be:

Brief

Extended

Regular

Irregular

Unknown

The existence of a gap does not automatically reset cumulative Exposure
significance.

  • Exposure Interruption


Exposure Interruption occurs when an active Exposure relationship
temporarily ceases.

Conceptually:

Exposure Active → Exposure Interrupted

HEDM must distinguish interruption from final termination.

Exposure Interruption ≠ Exposure End

  • Exposure Resumption


Exposure Resumption occurs when the same or materially related Exposure
relationship becomes active again after interruption.

Conceptually:

Exposure Interrupted → Exposure Resumed

Resumption should preserve continuity with prior Exposure history where
materially appropriate.

  • New Exposure vs Resumed Exposure


Not every later Exposure is a continuation of the previous one.

HEDM must distinguish:

Resumed Exposure

from:

New Exposure

This depends on:

  • subject continuity
  • condition continuity
  • pathway continuity
  • context
  • time gap
  • material relationship


  • Exposure Persistence


Exposure may persist even where the originating mechanism has stopped.

Examples:

Contaminant remains

Published content remains accessible

System state persists

Resource restriction continues

Therefore:

Mechanism Stop ≠ Exposure Stop

  • Residual Exposure Condition


Residual conditions may sustain Exposure after the initial mechanism ends.

Conceptually:

Source Ends → Residual Condition Persists → Exposure Continues

HEDM governs the continued Exposure interval.

It does not govern Residual Harm, which belongs later in HAGA™.

  • Exposure Latency Relevance


Exposure may occur long before Harm becomes observable or recognized.

Conceptually:

Exposure → Time Gap → Harm Recognition

HEDM preserves the Exposure timeline.

It does not infer Harm during the gap.

Latency ≠ Proof of Harm

  • Delayed Exposure Discovery


Exposure may only be discovered after it ended.

Conceptually:

Exposure Occurred → Exposure Ended → Later Discovery

HEDM must support retrospective temporal reconstruction.

Discovery Time ≠ Exposure Time

  • Recognition Time


The time Exposure becomes recognized is distinct from its actual start or
end.

Exposure Recognition Time ≠ Exposure Start

Exposure Recognition Time ≠ Exposure End

  • Reporting Time


Exposure may be reported later than it occurred.

Reporting Time ≠ Exposure Time

HEDM must preserve this distinction where material.

  • Observation Time


A sensor, person, system, or auditor may observe Exposure only during part
of its duration.

Observed Duration ≠ Actual Exposure Duration

  • Monitoring Window


Available monitoring may cover only part of the Exposure lifecycle.

Conceptually:

Exposure Begins → Monitoring Begins → Monitoring Ends → Exposure Continues

HEDM must not equate the monitoring window with the Exposure window.

  • Temporal Coverage Gaps


Missing time-series information may create gaps.

Possible governance states include:

Known Active

Known Inactive

Unknown

Estimated

HEDM must preserve these states explicitly.

  • Temporal Uncertainty


Temporal uncertainty may concern:

  • Start
  • End
  • Duration
  • Frequency
  • Interruption
  • Recurrence
  • accumulation
  • sequence


Uncertainty must remain visible through downstream Exposure governance.

  • Temporal Conflict


Different sources may provide conflicting Exposure timelines.

Example:

Operational Log: Exposure begins 10:00

Sensor Record: Exposure begins 09:42

HEDM must preserve the conflict until legitimately resolved.

Conflicting Timelines ≠ Permission to Select Preferred Time

  • Exposure Sequence


HEDM may establish the temporal order of Exposure episodes.

Conceptually:

E₁ → E₂ → E₃

Sequence is relevant to temporal governance.

But:

Sequence ≠ Causation

  • Concurrent Exposure


Several Exposure relationships may occur at the same time.

Conceptually:

Exposure A ∥ Exposure B

HEDM must support concurrency.

Temporal Overlap ≠ Causal Relationship

  • Overlapping Exposure Intervals


Exposure episodes may partially overlap.

This may matter in multi-condition or multi-domain contexts.

HEDM should preserve overlapping intervals rather than forcing them into
one timeline where distinct governance is required.

  • Multi-Condition Temporal Exposure


A subject may encounter several Harm-capable conditions over different or
overlapping periods.

Conceptually:

Condition A: t1–t3

Condition B: t2–t4

HEDM governs the Exposure timing.

It does not determine combined Harm.

  • Multi-Subject Temporal Exposure


Several subjects may enter Exposure at different times.

Conceptually:

Subject A → t1

Subject B → t2

Subject C → t3

Shared Scope does not require shared Exposure timing.

Shared Exposure Scope ≠ Shared Exposure Duration

  • Group Exposure Timing


A Defined Group may have a collective Exposure period while individual
members have different participation intervals.

HEDM must preserve both levels where materially relevant.

  • Population Exposure Timing


Population Exposure may begin gradually rather than at one single time.

Conceptually:

Partial Population Exposure → Expanded Population Exposure → Stabilized
Population Exposure

A single universal timestamp may be methodologically false.

  • Environmental Exposure Timing


Environmental Exposure may evolve as a condition moves or disperses.

Conceptually:

Source → Spread → Peak Reach → Contraction

HEDM must support moving temporal boundaries.

  • Digital Exposure Timing


Digital Exposure may continue after initial publication, transmission, or

Examples include:

Persistent access

Replication

Caching

Recommendation

Automated reuse

Downstream processing

Original Publication Time ≠ Complete Exposure Duration

  • AI-Mediated Exposure Timing


AI-related Exposure may depend on:

Output Generation Time

Delivery Time

First User Interaction

Repeated Recommendation

Downstream Execution

Removal

These times may differ materially.

HEDM must not collapse them into one “AI event.”

  • Autonomous-System Exposure Timing


Autonomous Exposure may change continuously with movement and operational

Conceptually:

Subject Outside Action Space → Subject Enters Action Space → Exposure →
Subject Leaves Action Space

HEDM must support dynamic Exposure windows.

  • Duration and Intensity Boundary


Duration and Exposure intensity are distinct dimensions.

A short intense Exposure and a long low-intensity Exposure may require
different interpretation.

Therefore:

Exposure Duration ≠ Exposure Intensity

and:

Longer Exposure ≠ Greater Exposure Magnitude by Default

  • Duration and Harm Severity Boundary


HEDM does not infer Harm severity from duration.

Long Exposure ≠ Severe Harm

Short Exposure ≠ Minor Harm

Harm severity belongs downstream.

  • Duration and Exposure Scope Boundary


Exposure Duration concerns time.

HESM governs who or what belongs within Scope.

Subjects within the same Scope may have different Exposure durations.

Shared Scope ≠ Shared Duration

  • Duration and Harm Emergence Boundary


Long, repeated, or accumulated Exposure may become relevant to Harm
Emergence.

But HEDM stops before that determination.

Accumulated Exposure ≠ Harm Emergence

The canonical handoff remains temporal, not substantive.

  • Duration and Causation Boundary


Temporal ordering may support later causal analysis.

But:

Earlier Exposure ≠ Cause

Longer Exposure ≠ Stronger Causation by Default

Repeated Exposure ≠ Causal Proof

  • Duration and Operational Reality Boundary


ORA™ may establish actual operating periods, system states, movements, or
environmental conditions.

HEDM determines how those periods map onto Exposure.

System Active Time ≠ Exposure Time by Default

  • Duration and Evidence Boundary


ADIT® may govern time-stamped evidence.

HEDM consumes that evidence but governs temporal Exposure interpretation.

Timestamp ≠ Exposure Duration by Itself

  • Duration and Validation Boundary


VALIDOS™ may validate measurement methods, timing models, or temporal
datasets.

HEDM uses validated inputs without allowing validation to establish
Exposure automatically.

  • Duration Reassessment


HEDM must reassess the Exposure Temporal State when:

  • earlier start is discovered
  • later termination is discovered
  • gaps become visible
  • repeated episodes are identified
  • assumed continuity is disproven
  • retrospective monitoring becomes available
  • time synchronization errors are corrected
  • new subjects are added to Scope


Reassessment must preserve previous temporal states.

  • Historical Temporal Integrity


The system should preserve:

Temporal State₁ → New Information → Temporal State₂

including:

  • previous Start
  • previous End
  • previous Duration
  • uncertainty
  • reason for revision
  • resulting Exposure interpretation


Current Exposure Timeline ≠ Historical Exposure Timeline

  • Temporal Sufficiency


HEDM reaches sufficient governance when the Exposure timeline is
understood well enough to determine whether the Exposure relationship is
ongoing, interrupted, recurrent, accumulated, or ended and whether
Exposure Transition governance can proceed without material temporal
assumptions.

The sufficiency test is:

Do we know enough about when Exposure exists and how it behaves across
time to govern its next legitimate transition?

  • Temporal Proportionality


Not every Exposure requires high-resolution temporal modeling.

A one-time event may require one timestamp.

A long-term environmental, digital, medical, organizational, or
AI-mediated Exposure may require:

Intervals + Recurrence + Gaps + Accumulation + Uncertainty

The rule is:

Temporal Governance Resolution Should Follow Exposure Reality

  • Minimum Implementation Framework


A minimum implementation of HEDM should provide eight capabilities.

  • Governed Input


Receive Governed Harm Exposure Scope, Exposure Conditions, relevant Harm
Temporal Context, and supporting operational/evidentiary time information.

  • Exposure Start / End Governance


Represent exact, estimated, bounded, unknown, or open Exposure start and
end states.

  • Interval Representation


Support single, continuous, intermittent, recurrent, and multiple Exposure
intervals.

  • Frequency & Accumulation Governance


Represent repeated and accumulated Exposure without converting repetition
into Harm.

  • Interruption & Resumption Logic


Distinguish interruption, resumption, termination, and new Exposure.

  • Temporal Uncertainty & Conflict


Preserve uncertainty, incomplete coverage, competing timelines, and
monitoring gaps.

  • Reassessment & Historical Continuity


Support retrospective temporal revision without erasing prior governed
states.

  • Governed Output


Produce a traceable Governed Exposure Temporal State capable of supporting
Exposure Transition governance.

A system that records only a single Exposure timestamp where the governed
reality is continuous, intermittent, recurrent, or accumulated does not
fully implement HEDM.

  • Use Case 1 — Repeated Occupational or Environmental Exposure


A subject repeatedly encounters a Harm-capable condition across many
separate periods.

No single Exposure episode is sufficient to represent the overall Exposure
reality.

A simplistic model records:

Exposure Event = One Date

HEDM instead preserves:

E₁ → Gap → E₂ → Gap → E₃ → … → Eₙ

including:

  • frequency
  • duration
  • interruptions
  • cumulative temporal structure
  • uncertainty
  • Exposure termination where known


HEDM does not declare that Harm exists.

It produces the governed temporal Exposure basis from which downstream
Harm Emergence may later be assessed.

The governing distinction is:

Repeated Exposure ≠ Harm by Default

  • Governance Requirements


HEDM requires that:

  • Exposure Start remain distinct from condition start
  • Exposure End remain distinct from condition end
  • exact and uncertain timing remain separately representable
  • instantaneous, short-term, long-term, continuous, intermittent,


recurrent, repeated, and accumulated Exposure remain supportable; -
repeated Exposure remain distinct from accumulated Exposure; - Exposure
gaps remain visible; - Exposure interruption remain distinct from
termination; - resumed Exposure remain distinguishable from new Exposure;
- Exposure persistence after source termination remain governable; -
delayed discovery remain distinguishable from Exposure timing; -
recognition, reporting, observation, and monitoring times remain distinct
from actual Exposure time; - temporal coverage gaps remain explicit; -
conflicting timelines remain preservable; - sequence and temporal overlap
remain distinct from causation; - concurrent and overlapping Exposure
remain supportable; - subject-specific timing remain supportable within
shared Scope; - temporal granularity follow actual domain requirements; -
aggregation preserve underlying temporal provenance; - duration remain
distinct from intensity and Harm severity; - historical temporal states
remain reconstructable.

  • Governance Logic


HEDM applies the following logic:

Has a Governed Harm Exposure Scope been established?

If no:

→ no Exposure Duration should be manufactured.

If yes:

→ identify Exposure Start.

Is the Start exact?

If no:

→ represent the appropriate uncertainty or interval.

Is Exposure continuous?

If yes:

→ establish continuous duration.

If no:

→ identify Exposure intervals and gaps.

Does Exposure repeat?

If yes:

→ determine whether episodes remain separate or require accumulated
temporal representation.

Has Exposure been interrupted?

If yes:

→ preserve the interruption.

Has Exposure resumed?

If yes:

→ determine whether it is resumption or a new Exposure.

Has the originating mechanism ended?

If yes:

→ determine whether Exposure nevertheless persists.

Is Exposure End established?

If no:

→ maintain Open Exposure.

Has material new temporal information emerged?

If yes:

→ reassess while preserving history.

Is temporal governance sufficient for Exposure Transition analysis?

If yes:

→ produce the Governed Exposure Temporal State.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HEDM — Harm Exposure Duration Module governs the start, end, temporal
extent, interval structure, continuity, frequency, recurrence, repetition,
interruption, resumption, accumulation, persistence, uncertainty,
reassessment, and historical preservation of Harm Exposure across governed
subjects and objects. It does not determine Harm Emergence, Harm severity,
Harm duration, causation, accountability, liability, or the operational
mechanism itself.

HEIM determines:

Which relationship requires Exposure governance?

HECM determines:

Under what conditions does it operate?

HESM determines:

Who or what belongs within its Scope?

HEDM determines:

For how long, how often, and in what temporal pattern does the Exposure
exist?

The next HES module determines:

How does that Exposure state change, terminate, or transition toward the
next Harm lifecycle stage?

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Exposure is not fully governed until its temporal structure is known.

A single moment, repeated interaction, continuous condition, interrupted
pathway, persistent environment, or long-delayed discovery may represent
fundamentally different Exposure realities even when the same subject and
Harm-capable condition are involved.

HEDM preserves that temporal reality so HAGA™ does not confuse when
Exposure was observed with when it existed, how long it lasted, or whether
it had already ended before Harm became visible.
