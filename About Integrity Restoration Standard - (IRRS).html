About Integrity Restoration Standard - (IRRS)

Understanding Governed Integrity Restoration

Responding to an integrity breach does not automatically restore integrity.

Once a breach has been contained, governance must ensure that integrity has
been objectively re-established before normal operations resume. Restoration
requires more than recovery—it requires validated evidence, accountable
governance, and confirmation that approved integrity conditions have been
fully restored.

Integrity Restoration Standard transforms operational recovery into a governed
and auditable restoration process.

What Is Integrity Restoration Standard?

Integrity Restoration Standard (IRRS) defines the governance principles
required to restore integrity following a verified integrity breach and
completed response activities. It ensures that restoration is performed
through a standardized governance methodology supported by objective
validation, documented evidence, and accountable decision-making.

Canonical Definition

Integrity Restoration Standard (IRRS) defines the governance conditions
required to restore integrity following a verified integrity breach and
completed response activities. It establishes the restoration principles,
recovery procedures, validation requirements, accountability mechanisms,
evidence requirements, and governance controls required to return a governed
entity to an approved state of integrity while ensuring that restoration is
objective, verifiable, and fully auditable throughout the complete lifecycle
of the governed entity.

Why IRRS Exists

Containing an integrity breach is not the same as restoring integrity.

Organizations require a standardized governance framework that defines how
integrity is re-established, who validates the restoration, what evidence is
required, when restoration is considered complete, and how restored integrity
is documented for future governance and audit purposes.

IRRS provides this canonical governance framework.

What IRRS Governs

IRRS governs the complete integrity restoration lifecycle, including:

  • restoration planning
  • restoration execution
  • integrity recovery
  • restoration validation
  • restoration evidence
  • restoration documentation
  • restoration governance.

transition from controlled response to Integrity Assurance Standard (IAS),
ensuring that integrity has been objectively restored before continuous
assurance begins.
