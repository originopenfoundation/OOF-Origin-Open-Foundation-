Replica Module (RPM)

OOF™ Origin Open Foundation™

Independent Methodological Authority

Category: AI and Interpretation
Subcategory: Representation and Copy
Module: Replica Module (RPM)
Type: Classification Module
Standard: Representation & Copy Standard (RCS™)
Status: Canonical Module
Version: 1.0
Effective Date: 19 March 2026


Canonical Definition

A Replica is a deliberately created asset that imitates,
reconstructs, or reinterprets an original asset
with the intention of resembling it.

A replica is not the original and is not a direct reproduction.
It is a constructed representation based on the original.

Why This Matters

Without clear classification of replicas:

  • replicas may be confused with originals
  • value may be misrepresented
  • authorship may become unclear
  • intentional imitation may be mistaken
    for reproduction

Replicas must be distinguished from both copies and originals.

Minimum Implementation Framework (MIF)

Step 1 — Identify Reference Original

Define the original asset being imitated or reconstructed.

Step 2 — Confirm Constructed Nature

Ensure the asset is intentionally created to resemble the original,
not directly reproduced from it.

Step 3 — Define Intent

Establish that the purpose is imitation,
reconstruction, or reinterpretation.

Step 4 — Declare Replica Status

The asset must be clearly identified as a replica.

Step 5 — Define Relationship

The connection to the original must remain identifiable
but must not imply identity.

Use Case 1 — Museum Reconstruction

Scenario

A museum creates a physical reconstruction of a historical object
(e.g. dinosaur, artifact).

Application

The object is classified as a replica.

Result

  • original remains protected
  • accurate representation is accessible
  • no confusion with the original asset

Use Case 2 — Artistic Interpretation

Scenario

An artist creates a painting inspired by a known artwork
(e.g. reinterpretation of Mona Lisa).

Application

The work is classified as a replica or reinterpretation.

Result

  • clear distinction from the original
  • new authorship is preserved
  • creative freedom remains intact

Structural Principle

A replica is defined by intent, not by duplication.

Closing Statement

Resemblance does not create identity.
A replica reflects the original but does not become it.
