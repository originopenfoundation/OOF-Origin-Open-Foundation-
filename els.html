Canonical Definition System

Ethical Layer Standard defines the structural conditions under
which a methodology, system, device, hardware structure, AI environment,
autonomous process, or future technology may be considered ethically
valid within the OOF® system.

Ethical Layer Standard operates as a foundational core parent
standard and a required ethical passage layer.

No system is valid within OOF® if it bypasses ethical passage
conditions.

Ethical validity exists only when:
  • human harm is prevented as a governing condition
  • sustainability is preserved as a structural condition
  • deployment remains bounded by ethical legitimacy
  • technical capability does not override ethical validity


A system may be functional, profitable, scalable, or advanced and still
remain invalid if it fails ethical passage.

A. Standard Abstract

Ethical Layer Standard establishes ethics as a validity
condition, not as a secondary review layer.

It defines when a system may be considered ethically governable before:
  • deployment
  • scale
  • automation
  • integration
  • commercial use
  • operational reliance


As a foundational core parent standard, it governs the ethical
passage condition under which derived protocols and modules may later
operate.

B. Core Principle

Ethics is not optional.

Ethical validity is a precondition of system validity.

No methodology, system, or technology may claim full validity if it
produces avoidable human harm, structurally ignores sustainability, or
bypasses ethical passage conditions.

C. Scope

Ethical Layer Standard applies to:
  • methodologies

  • AI systems
  • autonomous systems
  • robotics
  • hardware
  • chips and embedded technologies
  • digital infrastructures
  • simulation environments
  • future technologies and operational architectures


This standard applies regardless of industry, implementation model, or
technology generation.

D. System Position

Ethical Layer Standard operates as:
  • a foundational core parent standard

It does not describe ethics as philosophy only.

It defines ethics as an operational condition of validity.

E. Structural Base

Ethical Layer Standard is based on:
  • no-harm-to-humans condition
  • sustainability condition
  • bounded technology use
  • ethically valid interaction conditions
  • ethical legitimacy before deployment and scale


A system must pass ethical layer conditions before broader validity may
be recognized within the OOF® system.

F. Ethical Validity Conditions

A system implementing Ethical Layer Standard must ensure:

1. Human Harm Prevention

No system may be considered ethically valid if it creates avoidable
direct or indirect harm to humans as a normal or tolerated condition of
operation.

2. Sustainability Integrity

No system may be considered ethically valid if its operational logic
depends on structurally destructive, irresponsible, or unsustainable
conditions.

3. Bounded Use of Capability

Technical capability alone does not create ethical legitimacy.

A system must not perform actions, enable pathways, or normalize uses
that exceed ethically valid boundary conditions.

4. Ethical Interaction Conditions

Where systems affect humans, interaction conditions must remain
ethically bounded, non-coercive, and structurally governable.

5. Ethical Deployment Legitimacy

No system may be considered ethically valid for release, deployment,
scale, or operational reliance if ethical passage has not been
established.

Non-bypassable logic applies at validated state only.

G. Methodology

Implementation of Ethical Layer Standard requires:
  • 1. define the system or methodology under evaluation
  • 2. identify human, ecological, and operational impact boundaries
  • 3. identify whether avoidable harm pathways exist
  • 4. identify whether sustainability conditions are preserved
  • 5. identify whether capability exceeds ethical legitimacy
  • 6. verify whether ethical passage is satisfied before deployment or reliance


A system may be relied upon only after ethical passage conditions are
established.

H. Invalid Conditions

A system is considered ethically invalid if:
  • avoidable human harm is tolerated
  • sustainability is structurally ignored
  • technical capability is treated as sufficient justification
  • deployment occurs without ethical passage
  • ethical conditions are treated as optional, cosmetic, or post-corrective only
  • operational success is used to override ethical invalidity


Performance does not restore validity to an ethically invalid system.

I. System Impact

  • moral statement → into structural operational boundary


It establishes ethics as a governing layer through which systems must
pass before wider validity may be recognized.
