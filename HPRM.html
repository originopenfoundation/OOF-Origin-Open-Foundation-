HPRM — Harm Propagation Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HPRM — Harm Propagation Module governs how an already governed Harm
extends beyond its previously affected scope through materially traceable
propagation pathways into additional subjects, objects, systems, domains,
locations, organizations, infrastructure, environments, or populations.

HPRM establishes whether Harm has materially spread, where propagation
originates relative to the governed Harm, through what pathway it
proceeds, what additional scope becomes affected, whether the propagated
condition remains part of the same Harm or may constitute distinct
downstream Harm, and what propagation state requires Boundary
reassessment, Cascading governance, State reassessment, or Containment.

It establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Compounding / Change State → Propagation Signal →
Propagation Pathway → Additional Affected Scope → Propagation
Determination → Governed Harm Propagation State

Its central question is:

Has existing Harm materially extended into additional affected scope,
through what pathway, and what governed Harm relationship results from
that spread?

  • Operational Role


HCIM identifies material Harm Change.

HACM governs additive Harm build-up.

HCOM governs amplification through interaction.

HPRM governs:

the movement or extension of Harm into additional affected scope.

Its operational role is:

to preserve the continuity, pathway, scope, identity relationships, and
governance consequences of Harm as it spreads beyond its prior
manifestation.

  • Governed Object


The primary governed object is:

Propagation of Existing Harm

Propagation requires materially relevant extension beyond previously
affected scope.

Therefore:

Harm Increase ≠ Harm Propagation by Default

A Harm may become substantially worse while remaining entirely within the
same affected scope.

  • Foundational Propagation Principle


The foundational rule is:

Harm propagation is governed by where consequence materially extends, not
by where the originating mechanism operates.

The producing mechanism may remain in one place while Harm spreads
elsewhere.

Conversely, a mechanism may operate across many systems without Harm
propagating across all of them.

Therefore:

Mechanism Distribution ≠ Harm Propagation

HPRM should identify the relevant starting point of a propagation
relationship.

This may be:

the original Harm condition

an accumulated Harm condition

a compounded Harm condition

or:

an already propagated manifestation

Propagation origin does not necessarily mean original Harm-producing
mechanism.

Canonical distinction:

Propagation Origin ≠ Original Cause by Default

new environmental effects

other downstream manifestations

But:

Propagation Signal ≠ Established Propagation

The relationship must be evaluated.

  • Propagation Pathway


HPRM should identify the materially relevant pathway through which Harm
extends.

A pathway may involve:

physical transfer

digital transmission

information replication

dependency relationships

economic relationships

behavioral interaction

infrastructure interconnection

The pathway must be governed sufficiently to understand propagation

  • Pathway Continuity


A propagation pathway may cross several intermediate systems or
environments.

Conceptually:

Harm A → System B → Network C → Subject D

The intermediate elements do not automatically become separate Harm
objects.

HPRM must preserve the materially relevant propagation relationship across
the pathway.

Canonical principle:

Operational Intermediaries Must Not Break Harm Propagation Continuity.

  • Direct Propagation


Direct propagation occurs where existing Harm extends into additional
scope without a materially distinct intermediate Harm consequence.

Conceptually:

Harm₁ / Scope A → Scope B

where the propagated manifestation remains part of Harm₁.

  • Indirect Propagation


Propagation may proceed through intermediate mechanisms or dependencies.

Conceptually:

Harm₁ → Dependency → Additional Affected Scope

Indirectness does not prevent Propagation governance where the materially
relevant relationship can be established.

Therefore:

Indirect Pathway ≠ No Propagation

  • Multi-Step Propagation


Propagation may proceed through several stages:

Scope A → Scope B → Scope C → Scope D

HPRM should preserve the propagation chain where materially relevant.

However, if these stages generate distinct downstream Harm objects rather
than merely extending the same Harm, Cascading governance may become
necessary.

  • Temporal Propagation


Propagation may be:

Immediate

Delayed

Gradual

Intermittent

Latent

or:

Variable

Therefore:

No Immediate Spread ≠ No Propagation

The propagation timeline may differ materially from the originating Harm
timeline.

  • Delayed Propagation Recognition


Propagation may occur before it is detected.

Therefore:

Propagation Time ≠ Detection Time

and:

Detection Time ≠ Recording Time

Where supportable, HPRM should preserve the actual or best-governed
propagation interval separately from administrative discovery.

  • Propagation Velocity


Where relevant, HPRM may govern how quickly Harm is extending.

Possible patterns include:

Slow

Moderate

Rapid

Accelerating

Variable

or:

Uncertain

Propagation velocity may materially influence Containment urgency.

  • Propagation Magnitude


HPRM may represent the material extent of spread through:

number of additional subjects

additional affected area

additional systems

additional domains

additional infrastructure

or another appropriate measure.

But:

Propagation Magnitude ≠ Harm Severity

A Harm can spread widely while producing limited individual effects, or
remain localized while being extremely severe.

  • Subject-to-Subject Propagation


Harm may extend from one affected subject to others through materially
relevant pathways.

HPRM must preserve:

original affected subject

additional affected subjects

and:

the propagation relationship

without assuming that all subjects necessarily share an identical Harm
condition.

  • System-to-System Propagation


Digital, autonomous, infrastructural, or operational Harm may extend
across connected systems.

For example:

System A Harm → Dependency / Transmission Path → System B → System C

Canonical principle:

System Boundary ≠ Harm Propagation Boundary

  • Cross-Organizational Propagation


Harm may extend across organizational boundaries.

A supplier, platform, provider, operator, customer, public institution, or
downstream organization may become materially affected.

Therefore:

Organizational Boundary ≠ Harm Boundary

and:

Organizational Transfer ≠ Propagation Termination

  • Geographic Propagation


Physical, environmental, infrastructural, digital, economic, or societal
Harm may extend across geographic areas.

HPRM must preserve geographic scope where it materially affects
governance.

Administrative borders do not themselves determine whether Harm
propagated.

  • Cross-Domain Propagation


Harm may extend from one domain into another.

Examples:

Digital → Financial

Environmental → Health

Physical → Organizational

Infrastructure → Societal

However, cross-domain consequence requires an identity test.

The resulting condition may represent:

continued manifestation of Harm₁

or:

distinct downstream Harm₂

Therefore:

Cross-Domain Effect ≠ Same Harm by Default

  • Same-Harm Propagation


Where the additional affected manifestation remains methodologically part
of the existing Harm:

Harm₁ / Scope A → Harm₁ / Scope A+B

HPRM preserves the same Harm identity while recording expanded scope.

This may trigger HBS Boundary reassessment.

  • New Downstream Harm


Where propagation creates a materially distinct Harm condition:

Harm₁ → downstream consequence → Harm₂

HPRM preserves the relationship but does not silently absorb Harm₂ into
Harm₁.

Harm₂ may require:

Emergence

Recognition

Boundary

Determination

and:

State

governance.

Canonical principle:

Propagation Relationship Does Not Automatically Establish Harm Identity
Continuity.

  • Boundary Reassessment


Where established Propagation extends beyond the current Governed Harm
Boundary, HPRM must generate:

Boundary Reassessment Trigger

HBS remains responsible for formal Boundary governance.

Therefore:

Propagation Detection ≠ Automatic Boundary Expansion

  • Propagation vs Accumulation


Accumulation increases Harm through repeated or continuing contributions.

Propagation expands affected scope.

Therefore:

Increasing Harm ≠ Propagating Harm

A Harm may accumulate without spreading.

A Harm may propagate without materially increasing the Harm experienced by
its original subject.

  • Propagation vs Compounding


Compounding amplifies Harm through interacting conditions.

Propagation extends Harm into additional scope.

Therefore:

Amplification ≠ Propagation

Compounded Harm may later propagate, but these remain separate dynamics.

  • Propagation vs Cascading


Propagation concerns extension of Harm.

Cascading concerns sequential downstream Harm formation or consequence
chains.

Conceptually:

Propagation: Harm₁ / Scope A → Harm₁ / Scope B

Cascade: Harm₁ → Harm₂ → Harm₃

Therefore:

Spread ≠ Cascade by Default

  • Propagation After Mechanism Termination


The originating mechanism may stop while Harm continues spreading.

For example:

source system disabled

while:

already distributed effects continue reaching additional systems

or:

physical source removed

while:

environmental consequence continues moving

Canonical principle:

Termination of the originating mechanism does not necessarily terminate
Harm propagation.

  • Propagation During Containment


Containment may begin while propagation continues.

Therefore:

Containment Initiated ≠ Propagation Stopped

Actual Propagation must be monitored until sufficient governance
establishes that further spread has been bounded.

  • Propagation Cessation


Propagation ceases when the governed Harm no longer materially extends
into additional scope through the relevant propagation pathway.

But:

Propagation Stopped ≠ Harm Ended

Existing Harm in already affected scope may remain:

Active

Stabilized

Reduced

Recovering

or:

Residual

  • Distributed Propagation


Propagation may occur across many systems, agents, environments, or
organizations.

No single participant may observe the complete spread.

HPRM must preserve the coherent propagation trajectory across fragmented
operational observations.

Canonical principle:

Distributed Observation ≠ Fragmented Harm Reality.

  • Composite and Collective Propagation


Composite Harm may propagate through only some components while materially
changing the whole affected scope.

Collective Harm may expand across additional populations even where
individual outcomes vary.

Therefore:

Component Propagation ≠ Whole-Harm Propagation by Default

and:

Individual Non-Propagation ≠ No Collective Propagation

The appropriate Harm-object level must govern the conclusion.

Observed propagation may represent only part of actual spread.

HPRM must preserve uncertainty concerning:

pathway

velocity

magnitude

identity continuity

or:

Canonical principle:

Observed Propagation ≠ Complete Propagation by Default.

Use Case 1 — Digital Harm Continues After the Source System Is Disabled

An autonomous system creates a harmful digital condition that has already
entered connected downstream systems.

The originating system is disabled.

The harmful condition nevertheless continues reaching additional systems
through previously established dependencies and replicated information.

HPRM governs:

Existing Harm → Propagation Pathway → Additional Systems → Expanded
Affected Scope

rather than concluding:

Source Stopped → Harm Stopped

The governing principle is:

Harm propagation may continue after the mechanism that initiated it can no
longer act.

  • Use Case 2 — Environmental Harm Extends Into a New Domain


A governed environmental Harm spreads geographically and begins producing
materially distinct health consequences among an affected population.

HPRM governs the environmental spread and identifies the cross-domain
downstream relationship.

It does not automatically treat every health consequence as merely an
expanded environmental manifestation.

Where materially distinct Health Harm has emerged:

Environmental Harm₁ → Propagation / Downstream Relationship → Potential
Health Harm₂

appropriate HAGA™ lifecycle governance is initiated for Harm₂.

The governing principle is:

Propagation must preserve relationships without forcing every downstream
consequence into the identity of the original Harm.

  • Governance Requirements


HPRM requires that:

  • Propagation remain associated with an existing governed Harm
  • Harm Increase remain distinct from Harm Propagation
  • mechanism distribution remain distinct from Harm propagation
  • Propagation Origin remain distinguishable from original cause
  • Propagation Signals remain separate from established Propagation
  • additional affected scope remain explicitly represented
  • materially relevant pathways remain traceable
  • operational intermediaries not break Propagation continuity
  • direct, indirect, multi-step, delayed, intermittent, and latent


Propagation remain governable; - Propagation time remain distinguishable
from detection and recording time; - velocity and magnitude remain
representable where relevant; - subject, system, organizational,
geographic, and cross-domain Propagation remain governable; - same-Harm
Propagation remain distinguishable from new downstream Harm; - Boundary
expansion require appropriate HBS governance; - Propagation remain
distinct from Accumulation, Compounding, and Cascading; - mechanism
termination not automatically terminate Propagation; - Containment
initiation not establish Propagation cessation; - Propagation cessation
remain separate from Harm termination; - distributed observations not
fragment Harm reality; - uncertainty and visibility limitations remain
explicit; - Prediction and Simulation remain distinct from actual
Propagation; - Propagation history remain traceable; - Containment Need
remain derivable from the actual Propagation state.

  • Governance Logic


HPRM applies the following logic:

Does an existing governed Harm exist?

If no:

→ HPRM cannot establish formal Harm Propagation.

If yes:

→ establish the currently affected scope.

Has a Propagation Signal appeared?

If no:

→ preserve the existing Propagation state subject to Monitoring.

If yes:

→ identify the potential additional affected scope.

Has Harm actually extended beyond the previously affected scope?

If no:

→ Propagation is not established.

If yes:

→ identify the materially relevant pathway.

Then determine:

where did propagation begin relative to the governed Harm?

where has it extended?

when did it occur?

how quickly is it spreading?

how materially has scope expanded?

Next:

Does the additional manifestation remain part of the same Harm?

If yes:

→ preserve Harm identity and assess Boundary reassessment.

If uncertain:

→ preserve uncertainty and invoke appropriate HAGA™ governance.

If materially distinct downstream Harm has formed:

→ preserve the relationship and initiate the relevant Harm lifecycle for
the new Harm.

Then test:

Is the Harm also accumulating?

→ preserve Accumulation governance.

Is it materially compounding?

→ preserve Compounding governance.

Is it generating sequential downstream Harm?

→ continue into Cascading governance.

Does Propagation require immediate bounding?

→ produce Harm Containment Need.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HPRM — Harm Propagation Module governs how an already governed Harm
materially extends into additional affected scope through direct,
indirect, multi-step, distributed, cross-domain, geographic,
organizational, infrastructural, environmental, or other materially
relevant propagation pathways. It governs Propagation Signals, origin,
pathway, scope, timing, velocity, magnitude, identity continuity, Boundary
interaction, uncertainty, cessation, and historical propagation
continuity. It does not determine whether Harm originally exists, perform
Recognition or Determination, independently redefine the Governed Harm
Boundary, classify or transition Harm State, govern additive Harm build-up
as Accumulation, govern interactive amplification as Compounding, govern
sequential downstream Harm formation as Cascading, perform Containment or
Mitigation, govern Recovery, Residual Harm, Resolution or Finality,
determine general causation, assign accountability or liability, or
replace operational reality, evidence, validation, integrity, prediction,
simulation, AI behavior, autonomous execution, identity, Value, tax-event,
or jurisdiction-specific governance.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Harm is not confined to the place where it began, the system that produced
it, or the subject first affected by it.

It may move through dependencies, networks, organizations, environments,
infrastructure, and domains long after the originating mechanism has
stopped.

HPRM keeps that spread governable while preserving the distinction between
expanded Harm, new downstream Harm, and the pathways connecting them.

Where Harm materially extends, governance must be capable of following it.
