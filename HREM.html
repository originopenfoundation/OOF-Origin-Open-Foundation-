HREM — Harm Recovery Evaluation Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HREM — Harm Recovery Evaluation Module governs how the achieved
condition of Recovery from governed Harm is evaluated after Recovery has
been initiated and its progression has become sufficiently observable.

HREM determines what materially relevant condition, function, capability,
stability, integrity, independence, performance, or other recoverable
state has actually been regained; how that achieved condition compares
with the Recovery Reference Condition and Recovery Target; what dimensions
remain incomplete, unstable, dependent, adapted, or impaired; and what
Recovery conclusion can legitimately be established without confusing
improvement, adaptation, restoration activity, or Mitigation with
Recovery.

It establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Recovery Progress State + Recovery Reference Condition +
Recovery Target → Recovery Basis → Achieved Condition Evaluation →
Recovery Extent → Recovery Stability & Dependency Assessment → Recovery
Determination → Governed Harm Recovery Evaluation

Its central question is:

What has actually recovered, to what extent, and what Recovery condition
can legitimately be established?

  • Governed Object


The primary governed object is:

Achieved Recovery Condition

HREM evaluates what condition has actually been regained.

It does not evaluate how much Recovery activity occurred.

Therefore:

Recovery Effort ≠ Recovery Achievement

and:

Recovery Process Completion ≠ Recovery Condition Achieved

  • Foundational Recovery Evaluation Principle


The foundational rule is:

Recovery must be evaluated against the condition actually regained, not
against the activity performed or the expectation that Recovery should
have occurred.

This means:

Recovery Intended ≠ Recovery Achieved

Recovery Program Completed ≠ Recovery Achieved

Positive Progress ≠ Complete Recovery

  • Reference-to-Current Comparison


HREM compares:

Recovery Reference Condition → Current Condition

This establishes what has materially changed since Recovery began.

The comparison may identify:

substantial improvement

partial improvement

no material improvement

mixed change

or:

deterioration

But change alone does not determine the complete Recovery conclusion.

  • Target-to-Current Comparison


HREM separately compares:

Recovery Target ↔ Current Condition

This answers:

How much of the governed Recovery objective has actually been achieved?

The two comparisons must remain distinct:

Improvement from Reference ≠ Achievement of Recovery Target

A condition may improve substantially while remaining far below its
legitimate Recovery Target.

  • Recovery Extent


Recovery may be evaluated by extent rather than through a binary
recovered/not-recovered model.

Relevant conditions may include:

Minimal Recovery

Partial Recovery

Substantial Recovery

Target Recovery Achieved

or:

Recovery Not Materially Established

The terminology must correspond to the actual governed condition rather
than administrative completion.

  • Partial Recovery


Partial Recovery exists where materially meaningful Recovery has occurred
but relevant Recovery dimensions remain incomplete.

Canonical principle:

Partial Recovery is a legitimate Recovery condition—not a methodological
failure.

Therefore:

Partial Recovery ≠ No Recovery

and:

Partial Recovery ≠ Complete Recovery

  • Substantial Recovery


Substantial Recovery may exist where a major portion of the relevant
condition has been regained while some Harm, impairment, dependency, or
limitation remains.

Substantial Recovery must not be converted automatically into:

Full Restoration

or:

No Remaining Harm

Therefore:

Substantial Recovery ≠ Harm Absence

  • Recovery Target Achievement


A Recovery Target may be achieved even where the post-Recovery condition
differs from the pre-Harm state.

This preserves the HRRHS principle:

Pre-Harm State ≠ Mandatory Recovery Endpoint

A legitimate Recovery Target may represent a new stable condition.

Therefore:

Recovery Target Achieved ≠ Historical State Restored

  • Recovery Beyond Target


Actual Recovery may exceed the originally established Recovery Target.

HREM must permit this condition without artificially capping Recovery at
the target.

Conceptually:

Achieved Condition > Recovery Target

where meaningful in the governed domain.

The target is a governance reference.

It is not a ceiling on actual Recovery.

  • Recovery Without Restoration


HREM explicitly permits legitimate Recovery where:

Post-Recovery State ≠ Pre-Harm State

A person, object, system, organization, infrastructure, environment, or
society may recover through a materially different condition.

Canonical principle:

Recovery is evaluated by what has been regained—not by whether history has
been recreated.

  • Functional vs Structural Recovery


HREM must distinguish materially different Recovery dimensions.

A physical or digital structure may remain changed while relevant function
has recovered.

Conversely, structure may be restored while function remains impaired.

Therefore:

Structural Restoration ≠ Functional Recovery

and:

Functional Recovery ≠ Structural Restoration

Neither dimension should silently substitute for the other.

  • Operational Recovery


A system or organization may regain operational capability while remaining
dependent on:

workarounds

substitutes

manual intervention

external support

or:

degraded operating modes

HREM may recognize genuine operational Recovery while preserving those
conditions.

Therefore:

Operational Function Restored ≠ Original Capability Restored

  • Adaptation and Recovery


Adaptation may coexist with genuine Recovery.

The evaluation must distinguish:

condition regained through Recovery

from:

function achieved by compensating around continuing Harm

Conceptually:

Observed Function = Recovery Contribution + Adaptation Contribution

where such separation is materially possible.

Canonical distinction:

Successful Adaptation ≠ Complete Recovery

  • Dependency-Supported Recovery


Recovery may depend on continuing support.

HREM must determine whether the achieved condition is:

Independent

Dependency-Supported

or:

Dependency-Critical

where that distinction materially affects the Recovery conclusion.

A supported condition may still represent legitimate Recovery.

But:

Dependency-Supported Recovery ≠ Independent Recovery

  • Recovery Stability


The achieved condition must be evaluated for sufficient stability relative
to the Recovery claim.

HREM may distinguish:

Emerging Recovery

Unstable Recovery

Conditionally Stable Recovery

and:

Stable Recovery

Canonical principle:

Recovery strength must not exceed demonstrated Recovery stability.

  • Temporary Recovery


A condition may temporarily reach the Recovery Target before
deteriorating.

Such a condition may legitimately represent:

Temporary Recovery

but not:

Stable Recovery

Therefore:

Target Reached Once ≠ Stable Recovery Achieved

  • Recovery Plateau Evaluation


A plateau requires interpretation.

It may indicate:

temporary pause

maximum currently achievable Recovery

dependency limitation

unresolved Harm

or:

uncertain future trajectory

Therefore:

Recovery Plateau ≠ Recovery Endpoint by Default

HREM evaluates what the plateau means before Residual Harm governance
begins.

  • Regression in Recovery Evaluation


If Recovery has materially regressed, HREM evaluates the current condition
rather than preserving the strongest historical condition.

Therefore:

Best Historical Recovery State ≠ Current Recovery Evaluation

The Recovery record may preserve earlier achievement while the current
evaluation reflects deterioration.

  • Composite Recovery Evaluation


Where multiple Harm components exist, HREM evaluates each materially
relevant Recovery dimension before establishing the composite condition.

For example:

Component A → Recovered

Component B → Partially Recovered

Component C → Unrecovered

The complete Recovery Evaluation must preserve this structure.

Therefore:

Recovered Component ≠ Recovered Composite Harm

  • Collective Recovery Evaluation


Where Harm affects multiple individuals, a defined group, organization,
population, or society, HREM evaluates Recovery at the governed collective
level.

Improvement among selected members cannot automatically establish
collective Recovery.

Therefore:

Recovery of Selected Individuals ≠ Collective Recovery

Distribution remains part of the evaluation.

  • Distributed Recovery Evaluation


Distributed Harm may produce materially different Recovery conditions
across:

locations

systems

organizations

infrastructure components

or:

operational environments

Therefore:

Local Recovery ≠ Distributed Recovery

HREM must preserve where Recovery has and has not occurred.

  • Cross-Domain Recovery Evaluation


Recovery may differ across domains.

For example:

Physical → Substantial Recovery

Operational → Target Achieved

Financial → Partial Recovery

Cognitive → Uncertain Recovery

The complete Recovery Evaluation must not be represented solely through
the strongest domain.

Therefore:

Recovery in One Domain ≠ Complete Cross-Domain Recovery

  • Unequal Recovery Distribution


Aggregate Recovery may hide materially important differences.

A population may show strong average improvement while a subgroup remains
severely harmed.

Therefore:

Average Recovery ≠ Recovery for Every Affected Subject

HREM preserves materially significant distributional differences.

  • Recovery Evaluation Under Continuing Harm


Recovery may coexist with continuing Harm production.

HREM can evaluate the Recovery that has occurred while preserving that
additional Harm continues.

Therefore:

Recovery Achieved in a Dimension ≠ Harm Production Stopped

The relevant HAGA™ lifecycle spaces remain active.

  • Recovery Evaluation and New Harm


New Harm arising during Recovery must remain separately visible.

A strong Recovery outcome for the original Harm does not erase newly
produced Harm.

Canonical principle:

Recovery from one Harm does not neutralize another Harm.

  • Minimum Implementation Framework


A minimum HREM implementation requires eight capabilities.

  • Recovery Evaluation Basis


Maintain sufficient linkage between the Recovery Reference Condition,
Recovery Target, Recovery Progress State, current Harm condition, and
current affected condition.

  • Achieved Condition Evaluation


Determine what function, capability, stability, integrity, independence,
performance, or other materially relevant condition has actually been
regained.

  • Recovery Extent Determination


Distinguish Partial, Substantial, Target-Achieved, Mixed, Temporary,
Regressed, and Uncertain Recovery without forcing binary conclusions.

  • Stability, Adaptation & Dependency Assessment


Determine whether apparent Recovery is stable, temporary,
adaptation-supported, dependency-supported, or otherwise conditional.

  • Composite, Collective, Distributed & Cross-Domain Evaluation


Preserve materially different Recovery conditions across components,
subjects, groups, systems, locations, and Harm domains.

  • Unrecovered Condition Preservation


Identify and preserve materially relevant conditions that remain
unrecovered without prematurely classifying them as Residual Harm.

  • Uncertainty, Reassessment & Evaluation History


Preserve conflicting indicators and uncertainty, trigger reassessment when
post-Harm reality changes, and maintain material Recovery Evaluation
history.

  • Canonical Boundary


The HREM — Harm Recovery Evaluation Module governs how the condition
actually regained through Recovery is evaluated against the Recovery
Reference Condition, Recovery Target, Recovery Progress State, current
Harm condition, and materially relevant post-Harm reality. It governs
Recovery extent, target achievement, functional, structural and
operational Recovery, stability, temporary and dependency-supported
Recovery, adaptation, plateau interpretation, regression, composite,
collective, distributed and cross-domain Recovery, unequal Recovery
distribution, uncertainty, unrecovered conditions, reassessment, and
Recovery Evaluation history. HREM may identify conditions that remain
unrecovered but does not formally determine them as Residual Harm. It does
not perform Harm Recognition or Determination, independently establish the
Harm Boundary, perform Containment or Mitigation, establish Recovery
Initiation, govern Recovery Progress as its primary object, formally
identify or classify Residual Harm, establish Resolution or Finality,
determine general causation, or assign accountability, responsibility,
liability, claims, or compensation.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Recovery cannot be established by the amount of effort invested, the
duration of a recovery process, or the fact that improvement occurred.

It must be evaluated through the condition actually regained, the
stability and scope of that condition, and the difference between what has
recovered and what remains unrecovered.

HREM establishes that boundary before remaining Harm is allowed to become
Residual Harm.

Recovery Evaluation determines what has truly returned before HAGA™
determines what continues to remain.
