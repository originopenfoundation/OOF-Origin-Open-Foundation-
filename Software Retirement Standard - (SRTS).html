Canonical Definition

Software Retirement Standard (SRTS) is the Parent Standard within
SLGA™ — Software Lifecycle Governance Architecture that governs how
software products, versions, components, services, dependencies, and
associated operational capabilities are identified, evaluated,
prepared, transitioned, withdrawn, and formally retired from their
applicable lifecycle and operational environments.

SRTS establishes software retirement as a governed transition from an

identifiable supported, maintained, deployed, or otherwise active
software condition toward a defined retirement state in which the
software's operational role, support obligations, dependencies, data
relationships, authority conditions, and residual responsibilities
have been appropriately resolved or transferred.

The standard distinguishes the decision to retire software from the actual

withdrawal of its capabilities, the termination of support from the
termination of operation, the removal of a software artifact from the
elimination of its dependencies, and administrative retirement from
verified operational retirement.

It requires retirement decisions and activities to preserve the identity of

the retiring software, the reasons for retirement, affected users and
systems, operational dependencies, migration requirements, data
continuity, support boundaries, transition conditions, withdrawal
evidence, and remaining obligations.

SRTS governs planned retirement, end-of-support transitions, replacement

and migration, obsolete software withdrawal, dependency
discontinuation, unsupported component removal, and retirement
resulting from security, operational, economic, architectural, or
external requirements.

It recognizes that software may cease to receive support while
remaining operational, that retired software may retain active
dependencies or historical obligations, and that retirement cannot be
considered complete merely because a product is no longer distributed
or maintained.

Its principal governance output is the Software Retirement Governance
Profile.

Operational Role

SRTS establishes the governance framework for the final transition of

software from active lifecycle participation toward an explicitly
defined retirement condition.

It governs:

  • Software retirement identification
  • Retirement purpose and justification
  • Retirement object identity
  • Retirement scope and boundaries
  • Retirement readiness
  • Retirement planning
  • Retirement decision authority
  • End-of-support conditions
  • End-of-maintenance conditions
  • End-of-operation conditions
  • Software replacement
  • Migration requirements
  • Dependency identification
  • Residual dependency control
  • Data continuity
  • Interface continuity

  • Operational transition

SRTS does not require all software to follow an identical retirement
pathway.

Its governance requirements adapt to the software's actual operational

role, dependencies, criticality, and retirement consequences.

Core Question

How can software be legitimately, safely, and verifiably retired when
its operational use, support obligations, dependencies, data, users,
authority relationships, and residual responsibilities may continue
beyond the decision to discontinue it?

Cross-cutting: Software Lifecycle Control

SRTS is the tenth Parent Standard within SLGA™.

It governs the retirement stage of the software lifecycle while recognizing

that retirement may trigger renewed activity within earlier lifecycle
mechanisms.

A retirement plan may require new software requirements, migration
software creation, dependency changes, configuration modifications,
security verification, release preparation, deployment transitions, or
change management.

Software retirement is therefore not necessarily a single terminal
action.

It may be a sequence of governed transitions involving multiple
software objects, environments, actors, and residual obligations.

  • Governed Space

Software Retirement Governance

This governed space concerns the controlled discontinuation, withdrawal,

replacement, migration, or retirement of identifiable software objects
and their associated operational roles.

It begins when a software object becomes subject to a retirement
proposal, requirement, or decision.

It includes the evaluation of retirement feasibility, consequences,

dependencies, migration pathways, operational continuity, support
obligations, and retirement conditions.

It continues through transition, withdrawal, verification, residual
dependency management, and formal closure.

The governed space remains applicable where a software object has been
declared retired but unresolved operational dependencies, data
relationships, or obligations continue to exist.

The standard applies to software operating in development, testing,

production, embedded, industrial, autonomous, and distributed
environments.

It does not govern the physical disposal of hardware except where such
disposal materially affects software retirement conditions or requires
interaction with another applicable governance mechanism.

Foundational Principle

Software retirement is complete only when the software's intended
operational withdrawal has been established and its remaining
dependencies, data relationships, support conditions, and obligations
have been appropriately resolved, transferred, or explicitly retained
under governance.

A retirement announcement does not establish operational retirement.

End of support does not establish end of use.

Artifact deletion does not establish dependency elimination.

Migration completion does not automatically establish retirement
closure.

Software Discontinued ≠ Software Retired.

End of Support ≠ End of Operation.

Artifact Removed ≠ Operational Dependency Eliminated.

Core Modules

SRTS is implemented through five Core Modules. 69.1 RPM — Retirement
Planning Module

Governed Space: Software Retirement Planning Governance

Governs retirement initiation, purpose, feasibility, scope, affected

software objects, operational conditions, retirement readiness,
required transition activities, sequencing, and retirement planning
evidence.

Principal Output: Software Retirement Plan Profile

69.2 ESMM — End-of-Support Management Module

Governed Space: Software End-of-Support Governance

Governs support and maintenance termination conditions, unsupported

software operation, continuing obligations, affected stakeholders,
operational restrictions, support transition evidence, and
end-of-support continuity.

Principal Output: Software End-of-Support Profile

69.3 SMM — Software Migration Module

Governed Space: Software Migration Governance

Governs the transition of software functions, users, data relationships,

operational capabilities, and relevant dependencies from retiring
software to replacement or successor environments.

Principal Output: Software Migration Governance Profile

69.4 RDMM — Residual Dependency Management Module

Governed Space: Software Retirement Residual Dependency Governance

Governs the identification, assessment, classification, ownership,

restriction, transfer, replacement, and resolution of dependencies
that remain associated with software undergoing or having undergone
retirement.

Principal Output: Software Residual Dependency Profile

69.5 RCCLM — Retirement Closure Control Module

Governed Space: Software Retirement Closure Governance

Governs retirement outcome verification, intended–actual retirement

alignment, withdrawal evidence, migration and dependency
reconciliation, residual obligations, retirement finality, and
legitimate closure disposition.

Principal Output: Software Retirement Closure Profile

are established through their respective full canonical publications.

Feasibility → Retirement Decision → Retirement Planning →

Requirements → Transition Coordination → Retirement Readiness →
Governed Withdrawal → Withdrawal Verification → Residual Dependency
Assessment → Residual Obligation Reconciliation → Retirement Closure

Where replacement is required:

Migration → Compatibility Verification → Operational Transition →
Withdrawal

Cross-Architecture and Lifecycle Boundary

SRTS governs retirement-specific lifecycle transitions.

It does not replace the governance of software requirements, creation,
dependencies, configuration, quality, security, release, deployment,
or change management. SRS — Software Requirements Standard

Governs requirements affecting retirement, replacement, and migration.
SCS — Software Creation Standard

Governs new software artifacts created for migration, replacement, or

retirement support. SDS — Software Dependency Standard

Governs dependency identity, suitability, introduction, change, and

continuity. SCFS — Software Configuration Standard

Governs configuration and artifact changes associated with retirement.
SQS — Software Quality Standard

Governs quality verification of replacement or modified software. SSS
— Software Security Standard

Governs security requirements, exposure, testing, vulnerabilities, and

remediation. SRLS — Software Release Standard

Governs release-related activities required for replacement or migration

software. SDPS — Software Deployment Standard

Governs deployment and operational transition activities. SCMS —
Software Change Management Standard

Governs identifiable software changes initiated or required by
retirement. SLCS — Software Lifecycle Control Standard

Governs lifecycle state consistency, transition control, exceptions,

authority transfer, and continuity.

Canonical Boundary

Software Retirement Standard (SRTS) governs the controlled transition of

identifiable software objects from active, supported, maintained,
deployed, or otherwise operational conditions toward defined
retirement states.

It establishes the governance requirements for retirement identification,

justification, planning, end-of-support management, migration,
dependency resolution, operational withdrawal, verification, residual
obligations, and closure.

SRTS does not require every software retirement to include replacement
or migration.

It does not establish that support termination automatically ends
operational use.

It does not treat artifact removal as proof that all dependencies have
been eliminated.

It does not replace specialized data, security, authority, or
operational governance mechanisms.

Its principal governance output is the:

Software Retirement Governance Profile

This output establishes the evidence-supported relationship between the

software being retired, its intended retirement state, the activities
performed, the operational consequences, and the obligations that
remain.

Canonical Closing Statement

Software does not cease to matter merely because an organization stops
maintaining, distributing, or using it.

An unsupported application may remain essential to an operational
process.

A retired library may remain embedded in an active product.

A discontinued interface may still serve external consumers.

A withdrawn software service may retain data that must remain
accessible.

An autonomous agent may stop executing while delegated permissions or
unresolved tasks continue to exist.

And a firmware version may remain installed in physical equipment long
after its official support period has ended.

SRTS establishes the governance structure required to distinguish

retirement decisions from retirement reality.

It requires software withdrawal to remain connected to the identity,

purpose, dependencies, operational role, and support conditions of the
retiring object.

It governs the transition of functions, users, data, and dependencies
where replacement or migration is necessary.

It preserves the distinction between ending support, ending maintenance,

ending execution, and achieving operational retirement.

It requires residual dependencies and obligations to remain identifiable

rather than disappearing behind an administrative retirement status.

And it establishes retirement closure only through an
evidence-supported determination of the actual retirement state and
the conditions that remain governed.

The retirement of software is not simply the end of its execution. It
is the controlled termination or transfer of its operational role,
dependencies, authority, and continuing obligations.

Software Retirement Standard (SRTS) transforms software retirement from a

discontinuation event into a verifiable lifecycle transition that
preserves operational integrity, dependency accountability, and
governance continuity beyond the software's active life.
