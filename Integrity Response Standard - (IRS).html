Governed Space

Integrity Response

Core Question

How should a governed entity respond once an integrity breach has been
verified?

Canonical Definition

Integrity Response Standard (IRS) defines the governance conditions required
to authorize, coordinate, execute, monitor, document, and govern responses to
verified integrity breaches. It establishes the response principles,
operational procedures, accountability requirements, escalation mechanisms,
communication rules, and governance controls required to contain integrity
failures, minimize operational impact, and preserve governance continuity
throughout the complete lifecycle of a governed entity.

Governance Flow Position

Definition → Structure → Conditions → Measurement → State → Deviation → Breach
→ Response → Restoration → Assurance

Standard Abstract

Recognizing an integrity breach alone is not sufficient.

Once a breach has been verified, governance must transition from detection to
controlled operational action. Every response must be timely, proportionate,
accountable, transparent, and fully auditable to prevent further integrity
degradation and protect the governed entity.

Building upon the verified integrity breaches established by IBS, Integrity
Response Standard defines how governed entities respond to integrity failures
before restoration activities begin.

IRS transforms operational reaction into a standardized governance process
supported by documented authority, controlled execution, and objective
accountability.

Operational Role

Integrity Response Standard governs the execution and management of integrity
responses. It establishes the governance framework required to authorize
response activities, coordinate operational actions, contain integrity
failures, manage escalation, communicate with stakeholders, and document
response execution under controlled governance.

Operational Space

IRS governs:

  • response authorization
  • response coordination
  • response execution
  • containment
  • escalation
  • stakeholder communication
  • response documentation.


This governed space ensures that every verified integrity breach is managed
through a consistent, accountable, transparent, and auditable governance
response across all operational environments.

Why This Standard Exists

A verified integrity breach requires more than recognition—it requires
governed action.

Without standardized response governance, organizations risk inconsistent
decision-making, delayed containment, uncontrolled escalation, fragmented
accountability, and increased operational damage.

IRS exists because Integrity Response represents the eighth governed space of
INTEGROS® — Integrity Governance Architecture, providing the canonical
governance framework for responding to verified integrity breaches before
integrity restoration and long-term assurance activities begin.

Foundational Principle

A verified integrity breach requires a governed response—not an improvised
reaction. Every integrity response shall be authorized, proportionate,
accountable, evidence-based, and fully auditable.

Canonical Closing Statement

Integrity Response Standard establishes the canonical governance framework for
authorizing, coordinating, executing, and governing responses to verified
integrity breaches. By defining how governed entities respond to material
integrity failures, IRS enables controlled containment, accountable
operational action, governance continuity, and structured transition toward
integrity restoration throughout the complete lifecycle of every governed
entity.
