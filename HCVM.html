HCVM — Harm Containment Verification Module

OOF® Origin Open Foundation®

Independent Methodological Authority

Canonical Definition

The HCVM — Harm Containment Verification Module governs how it is
determined whether the targeted further development of an already governed
Harm has actually been bounded or stopped following Containment
intervention.

HCVM verifies Containment by evaluating the resulting Harm condition
rather than treating action execution, mechanism shutdown, Boundary
establishment, control persistence, or absence of immediately observable
additional Harm as sufficient proof of successful Containment.

It determines whether the Containment Objective has been materially
achieved, whether relevant Harm pathways remain active, whether additional
Accumulation, Compounding, Propagation, Cascading, or other further Harm
development continues, whether Containment is complete, partial,
uncertain, degraded, escaped, or failed, and whether the resulting state
is sufficient for transition toward Harm Mitigation while continuing
Containment where required.

It establishes the transition:

Governed Harm Containment Need + Governed Harm Containment Action +
Governed Harm Containment Boundary + Governed Harm Containment Persistence
State → Verification Basis → Containment Effect Evaluation → Residual
Pathway Assessment → Verification Determination → Governed Harm
Containment State

Its central question is:

Has the targeted further development of Harm actually been bounded or
stopped, and what Containment state can legitimately be established?

Containment Verification

Harm Mitigation

  • Governed Object


The primary governed object is:

Verified Effect of Harm Containment

HCVM does not primarily verify whether:

an action was performed

a system was stopped

a barrier was installed

access was revoked

a pathway was blocked

or:

a procedure was completed

It verifies whether the targeted Harm development has actually stopped.

Therefore:

Intervention Completion ≠ Containment Verification

  • Foundational Verification Principle


The foundational rule is:

Containment must be verified by consequence, not merely by intervention
completion.

The governing chain is:

Action → Effect → Harm Dynamic → Verification

not:

Action Completed → Containment Assumed

  • Verification Basis


Verification must remain traceable to the original:

Containment Need

Containment Objective

Containment Action

Containment Boundary

and:

Persistence requirements

The question is not simply:

Did something improve?

It is:

Was the specific Harm development that Containment was intended to stop
actually stopped?

  • Objective-Based Verification


Every verification determination must evaluate the relevant Containment
Objective.

If the objective was:

prevent further Propagation beyond Scope A

verification must determine whether that Propagation stopped.

If the objective was:

prevent additional Accumulation

verification must determine whether further harmful contribution ceased.

If the objective was:

interrupt Harm₁ → Harm₂ → Harm₃ cascade development

verification must determine whether the targeted cascade pathway was
actually interrupted.

Canonical principle:

Containment can only be verified against the Harm dynamic it was intended
to contain.

  • Action Verification ≠ Containment Verification


An intervention may be successfully executed while Harm continues.

Examples:

System shut down → Harm continues through delegated execution

Barrier installed → Harm escapes through another pathway

Access revoked → alternative account continues activity

Source removed → accumulated Harm continues propagating

Therefore:

Verified Action ≠ Verified Containment

  • Mechanism Termination ≠ Containment Verification


Stopping the originating mechanism may be relevant evidence.

It is not sufficient by itself.

HCVM must determine whether the Harm remains capable of continuing
through:

existing effects

dependencies

secondary mechanisms

Propagation pathways

cascade chains

distributed execution

or:

previously initiated processes

Canonical principle:

Mechanism termination is evidence about the mechanism. Containment
Verification is a determination about Harm.

  • Boundary Verification


HCBM may establish an adequate Containment Boundary.

HCVM must determine whether Harm has nevertheless:

crossed

leaked through

bypassed

escaped

or:

developed outside

that boundary.

Therefore:

Adequate Boundary ≠ Verified Containment

  • Persistence Verification


HCPM may establish that required controls remain operationally persistent.

HCVM must still determine whether those controls are producing the
required Harm consequence.

Therefore:

Persistent Control ≠ Verified Containment Effect

Persistence concerns continued control effectiveness.

Verification establishes the resulting Containment determination.

  • Harm Dynamic Verification


HCVM should evaluate the materially relevant Harm dynamics identified
through HCPS and HCOS.

These may include whether:

additional Harm production continues

Accumulation continues

Compounding continues

Propagation continues

cascade development continues

new exposure continues

affected scope continues expanding

or:

new Harm continues emerging from the governed Harm pathway

Verification must be specific to the applicable dynamics.

  • Accumulation Verification


Where Containment targeted additional Accumulation, HCVM determines
whether further contribution has materially ceased.

It does not require the accumulated Harm already present to disappear.

Therefore:

Accumulation Stopped ≠ Accumulated Harm Eliminated

Successful Containment may coexist with substantial existing Harm.

  • Compounding Verification


Where Containment targeted a Compounding relationship, HCVM determines
whether the materially relevant interaction producing further
amplification has been interrupted.

Therefore:

Compounding Interrupted ≠ Compounded Harm Reversed

Existing compounded Harm may still require Mitigation and Recovery.

  • Propagation Verification


Where Containment targeted Propagation, HCVM determines whether the Harm
remains materially capable of extending into additional scope through the
targeted pathway.

Verification should account for:

known pathways

newly discovered pathways

delayed Propagation

distributed pathways

and:

uncertain pathway coverage

Canonical principle:

No Newly Observed Propagation ≠ Verified Propagation Containment by
Default

  • Cascade Verification


Where Containment targeted a Harm cascade, HCVM determines whether the
relevant downstream Harm-generating relationship has actually been
interrupted.

A stopped upstream mechanism does not prove that already initiated
downstream Harm has ceased producing further Harm.

Therefore:

Upstream Containment ≠ Complete Cascade Containment by Default

  • Composite Containment Verification


One Harm may depend on several controls or actions.

Conceptually:

Action₁ + Action₂ + Action₃ → Composite Containment

Verification must evaluate the composite Harm outcome.

If two components succeed but a third leaves an active Harm pathway:

Partial Component Success ≠ Complete Containment

  • Distributed Containment Verification


Distributed Harm may span:

agents

systems

providers

organizations

locations

infrastructure

or:

environments

HCVM must avoid treating local success as global Containment.

Canonical principle:

Local Containment ≠ Distributed Harm Containment by Default

  • Temporal Verification


Containment may appear successful immediately after intervention while
Harm later resumes.

HCVM must therefore consider whether the observation period is sufficient
for the Harm dynamic being evaluated.

Relevant factors may include:

Propagation delay

latency

restart potential

reconnection potential

cascade timing

environmental movement

and:

delayed manifestation

Canonical distinction:

Immediate Silence ≠ Persistent Harm Containment

  • No Observable Harm ≠ Verified Containment


Absence of currently observed additional Harm may have several
explanations:

Containment succeeded

Harm has not yet manifested

monitoring is incomplete

the pathway is latent

the effect is delayed

or:

the Harm moved outside observed scope

Therefore:

No Observed Additional Harm ≠ No Additional Harm by Default

  • Verification Uncertainty


Verification must preserve uncertainty where the available basis does not
support a complete determination.

Possible conditions include:

Incomplete Observation

Unknown Pathway

Insufficient Observation Duration

Uncertain Boundary Coverage

Uncertain Persistence

or:

Conflicting Harm Signals

Uncertainty must not be converted into successful Containment.

Canonical principle:

Insufficient Verification Basis ≠ Verified Containment

  • Partial Containment


HCVM must support legitimate Partial Containment.

Examples:

one Propagation pathway stopped while another remains active

some distributed systems contained while others remain uncertain

one cascade branch interrupted while another continues

Accumulation reduced but not stopped

The correct determination is:

Partially Contained

not:

Contained

  • Containment Degradation


Verification may establish that previously successful Containment is
degrading.

For example:

new pathway emerges

boundary weakens

control effectiveness declines

or:

Harm begins expanding again

A prior successful verification must not permanently lock the Harm into a
Contained state.

Canonical principle:

Verified Containment Is Reassessable When Material Harm Conditions Change.

  • Criteria for Contained State


A Harm should be classified as:

Contained

only where there is sufficient basis that:

the targeted further Harm development has materially stopped

the relevant Containment Objective has been achieved

material known pathways are under effective control

no material unresolved pathway contradicts the determination

and:

required Persistence conditions are sufficiently established

Containment does not require existing Harm to disappear.

  • Contained ≠ Stabilized


A Harm may be contained while its existing effects remain unstable.

Conversely, a Harm may appear temporarily stabilized while an uncontrolled
Propagation pathway remains active.

Therefore:

Contained ≠ Stabilized by Default

HSS owns Harm State governance.

  • Contained ≠ Mitigated


Containment concerns:

preventing additional Harm.

Mitigation concerns:

reducing existing Harm or its consequences.

Therefore:

Contained Harm May Still Require Extensive Mitigation

The HCVM output enables transition toward HMS without claiming Mitigation
has occurred.

  • Contained ≠ Recovered


Affected subjects, systems, objects, environments, or organizations may
remain significantly impaired after Harm has been contained.

Therefore:

Contained ≠ Recovered

Recovery belongs to HRRHS.

  • Contained ≠ Resolved


Containment does not establish that the Harm lifecycle has reached
Resolution.

A contained Harm may still involve:

existing effects

Mitigation

Recovery

Residual Harm

and:

continuing governance

Therefore:

Containment Success ≠ Harm Resolution

  • Contained ≠ Final


Even a contained, mitigated, and recovered Harm may still require later
Resolution and Finality governance.

Therefore:

No Further Harm Expansion ≠ Harm Lifecycle Finality

HCVM must never convert Containment into lifecycle closure.

A minimum HCVM implementation requires eight capabilities.

  • Verification Objective Mapping


Link Verification directly to the Governed Harm Containment Need,

  • Harm Dynamic Effect Assessment


Determine whether targeted additional Harm production, Accumulation,

  • Pathway & Coverage Verification


Assess known Harm pathways, Boundary coverage, distributed execution,

  • Temporal & Persistence Verification


Determine whether apparent Containment remains valid across a sufficient

Represent Partial Containment, uncertainty, degradation, Escape, and

Establish the legitimate current Governed Harm Containment State using

  • Reverification & Lifecycle Handoff


Trigger reverification when material conditions change and transfer the
verified state toward Harm Mitigation and continuing Harm State
governance.

  • Governed Output


Produce a traceable Governed Harm Containment State together with its
basis, uncertainty, limitations, and reassessment conditions. 38. Use Case
1 — System Shut Down but Distributed Execution Continues

An autonomous system producing Harm is shut down successfully.

Operational records confirm the shutdown.

However, delegated execution previously initiated in an external
environment remains active and continues producing the targeted Harm
consequence.

HCVM therefore rejects:

Verified Shutdown → Verified Containment

and establishes:

Containment Failed / Partially Contained

according to the remaining Harm pathways.

The governing principle is:

Containment is verified against Harm continuation, not against the
successful shutdown of the system that originally initiated it.

  • Use Case 2 — Propagation Stops but Existing Harm Remains


An environmental Harm is propagating toward additional affected areas.

Containment intervention successfully blocks the relevant pathway, and
sufficient observation establishes that further Propagation has stopped.

Significant Harm remains within the already affected area.

HCVM can establish:

Contained

without claiming:

Mitigated

Recovered

Resolved

or:

Final

The existing Harm then continues through subsequent HAGA™ lifecycle
governance.

The governing principle is:

Successful Containment prevents additional Harm; it does not erase Harm
already produced.

  • Governance Requirements


HCVM requires that:

  • Verification evaluate Harm consequence rather than intervention


completion alone; - every determination remain linked to the relevant
Containment Objective; - action execution remain distinct from Containment
effect; - mechanism termination not automatically establish Containment; -
Boundary adequacy remain distinct from verified Containment; - Persistence
remain necessary but not sufficient for final Verification; -
Accumulation, Compounding, Propagation, and Cascading be verified
according to their relevant dynamics; - composite and distributed
Containment be assessed at the appropriate complete Harm level; -
observation duration reflect materially relevant latency and recurrence
conditions; - absence of observed additional Harm not automatically
establish Containment; - verification uncertainty remain explicit; -
incomplete evidence not be converted into successful Containment; -
Partial Containment remain representable; - degradation, Escape, and
Failure trigger reassessment; - Containment state remain current and
revisable; - Contained remain distinct from Stabilized, Mitigated,
Recovered, Resolved, and Final; - HCVM support transition into Harm
Mitigation without claiming lifecycle completion; - reverification occur
when material conditions change; - Verification history remain traceable.

  • Governance Logic


HCVM applies the following logic:

Does a Governed Harm Containment Need exist?

→ identify what further Harm was intended to be prevented.

Was a Governed Harm Containment Action established and executed?

→ record execution but do not infer success.

Was a Governed Harm Containment Boundary established?

→ assess whether the relevant Harm pathways fall within effective control.

Are required Persistence conditions established?

→ determine whether Containment can remain effective.

Then ask:

Has the targeted Harm dynamic actually stopped?

Evaluate, where applicable:

additional Harm production

Accumulation

Compounding

Propagation

Cascading

new exposure

and:

scope expansion

Then ask:

Do any material pathways remain active?

If yes:

→ establish Partial, Degraded, Escaped, or Failed Containment as
appropriate.

Is the available Verification basis incomplete or uncertain?

If yes:

→ establish Containment Uncertain rather than Contained.

Has the objective been materially achieved across the relevant Harm scope
with sufficient Persistence?

If yes:

→ establish:

Contained

Then:

Governed Harm Containment State → Harm Mitigation Need

  • Canonical Boundary


The HCVM — Harm Containment Verification Module governs whether the
targeted further development of an already governed Harm has actually been
bounded or stopped following Containment intervention. It governs
Verification basis, objective achievement, Harm-dynamic cessation, pathway
and coverage assessment, temporal sufficiency, Persistence relevance,
Partial Containment, uncertainty, degradation, Escape, Failure,
Containment State determination, reverification, and lifecycle handoff.
HCVM does not determine whether Harm originally exists, perform Harm
Recognition or Determination, independently define the Governed Harm
Boundary, establish the original Containment Need, select or execute
Containment actions, establish the Containment Boundary, replace
Containment Persistence governance, perform Harm Mitigation, govern
Recovery or Residual Harm, establish Resolution or Finality, determine
general causation, or assign accountability, responsibility, or liability.

  • Canonical Closing Statement


Containment is not proven because an intervention happened, a system
stopped, a boundary was established, or a control remained active.

It is proven only when the Harm development that the intervention was
intended to stop has actually been bounded or stopped on a sufficient
governed basis.

HCVM therefore closes the Containment governance loop by moving the
determination from action to consequence.

Containment is verified by what Harm can no longer continue to do.
