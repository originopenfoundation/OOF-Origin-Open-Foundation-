Canonical Definition

Lifecycle Re-entry Control Module (LRECM) is the Core Module within
Software Change Management Standard (SCMS) that governs how proposed,
approved, or materially evolving software changes are directed into
the applicable software lifecycle governance mechanisms according to
their assessed consequences, affected software objects, existing
evidence, operational conditions, and required verification
obligations.

LRECM establishes lifecycle re-entry as a controlled, evidence-supported

relationship between an identifiable software change and the specific
lifecycle governance activities that must be performed, repeated,
extended, or verified because of that change.

The module distinguishes the need to revisit a lifecycle governance

mechanism from the need to repeat every activity previously performed
within it, the existence of historical verification evidence from its
continued applicability, and the authorization of a change from the
satisfaction of its lifecycle obligations.

It requires each material lifecycle re-entry decision to preserve its

basis, affected governance mechanisms, entry conditions, required
activities, evidence reuse decisions, completion criteria, applicable
authority, and unresolved obligations.

LRECM recognizes that software changes may originate at any lifecycle
stage and that their consequences may require movement backward,
forward, or across the established software lifecycle governance
structure.

It applies to human-directed modifications, automated development

processes, dependency changes, security remediation, production
corrections, AI-generated software changes, autonomous agents, and
software changes affecting embedded, industrial, or robotic systems.

Its principal governance output is the Software Lifecycle Re-entry
Profile .

Operational Question

How can a software change be directed back into precisely those
lifecycle governance mechanisms materially affected by its
consequences, while preserving applicable evidence, avoiding
unjustified repetition, preventing necessary verification from being
bypassed, and ensuring that every required lifecycle obligation
remains identifiable and verifiably satisfied?

LRECM is the fourth Core Module within SCMS.

CRMM establishes the change request.

CIAM identifies the consequences and affected governance mechanisms.

CHCM establishes material coordination relationships, prerequisites, and

sequencing conditions.

LRECM determines the required lifecycle governance pathway and controls the

traceability of the change through the applicable lifecycle
mechanisms.

CCLM subsequently evaluates whether the resulting change and its

obligations have been appropriately resolved.

Lifecycle re-entry may be reassessed repeatedly when new evidence,
changed scope, or operational deviations alter the applicable
governance requirements.

Foundational Principle

A software change must re-enter every lifecycle governance mechanism
materially affected by the change, but it must not be forced through
unrelated mechanisms without a justified governance need.

Lifecycle governance must be proportional to the actual change
consequences.

A documentation correction may require limited governance activity.

A security-sensitive dependency replacement may require dependency,
configuration, quality, security, release, and deployment
reassessment.

A change to robotic control logic may require renewed functional,
integration, operational, and physical-system verification.

The appropriate pathway depends on the governed reality rather than the

apparent size of the modification.

Lifecycle Re-entry ≠ Complete Lifecycle Repetition.

Historical Evidence Available ≠ Historical Evidence Still Applicable.

Change Authorized ≠ Lifecycle Obligations Satisfied.

Re-entry Necessity

LRECM must determine whether a proposed change materially affects an
existing lifecycle governance conclusion.

Relevant questions include:

Does the change alter a governed software object?

Does it invalidate an existing requirement?

Does it introduce or modify a dependency?

Does it affect configuration or artifact identity?

Does it alter quality or security conditions?

Does it affect release or deployment readiness?

Does it change operational assumptions?

Does it introduce new verification obligations?

A negative re-entry determination must be supported by a documented
assessment basis.

Governance Requirements

A conforming LRECM implementation must satisfy the following
requirements. Re-entry Identity and Determination

  • Establish a stable lifecycle re-entry case identity
  • Associate the case with an identifiable software change
  • Preserve relevant impact assessment references
  • Preserve relevant coordination references
  • Identify the affected software objects
  • Establish the current lifecycle state
  • Determine whether lifecycle re-entry is required
  • Preserve the basis for the determination

Scope and Governance Mapping

  • Identify materially affected Parent Standards
  • Identify applicable Core Modules
  • Establish required lifecycle activities
  • Define the re-entry scope
  • Preserve justified exclusions
  • Identify cross-standard dependencies
  • Establish required activity sequencing
  • Identify permitted parallel activities

Evidence and Verification

  • Identify relevant historical evidence
  • Evaluate historical evidence applicability
  • Preserve evidence reuse decisions
  • Identify required reverification
  • Identify new governance obligations
  • Establish required evidence for each activity
  • Preserve specialized verification references
  • Prevent unsupported evidence reuse

Authority and Conditions

  • Identify applicable decision authority
  • Preserve execution authority boundaries
  • Establish entry conditions
  • Identify prerequisite activities
  • Preserve applicable restrictions
  • Govern emergency re-entry conditions
  • Preserve exception authority and scope
  • Prevent re-entry decisions from substituting for specialized

authorization.

Execution and Continuity

  • Preserve lifecycle activity status
  • Maintain execution traceability
  • Identify material deviations
  • Establish reassessment triggers
  • Preserve material pathway revisions
  • Verify required activity completion
  • Identify partial completion
  • Preserve unresolved obligations
  • Maintain historical lifecycle continuity
  • Establish evidence-supported re-entry classification
  • Provide structured references to CCLM
  • Prevent incomplete lifecycle processing from being presented as

fully satisfied.

Governance Logic

Software Change → Impact Assessment → Coordination References → Lifecycle

Re-entry Trigger → Current Lifecycle State → Affected Governance
Mechanisms → Re-entry Necessity → Re-entry Scope → Historical Evidence
→ Evidence Applicability → Required Activities → Entry Conditions →
Authority References → Lifecycle Sequence → Governed Processing →
Verification Evidence → Completion Assessment → Residual Obligations →
Software Lifecycle Re-entry Profile

Where historical evidence remains applicable:

Existing Evidence → Applicability Assessment → Reuse Decision → Preserved

Evidence Reference

Where evidence is invalidated:

Material Change → Invalidated Assumption → Reverification Requirement →

Specialized Governance Activity

Where multiple standards are affected:

Change Impact → Affected Standards → Dependency Mapping → Coordinated

Re-entry Pathway

Where emergency processing is required:

Emergency Condition → Applicable Authority → Accelerated Pathway →

Preserved Mandatory Obligations → Deferred Verification Tracking

Where an autonomous agent participates:

Agent Objective → Applicable Authority → Permitted Lifecycle Activity →

Tool-Path Boundary → Execution Evidence → Verification

Where implementation deviates:

Established Pathway → Material Deviation → Reassessment → Revised Pathway

/ Restriction / Escalation

Minimum Implementation Framework

A minimum LRECM implementation must establish eight governance layers.
Layer 1 — Change and Lifecycle Identity

Identify the software change, current lifecycle state, relevant impact

assessment, and coordination references. Layer 2 — Governance Mapping

Determine which Parent Standards, Core Modules, and governance requirements

are materially affected. Layer 3 — Evidence Applicability

Identify historical evidence, assess its continued validity, and establish

reuse or reverification requirements. Layer 4 — Re-entry Pathway

Define required activities, applicable exclusions, sequencing,

dependencies, and completion conditions. Layer 5 — Authority and
Preconditions

Identify responsible functions, decision boundaries, execution permissions,

mandatory prerequisites, and applicable exceptions. Layer 6 — Governed
Processing

Preserve the status and evidence of required lifecycle activities without

replacing specialized governance mechanisms. Layer 7 — Verification
and Continuity

Assess completion, identify deviations, preserve reassessment triggers, and

maintain unresolved obligations. Layer 8 — Governed Output and Handoff

Establish the Software Lifecycle Re-entry Profile and provide

evidence-supported references to CCLM.

The implementation may be manual, workflow-supported, automated, or
AI-assisted.

The software used to implement LRECM must adapt to its governance
requirements rather than redefine them.

Cross-Module Boundary

CRMM → LRECM

CRMM establishes the original change request identity, purpose, preliminary

scope, and evidence.

LRECM preserves these references while determining the applicable lifecycle

pathway.

CIAM → LRECM

CIAM identifies direct and indirect consequences, uncertainty, verification

implications, and affected lifecycle mechanisms.

LRECM determines which governance activities must be performed or
revisited.

CHCM → LRECM

CHCM establishes coordination dependencies, actor relationships,

compatibility conditions, and sequencing constraints.

LRECM incorporates applicable coordination conditions into the re-entry

pathway.

LRECM → CCLM

LRECM establishes the required lifecycle obligations and their completion

status.

CCLM evaluates the resulting change outcome, outstanding obligations, and

legitimate closure disposition.

Lifecycle Processing Completed ≠ Change Outcome Verified.

Practical Applications

Enterprise Software Maintenance

Directing changes into only those lifecycle governance activities

materially affected by the modification.

Continuous Integration and Delivery

Determining which verification and governance stages must be repeated
after changes to software artifacts, dependencies, or configurations.

Security Remediation

Establishing appropriate dependency, configuration, quality, security,

release, and deployment re-entry after vulnerability corrections.

AI-Assisted Software Development

Preventing AI-generated patches from bypassing necessary lifecycle

governance while avoiding unnecessary repetition of unaffected
activities.

Autonomous Software Operations

Constraining agents to legitimately assigned lifecycle activities and

preserving the distinction between automated execution and governance
completion.

Critical Infrastructure

Establishing traceable lifecycle re-entry for changes affecting service

continuity, security, operational restrictions, or recovery
capability.

Robotics and Embedded Systems

Determining when firmware or control logic modifications require
renewed integration, operational, or physical-system verification.

Use Case 1 — A Security Dependency Update Requires Selective Lifecycle

Re-entry

A software team identifies a security vulnerability in a third-party

dependency.

A corrected dependency version is available.

The proposed update does not alter the application's stated functional
requirements.

However, the new dependency version changes internal interfaces and

introduces different runtime behavior.

  • Configuration and build compatibility must be verified

Canonical Boundary

LRECM governs the determination, structuring, evidence applicability,

sequencing, execution traceability, and verification of software
lifecycle governance activities required because of a proposed or
implemented software change.

It establishes the relationship between assessed change consequences and

the specific lifecycle mechanisms that must be performed, repeated,
extended, or verified.

It preserves justified evidence reuse, required reverification, authority

boundaries, exceptions, material deviations, and unresolved lifecycle
obligations.

LRECM does not replace specialized lifecycle standards, independently
authorize implementation, or establish final change closure.

Its principal governance output is the:

Software Lifecycle Re-entry Profile

This output provides the evidence-supported record of which lifecycle

governance mechanisms were required, why they were required, how their
obligations were addressed, and which conditions remain unresolved.

Canonical Closing Statement

Software lifecycle governance loses its meaning when changes can
invalidate earlier conclusions without triggering the activities
necessary to establish their continued validity.

A security patch may require renewed dependency and quality
assessment.

A configuration change may invalidate previously verified runtime
conditions.

A new interface may require integration testing even when its
individual components function correctly.

A deployment correction may require renewed release and production
authorization.

An AI-generated modification may satisfy development tests while
remaining outside its legitimate operational authority.

And a minor change may require extensive governance because of the
consequences it introduces, while a larger change may leave some
historical evidence fully applicable.

LRECM establishes the methodological mechanism required to govern these

differences.

It does not require every software change to restart the entire
lifecycle.

It does not permit necessary lifecycle obligations to disappear merely
because a modification is urgent, automated, or technically simple.

Instead, it determines which governance mechanisms are materially
affected, which historical evidence remains applicable, which
activities must be repeated or extended, and which conditions must be
verified before the resulting software state can be considered
adequately governed.

It preserves the distinction between a completed technical action and a

satisfied governance requirement.

It maintains continuity across lifecycle revisions, exceptions,

reassessments, and automated execution.

And it ensures that unresolved obligations remain visible until they
are legitimately resolved or transferred.

The purpose of lifecycle re-entry is not to repeat the past. It is to
determine which conclusions from the past remain valid after software
reality has changed—and to re-establish those that no longer do.

Lifecycle Re-entry Control Module (LRECM) transforms software change

processing into a proportional, evidence-supported mechanism for
preserving lifecycle governance integrity as software evolves.
